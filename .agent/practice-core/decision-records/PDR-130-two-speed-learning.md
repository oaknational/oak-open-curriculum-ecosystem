## Context

This estate's learning pipeline (PDR-014) runs at one speed: fast. A
lesson is captured in the napkin, distilled, and graduated to a rule,
skill clause, or validator — often within a day. This is a strength for
operational lessons: the pipeline's far end is _enforcement_, and lessons
here genuinely become machine checks. But the same day-scale mill is the
only path for everything, including constitutional-class concepts —
changes to how the estate learns, decides, and frames its work. Those
graduate on worked instances plus owner judgement: retrospective success
narratives, with no prospective prediction, no comparator, and no
scheduled review. An estate that promotes its own mechanisms on narrative
cannot tell its compounding infrastructure from its elaborate
self-portraiture.

## Decision

The estate learns at **two declared speeds**, and **every graduation at
either speed carries a prediction**.

1. **The fast lane is unchanged in mechanics** — napkin → distilled →
   graduation to rules, skills, and validators, at whatever pace the
   evidence supports — **plus one new obligation**: every graduation
   states, in one sentence, its **expected observable effect and its
   falsifier** ("this rule should make X stop happening; if X recurs
   twice with the rule loaded, the rule is not working"). The sentence
   lives in the graduated artefact or the register row that promoted it.
2. **The slow lane is new**: concepts that would change how the estate
   learns, decides, or frames work — learning-process changes,
   organisational doctrine, deep frames imported from research — enter a
   **slow-lane register** instead of the daily mill. A slow-lane entry
   is a live deliverable under a NAMED gate: it carries the concept, its
   prediction (an observable effect expected within the review window),
   its falsifier, and a **review date** (default: quarterly) — the gate
   at which it MUST be decided. Promotion to PDR or directive happens
   **only at review, only if the prediction held**; a fired falsifier
   kills the entry with its reasoning retained (the estate's conservation
   doctrine — the kill is recorded, not erased). There is no third
   outcome and no undated holding state.
3. **Register home**: a `## Slow lane` section of the pending-graduations
   register — the existing graduation register, one home for both speeds.
   Slow-lane rows are NOT decision-debt in the register's drain metric:
   each is gated on its named review date, is decided at that date, and a
   dedicated consolidation passes it by unless its date has arrived.
4. **Proportionality guard**: the slow lane is for constitutional-class
   change only. Routing an ordinary operational lesson into the slow lane
   to slow it down is invented optionality; routing a constitutional
   change through the fast lane to expedite it is the failure this record
   exists to prevent. When in doubt, the question is: does this change
   how we work, or how we _change how we work_?

## Consequences

- Practice changes acquire a track record: after a few quarters the
  estate can answer "which of our own changes worked?" from records
  rather than recall — meta-learning in the strict sense, measurable.
- Deep concepts get a legitimate slow home that is neither a same-day
  graduation nor an unbounded backlog: gated WITH a prediction and a
  date, so slowness is a commitment, not a shelf.
- The cost is one sentence per graduation and one register review per
  quarter — deliberately below the threshold where the discipline itself
  becomes overhead.

## Falsifiability (PDR-026)

This record is self-referential by design: if the slow-lane register is
untouched at its first quarterly review (no entry promoted, killed, or
reviewed), the lane is theatre — retire this PDR under its own rule. For
the fast lane: if graduations routinely land without prediction lines
three months after adoption, the obligation has no traction and needs a
mechanical check or retirement.
