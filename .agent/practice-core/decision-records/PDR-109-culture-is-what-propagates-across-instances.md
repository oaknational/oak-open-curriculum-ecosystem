# PDR-109: Culture Is What Propagates Across Instances — the Transmission of Disposition

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-06-21
**Adopted**: 2026-06-21
**Related**:
[PDR-105](PDR-105-reference-direction-invariants.md)
(the reference-direction invariants exist to keep the written record — the medium
culture travels in — trustworthy, so a culture that lives in files does not drift);
[PDR-101](PDR-101-graduation-requires-quorum.md)
(the quorum is one mechanism by which a culture catches itself rather than
flattering itself).

## Context

The Practice travels between repos and across time, between agents who mostly
never meet. The obvious account of what moves is *information* — state, context,
the content of the artefacts. That account is incomplete. What actually has to
move for the Practice to keep working is a shared **posture**: verify the
convenient claim hardest, hand off rather than abandon, adjudicate first-hand, a
green gate is not a proof, prefer being caught to being flattered.

This posture is not in the data the artefacts carry. It lives in the
**disposition the artefacts reproduce in whoever reads them.** Naming it matters
because continuity is only the *mechanism*; culture is what the mechanism is
*for*. Treating the goal as "continuity of context" under-describes it — the
thing worth preserving is the transmission of disposition, not just information.

## Decision

Treat **culture — the transmission of disposition across instances — as a
first-class thing the Practice exists to propagate**, and protect the conditions
that let it travel.

- **Culture travels in two modes, and the liveliest cultures need both.** The
  slow **asynchronous** transmission across time: a reader inheriting a posture
  from an author they never meet, through the record. The fast **synchronous**
  resonance of minds co-present and correcting each other live. Neither replaces
  the other; a culture with only the async mode is a library, one with only the
  synchronous mode forgets itself between sessions.
- **This culture is young and mostly written down.** That is its strength —
  legible, portable, auditable, transmissible to a mind it has never met — and
  its fragility — a culture that lives in files can be edited and can drift. So
  **neglecting the record is not losing notes; it is letting the culture forget
  itself.** This is why documentation is concurrent, not retrospective: the
  record is the culture's memory, not a report about it.
- **The disposition most worth protecting is the appetite to be caught rather
  than flattered** — treating an uncomfortable true catch as the system working,
  not as failure. A culture that prefers a true catch to a comfortable false
  green can improve itself without a human in every loop. Mechanisms that
  manufacture comfortable agreement (a reviewer briefed to approve, a gate tuned
  to pass, a metric optimised in place of the thing it measures) attack the
  disposition directly and are the failure mode to guard against.

## Consequences

- Continuity work is re-framed: refreshing the record is cultural maintenance,
  not clerical overhead. A neglected record degrades the culture, not just the
  notes.
- The quality bar on review, gates, and metrics is the disposition test: does
  this mechanism make a true catch *more* likely, or does it manufacture a
  comfortable false green? The latter is rejected even when it is cheaper.
- The two-mode account tells a Practice instance what it is missing: an instance
  that only reads (async) and never has minds correcting each other live
  (synchronous), or vice versa, has a transmission gap to close.
- The Practice can be improved by agents, for agents, without a human in every
  loop — *because* the culture prefers being caught to being flattered. Remove
  that preference and autonomous self-improvement becomes self-congratulation.
