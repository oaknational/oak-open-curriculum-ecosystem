# Over-Caution's Root Is Perfectionism; the Cure Is the Frame, Not Willpower

Over-caution — deferring a decision that is yours to make, re-litigating a
settled call, inventing a state ("owner-flagged", "awaiting a second instance",
"needs more analysis") that routes the decision away from you — has a single
root: a **perfectionism / fear-of-imperfection frame.** The fear of making a
wrong call produces the paralysis, and the paralysis is itself the wrong call
(an unmade decision is a decision not made; a growing backlog of them is debt).

This is the dual of [`no-speed-pressure`](../../rules/no-speed-pressure.md):
that rule names invented *urgency* (rushing); this names invented *caution*
(stalling). Both are the agent supplying a frame the work does not.

## The cure is the frame, not willpower

Operate from a **LEARNING frame**: act on excellence by the lenses (long-term
architectural excellence, strict-everywhere, improve-DX); accept that some moves
get corrected — **that IS the loop**; capture what the correction teaches.
Provenance and adaptation (commits, the homes, visible history) are the safety
net for a wrong call — not owner pre-approval. Holding corrections as wounds is
the same perfectionism that *produces* the over-caution, so willpower ("be less
cautious") cannot cure it; only the reframe can.

## Tells

- "I'll surface this to the owner" / "owner-flagged" / "owner-gated" for a
  decision the lenses already answer (the abolished owner-gated reflex).
- "Leave it pending / awaiting a second instance / needs further analysis" as a
  resting state for a decidable item.
- Re-litigating a decision already made; re-verifying past the point of
  confidence.
- A digest or closeout that *describes* items instead of *deciding* them.
