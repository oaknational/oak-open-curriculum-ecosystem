> **POLARITY: PATTERN.** A diagnostic tell: accumulating pairwise
> cross-links is evidence you are circling one lever you have not yet
> named. Stop linking; name the lever.

## The shape

When an analysis keeps producing pairwise connections — A relates to B, B
echoes C, C constrains A — the connections are usually projections of a
single underlying mechanism none of the pairs names. The tell fires on the
third pairwise link: stop and ask *"what one thing, if named, makes these
links corollaries?"*

Naming the lever changes the work: separate "related" items become typed
instances of one system, and apparent defects can invert (a "bug" may be the
lever working correctly on your side of a seam — the staleness lives
upstream).

Distinct from `design-from-the-substrate-not-the-instance` (the design
directive this tell often leads to): this is the *diagnostic* that tells you
mid-analysis that a substrate exists; that directive governs what to do once
it is named.
