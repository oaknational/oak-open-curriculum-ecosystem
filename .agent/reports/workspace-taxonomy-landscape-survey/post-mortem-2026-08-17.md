## 1 · The drift chain

The programme's founding ask and its final state are the same ask.
What happened in between was drift through elaboration:

- **2026-08-02** — the thread founds on the owner's question: which
  repeated TypeScript structures and algorithms should be generated,
  shared, inlined, deleted, or kept, and which context-independent
  responsibilities could earn promotion into meticulously assured
  core packages. This is, recognisably, the five-point brief's point 3.
- **2026-08-12** — owner cards ratify a census (workspace-scale
  Oak-specificity evidence) and a deconstruction of the proven
  machinery, with the fresh design to deliver "a reference corpus of
  foundational patterns PLUS the estate measured against it". Still
  the original ask, at survey grain.
- **2026-08-14** — the census runs twice; the owner's round-1 card
  supplies back-to-basics questions (what transformations, who
  consumes, could it be simpler split more) and round 2 produces a
  34→66 target inventory, confirmed at a card. The workspace TAXONOMY
  has now become the object of the work.
- **2026-08-16/17** — a basis panel grades axes; the owner reopens the
  space ("the original target architecture was WRONG"); the landscape
  survey is born as the primary search instrument; round 1a (46
  walkers) and round 1b (77 seats: baselines, decoys, falsifier arms,
  cross-vendor codex relays) execute; a round-2 grounded adjudication
  is designed and ratified.
- **2026-08-17 evening** — the owner cancels the programme and states
  what he actually wants in five lines. Point 3 is the 2026-08-02
  founding ask, near-verbatim. The programme had drifted from
  "identify and assure the foundational building blocks" to "search
  the space of workspace taxonomies" — a means elaborated until it
  replaced its end.

At each boundary (census → decomposition; decomposition → basis
question; basis → landscape search; 1a → 1b scale-up) the work
re-anchored on the previous elaboration rather than the founding ask.
`re-apply-first-question-at-elaboration-boundaries` names exactly this
firing moment; it did not fire at any of the four.

## 2 · The category errors

1. **Owner-fact questions measured by fleet.** The repo's structure
   was a ratified, actively-amended owner decision (ADR-041 and its
   lineage); edit authority lives in the owner's word, not in the
   bytes; and the genuinely open grouping question was already scoped
   in the owner's own note and the census's `openQuestionsForOwner`.
   The fleets measured "what should the architecture be" as if it
   were territory to be surveyed. It was a question with an owner.
2. **Goal projection.** The programme projected a goal — find the
   best workspace taxonomy — that the owner never held. His goal was
   the toolkit; the taxonomy was at most a consequence.
3. **Instrument self-ratification reported as confirmation.** Fleet
   convergence was reported as evidence about the territory while the
   instrument taught the vocabulary it then measured (the 1b
   instrument-falsifier's own finding, corroborated independently by
   reducer Y). Convergence of many agents on one frame is evidence
   about the frame's transmissibility, not its truth.

## 3 · The warrant gap

Every internal check the programme ran — fleet-design review,
falsifier arms, cricket passes, adversarial panels — audited the
INSTRUMENT: its leaks, its controls, its budgets, its schemas. None
asked the warrant question: what decision does this spend inform,
whose question is it, and has the owner seen the warrant? The
fleet-design review caught a verdict-invalidating leak (worth its
cost) and still approved a fleet whose question was not ours to
answer. Only the owner's outside read caught the category error.

Candidate amendment, routed for owner ratification and not enacted by
this record: the fleet-design-review rule opens with the warrant
gate — the what/why/value statement, owner-seen, before instrument
mechanics are examined at all (`spend-scales-certainty-of-measurement`
made the review's first clause).

## 4 · The race/provenance sub-incident

The 1b CLI relay arm gave every concurrent codex seat the same scratch
filenames in one shared scratchpad. One seat's prompts were overwritten
mid-window (detected, refused, arm lost); two seats returned
byte-identical designs across different groundings (silent, one
observation masquerading as two). Owner-directed forensics measured
7/28 crossed spawns; every CLI-relayed row was STRUCK — unverifiable
provenance is struck, never down-weighted — and all 16 tasks re-ran
collision-free (16/16 schema-valid, first attempt), reversing the
manufactured terra/sol support for the tiered shape. The owner's
principle, verbatim, now standing doctrine in memory and the fleet
lessons: "nothing we do should ever, ever rely on timing or races, we
build things so they WORK."

## 7 · What the Practice caught, and what only the owner caught

Caught internally, by the machinery working as designed: the relay
race (annotated, struck, re-run); the budget overshoots (measured and
banked, twice); the ADR-041 blindness leak (fleet-design review,
pre-launch); the 1a incumbent's refutation (the falsifier arm, 3-of-4).

Caught only by the owner: the category error (the programme's warrant);
the deference contamination of the adjudication briefs
("self-congratulatory theater"); the drift from the founding ask.

The asymmetry is the post-mortem's core lesson, and it was already the
handoff record's closing observation: internal instruments audit
mechanics; only external reads audit warrants. Point external scrutiny
at warrants. The structural form of that lesson is the §3 candidate
amendment; its behavioural form is that the first question at every
fleet gate is not "will this instrument work?" but "whose question is
this, and did they ask it?"
