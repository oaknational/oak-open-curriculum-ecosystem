## What the review is doing and why

The original question was broad: which data structures, algorithms, patterns,
and repetitions recur across the repository, and which should be factored out?
The review widened deliberately to every tracked `.ts` and `.tsx` file,
including generated code, generator implementations, low-level internal
libraries, applications, agent tools, packages, core workspaces, and all
authored and generated curriculum-SDK code.

The deeper purpose is to reduce the cost of innovation through two related
mechanisms:

1. remove duplicated responsibility so one change and one assurance burden do
   not have to be paid repeatedly; and
2. place genuinely context-independent mechanics in the lowest coherent layer,
   prove them meticulously once, and let higher layers inherit them.

This creates a cost-of-change gradient: generality and assurance increase
downward; foundations compose core mechanics; SDKs and libraries own domain
capability; vendor- and consumer-specific applications stay thin and mostly
express policy, experience, and real user need. “Thin” does not mean trivial,
and “near-zero innovation cost” refers to marginal reuse of already-assured
capability, not the disappearance of product thinking, integration, or learning.

That framing does not make every repetition a core-package candidate. Similar
syntax may encode different responsibilities, while semantically shared
authority may correctly have several local projections. Generated code is not
excluded: this repository controls its form and organisation, so generated
carriers are observable evidence. The intervention locus usually remains the
semantic source or generator rather than the carrier. The curriculum SDK is
also first-class because source shape alone cannot establish its shipped public
form; the plan therefore requires a packed isolated-consumer proof.

## Slice-specific finishing notes

### Secure identity

- Keep the phase distinction visible; do not collapse both path observations
  into a generic “validate” call at the consuming boundary.
- The Node adapter smoke must exercise `O_NOFOLLOW`, `lstat`/`realpath` before
  and after, regular-descriptor enforcement, unconditional close, drift
  refusal, and symlink refusal.
- Closing a descriptor does not excuse losing the primary failure. When read
  and close both fail, preserve both causes.

### Atomic publication

- The previous final artefact must survive every failure before the successful
  rename.
- Cleanup accepts only a temporary target. A final target must remain
  unrepresentable at that call site.
- A filesystem smoke, not only an in-memory port test, is required before this
  boundary can protect a census artefact.

### Auxiliary Git reads

- All bytes must come from the pinned commit, never from the live filesystem or
  lazy network fetch.
- TypeScript bytes already captured by the snapshot are reused without a
  second charge or read. Each unique non-TypeScript path is read, cached, and
  charged once.
- Missing and non-regular paths are per-path refusals. Size mismatch, thrown
  reads, and resource-bound failures latch the run-wide fatal state as frozen
  by the contract.
- The real adapter must accept structured arguments; do not interpolate a
  caller-controlled ref or path into a shell command.
