2. The destination was deliberately withheld during the early sweep so the

7. The durable insight worth surfacing is: **we are building a cost-of-change

## Two-step reflection

### Near step: from syntax to duplicated responsibility

Frequency and structural similarity are useful discovery signals. They are not
architectural authority. The review must identify whether repeated forms encode
the same behavioural responsibility and whether independent production
consumers pay repeated change and assurance cost.

The decisive path is:

`authority -> implementation or generator -> package surface -> consumer -> shipped composition`

Repetition opens the inquiry. Shared responsibility, consumer independence,
change evidence, and correct placement close it.

### Far step: from consolidation to innovation economics

The architectural programme is a cost-of-change system. Lower layers absorb
stable recurring mechanics and their proof; higher layers become progressively
more contextual. The expected economic transition is:

`many implementations + many assurance burdens`
`-> one authority + one implementation + one assurance case`
`-> thin consumer composition`

The “near-zero” claim is treated as a directional, falsifiable statement about
the marginal cost of reusing an already-understood capability. It does not
claim that discovery, domain judgement, integration of genuinely different
contexts, operations, or human validation disappear. The intended gain is that
these higher-value costs stop being multiplied by avoidable reinvention.

### Furthest step: from one architecture to a portable systems pattern

The first draft made the software architecture page the concept's authority.
That was still too close to the context that revealed the idea. The three
independent worked projections changed the frame:

- software shows one assured mechanism inherited by foundations, domain
  libraries, and thin products;
- Parallax shows shared invariants and artifact protocols beneath stackable
  profiles, typed overlays, host execution, vector-valued scale, and
  World-Return; and
- the Practice shows one portable semantic authority and shared assurance
  obligation with intentionally local host implementations and platform
  adapters.

The common pattern is not one literal implementation stack. It is a declared
generality ordering, recursive scale placement, independent dimensions, and a
world-facing learning loop.

## Concept-exploration result

### Problem frame and load-bearing observations

Recurring responsibilities and their assurance are paid for repeatedly when
they remain trapped in specific consumers. Recurring ideas are likewise paid
for repeatedly when their identity remains trapped in a report or first worked
example. People and agents bear the resulting search, reconstruction,
coordination, drift, and validation burden. The constraint is to generalise
without erasing differences that carry real utility.

The load-bearing observations are duplicated responsibility, the separate
authority/generation/runtime roles in software, Parallax's multiple ramps and
vector-valued scale, the Practice's semantic-authority/local-phenotype split,
and the repository doctrine that a portable pattern must survive three
unrelated contexts.

### Assumptions that changed

- **Architecture page as canonical home** changed to **portable PDR as identity;
  architecture page as one projection**.
- **One shared implementation** changed to **two explicitly different paths:
  one reusable implementation and reusable proof where the claim permits it;
  or one portable semantic owner and shared assurance design with deliberately
  local implementation and proof at each host boundary**.
- **One stack or ladder** changed to **a generality ramp plus recursive scales
  and orthogonal dimensions**.
- **Foundational changes are cheap** changed to **foundational changes have high

  an already-understood shared implementation**. Portable contracts with local
  implementations instead amortise discovery, specification, assurance design,
  and governance; they do not erase per-host implementation and proof.

## Architectural synthesis

The working semantic layers are:

1. **Core building block** — one context-independent law, type, pure mechanism,
   or provider-neutral boundary contract; small, total, minimally dependent,
   and usable unchanged by independent consumers.
2. **Foundational system** — a coherent reusable technical capability composed
   from core blocks behind explicit ports, owning lifecycle, failure semantics,
   and orchestration without contaminating core.
3. **SDK or domain library** — reusable purpose or domain capability and its
   deliberate public contract, including schema-derived surfaces where
   appropriate.
4. **Product or service** — thin composition, policy, operations,
   presentation, experience, and the outcome visible to a person or agent.

Semantic authority and generation are orthogonal roles. Context specificity is
an independent gradient across all four layers. Provider-specific adapters
remain separate from provider-neutral foundations.

## Promotion and losing conditions

A core candidate must pass conjunctive gates rather than achieve an aggregate
score:

- at least two independent non-verification production consumers;
- one stable behavioural responsibility, not matching syntax alone;
- provider-neutral and low-context public contract;
- named canonical authority and intervention locus;
- minimal dependencies and downward-only monorepo flow;
- thinner consumer contracts after composition;
- concrete reduction in repeated implementation, assurance, documentation, or
  maintenance work;
- strict total types, explicit `Result<T, E>` failure, boundary validation,
  and deterministic behaviour where required;
- proof at the actual shipped or packed surface; and
- canonical naming, stewardship, TSDoc, README, examples, and removal
  condition.

The candidate loses core placement when it has one consumer, hides different
policy behind flags or callbacks, retains Oak or provider concerns, requires an
upward dependency, recreates complexity in adapters, duplicates an existing
authority, belongs at a generator, or cannot state the avoided repeated cost.

## Excellence and assurance contract

Lower layers do not receive lighter assurance. Their fan-out increases the
blast radius of error; the exact assurance tier remains keyed to harm.

Future package delivery must include:

- TDD at every affected level with atomic test and product-code landings;
- pure mock-free unit tests over observable behaviour and failure states;
- property or generative proof where the contract has laws or a large input
  space;
- integration tests only at real composition boundaries with simple injected
  fakes;
- E2E proof only for running-system behaviour;
- built or packed smoke for the form consumers actually execute or install;
- mutation testing in which every meaningful survivor is killed or explicitly
  proved equivalent or unreachable under the public contract;
- strict types, lint, static and dependency-boundary enforcement;
- meticulous TSDoc, progressive README, examples, troubleshooting, and ADRs
  where a durable architectural decision is created; and
- assurance and real-world signals appropriate to the actual claim.

Comprehensive testing means complete coverage of the public behavioural
contract and its failure modes. It does not mean implementation-coupled tests or
repeating the same proof at every scale.

## Intended impact and value

The causal bridge is:

`excellent shared foundations`
`-> one implementation and assurance burden`
`-> thinner foundations, libraries, and products`
`-> cheaper and safer change`
`-> more capacity for useful experiments and services`
`-> observed value for real people`

Maintainers, contributors, and agents are the immediate beneficiaries: they
gain clearer authority, less search and cognitive load, fewer drift-prone
copies, and trusted composable capabilities. Product teams can concentrate more
effort on domain and experience questions. Teachers, pupils, and other users
benefit only when products use that released capacity well; evaluation,
observability, usage, and human feedback close the final loop.
