Science Year 7 Separation techniques

Separating mixtures: filtration and evaporation

Pupil outcome

I can use filtration and evaporation to separate the parts of a mixture, and explain why each method works.

Key learning points
  1. A mixture is two or more substances combined together without a chemical reaction taking place, so each substance keeps its own properties.
  2. Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid: filter paper lets the tiny liquid particles through but traps the larger solid particles as a residue.
  3. Evaporation separates a dissolved solid from a solution by heating it, so the solvent turns to vapour and escapes, leaving the solute behind.
  4. Choosing the right technique depends on the properties of the mixture — whether the solid is soluble or insoluble in the liquid.

Keywords

mixture
—
two or more substances combined together without a chemical reaction.
filtration
—
a technique that separates an insoluble solid from a liquid using filter paper.
residue
—
the solid left behind in the filter paper after filtration.
filtrate
—
the liquid that passes through the filter paper during filtration.
solvent
—
the liquid part of a solution, which evaporates away to leave the solute behind.
evaporation
—
a technique that separates a dissolved solid from a solution by heating it until the solvent turns to vapour.
Starter quiz — checks prior knowledge
  1. What do we call a substance that dissolves completely in a liquid? (soluble)
  2. True or false: mixing sand into water makes a new substance. (false — it's still sand and water, just mixed together)
  3. What's the general name for a mixture made when a solid dissolves in a liquid? (a solution)
Worked example

Separating a mixture of sand and salt water

  1. Pour the mixture through filter paper set in a funnel. The sand is insoluble, so it's trapped as the residue; the salt solution passes straight through as the filtrate.
  2. Pour the filtrate into an evaporating basin and heat it gently.
  3. As the water heats up, it turns to vapour and escapes into the air — leaving dry salt crystals behind in the basin.
Check your understanding

In the worked example, what is the residue — the solid left in the filter paper?

Practice

You've been given a mixture of gravel and salty water. Write the steps you'd take to separate it into three separate substances: gravel, water and salt. Use the keywords filtration, residue, filtrate and evaporation in your answer.

Misconception and common mistake

"Filtering the mixture removes the salt as well as the sand."

Response: filtration only catches insoluble solids. Salt is soluble, so once it's dissolved it passes straight through the filter paper as part of the filtrate — you can't see it, but it's still there. You need evaporation, not filtration, to get the salt back out.

Teacher tip

Before you filter, ask pupils to predict what the filtrate will look like. Many expect it to be clear of everything solid, including the dissolved salt. Reveal that the filtrate is clear, then evaporate a small sample on a watch glass over a beaker of hot water so pupils watch the crystals appear in real time.
