## The named mechanism

**An owner ruling on doctrine that is recorded outside the queue waits for the owner to repeat
it.** Two instances and one control; a candidate, not a law.

The queue is `pending-graduations.md`. It is what a consolidation's directive pass drains, and
the dedicated consolidation's launch prompt defines done as that queue empty. The control: the
IO-admission cure, a row since 2026-09-14, landed at the 09-19 drain after an owner word that
named the drain, not the cure. The instances: the 2026-09-24 test ruling, recorded in a comms
event, then inside an eight-file branch, then in a chat, then in another event, and never in
the queue, has needed three owner words naming it and is unlanded; O1, recorded as a doctrine
row in the exchange channel and told to "ride its own lane", has been applied by hand instead,
three cards in three hours, and the lane it rides reached the trigger O1 retires and rested for
six hours.

The fresh session the ruling was sent to did arrive, at 00:28Z, at 10%. The ruling still did not
move, because by then it had been bundled: card two named "the test-doctrine intake" as the
unit, the seat took the second estate's test doctrine as bytes (PDR-142's unit is the file), and
the ruling's two removals became part of a 412-line three-way merge that two reviews rejected.
From that moment the ruling waited on K4, and K4 on the partner's fresh session.
`ship-independent-coordinate-dependent` names this case and did not fire; on this seat's reading
of the 10:50Z record (A8, "the test-doctrine intake's cure list"), the intake was one thing, so
there was no bundling moment at which the rule could bite.

## Causal stack

1. **Technical root.** Neither ruling had a queue row. The estate's landing route for doctrine
   edits exists and reads the queue; a ruling recorded anywhere else (a relay event, a branch, a
   channel row "riding its own lane") competes with that lane's other work, and the drain does
   not read it (the drain reads the queue and the buffers; neither carries this ruling, checked
   above). `directive-file-context-budget.md` already says "queue the directive work for a fresh
   session"; in practice the ruling went to "a fresh session of this seat" through a handoff
   opener, which is the same rule's other option, and the fresh session that came had a newer
   owner word to serve.
2. **Process root.** The ruling was carried inside the intake, so its release became the
   intake's release, which depends on the second estate. The card's wording set the unit and the
   bytes-first framing kept it. O1, meanwhile, rode a lane whose card order (batch two, batch
   four, the K Core) did not include it, so a Core amendment carrying the owner's word sat behind
   batch work for four days while the seats hit the trigger it retires.
3. **Meta root.** At the 04:15Z gate doctrine pointed three ways: PDR-063's pause vocabulary
   permits "the owner's word" as a resume trigger (line 419); PDR-063's ruling 3, the clause for a
   measured trigger with the owner absent, defaults to retirement (line 332); and
   `confident-seats-proceed-and-report` says hold only what an answer gates, never the whole
   lane. O1 is the owner's word on which applies, and O1 had not landed. So the seat chose the
   pause, and the owner, who launches the seats and holds the gate's override, had to stop it,
   hand the lane over and re-make the ruling. This is the 2026-09-16 retrospective's meta root,
   "the owner is the balancing loop for purpose", one level down: in every instance above, the
   actor who moved an unqueued ruling was the one who made it. The next "why" is the operating
   model (seats are owner-launched), and the stack stops there.
