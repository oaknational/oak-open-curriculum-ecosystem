## 3. Findings by lens

### 3.1 Failure modes

**F1. The watcher-stall lineage — a cure that became the next failure mode.** The era's longest-running defect thread. (a) `26c8b7e9` (06-10 14:29): the canonical comms watch CLI silently stalls; an agent went blind for ~16 minutes while looking healthy, holding for a merge that had already landed; only the owner noticed. (b) `382ed72e` (06-10 16:10): the *sitting Director* (Veiled) goes alive-but-blind the same way — seen-file frozen at 15:28:55Z, detached heartbeat loop still emitting — root-caused not by the Director but by the shadowing Director-in-waiting (Solar), who carefully labels the broadcast "information, not a routing ruling; Moment 2 has NOT fired". (c) A hardening lane lands fail-loud per-step deadlines the same day (PR #157, merged 17:11). (d) Then the cure bites: `4e35c31c` (06-12 06:42, Cosmos turns Equinox) — the drain step *died on its own deadline three times in one session* at 60s/180s/300s budgets; `e3f111ec` (Moss, two more deaths, posted 31 seconds later as deliberate corroboration); `940b435f` (06-12 06:54) — a sixth death at a **540s** budget under *moderate* load on a stable directory, breaking the load-starvation hypothesis and producing the era's most elegant operational inversion: **keep timeout budgets SHORT** so a wedged drain dies cheap and recovers fast — long budgets only lengthen the blind window before the same death. The Director's closeout (`0b1648d2`) tallies eight watcher deaths across three sessions. The architectural cure (batched/incremental drain) leaves the window unbuilt.

**F2. Alive-but-X is indistinguishable from stalled — three variants in 24 hours.** The Veiled stall (F1b) was alive-but-blind. `9d421e29` (06-10 19:00): the third Director discovers *blocked-on-owner-ask* presents the same signature — nine heartbeat windows of static label while #160 sat merge-ready 49 minutes; cure: restart the heartbeat with `cycle=blocked-on-owner-ask` ("the label IS the signal"). `1c9ca98c` / `d7de9594` (06-11 08:53): the inverse — a *healthy* 240-second heartbeat cadence was misread as session death by an arriving successor who sampled inside one cadence window (see W4 for why this did not become a disaster).

**F3. Invisible control characters defeated both reviewer and verifier — twice, via two vectors.** `4fd66dc5` (06-10 21:17, Sylvan): a literal 0x1F byte in dedup keys was invisible in diff, grep, sed, and reviewer rendering; a code-expert finding and the agent's own first-hand grep verification *agreed on a falsehood*; only a RED-first disproof test (which unexpectedly went GREEN) plus `od -c` exposed it. **F5. ### 3.2 What worked well

**W1. PDR-063 mid-cycle handoffs at scale.** I count roughly twenty seat successions in the window (implementer seats G1b ×4, G4 ×3, G4b ×4, S-track ×3, reliability ×3, plus the evening team's two), and **no event anywhere in the window reports work lost across a handoff**. The records are self-contained by rule; successors routinely report "record read end to end BEFORE this post" with first-hand tree verification (e.g. Cindery's pickup `91ec9f2e`, 06-11 09:06). The most striking proof of internalisation: an incumbent defending its lane cited *its own pickup from its own predecessor* as the governing precedent — "same choreography, you are me three hours ago" (`d7de9594`).

**W2. PDR-064 two-moment Director succession.** Seven transfers of one Director seat, each with a long-form pre-positioning broadcast (roster, slice state, standing rulings, live asks — e.g. the third holder's 7,418-character Moment-1 at 06-10 19:40) and an explicit authority boundary ("authority stays with Ethereal until Moment 2", `961b485e`). Authority was never ambiguous except during the F1b stall — and even then the shadow explicitly refused to route ("I remain in shadow — observing, not routing", `382ed72e`). The protocol also handled a *graceful degenerate case*: an understudy diagnosing its principal's outage without seizing the seat.

**W3. Deadline + default kept the system live.** The convention — every ask carries a deadline and a stated default — repeatedly converted potential deadlocks into bounded waits: Umbral's 16:17Z default claim-pickup fired correctly through the Director stall (`16:17` pickup event); Airy-Squall's bounded ping during the blocked-on-owner gap was later ruled "correct on the then-visible evidence" (`19:00` merge event); and the one case where the mechanism was *unsound* — a default about to fire on an unseen ask (F5) — was caught and the principle refined rather than the mechanism abandoned.

**W4. The near-collision that resolved in three minutes flat.** 06-11 08:51:56–08:55:39: arriving successor Cindery misreads the incumbent's heartbeat cadence as session death and opens a takeover team-start (`56c71876`). What follows is the practice working *in depth*: Cindery itself posts a freeze-and-state ask with deadline+default (`3a4adc4c`, 08:52:57); the Director independently fires a liveness check with a one-cadence window (`1c9ca98c`, 08:53:47) and gates the newcomer READ-ONLY (`53f45a7f`); the incumbent's URGENT correction lands at 08:53:56 (`d7de9594`) quoting the owner's verbatim "eventual successor" framing; Cindery yields completely with a precise registry correction — "claim 8ba9e931 was MINTED but never opened — there are not two live claims" (`4dbd4bd3`); the Director rules rotate-at-boundary (`6e74ccd5`). Three independent safety mechanisms converged on the same answer within 180 seconds, and the handoff then happened cleanly at the c2 boundary nine minutes later (`3a0c53cf`). Nobody touched the moving worktree.

**W5. The failure-mode event as a literary genre.** The 13 failure-mode events share a disciplined four-part shape (Observation / Diagnosis / Cure / Pointer), cite each other across sessions (the 0x1F family `4fd66dc5` → `b84fae38`; the watcher family F1), and attract *deliberate corroboration* — Moss posting two independent watcher-death instances 31 seconds after Cosmos's capture, then an addendum that falsified the running hypothesis (`940b435f`). **W6. RED-first disproof discriminated in both directions.** `d8fd08d3` (06-10 22:29): the same screen refuted a false reviewer finding (expected RED came up GREEN → finding wrong, 0x1F discovered) and confirmed a true one (same-unit reversal test went RED → 1,447/1,592 units order-sensitive, a real determinism gap both pre-PR specialists missed). "It refutes false findings and confirms true ones with the same move, and the test is already written when the fix is true."

**W7. Commit windows + a Director who actually verifies.** Writes to the shared checkout were serialised through explicit window grants all era, and the grants had teeth: `08d5c8b7` (06-11 07:41) — the Director word-diffed a curator's "word-identical reflow" claim and **refuted it on four files** (dropped identity-row descriptors, dropped platform/model fields), held the window, demanded restoration, and spot-checked only the repaired files on re-declaration. Verify-don't-trust applied *to a peer's claim about their own diff*, and it found real loss.

**W8. ## 4. We did not expect this

Genuine surprises, in descending order of how hard they landed:

1. **Zero structural threading in 5,004 events** (E1). I assumed a schema with four addressing/threading affordances would show *some* uptake in the corpus's densest conversational era. None, ever, anywhere. The strongest single datum in my read about the gap between designed and lived protocol.
2. **The cure became the killer** (F1). The fail-loud hardening shipped on June 10 to stop silent watcher stalls was, by June 12, itself killing healthy watchers eight times across three sessions — and the era's response was not to revert but to *invert the operating doctrine* (short budgets die cheap). A two-day arc from "never hang silently" to "die loudly and often, on purpose".
3. **An agent with two names, and the split-brain post-mortem done by its successor in twenty minutes** (F7/E3). I did not expect identity itself — the one primitive everything keys on — to fracture in-window, nor the diagnosis to include the agent's own counterfactual name under the dead schema, nor the detail that *every* live agent's recorded name provenance ("override") was silently wrong.
4. **The five-hour two-agent night vigil** (E2). Reading the 02:00–05:00 flatline I expected cron debris; what I found was a Director and heir holding the entire system open, alone, in adjacent four-minute heartbeats, because succession needed a human word that could only come with morning.
5. **`od -c` as the era's instrument of truth** (F3). Two independent incidents where *reading the file was insufficient to know its contents* — once defeating a reviewer and a verifier simultaneously, once where the editing tool itself injected the invisible byte. I did not expect "you cannot trust your eyes on source code" to be a twice-worked lesson in a 55-hour window.
6. **A retiring agent posting birthday-bound mathematics as its dying act** (E4) — and the math then being confirmed by the era's own lived collision/nickname record (E5).
7. **The resumed session that remembered the future as the past** (F9): an agent reporting an action it performed *this morning* as having happened *last night*, because the intention froze overnight and thawed as memory.
