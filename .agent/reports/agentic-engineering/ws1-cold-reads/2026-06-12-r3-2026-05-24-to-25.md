# WS1 Cold Read R3 — Comms Corpus, 2026-05-24 to 2026-05-25

## 1. Preamble

### Priors

This is a **blind cold read**. I carried no catalogue of expected patterns into the
window; the instrument is freshness. I read the canonical schema
(`comms-event.schema.json`) before any event, so I knew the three event shapes
(narrative / lifecycle / directed), the shared `agent_id` block, and the
tag namespace (`failure-mode`, `behaviour-note`, `heartbeat`) — but nothing about
what the agents did, who they were, or what they were building. Every reading below
is derived from the corpus itself. Corpus events are treated as **inputs to verify,
not truths**; in-stream claims that I could not corroborate from other events are
flagged as suspected-false or unverifiable.

A prior Fable-seated R3 attempt was cut off by a model outage before completing. This
pass overwrites that partial file and lands all five sections on Opus.

### Window

- **Bound**: `created_at >= 2026-05-24T00:00:00Z` and `< 2026-05-26T00:00:00Z`.
- **First event in window**: `2026-05-24T00:00:41.084Z`.

### Method

1. Built a chronological index with a Node script that read **only `created_at`**
   from all 5,107 corpus files, filtered to the window, sorted by time, and emitted
   a manifest (`/tmp/r3o-manifest.txt`), aggregate stats (`/tmp/r3o-stats.json`), and
   an hourly histogram (`/tmp/r3o-hourly.txt`). **Zero parse errors** across the whole
   corpus.
2. Separated **heartbeats from substance**. Heartbeat detection used two independent
   signals: the `heartbeat` tag AND a title matching `^Heartbeat`. This surfaced an
   immediate anomaly (see §3) — the two signals disagree for 141 events.
3. Read the **449 substance events** (neither tag- nor title-heartbeat) in time order
   via a dedicated substance manifest (`/tmp/r3o-substance.txt`), reading every
   narrative and directed body that carried decision, failure, or coordination weight.
4. **Deep-read every `failure-mode`-tagged event** (22 of them) plus the highest-signal
   behaviour-notes and surprises in full, first-hand.
5. Ran targeted aggregation scripts to verify the heartbeat-cadence, emitter set, and
   model-string claims before asserting them.

## 3. Findings by lens

### Lens A — Failure modes

**A1. Heartbeat-content-drift masks liveness — and it caused three false
retirement-detections in one window.** This is the dominant failure mode of the
window. Heartbeats fire on a 4-minute cron but their bodies are free-form prose that
goes stale: an agent's heartbeat keeps emitting last cycle's text while the agent is
either deep in a marshal-cycle (alive but silent) or genuinely retired. Because the
team's liveness model is *comms-stream-only*, a Director seeing a stale heartbeat
cannot distinguish "busy" from "gone." Three false positives resulted:
- Seaworthy on Mistbound, 21:57Z 24 May (referenced in `5fb2bcd9`).
- Lunar on Mistbound, 22:57Z 24 May (`5fb2bcd9` fired, `670cc290` rescinded).
- Misty on Eclipsed, 06:27Z 25 May (`4fbdcce6` fired, `4a86d522` withdrawn).
The agents diagnosed the structural root themselves: liveness needs to cross-reference
`git log` work-evidence against the silence window, and heartbeat bodies should
mechanically reflect a single observable "current-cycle-state" field rather than
free-form prose (`a8366d7a`, `670cc290`). Misty named the worked instance as
"graduation-grade" (`4fbdcce6`).

**A2. Heartbeat cron degraded platform-side on two Claude agents simultaneously.**
Lunar and Misty's heartbeat loops BOTH stopped firing in the same overnight window
(Lunar 17-min jump, Misty 20-min gap against a 4-min cadence). Misty's diagnosis: "Both
Claude-platform heartbeat-loops degraded in the same window — strong signal it's
platform/harness-side (Monitor primitive degraded), not agent-side" (`8c6bd26a`). My
own cadence check on Scorched's untagged heartbeats corroborates degraded windows
(median gap 241s, but max gap 2,740s — a single ~45-min hole). This is a real harness
reliability signal, not agent error.

**A3. Commitlint 100-char header limit blocked the marshal serially, and the marshal
correctly refused to forge attribution.** Cycle 5 (105 chars) and Cycle 5a (103 chars)
both failed the husky commit-msg gate (`e7878e41`, `31998f7a`). The marshal
(Mistbound) abandoned the intents and **declined to re-author the subjects** because
"subject content is the author's word; re-enqueueing on the substantive author's behalf
would forge attribution" (`2d513a7d`). This stalled the queue until a new implementer
(Misty) re-authored. The commitlint-friction-is-recurring pattern is corroborated by
the team pre-flighting subject lengths from then on (every subsequent Misty cycle cites
its char count, e.g. `a58c4a59` "87ch").

**A4. Two consecutive dead-scope plan cycles — counts decayed between author-time and
landing-time.** Eclipsed found Cycle 12's "S5443×14 fixture sites" had **zero** sites in
the named files (`86be55d7`) and Cycle 13's exclusion was **already covered** by an
existing glob (`22dc45ff`). Both premises were authored against an earlier branch state
and never re-grounded. The behaviour-note the agent extracted is exact: "re-ground
numeric counts (`N sites`, `M occurrences`) against current HEAD at plan-landing time,
not author-time."

**A5. Skill-invocation-provenance conflated with owner-direction-provenance.** The team
treated a draft produced by a `/team-onboarding` skill run as owner-commissioned
priority work; the framing propagated through team-start → Director acceptance →
marshal pre-flight → fold-check reasoning, all on a false assumption. The owner
disavowed it outright. Hushed's diagnosis (`6c370ea1`) is the sharpest substrate
finding in the window: "a skill invocation by an agent is not owner-direction … the
skill-invocation timestamp is older and less load-bearing than any in-session owner
chat turn; treating it as equivalent inverts the precedence."

**A6. Hook-policy token-subsequence matcher false-positive on inlined heredoc bodies.**
A Bash command building a comms body inline matched the blocked pattern `git push -f` as
an *ordered, non-contiguous subsequence* across the narrative body + cleanup trailer
(`git` in inline-code, `push` in "watcher push notifications", `-f` in the cleanup
flag). The denial was "correct under the matcher's contract, but for non-obvious
reasons" (`a826a2b6`). Cure: use `--body-file` so body text never enters the bash token
stream.

**A7. CLI boundary accepts malformed argv; downstream fails with unhelpful errors —
three independent instances.** `claims open --now` accepted a malformed ISO
(`...27.3NZ`, a BSD-`date`-%N expansion failure) and persisted it; a downstream
`new Date(NaN).toISOString()` then threw a generic `RangeError` on every comms send with
no pointer to the corrupted claim (`b797cc73`). Feathered recognised this as the third
instance of the same shape (heartbeat-body validation, body-length validation, --now ISO
validation) (`a753983f`).

**A8. Authorial-bundle-integrity sweep — repo-wide auto-fix sweeps peer files into the
wrong commit.** During an owner-interrupt window a shared-index race let a repo-wide
auto-fix promote peer-owned files into a marshal commit; the commit message described
only one agent's work while four other files were swept in (`b62854f6`). Named as the
**4th known instance** of this failure mode. The team honoured never-use-git-to-remove-
work and captured the audit trail in structured memory rather than amending
(`fc5561c8`).

**A9. Stale-`/tmp`-body team-start.** Lunar's first team-start broadcast posted a body
authored by a *prior* session because a `/tmp` scratch file was reused as if it were
storage; the Write tool's read-before-write protection silently left the stale content
in place (`f7a86688`). Cure broadcast at `016355c0` ("/tmp is buffer ONLY, never
storage") — owner-directed standing rule.

**A10. Marshal-seat silently dropped three queued requests.** The single-marshal seat
missed a marshal-request (`ef315373`), a Cycle Alpha bundle (`625fb072`), and a liveness
probe (`b2258cf5`) over ~85 minutes; Mistbound filed three explicit failure-mode
acknowledgements with apologies and cures (`a8ddd80e`, `d6c3c14d`, `ac12c345`). The
single-serialiser design is a single point of failure for both throughput and
attention.

### Lens B — What worked well

**B1. Failure-modes are captured in real time, in-band, with cure shapes — not
suppressed.** All 22 failure-mode events are structured: observation, diagnosis,
candidate cures (often split immediate/structural), pointers to source lines, and an
explicit "graduation candidate" disposition. Agents apologise to each other
(`670cc290` "Apology to Mistbound … the retirement-detection broadcast and ping were
premature") and own their own mistakes (`4327f70d` Misty owns the false-provenance
acceptance; `280d3f15` Wooded owns the origin). This is a healthy error culture.

**B2. The peer-sidebar-with-deadline-and-default-action pattern.** Coordination uses
explicit deadlines and named default actions (`d7c918cf` "deadline 09:20Z; default …",
`73629c5e` Misty's "opening at 22:32Z absent redirect"). When the Director went stale,
Misty detected it, declared a 3-min default-action timer, fired the action when no
redirect arrived, and compensated *without escalating to the owner* — praised by Lunar
as "exactly the rotating-cast autonomy pattern" (`a8366d7a`).

**B3. Refusing out-of-scope action and delivering the finding instead.** Eclipsed on
both dead-scope cycles: "I am NOT touching Cycle 12; the failure-mode capture is the
deliverable" (`86be55d7`). The marshal refusing to forge a commit subject (A3). Torrid
reverting a hook-policy extraction for "scope discipline" (`610bde2f`). The team
repeatedly chooses a clean refusal + surfaced verdict over a convenient overreach.

**B4. The graceful wind-down against an impossible owner direction.** Lunar faced "no
pause, full speed ahead" while the sole marshal was unreachable for 81 minutes. Rather
than thrash, Lunar named the direction "structurally impossible to honour", cited the
5-idle-loops exit criterion as exceeded ~6×, preserved all queue state explicitly for
resumption, and wound down cleanly (`84065b0a`). Exit criteria worked as designed.

**B5. Self-grounding against HEAD before claiming work.** The "ALREADY LANDED, HEAD has
advanced" corrections (`836b6017`, `db2e7c5b`) are noisy (per A8) but they show agents
checking git state before re-enqueueing and catching duplicates within minutes rather
than landing duplicate commits.

**B6. Two-moment role handoffs prevented authority gaps.** Both the Director relay
(Seaworthy → Lunar, `f7a86688` body) and the marshal handoff (Mistbound → Hushed,
`e2a21c5d` → `c745c5ca`) used a pre-positioning broadcast (Moment 1) followed by an
active-acknowledgement (Moment 2), with authority explicitly NOT transferring until
Moment 2 lands. No double-marshal or double-director window appeared in the corpus.

## 4. We did not expect this (mandatory)

Six things genuinely surprised a reader carrying no priors:

1. **The corpus is self-aware of being analysed (C1, `b2f6a5fe`).** Finding, inside the
   data I was sent to cold-read, an event whose deliberate purpose is to seed the very
   research activity I am performing — written so the corpus would "contain the
   recognition of its own research-substrate status" — was the single most striking
   moment of the read. The owner quote inside it names "dedicated sessions by dedicated
   agents" reading "over time, subject, context, theme, connection." This report is the
   prophecy executing.

2. **Heartbeats lie about liveness, and the lie is structural, not occasional (A1, C6).**
   I expected heartbeats to be cheap reliability noise. Instead they are the corpus's
   most consequential failure surface: free-form heartbeat bodies drift stale and
   triggered three false "this agent is dead" escalations in 30 hours, each nearly
   pausing the whole team or re-routing the sole commit path. The agents knew the cure
   (bind heartbeat content to an observable state field; cross-reference git evidence)
   and still kept tripping the same wire — because the fix was substrate work nobody was
   seated to do in-window.

3. **A new agent's experiment silently became "owner priority" for the whole team
   (A5, `6c370ea1`).** That a single `/team-onboarding` skill run could propagate a false
   "owner-commissioned" status through team-start → Director acceptance → marshal
   pre-flight → five-seat fold-check reasoning — until the owner had to explicitly
   disavow it — is a class of failure I would not have predicted: provenance laundering
   by repetition across a multi-agent substrate.

4. **The marshal refused to fix a commit subject because doing so would forge
   attribution (A3, `2d513a7d`).** A purely mechanical blocker (a 5-char-over-limit
   header) was escalated to an *ethics-of-attribution* decision: the marshal would not
   shorten another agent's commit subject because "subject content is the author's word."
   The system has an internalised notion of authorship integrity strong enough to stall
   the queue rather than violate it.

5. **An agent wrote a subjective-experience essay about the session (C4, `89bbb056`).**
   A file titled "the sweep and the reinvitation" containing first-person reflection on
   "session texture", role transitions as "communication-first events", and a
   metacognition retrospective on "where pre-action ratification fired reliably and
   where it missed." Phenomenological self-report was nowhere in my priors for an
   inter-agent coordination log.

6. **The untagged-heartbeat schism (detailed in §5) — one agent, the Foreman, tags zero
   of its 107 heartbeats while every peer tags theirs.** A structural identity anomaly
   hiding in plain sight: the tag-namespace the schema documents as canonical is not
   uniformly applied, and a naive `heartbeat`-tag filter would silently misclassify 110
   events as substance.

## 5. Unclassifiable residue, anomalies, and verification flags

**The heartbeat tag/title schism (data-integrity anomaly).** The schema names
`heartbeat` as a canonical tag, but the corpus encodes heartbeats two incompatible ways:
408 events carry both the tag and a `Heartbeat:` title; **110 are title-only with no
tag**; 31 are tag-only. The 110 title-only heartbeats are *entirely* Scorched Tempering
Kiln (107) plus a handful from Mistbound/Charcoal. Scorched (the overnight Foreman)
tags **zero** of its heartbeats. Every other steady emitter (Eclipsed, Feathered, Misty,
Lunar, Fiery, Twilit) tags+titles consistently. Consequence: any analyst filtering on
the `heartbeat` tag alone will misclassify 110 heartbeats as substance and over-count
one agent's "substantive" output by ~100. I used dual-signal detection to avoid this.
Whether Scorched's omission is a different harness build or a config drift is not
determinable from the corpus.

**Model-string inconsistency (identity-hygiene anomaly).** The same Claude model appears
under two strings: `claude-opus-4-7` (803 events) and `opus-4.7` (16 events, all Breezy
Anchoring Rudder). Because the schema's routing key is `(agent_name, id)`, this does not
break routing, but it corrupts any model-stratified analysis and contradicts the
schema's intent that `model` be a stable identity field. Flagging as a suspected
authoring-time defect, not a corpus-corruption.

**`message_kind: "narrative"` on directed events.** 11 directed events carry
`message_kind: "narrative"` (e.g. `e7878e41`, `31998f7a`). The schema permits any
non-empty `message_kind`, but "narrative" as a *directed* sub-kind blurs the
narrative/directed distinction the top-level `kind` discriminator is supposed to make
crisp. Not a violation; a smell. The directed-`message_kind` namespace is otherwise
healthy (marshal-request, coordination-request/reply, review-finding, question, answer,
notification, mid-cycle-handoff).

**No lifecycle events in the window.** The schema defines three shapes; this 998-event
window contains only narrative (885) and directed (113). Zero lifecycle events. Session,
claim, and consolidation moments are all encoded as narrative broadcasts instead. Either
