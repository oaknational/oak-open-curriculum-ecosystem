# The Intelligence of Inhibition

## Operational eligibility, regulator ecologies, and the governance of action, non-action, and reconsideration in the Practice

> **Intelligence is not only the ability to select an action. It is the evidence- and
> purpose-governed ability to allocate, withhold, transfer, narrow, suspend, restore, and retire
> operational eligibility across actors, obligations, generators, environments, designs, and
> timescales.**

## Review contract

This report develops one question:

> **What is permitted to influence, generate, decide, execute, or continue here and now; who may
> change that permission; for how long; and what evidence should cause reconsideration?**

Review should test:

1. whether operational eligibility names a real common structure without flattening existing
   distinctions;
2. whether causal localisation improves intervention;
3. which decisions can be mechanical, which need bounded agent judgement, and which remain
   constitutively human;
4. whether non-action can remain active, reviewable, and reversible;
5. whether revisit remains separate from automatic restoration;
6. whether regulators can be evaluated, switched off, and retired;
7. whether the proposed machinery removes enough existing burden to justify itself.

## Executive synthesis

The Practice already separates:

- evidence from present authority;
- operational state from durable memory;
- portable substance from host phenotype;
- execution autonomy from normative authority;
- preservation from eligibility to act.

Those separations create freedom, but they do not by themselves govern movement. The Practice also
needs to decide when an entity becomes eligible, remains eligible, loses eligibility, recovers
eligibility, or leaves the operational field.

This changes the centre of the problem from persistence to **selective causal participation**.

A record may exist but no longer govern. An actor may be alive but unauthorised. A capability may be
present but inactive, available but prohibited, or permitted only for one obligation. An obligation
may remain valid while its current attempt stops. A recurring generator may remain inspectable while
being prevented from creating more work. A host may be reachable while quarantined from
consequential execution. A governing design may remain historically important while no longer
shaping current Practice.

Five findings follow.

1. **Inhibition is an active capability.** A regulator that can only initiate, route, retry, and
   escalate is an accelerator.
2. **Failure location is a causal hypothesis.** Operation, attempt, actor, obligation, generator,
   host, and governing design are distinct possible loci.
3. **Intelligent restraint has a contract.** It names scope, reason, authority, duration,
   preservation, prevention, reconsideration, and restoration.
4. **The computational skeleton is hybrid.** State and schema make distinctions durable; machines
   establish invariants; agents reason under uncertainty; human authority remains where legitimacy
   and consequential judgement are essential; actuators change the world; reconciliation proves the
   effect.
5. **The ecology regulates itself.** Every control carries its own cost, failure mode, reset, review,
   and retirement condition.

## 1. Operational eligibility

### 1.1 Eligibility is neither existence nor capability

Operational eligibility asks:

> May this subject participate in this present activity, for this purpose, at this scope, under this
> authority and evidence state?

A useful abstract relation is:

```text
eligible(subject, action, context, purpose, authority, evidence, time)
```

This is a reasoning model, not a proposed function signature.

| Dimension | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject** | Which record, actor, obligation, generator, host, capability, or design is governed? |
| **Action** | Which influence, generation, decision, execution, propagation, or restoration is considered? |
| **Context** | In which repository, team, workflow, incident, or decision? |
| **Purpose** | Which outcome or harm-prevention aim makes the action relevant? |
| **Authority** | Who may permit, refuse, narrow, or restore it? |
| **Evidence** | Which observations, uncertainty, and contradictions support the current posture? |
| **Time** | Since when, until when, and under which review or expiry conditions? |

Eligibility is contextual. A superseded plan can support historical reconstruction while remaining
ineligible for current sequencing. A tool can be eligible for read-only diagnosis and ineligible for
mutation. An actor can be eligible to attempt one obligation and ineligible to amend the generator
that created it.

### 1.2 Eligibility transitions

Eligibility may be:

- granted;
- narrowed;
- delegated;
- transferred;
- suspended;
- revoked;
- restored;
- superseded;
- retired;
- refused.

A consequential transition preserves:

- previous and new state;
- legitimate decider;
- triggering evidence or decision;
- intended effect;
- propagation obligations;
- review, reset, or terminal condition;
- enough history for audit and reconsideration.

## 2. Non-action is not one state

“Stop” hides different decisions.

| Decision | Meaning | What remains | What is prevented | Typical reconsideration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pause** | Cease one active attempt temporarily | attempt identity, context, partial evidence | execution for now | short-lived condition or direction |
| **Wait** | Keep an obligation alive without an active actor | obligation, context, resumption condition | execution before named evidence | condition, evidence, date, or decision |
| **Defer** | Preserve an option without creating present work | intent and option value | activation and scheduling | priority, capacity, cost, or context change |
| **Suppress** | Prevent a generator producing more obligations | definition, history, repair option | new occurrences | repair and bounded re-evaluation |
| **Quarantine** | Preserve an entity while containing its effects | identity, evidence, inspectability | propagation or consequential action | trust, integrity, or safety established |
| **Cancel** | Intentionally end an attempt or obligation | causal record and rationale | further work on that identity | usually terminal; a new obligation may arise |
| **Revoke** | Remove an actor’s or capability’s authority | identity, history, possible technical capability | authorised action | explicit restoration under current conditions |
| **Retire** | Remove a mechanism or concept from ordinary present use | historical and causal evidence | ordinary activation | materially changed premises and fresh review |
| **Refuse** | Declare an action incompatible with purpose, values, safety, or authority | reason, evidence, governing premise | the action | change to the governing premise |
| **Operationally forget** | Preserve a trace while withdrawing routine causal eligibility | provenance and diagnostic access | ordinary influence | new evidence makes review worthwhile |

The Practice should be able to express:

- not now;
- not until a named condition;
- not this way;
- not by this actor;
- not on this host;
- not again from this generator;
- not under the current premise;
- not as an ordinary current influence.

### 2.1 Dormancy preserves option value

Dormancy preserves a legitimate option while avoiding premature commitment. Work on irreversible
investment treats waiting as valuable when uncertainty may resolve and action would remove options
([McDonald and Siegel, 1982/1986](https://doi.org/10.3386/w1019)).

A dormant obligation or generator should name:

- the information expected;
- the cost of waiting;
- the option preserved;
- the review horizon;
- the condition that can reopen the question;
- the terminal result if the information never arrives.

### 2.2 Refusal has a higher authority bar

Refusal closes more option space than pause or defer. It should name the governing premise and the
authority able to change that premise. A categorical boundary whose premise can be changed by any
local actor is not categorical.

## 3. Regulator ecology

### 3.1 Regulator contract

| Property | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Scope** | Which object is controlled? |
| **Controlled variable** | What condition should remain within bounds? |
| **Sensor** | Which observation indicates deviation? |
| **Actuator** | Which action can change the condition? |
| **Budget** | How much attempt, time, cost, risk, or attention is permitted? |
| **Reset or decay** | When does old evidence cease to count? |
| **Scale shift** | What evidence implicates another locus? |
| **De-escalation** | What proves lower-level activity can resume? |
| **Evidence** | What remains for recovery and learning? |
| **Authority** | Who or what may intervene? |
| **Failure posture** | What happens when the regulator fails? |
| **Retirement** | When does the regulator cease to justify itself? |

Classical cybernetics argues that effective regulation requires response variety and a model
adequate to relevant disturbance
([Ashby, 1956](https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/books/IntroCyb.pdf);
[Conant and Ashby, 1970](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207727008920220)). These are
question-generating constraints, not a blueprint for one controller.

### 3.2 Causal loci

| Locus | Possible evidence | Possible intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operation** | transient error, malformed input, timeout | correct, retry, back off, compensate |
| **Attempt** | repeated local failure, no information gain, strategy mismatch | change strategy, pause, terminate attempt |
| **Actor** | capability absence, lost liveness, repeated cross-task defect | re-ground, narrow authority, replace |
| **Obligation** | several actors fail, contradiction, infeasibility, loss of value | wait, reshape, supersede, cancel |
| **Generator** | similar failures across generated obligations | suppress, revise, test, retire |
| **Host** | unrelated work fails only in one environment | quarantine, repair, restart, reroute |
| **Governing design** | recurring failure class across the estate | replace mechanism, amend doctrine or authority |

Some loci are nested, while actor and host cut across many obligations and generators. The ecology is
a causal lattice.

### 3.3 Narrowest effective intervention

Intervene at the narrowest locus capable of changing the likely cause. This protects unaffected work
while preventing repeated local repair from masking a systemic source.

The generator is particularly important. It permits the system to halt production of malformed work
without immediately indicting the entire architecture.

## 4. Causal localisation and discriminating probes

Failure attribution remains uncertain until evidence separates hypotheses.

| Probe | Distinguishes |
| --- | --- |
| Same actor, different obligations | actor-wide versus obligation-specific failure |
| Different actors, same obligation | actor-specific versus malformed obligation |
| Same generator, different hosts | generator versus host failure |
| Different generators, same host | host versus generator failure |
| Corrected definition, same obligation | definition defect versus persistent environment |
| Dependency recovered, same operation | transient dependency versus deterministic defect |

A probe is worthwhile when its expected information can change the intervention decision and exceeds
its cost and risk. Rational metareasoning supplies the relevant decision shape
([Hay et al., 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5879)); the Expected Value of Control
framework similarly treats control allocation as a benefit-cost trade
([Shenhav, Botvinick, and Cohen, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.007)).

The system should represent competing hypotheses, supporting and contradicting evidence, unknowns,
useful probes, and the safest interim posture. `Indeterminate` is a valid result.

## 5. Scale-shift predicate

A defensible scale shift requires:

1. **Causal similarity** — repeated failures plausibly share a cause.
2. **Local budget exhaustion** — another same-level attempt is unlikely to recover or change the
   decision.
3. **Effective actuator** — the next locus can change a condition the current one cannot.
4. **Proportionate blast radius** — intervention scope matches evidence and consequence.
5. **Evidence preservation** — movement does not erase attempts, uncertainty, or minority evidence.
6. **Reset and de-escalation** — named evidence can permit safe restoration.
7. **Regulator failure posture** — absence of the regulator has a defined safe result.

Budgets can be attempts, elapsed time, attention, compute, money, risk, side effects, or opportunity
cost. A cheap high-information retry differs from expensive repetition.

## 6. Computational skeleton

The operational substrate can be stated compactly:

```text
state + events
schemas + invariants
validators + judgement
authority + purpose
transforms + actuators
reconciliation + learning
```

### 6.1 State

| State kind | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Desired state** | What should be true? |
| **Observed state** | What appears true in the environment? |
| **Grounded state** | What can be established mechanically? |
| **Derived state** | What follows from authoritative inputs and rules? |
| **Epistemic state** | What is believed, with what confidence and alternatives? |
| **Authority state** | Who may decide or act? |
| **Eligibility state** | What may influence, generate, execute, or continue? |
| **Historical state** | What was true but no longer governs? |

[PDR-118](../../practice-core/decision-records/PDR-118-agent-work-state-model.md)
provides the model discipline: claimed, observed, and grounded facts remain distinct at source and
unify only in a derived read.

### 6.2 Events and evidence

Current state does not explain why it changed. Durable events support:

- audit and recovery;
- causal reinterpretation;
- minority and contradicting evidence;
- authority review;
- restoration or continued inhibition;
- learning and regulator evolution.

[PDR-119](../../practice-core/decision-records/PDR-119-agent-memory-as-an-event-graph-with-renderers.md)
provides a related distinction between immutable source events and current projections.

### 6.3 Schemas

A consequential inhibition or restoration may need:

- subject identity and kind;
- action and scope;
- previous and new eligibility;
- reason and causal hypotheses;
- evidence and counterevidence;
- confidence or indeterminacy;
- legitimate decider;
- effective time;
- review trigger and deadline;
- restoration conditions;
- propagation obligations;
- retention policy;
- reversible or terminal status.

Schemas prove shape. They do not prove causal adequacy, value, or legitimacy.

### 6.4 Validators

#### Structural

- required fields exist;
- identities resolve;
- references point to evidence;
- times are coherent;
- the decider has the required authority class.

#### Invariant

- an attempt belongs to one obligation;
- waiting has a resumption condition;
- cancelled work cannot silently appear running;
- a superseded actor is not current authority;
- a retired generator cannot create new obligations;
- a projection agrees with its source.

#### Temporal

- budget exhausted;
- claim freshness expired;
- review date reached;
- inhibition unresolved beyond its horizon;
- evidence outside its relevance window.

A temporal trigger opens review; it does not decide the outcome.

#### Authority

- this actor may pause one attempt but cannot cancel the obligation;
- this coordinator may release a claim but cannot amend intent;
- this human authority may accept consequential risk or alter doctrine.

#### Evidence sufficiency

Machines can verify that required evidence classes are present. Whether the evidence supports the
causal conclusion usually remains judgement-bearing.

#### Reconciliation

- generator recorded as suppressed but still producing work;
- actor authority revoked while credentials remain active;
- host quarantined but still receiving consequential assignments;
- old guidance superseded but still loaded;
- waiting condition satisfied without an authorised resumption decision.

Reconciliation distinguishes enacted governance from administrative fiction.

### 6.5 Transforms and actuators

Pure state transitions change representation. Interpretive transforms turn events into diagnosis or
learning. Governance transforms alter the system that governs future decisions.

Actuators change the environment:

- stop a process;
- cancel a child agent;
- release or transfer a claim;
- remove a capability from discovery;
- revoke a credential;
- route work away from a host;
- prevent a generator creating obligations;
- remove obsolete guidance from activation;
- restore a repaired capability.

A state record without actuation changes description. Reconciliation proves whether the real effect
occurred.

## 7. Mechanical proof, agent judgement, and human authority

### 7.1 Mechanical work

Machines should own:

- parsing and schema validation;
- identity resolution;
- expected-state transitions;
- time and budget arithmetic;
- deduplication and idempotency;
- projection convergence;
- capability and permission lookup;
- deterministic actuation after approval;
- verification of effect;
- bounded review packets.

### 7.2 Agent judgement

Agents are useful for:

- forming competing causal hypotheses;
- distinguishing superficially similar failures;
- selecting probes;
- assessing information value;
- comparing intervention scope and blast radius;
- interpreting counterevidence;
- recommending review and restoration conditions;
- identifying structural cures and expected deletions.

Agent fluency is not authority.

### 7.3 Human or constituted authority

Human authority remains essential for:

- strategic value and contested priorities;
- substantial or irreversible risk;
- suspension of strategically important generators;
- changes to organisational policy or Practice doctrine;
- sensitive data use;
- categorical refusal;
- restoration after serious trust or safety failure.

A machine can validate and propagate a decision. It cannot manufacture legitimacy.

### 7.4 Maturity progression

Repeated judgement can sometimes become mechanical:

```text
ambiguous judgement
    → repeated and evidenced decision pattern
    → explicit policy and authority boundary
    → mechanical invariant or actuator
```

This progression should be deliberate. An agent habit must not silently become a rule.

## 8. Time and reconsideration

Distinct temporal meanings include:

- `review_at` — reopen the question;
- `expires_at` — authority or claim needs renewal;
- `suppressed_until` — minimum inhibition interval;
- `reopen_when` — evidence that makes review worthwhile;
- `retired_at` — ordinary eligibility ended;
- `evidence_relevance_until` — old evidence needs renewed justification.

Time elapsed is not evidence of repair.

Restoration is a separate decision and actuator. It should name restored scope, current authority,
successful evidence, probation if any, monitoring, rollback, and the condition that would suspend it
again.

Different thresholds for inhibition and restoration may be warranted when false activation and false
inhibition have asymmetric costs. This should be tested on actual episodes.

## 9. Regulating the regulators

Controls accumulate. A locally useful regulator can become harmful:

- retries continue after information value reaches zero;
- a liveness monitor outlives the actor it represents;
- a review gate remains after prevention made the failure impossible;
- a suppression never receives review;
- a detector becomes noise;
- a wrapper becomes the main source of complexity;
- adaptive response becomes rigidity.

Research on allostatic load provides a bounded analogy: repeated adaptation can itself impose
cumulative cost ([McEwen, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9629234/)).

A regulator should be recalibrated, narrowed, suspended, replaced, or retired when:

- its failure class becomes impossible by construction;
- a lower-level invariant absorbs the need;
- detection yield falls below cost;
- false positives dominate;
- the sensor no longer predicts the decision;
- no consumer uses its evidence;
- the environment changes;
- the regulator becomes the main instability source.

Partially independent regulators can provide resilience when they observe different failure paths.
They need visible boundaries, authority precedence, shared evidence identity where appropriate, and
arbitration when actuators conflict.

The highest-level regulator can gather evidence and propose a governing change. It cannot silently
amend the doctrine that authorises it.

## 10. Worked examples

### 10.1 One operation fails transiently

A dependency call times out once while the dependency is otherwise healthy. A bounded retry with
backoff is proportionate. Broader loci remain eligible.

### 10.2 Several actors fail one obligation

Different capable agents independently identify the same contradiction. Pause attempts and route the
obligation for reshaping, supersession, or cancellation. Reassigning it unchanged produces little
information.

### 10.3 One actor fails unrelated obligations

Peer actors succeed while one session lacks a capability or creates invalid state. Re-ground,
repair routing, narrow authority, or replace the actor. The obligations remain eligible.

### 10.4 One generator produces malformed work

Several obligations from one rule fail in causally similar ways across actors. Suppress new
occurrences, preserve evidence, test a corrected generator, and restore under a named authority and
evidence condition.

### 10.5 Unrelated work fails on one host

Different actors and generators fail only in one environment. Quarantine consequential work there,
retain diagnostic access, repair, and run a controlled re-entry test.

### 10.6 A governing design creates recurring workarounds

Many agents encounter the same structural friction and local instructions accumulate. Stop adding
local fences, inspect the governing design, and propose a structural replacement with an explicit
deletion set.

### 10.7 Retired guidance remains active

The authoritative decision has changed but an operational README or adapter still teaches the old
shape. Reconcile live readers, prove current projection convergence, and preserve history in its
appropriate record.

### 10.8 An obligation waits across zero-agent periods

Persist the obligation and resumption condition, end the session, trigger review when evidence
arrives, and resume once through an expected-state transition. The trigger does not automatically
restore execution.

## 11. What the Practice should stop doing

Stop:

- treating activity as progress;
- retrying after evidence changes causal locus;
- interpreting every failure as actor performance;
- leaving generators active while repairing their output;
- using time as automatic restoration;
- deleting as the mechanism of cancellation;
- assuming archive movement withdraws influence;
- adding doctrine where actuation or reconciliation is missing;
- allowing controls to become permanent by default;
- centralising merely because coordination is difficult;
- pretending causal and normative judgement is mechanical;
- making agents perform deterministic transaction choreography;
- retaining every regulator after it succeeds.

## 12. Evaluation and falsifiers

| Dimension | Candidate measure |
| --- | --- |
| **Localisation** | accuracy and review agreement about causal locus |
| **Misdirected retry** | attempts after evidence implicates another locus |
| **False inhibition** | valuable work suppressed incorrectly |
| **Administrative fiction** | recorded eligibility disagreeing with observed effect |
| **Restoration quality** | restored entities actually repaired, authorised, and reconciled |
| **Waiting integrity** | obligations surviving zero-agent periods and resuming once |
| **Stale authority** | actions based on retired projections |
| **Human control** | time to correct, cancel, or restore consequential work |
| **Regulator cost** | attention, context, latency, compute, and maintenance |
| **Subtraction** | controls, instructions, states, or processes removed |
| **Learning** | evidence that changed action or governing design |

Narrow or reject the theory if:

1. operational eligibility collapses existing distinctions;
2. representative cases cannot identify legitimate authority and scope;
3. causal localisation does not improve current decisions;
4. probes cost more or create more risk than direct intervention;
5. state requires manual propagation everywhere;
6. validators routinely overrule better contextual judgement;
7. inhibition states accumulate without closure;
8. humans cannot understand or interrupt the machinery;
9. actuation needs one central authority store;
10. no current procedure, status, gate, or projection can be removed;
11. evidence collection creates disproportionate privacy or safeguarding risk;
12. the theory rewards governance activity without useful effect.

## 13. Restrained research sequence

1. Reconstruct real episodes with suspected operation, actor, obligation, generator, host, and design
   causes.
2. Record competing hypotheses, interventions, information gained, actual cause where known, and
   authority path.
3. Test one reversible eligibility transition such as waiting, temporary capability suspension,
   actor succession, or projection reconciliation.
4. Build test-only scenarios for duplicate resumption, stale actor result, ineffective generator
   suppression, host quarantine, review without restoration, and regulator failure.
5. Compare ordinary agent judgement, structured causal briefs, independent review, and mechanical
   validation plus bounded judgement.
6. Remove one current procedure or control made unnecessary.
7. Decide the smallest durable home for any recurring result.

## Closing synthesis

The Practice asks how agency and learning can persist while every component remains replaceable and
inherited authority remains contestable.

The complementary question is:

> **How can the system decide what should be allowed to keep causing things?**

The answer is a governed field of eligibility:

- state that distinguishes desire, observation, ground truth, belief, authority, and history;
- events that preserve why transitions occurred;
- schemas and invariants that keep distinctions composable;
- validators that prove only what their evidence supports;
- agents that perform bounded causal and proportional judgement;
- humans and constituted authorities that retain legitimacy over values and consequential risk;
- transforms and actuators that change the environment;
- reconciliation that detects divergence;
- time and evidence that govern reconsideration;
- learning and forgetting that modify or retire the regulators.

> **Choosing what to do is smart. Choosing what not to do, here and now, for how long, under whose
> authority, and what evidence would warrant doing it again is intelligent.**
