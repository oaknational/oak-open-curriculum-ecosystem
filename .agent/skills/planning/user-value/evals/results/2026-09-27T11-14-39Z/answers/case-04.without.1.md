No. Neither outcome is supported by the evidence you have, so marking them as achieved would overstate what you know.

**What you have shows:**
- **Passing tests:** the feature behaves the way it was specified. That's about correctness. It says nothing about how people use it or what happens as a result.
- **Ten staff liked the demo:** internal staff find it appealing. That's a small, biased sample. They aren't the patients, and liking a demo isn't the same as changing behavior.

**What the outcomes require:**
- **Reduced missed appointments:** you need no-show rates after launch compared with a baseline. That could be a before/after comparison with seasonality accounted for, or ideally a comparison against clinics or patients without the feature. Without live usage data, there's nothing to measure yet.
- **Equitable access:** you need usage and no-show outcomes broken down by relevant groups, such as age, language, disability, digital access, insurance, and geography. You then check whether the gaps narrow or widen. Self-service rescheduling tools can widen gaps if they favor people who are comfortable with digital tools. So equity can't be assumed. It has to be measured.

**What you can honestly claim now:**
- "Feature complete and verified against requirements (all automated tests passing)."
- "Positive initial internal reception (demo with 10 staff)."
- Outcomes status: **"Not yet evaluated"** or **"Hypothesized / pending measurement."**

**To get to "achieved":**
1. Define the metrics and targets now, such as no-show rate and reschedule-vs-cancel rate by subgroup, and record the current baseline.
2. Launch as a pilot or staged rollout so you have a comparison group.
3. Run long enough to get meaningful numbers, then compare overall and by subgroup.
4. Collect patient feedback, especially from groups at risk of being left out, like non-English speakers, people without smartphones, and people who use assistive technology.
5. Consider an accessibility review (e.g., WCAG) and a non-digital path, like phone or SMS rescheduling, as supporting evidence for the equity goal. These still won't prove the outcome on their own.
