# Proposed journey: completing an application without the online service

## 1. Scope and status

- **Map status:** Proposed. No stage is observed.
- **Journey starts:** the person needs to apply. It includes people who never try the online service.
- **Journey ends:** a decision is received and understood. The other endings are a truthful blocked outcome, or a person who stops (§5).
- **Principle (owner constraint, to be confirmed):** people who use another route get the same entitlement, the same "date of application" and equivalent timescales. They are not penalised for not using the online service.
- **What the team probably controls:** the online service, the helpline scripts and tools, the assisted-application workflow, and the paper or alternative formats. **Outside the team's control:** libraries, charities, advisers, family members and identity providers. They still belong on the map.

## 2. Who is involved

**Applicants who cannot complete online.** These are variants on one shared journey, not separate personas.

| Variant | Why they can't complete it (H) |
|---|---|
| A. No access | No device, data or internet, or no email or phone number |
| B. Low digital confidence | Can't get through the online steps alone |
| C. Access needs | Assistive technology fails, cognitive load, sensory impairment |
| D. Language or literacy | The content isn't understood |
| E. Circumstances don't fit | The form has no option for their situation, e.g. no fixed address or complex household |
| F. Blocked by the system | Identity verification fails, locked out, technical error, missing evidence |
| G. Someone acting for them | Appointee, carer, adviser or family member, with or without formal authority |

**Support providers:**
- **S1 Frontline contact staff:** phone, webchat, face-to-face.
- **S2 Assisted-digital helpers:** in-house staff or partners such as libraries and charities.
- **S3 Caseworkers or back office:** process paper and assisted applications, chase evidence, make decisions.
- **S4 Informal helpers:** family and friends. They take on real work that no one records.

## 3. The applicant's journey stage by stage

**Stage 0: Before entry (the person who never tries online)**
- **Need:** know that they can apply and that there's a way other than online.
- **Channels:** letters, GP, job centre, charities, word of mouth, the phone number on printed material.
- **Staff involved:** partners and referrers need a simple way to tell people about the other route.
- **Exit:** the person knows how to ask for help.
- **Risk:** "apply online" messaging reads as "online only", so the person gives up before starting.
- **Question:** where do people in variants A and D first hear about the service?

**Stage 1: Trying and getting stuck (variants B, C, E, F)**
- **Need:** realise early that they can't continue, without losing what they've already entered or their place.
- **Proposed response:** clear "get help applying" routes from every page and error state, especially identity checks and unsupported circumstances. Save progress and give a reference number.
- **Exit:** the person has a reference number and a route to help. The claim date is protected from this point (H, needs policy).
- **Risk:** the person keeps retrying, or thinks they are ineligible when the problem is really the form.

**Stage 2: Finding and reaching help**
- **Need:** reach a person without a long wait or cost, in a format they can use.
- **Channels:** free phone line, text relay or BSL video, webchat, face-to-face appointment, partner organisation, request for a paper form.
- **Staff involved:** S1 handles the demand, and queue times fall on the applicant.
- **Exit:** first contact is made.
- **Risk:** long waits, call costs, fixed opening hours that clash with work or caring.

**Stage 3: First contact and triage (S1)**
- **Applicant need:** explain their situation once, then be told what happens next and how long it takes.
- **Staff need:** quickly work out why online failed and pick the right route. Record the contact and any adjustments needed, and link them to any partial online application.
- **Routes:**
  - **R1:** staff complete the application with the person on the call. This suits most of variants B, D and E.
  - **R2:** a face-to-face or home appointment, for variants A and C, or where identity checks need to happen in person.
  - **R3:** a paper or alternative format such as large print, Braille, easy read or another language, for people who prefer to do it themselves offline.
  - **R4:** someone applies on the person's behalf (variant G), with an authority check.
  - **R5:** fix the blocker and go back online, for simple variant F cases. Only if the person *wants* this. Never make it a condition.
- **Exit:** a route is agreed, and the person has a reference number and a named next step with a date.
- **Risk:** being pushed back to online ("have you tried the website?"). Being passed between teams and repeating their story.

**Stage 4: Completing the application (assisted or offline)**
- **Applicant need:** understand the questions, give accurate answers, and check what was recorded on their behalf.
- **Staff need (S1, S2):** the same question set and rules as online, not a separate script that drifts from them. Ability to pause and resume. Guidance on complex circumstances.
- **Exit:** the application is complete, or only evidence is outstanding.
- **Risk:** errors in answers staff enter for the person, the person not agreeing to what was submitted, and long calls with no way to stop and restart.

**Stage 5: Evidence and identity**
- **Need:** prove who they are and their circumstances without online identity checks or document upload.
- **Proposed responses:** verify identity in person or by phone with a trusted third party. Post originals with a guaranteed return, or have them checked at a counter. Caseworker (S3) discretion where evidence can't be obtained.
- **Exit:** evidence is accepted, or the person has a clear list of what's outstanding and a deadline.
- **Risk:** original documents lost in the post, and identity rules that exclude the very people this route exists for.

**Stage 6: Submission and confirmation**
- **Need:** certainty that it was received, what was submitted, and the date it counts from.
- **Proposed response:** confirmation in the person's chosen format, including a summary of their answers and a reference number. Paper forms get an acknowledgement on receipt.
- **Exit:** the person has proof of application.

**Stage 7: Waiting and updates**
- **Need:** know the progress, respond to requests for more information, and report changes, all without needing online access.
- **Staff involved:** S3 contacts the person through their recorded channel and adjustments, not by email by default.
- **Risk:** follow-up requests go to an online account the person can't use, so the application lapses.

**Stage 8: Decision and after**
- **Need:** understand the decision and what to do next, including challenging or appealing it, through the same accessible channel.
- **Exit:** the decision is received and understood.

## 4. The support staff's journey

| Moment | Staff need | Burden or risk (H) |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving a contact | See any partial online application, earlier contacts and recorded adjustments | Starting from nothing, and the person having to repeat themselves |
| Triage | Clear rules for choosing between routes R1–R5, and permission to offer offline routes | Pressure from targets to send people back online |
| Completing for someone | The same questions and validation as online, the ability to save and resume, guidance for edge cases | Long calls, handling time targets, liability for errors |
| Handoff to S3 or a partner | Structured handoff that says what's outstanding, not free-text notes | Lost context, and the person being contacted twice |
| Difficult situations | Guidance for safeguarding, distress and disputed authority, plus an escalation route | Emotional load that isn't counted anywhere |
| Partners (S2) | A route into the service, training, and feedback on outcomes | Unfunded work, no visibility of what happens next |

The team can't see informal helpers (S4). Research should ask how much of the "online" success really depends on unpaid help from S4.

## 5. Endings

- **Successful:** the application is submitted through any route and a decision is received and understood.
- **Truthful blocked ending:** the person can't apply yet, for example because evidence is unavailable or they're not eligible. They are told why, what would change it, whether their claim date is protected, and where to get independent advice. This is **not** counted as completion.
- **Stopped without success:** the person gives up at stage 0, 2, 5 or 7. The design should detect this where it can, for example with a follow-up call when an assisted application goes quiet. It should also be measured separately so that online completion rates don't hide it.

## 6. Candidate stories (all at the *candidate* stage)

| ID | Actor | Change and purpose | Stage | Key acceptance observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AJ-01 | Stuck online applicant | A "get help" route from every blocking error, with a saved reference, so they can continue with support without starting again | 1–2 | Staff can find the partial application using the reference |
| AJ-02 | S1 staff | Complete the application on the person's behalf using the same question set as online, so the answers and rules match | 3–4 | Assisted and online submissions produce the same case data |
| AJ-03 | Applicant | Protect the application date from first contact, so waiting for help doesn't cost them money | 1–3 | *Needs policy authority first* |
| AJ-04 | Applicant with no online identity | Verify identity by a non-digital route | 5 | Tested with variants A, C and F |
| AJ-05 | Applicant | Choose a contact channel and adjustments once and have every later contact respect them | 3, 7, 8 | No follow-up goes only to a channel they can't use |
| AJ-06 | S1/S3 staff | Structured handoff showing what's outstanding | 4–7 | The person isn't asked again for information already given |
| AJ-07 | Service owner | Measure completions and drop-offs for each route separately | All | Stopped and blocked endings can be reported |

None of these is *ready*. Each needs an owner, the authority it depends on, and a way to check it.

## 7. Gaps and research needed

1. **Who is excluded, and why:** how many people fall into variants A–G, and where they drop off. This needs research with people who *don't* use the online service, recruited through offline and partner channels, not online surveys.
2. **Staff reality:** current call reasons, handling time, workarounds, and the pressure to redirect people online. Use contact-centre observation and call data.
3. **Policy:** date-of-claim protection, rules for acting on someone's behalf, and whether offline identity checks are permitted.
4. **Partner capacity:** can libraries and charities take on this demand, and who funds it?
5. **Review question 5 test:** each story could pass while the whole journey still fails, for example if AJ-02 works but stage 7 follow-ups go only by email. End-to-end acceptance should follow a person from stage 1 to stage 8.
