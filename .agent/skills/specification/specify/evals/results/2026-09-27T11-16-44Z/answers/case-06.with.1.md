# Specification: Reflective prompts and pupil revision (exploratory study)

**ID / revision / status:** RPR-SPEC r0.1. This is a draft for discussion with the funder and has not been adopted. There was no earlier material in the working directory, so I wrote it from scratch. Anything in [brackets] is a decision your team still has to make. I haven't made up sample sizes, budgets or dates.

**Profiles used:** Research and exploratory project (main), Domain meaning, Data and stewardship, Measurement, Governance. Safeguarding and inclusion are built into the obligations rather than added at the end.

---

## 1. What a funder is buying

| The funder **is** promised | The funder is **not** promised |
|---|---|
| A defined inquiry carried out as specified, or stopped under the stated conditions | Any finding that prompts work, or that they change revision |
| A clear description of how the pupils in the study revise, with and without prompts, using named measures | An estimate of effect on attainment or grades |
| All results reported, including null, mixed and negative ones, on a fixed date whatever the direction | A causal claim that extends beyond the participating classes |
| A verdict on whether the measures and procedures are feasible, and whether a confirmatory trial is justified | A ready-to-use classroom intervention |
| Ethical, safeguarding and data obligations kept throughout | Continuing past a stopping condition to rescue a result |

In short, this project delivers **well-founded questions, workable measures and an honest account of what happened**. It does not deliver a result that makes the intervention look good.

## 2. Terms (a definition is needed before any measurement)

- **"Revise": this term is ambiguous and must be settled first.** It can mean (a) studying before a test, as in UK usage, or (b) redrafting one's own written work. Each reading leads to different measures, settings and literature. The project cannot start until the team picks one. [DECISION]
- **Short reflective prompt:** a question or instruction that takes [≤ N] minutes and asks pupils to think about their own understanding or strategy, for example "What will you do differently next time, and why?" The prompt wording, how it is delivered (paper or digital) and when it is given are fixed in a prompt protocol before data collection starts. Prompts that teach new content are excluded.
- **Change in how pupils revise:** a difference in *observable revision behaviour*, meaning which strategy is chosen, how time is spent, what gets revisited, or which kinds of edits are made. It does not mean attainment. The behaviour measures are listed in §5, obligation O3.
- **Pupils:** [age range / key stage, number of schools, country]. Who is included and excluded is set out in §4.

## 3. Cases

**Successful case:** A pupil in a participating class gets the prompt at the agreed point. Their revision behaviour is recorded with the protocol instruments, and their data is pseudonymised. At the end, the team reports what changed or didn't, together with how uncertain that is. This counts as success **whether or not** a difference appears.

**Adverse cases the specification must cover:**
1. **Null result.** No detectable difference. It is reported with equal prominence, along with what the study could and could not have detected.
2. **Adverse signal.** Prompts appear to cause anxiety or take time away from revision. This triggers review under O7 and is reported.
3. **Measurement fails.** The instruments don't capture revision behaviour reliably. This becomes a finding in its own right (feasibility), not a reason to swap measures quietly.
4. **Excluded or disadvantaged pupils.** Pupils with SEND, EAL pupils and absent pupils may be unable to engage with written prompts. Accommodations are specified; any pupils excluded are counted and the reasons given.
5. **A school or teacher withdraws, or delivers the protocol differently.** Deviations are logged, and analysis reports what was actually delivered.
6. **Consent is withdrawn** by a parent or pupil. Their data is deleted within [X days] where that is feasible (see O5).
7. **The research team has a stake in a positive result.** This is handled by pre-registration and the reporting rules (O2).

## 4. Obligations (the promises)

Each obligation has an owner and an observable test.

| ID | Obligation | Evidence it was kept |
|---|---|---|
| **O1** | Before data collection, the PI must fix and timestamp the research questions, the prompt protocol, the behaviour measures, the comparison design and the analysis plan. Anything added later is labelled *post hoc*. | Pre-registration or protocol deposit [OSF / other] dated before first contact with pupils |
| **O2** | Within [N months] of data collection ending, the team must report every pre-registered question, whatever the direction of the result, with estimates and their uncertainty. They must not report only favourable subgroups or measures. | Final report, cross-checked line by line against O1 |
| **O3** | Revision behaviour must be measured with the named instruments, e.g. [revision logs, observation schedule, edit analysis, short pupil interviews]. The report must state how reliable each instrument was, e.g. inter-rater agreement where coding is used. | Instrument versions and reliability statistics in the report |
| **O4** | There must be a comparison: [within-pupil prompt/no-prompt periods, or classes with and without prompts; DECISION]. The design is exploratory, so the report must not present estimates as confirmatory effects. | Design section, and wording checked against this rule |
| **O5** | Before any participation: ethical approval from [committee], school agreement, parental consent and pupil assent. Data is pseudonymised at collection, access is limited to [named roles], retention is [period], and deletion happens on withdrawal where feasible. | Approval letter, consent records, data management plan |
| **O6** | Accommodations must be offered for pupils who can't use the standard prompt format (oral option, read-aloud, translated wording). Every pupil excluded is counted with the reason. | Participation flow table in the report |
| **O7** | If safeguarding concerns or adverse effects appear, they must be escalated through the school's safeguarding lead within [the school's policy timeframe]. The PI may pause the study. | Incident log (anonymised summary in the report) |
| **O8** | Anonymised data and instruments must be deposited as [open / restricted-access] materials, as far as consent allows. | Repository DOI, or a stated reason for restriction |
| **O9** | A **go / no-go recommendation** must be given for a confirmatory study. It must include design parameters that would be needed, such as variance estimates and plausible effect ranges, and must state the uncertainty in each. | A section of the final report |

## 5. Hypotheses (being investigated, not promised)

- H1: Prompts shift strategy choice towards [e.g. self-testing, or substantive rather than surface edits].
- H2: Any shift differs across pupil groups, e.g. by prior attainment.
- H3: Pupils' answers to the prompts predict the revision behaviour that follows.

All three may turn out false. A finding that disconfirms one of them counts as a full deliverable.

## 6. What the team may change without renegotiating

- **Allowed:** the exact wording of prompts during the pilot phase, before O1 is locked; qualitative follow-up questions; additional exploratory analyses, as long as they are labelled.
- **Needs funder notification:** changing the definition of "revise", the primary measures or the comparison design after O1; any shortfall in recruitment below [threshold].
- **Never allowed:** dropping pre-registered measures from the report; continuing past a stopping condition.

## 7. Stopping conditions

The study stops, and the team reports what it has so far, if:
- (a) the ethics approval or the school's agreement is withdrawn,
- (b) there is an adverse signal under O7 that the PI and the school judge to be unacceptable,
- (c) recruitment falls below [minimum] by [date], in which case it becomes a feasibility-only report, or
- (d) the end date is reached.

A stopped study still produces the O2 and O9 outputs using whatever data exists.

## 9. Open decisions and what happens to each

| Unknown | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| What "revise" means (study vs redraft) | **Blocks** O1 and O3 | The spec can't be adopted until this is decided |
| Age range, number of schools, sample size | **Blocks** adoption; settled from the budget and a precision estimate | O4 and O9 can't be worded fully until then |
| Comparison design (within-pupil or between-class) | **Bounded investigation** in the pilot, fixed at O1 | May change what O4 can support |
| Whether the instruments capture behaviour | **Bounded investigation**; failure is reported as a finding (adverse case 3) | May reduce O9 to "measures are not feasible" |
| Whether findings carry over to other schools | **Accepted residual** held by the PI and stated in the report | No claim to generalise is made |

## 10. Readiness

This is **ready for discussion with a funder, not for adoption.** Adoption needs three things: a decision on what "revise" means, the sample and setting filled in, and the comparison design chosen. Once those are settled, the measurement design in O3 and O4 would benefit from an independent review before pre-registration.
