# Project specification: Reflective prompts and pupil revision (exploratory study)

## 1. Summary
We will try out short written reflective prompts with pupils in [Year X / ages N–M] across [N] classes in [N] schools over [one term]. We will describe whether and how the prompts change how pupils revise: what they do, when they do it and how they judge their own readiness. This is an **exploratory** study. It will produce a clear description, a first estimate of any effect with honest uncertainty, and an evidence-based decision on whether a full trial is justified.

## 2. What we are promising and what we are not

| We **are** promising | We are **not** promising |
|---|---|
| To deliver the prompts as specified in §4 and record how faithfully they were delivered | That the prompts improve revision or attainment |
| To collect the measures in §6 at the stated time points | A result that is statistically significant or conclusive |
| To report every pre-specified measure, whatever the result, including null and negative findings | That findings will generalise beyond the participating schools |
| To keep exploratory findings clearly separate from confirmed ones | A final effect size suitable for policy decisions |
| To state in advance the criteria for recommending a follow-up trial (§8) and to apply them | That a follow-up trial will be recommended |
| To deliver the outputs in §9 on the stated dates | |

**"Success" for this project means answering the questions well, not getting a positive answer.** A well-evidenced "this doesn't seem worth pursuing" is a full deliverable.

## 3. Research questions
1. **Feasibility:** Can teachers deliver the prompts in normal lessons? Do pupils complete them?
2. **Behaviour:** Are there observable differences in revision behaviour (strategies used, timing, amount) between pupils who get the prompts and those who don't?
3. **Metacognition:** Do the prompts change how accurately pupils judge their own readiness (predicted vs. actual score)?
4. **Mechanism and variation:** What do pupils actually write in response to the prompts? For whom, and under what conditions, do the prompts seem to matter?
5. **Signal for a trial:** Is there enough of a signal, and is the study practical enough, to justify a larger confirmatory study?

None of these is a hypothesis we are testing to confirm or reject. They are questions we are estimating and describing.

## 4. Intervention (fixed before the study starts)
- **Prompts:** [3–4] short written questions, e.g. *"What will you revise next, and why?"*, *"How confident are you, 1–5?"*, *"What did you do last time that didn't work?"*
- **Dose:** about 3 minutes, [once a week] for [8] weeks, at the end of a lesson.
- **Delivered by** classroom teachers after a [1-hour] briefing, using a script.
- **Comparison:** usual practice. Alternatively, a neutral 3-minute task, which controls for time on task. **Decision needed:** choose one of these.
- Any change to the prompts once the study has started will be logged and reported.

## 5. Design
- **Setting:** [N] schools, [N] classes, about [N] pupils.
- **Allocation:** randomised at class level, to avoid spillover between pupils in the same class. We will also use a mixed-methods element (see §6).
- **Sample size:** set by what is practical, not by statistical power. We will say this openly. We will not claim the study can detect an effect of a given size.
- **Pre-registration:** before data collection we will register the questions, measures and analysis plan on [OSF] and label it *exploratory*. Any analysis not in that plan will be reported as post hoc.

## 6. Measures

| Construct | Measure | When |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery and fidelity | Teacher log; completion rate of prompt sheets | Weekly |
| Revision behaviour | Short pupil revision diary or checklist (strategies, minutes, timing) | Baseline, mid-point, before the assessment |
| Judgement accuracy | Pupil's predicted score vs. actual score on the [end-of-unit test] | End of unit |
| Attainment (secondary, low power) | [End-of-unit test] | End of unit |
| Mechanism | Coding of prompt responses; interviews with [N] pupils and [N] teachers | Throughout / end |
| Acceptability | Short pupil and teacher survey | End |

## 7. Analysis
- We will report effect estimates with confidence or credible intervals. We will not base conclusions on p-value thresholds.
- Qualitative data will be coded thematically. We will set out an initial codebook and allow new codes to emerge.
- Where subgroup patterns appear (e.g. by prior attainment), we will report them as hypotheses for future work, not as findings.

## 8. Decision criteria for a follow-up trial (fixed in advance)
We will recommend a confirmatory trial only if **all** of the following hold:
1. At least [70%] of scheduled prompt sessions are delivered.
2. Median pupil completion is at least [70%].
3. Recruitment and retention are at least [80%] of target.
4. At least one behaviour or judgement-accuracy outcome shows a direction and size of effect that is plausibly worth pursuing, defined as [e.g. the interval includes an effect of ≥ X].
5. Teachers rate the prompts as acceptable.

If these criteria are not met, we will report what didn't work and why. That report is itself a deliverable.

## 9. Deliverables and timeline

| Month | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| [1] | Ethics approval; pre-registration published; materials finalised |
| [2–4] | Delivery and data collection; interim fidelity check reported to funder |
| [5] | Analysis |
| [6] | Final report (all pre-specified outcomes), anonymised dataset and materials released, go/no-go recommendation |
| [6+] | Practitioner summary for schools; open-access paper submitted |

## 10. Risks and mitigations
- **Poor fidelity (teachers skip sessions):** keep sessions short and scripted, send reminders, monitor fidelity weekly.
- **Self-reported revision data is unreliable:** triangulate with the prompt responses and interviews, and report the limitation.
- **Contamination between classes:** randomise by class, not by pupil.
- **Burden on pupils and wellbeing:** prompts are low-stakes and not graded; pupils can opt out.
- **Ethics:** parental and pupil consent, safeguarding, data protection under [UK GDPR], and approval from [committee].

- Budget, staffing and the exact thresholds in §8
