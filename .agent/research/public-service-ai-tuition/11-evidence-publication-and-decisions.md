# Evidence, publication and institutional decisions

**Reconstruction date:** 6 September 2026. **Status:** source-based research synthesis and proposed operating contracts. External descriptions retain the 4–5 September 2026 evidence dates; none is a fresh legal, departmental or platform audit. The proposed government evidence profile is an unadopted research idea, separate from the tuition POC. This document preserves practical reporting guidance, applicable instruments, complete useful evidence-object fields, conformance and receiving tasks.

## 1. Purpose: make evidence usable in real decisions

The tuition service must use educational knowledge responsibly, establish what its operation achieves, and let others inspect, challenge and reuse that evidence. A source, method-produced finding, interpretation, recommendation, authorised decision and later outcome are distinct objects. Their uncertainty, scope, dissent, restrictions and changes need to survive movement between people, organisations and tools.

The useful intermediate is an **interpreted, governed evidence assertion**. Files, identifiers, hashes, graphs and search enable it but do not decide whether a source warrants a teaching activity or whether an effect permits a release. A technically valid record can be unsupported; supported evidence can remain outside an actor's authority to act; a decision can lawfully depart from advice for reasons that are not reducible to an empirical score.

For OCE work this means a reader should be able to follow educational purpose to a proposed activity, its evidence and contrary explanations, inspected engineering behaviour, independently measured outcomes and the accountable next decision. [01](01-service-concept.md) gives purpose, [04](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md) gives the evaluation contract, [07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md) gives cross-scale proof, and [09](09-public-value-standards-and-commons.md) gives wider public stewardship. This evidence contract supports those tasks without requiring a new national platform.

## 2. Establish applicability before asserting compliance

“Research for government” includes independent evidence offered to officials, commissioned research, analysis within/on behalf of departments, formal social research, consultation evidence, policy advice, official statistics, Parliamentary evidence and public publication. Their duties differ. Central government, arm's-length bodies, schools, devolved administrations, local authorities and independent suppliers are not one institutional scope.

A proportionate applicability declaration should record:

| Field | Required information for the particular work |
|---|---|
| Object and purpose | Activity/product type, intended use, affected decision, deadline and uses it does not establish; exploratory work may have a knowledge goal without an immediate decision |
| Responsible actors | Commissioner, owner/operator, researcher/analyst, assurer, publisher, recipient and authorised decision-maker; role and conflicts |
| Scope | Organisational and territorial jurisdiction, population, processing, delivery setting, lifecycle and formal-research versus other-analysis classification |
| Instrument identity | Issuer, title, canonical URL, exact provision, version/update and verification dates |
| Nature and force | Legislation, statutory guidance/code, functional/professional standard, policy/spend control, contract, regulator guidance, technical schema, voluntary convention or explicit project proposal; distinguish `shall` and `should` |
| Trigger and exceptions | Why it applies, exclusions, local/professional overlays, exemptions and authorised exception process |
| Operational treatment | Required behaviour/evidence, owner, effect on method or decision, unresolved interpretation and review point |
| Access and assurance | Classification, privacy/confidentiality/privilege/IP/security restrictions; permitted recipients; method/reviewer/date/independence, outstanding review points and sign-off |
| Publication and retention | Accessible manifestation, public/restricted/redacted treatment and reasons; lawful retention, correction and preservation route |

This is a proposed practical control, not a universal government form. No reviewed source prescribed one universal report template, slide count, confidence scale or ministerial word limit. The appropriate format follows the activity and audience. A one-page brief is useful only where the decision and evidence fit.

## 3. Practical reporting and presentation from one evidence base

Work backwards from the actual question: who decides/advises, affected people and distribution, deadline, baseline, feasible options, cost of delay, finance/law/security/data/devolution/Parliamentary constraints, and what can be decided now. A neutral question must allow contrary findings. Declare relevant population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, horizon, threshold, alternative explanations and falsifier.

| View | Purpose | Information that must survive |
|---|---|---|
| Decision brief | Rapid action or commissioning conversation | Answer, purpose/requested decision, why now, options, strongest evidence, material uncertainty/counterevidence, benefits/harms/distribution, implementation and next step |
| Oral briefing/slides | Explanation and challenge | Decision story; evidence versus judgement; accessible decision-relevant visuals; sources/denominators/units/dates; questions and a route to detailed written follow-up |
| Full report | Complete human account | Status, owner/version, scope/questions, method/search/sampling, findings, counterevidence, assumptions/uncertainty, affected people, options, further tests, sources and accountable decisions |
| Technical/assurance annex | Specialist scrutiny and reproducibility | Data/code, transformations, protocol/amendments, methods, robustness/sensitivity, ethics, conflicts, QA, unresolved points, scope, sign-off |
| Structured sidecar/register | Discovery, exchange and traceability | Stable object/claim identities, source passage/version, method, type, status, authority/access, correction and dependency; recommended only where it earns its burden |

Views refer to the same underlying work/claim identities but have their own manifestation identity, version and hash. They need not share a revision number or bytes. A consequential limitation belongs in the brief even if full reasoning sits in an appendix; a machine sidecar must not conceal a claim missing from the human account.

A useful brief sequence is purpose/decision, main finding and uncertainty, why it matters, strongest evidence, options, distribution/delivery, counterevidence and next action. The full report can elaborate without forcing all readers to the same depth. An exploratory report can immediately capture stable source, claim, assumption and decision identities, confidence rationale, counterevidence, QA and next tests without waiting for an interoperability standard.

Before oral presentation, establish chair, owner, attendees, time, classification/distribution, current knowledge/disagreement, sponsor/private-office expectations and an accessible pre-read. During it, give the conclusion and requested action, distinguish observation/inference/advice, state uncertainty, show the strongest contrary evidence and answer only within the evidence. Afterwards, record questions, corrections, decision/dissent and outstanding actions; update by versioned change and preserve superseded reasoning.

Charts/tables should show a meaningful comparator, dates/population/units/denominators, uncertainty, exclusions/missingness and series breaks, accessible underlying data and a versioned method. Distinguish observed, estimated, forecast and target values. Avoid unexplained composite scores, ungrounded confidence percentages and traffic lights without thresholds. A citation establishes origin rather than validity; explain relevance, method, independence and weight.

### 3.1 Assurance and uncertainty as usable information

Scope assurance by consequence, novelty, complexity, contestability and reversibility. Where applicable preserve preregistered protocol, search/sampling and exclusion decisions, transformations/missingness, analytic/interpretive method, assumptions/robustness, deviations, ethics/privacy/equality, interests/funding/supplier roles, peer/analytical/policy/access review, outstanding findings and named sign-off. [Reproducible Analytical Pipelines](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/support/reproducible-analytical-pipelines/) provide version control, automated checks, peer review and reusable code; reuse still needs revalidation.

Distinguish measurement uncertainty, sampling/coverage, model/causal, transport, delivery and value uncertainty. State what would reduce each material uncertainty, the time/cost of doing so and whether it can change the decision. Explicit falsifiers are the report's recommendation operationalising challenge and validation, not a universal government field.

### 3.2 Accessible publication, restriction and correction

Publish accessible HTML where appropriate; use semantic headings, simple tables and reading order, meaningful alt text, non-colour cues, expanded abbreviations, descriptive links, captions/transcripts, and keyboard/zoom/AT checks. Use PDF/A or Parliamentary manifestations where their route requires them, with accessible alternatives. Outsourcing does not transfer the public body's accessibility responsibility; assessed exceptions do not remove other reasonable-adjustment duties.

Preservation and public disclosure differ. For restricted material record reason/basis, authorised decision-maker, review date, permitted audiences/access route and a safe redacted/aggregate or metadata-only view where possible. Even metadata, a relationship or an identifier may reveal protected facts; use opaque internal references or withhold the public relation when necessary. Retain lawful source/method/code/correspondence/QA history under purpose-specific retention, not indiscriminate preservation of learner conversations.

[ICO research guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/the-research-provisions/principles-and-grounds-for-processing/) states research is not itself a lawful basis and high-risk processing can require a DPIA. [Pseudonymised data remain personal data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/introduction-to-anonymisation/); anonymity requires a contextual identifiability/re-identification assessment. The source research noted ICO guidance under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act; current application needs checking. Tuition data continues under [06](06-state-rights-and-context.md)'s narrower state/rights controls.

Corrections record owner, reason, date, old/new scope, affected dependants and receiving notification. Revise current human/machine views together, retain the historical manifestation and follow specialised Parliamentary correction rules. A later source acquisition may repair evidence coverage without proving a later external-world change. Record event, publication, evidence cut-off, acquisition, analysis and verification dates separately.

## 4. The evidence continuity opportunity and its limits

The 4 September research found strong stage-specific machinery but no reviewed cross-government semantic model reliably connecting research need, source, evidence, claim, uncertainty, assumption, option/appraisal, consultation input, decision, intervention, evaluation and revised evidence. This is a bounded analyst finding; an existing mandated cross-government claim-level model with those relations would overturn it. Departmental/sectoral/devolved/local implementations may be unindexed. The studies included no interviews or system audits, and adoption/outcome evidence was weaker than policy evidence.

Useful historical diagnostics explain the hypothesis: the [2019 Public Accounts Committee inquiry](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm201719/cmselect/cmpubacc/2492/249207.htm) reported over 20 person/organisation identifiers across 10 departments and inconsistent adoption; [2023 CDDO discoverability research](https://cddo.blog.gov.uk/2023/04/12/how-we-are-improving-data-discoverability/) reported some searches could take six months; the [2026 data.gov.uk roadmap](https://www.data.gov.uk/roadmap/) and [service account](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/2026/03/25/whats-changing-on-data-gov-uk-and-why/) reported stale/irrelevant entries, low use and over a quarter broken links. The [State of Digital Government Review](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/state-of-digital-government-review/state-of-digital-government-review) is a commissioned diagnosis of fragmentation, not an independent impact evaluation. These sources do not establish today's exact performance or this proposed intervention's effect.

The composite report's 4 September developer-documentation snapshot counted 17,855 `research` pages and 15,115 `policy_paper` pages. These are volatile search-index counts, not the number or quality of research projects. Their useful meaning is scale and the distinction between document classification and evidence semantics; the diagram adds no independent evidence.

The proposed improvement is relational: connect the exact question to commission/protocol, source/data/code to method-produced findings, passage to supporting/contradicting/qualifying claims, claims/assumptions to options, advice to authorised treatment, decisions to delivered intervention and evaluation back to original assumptions. Build on existing obligations and systems; a catalogue without ownership, validation, maintenance and receiving use can decay into another burden.

## 5. Proposed evidence objects and assertion contract

Objects need stable identities at the scope required for reference over time or across systems. Sensitive/local objects need not be publicly resolvable. Candidate fields become mandatory only in an object-specific, risk-based receiving profile.

| Object | Candidate fields |
|---|---|
| Research need or question | Sponsor, intended decision/user, population/place, date needed, status |
| Project or commission | Question addressed, commissioner, supplier/team, contract/grant, dates, outputs expected |
| Protocol or analysis plan | Frozen version, preregistration time, method, outcomes, deviations |
| Source or evidence object | Author/owner, version, licence/access, exact locator, method, population and period |
| Dataset or code | Provenance, definitions, transformations, environment, tests, checksum and access |
| Finding | Method-produced result, scope, uncertainty, assurance and source objects |
| Claim | Plain-language proposition, status, author, scope, supporting and contrary relations |
| Assumption | Rationale, owner, sensitivity, expiry/review condition |
| Option or appraisal | Baseline, dependencies, costs, benefits, risks, distribution and claims relied upon |
| Consultation submission/theme | Optional and lawfully processed respondent class, normally aggregated; source locator, coding provenance, counts and limitations |
| Recommendation | Author, reasoning, evidence dependencies and non-evidential judgement |
| Decision | Authorised actor, date, reasons, accepted/rejected alternatives, dissent and review point |
| Intervention version | Owner, start/end, population, components, implementation changes |
| Indicator or outcome | Definition, source series, baseline, target, subgroup and frequency |
| Evaluation | Registry ID, questions, design, protocol, intervention, findings and publication status |


A source owner, host, author, funder, implementer and evaluator are different actors. A Parliamentary-hosted submission remains attributed to its author. A finding is produced by a method; a claim may be empirical, interpretive or normative; an assumption is a premise with sensitivity/reopening; a recommendation is advice; an authorised decision is an accountable choice. Knowledge can remain a question, value, alternative or negative finding without becoming a requirement.

### 5.1 Relations are first-class assertions where meaning is consequential

| Relation | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| `addresses` | A project/output addresses a question; it need not answer it conclusively |
| `uses` | An activity consumed a source/dataset/method/code object |
| `wasDerivedFrom` / `derivedFrom` | Transformation/provenance; source vocabularies differ and require an explicit mapping |
| `supports`, `contradicts`, `qualifies` | An identified assertion says how particular evidence bears on a claim; citation co-occurrence does not establish support |
| `dependsOnAssumption`, `testsAssumption` | Premise dependency or what an evaluation tests; a result may revise only its bounded scope |
| `informs` / historical `informedDecision` | Evidence was considered in advice/appraisal/decision; not proof it determined that decision |
| `accepts`, `rejects`, `setsAside` | An authorised record's treatment of advice/option, with reasons |
| `implements` | A delivered intervention gives effect to a decision; not proof the mechanism works |
| `evaluates` | What an evaluation tests: intervention, mechanism, indicator or outcome |
| `revises`, `supersedes`, `disputes` | Versioned correction or reasoned disagreement without erasing the earlier position |
| `generatedBy` | Result-to-producing-activity provenance; preserve direction and domain/range |

A consequential relation should carry endpoints and exact semantics/direction/domain/range; assertion actor/date/method; source version and passage/observation; validity domain; support, contrary evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and rationale; evidence dependence and defeater; authority to accept/act, visibility/retention and assurance; change/dependent-claim history; receiving treatment, loss/unsupported constructs and reopening.

The focused evidence-flow report explicitly corrects a bare subject–predicate–object edge: it cannot portably carry agent, method, confidence and visibility, particularly when extra properties are forbidden. Represent such relations as addressable **assertion resources**, potentially mapped to Web Annotation passages and PROV activities/agents. Low-risk metadata edges need not carry every field, but the receiving profile must distinguish absent, unknown, withheld, invalid and not-applicable. Missing evidence must not become invented confidence.

A tuition example is concrete: a retrieval-practice study may support a bounded design hypothesis; subject experts authorise a particular profile activity; engineering checks establish the selected policy/representation was delivered; measurement establishes what an observed response means; a credible comparison supports a causal effect; an authorised body decides whether that evidence supports the next stage. Each transition has its own warrant. [07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md) provides the fuller bridge contract.

## 6. Candidate standards, conformance and dual publication

The proposed government Evidence Interoperability Profile is federated, composable, minimal-core, profile-extensible, human/machine readable, access-aware, plural and testable. It is not an adopted UK standard or a tuition architecture mandate. Records remain with accountable organisations; connectors and supported exports may be more useful than replacing repositories.

| Need | Existing standard or pattern | Proposed use |
|---|---|---|
| Persistent identity | Government [persistent resolvable identifier standard](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/open-standards-for-government/persistent-resolvable-identifiers); DOI, ORCID, ROR or RAiD where appropriate | Use government HTTP identifiers where continuity governance exists; DOI for outputs, ORCID for people, ROR for organisations and RAiD for projects only where governance, disclosure and cost fit the object |
| Discovery/catalogues | [DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/), Dublin Core, schema.org | Federated discovery, distributions, licences, access, versions and catalogue records |
| Provenance | [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) | Entities, activities, agents, derivation, revision and responsibility |
| Exact source passages | [W3C Web Annotation](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/) | Version-specific claim-to-passage links and reviewer annotations |
| Concepts and mappings | [SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/) and existing GSS/domain vocabularies | Controlled terms, synonyms and explicit contested or mapped concepts |
| Statistics | [SDMX](https://sdmx.org/about-sdmx/) and GSS harmonisation | Indicators, series, dimensions and codelists |
| Exchange and validation | [JSON Schema 2020-12](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12), OpenAPI 3.1, JSON-LD/RDF, SHACL if selected, and CSVW where relevant | Closed-world JSON-instance validation, HTTP interface contracts, semantic graph representation/constraints and tabular interchange |
| Research packaging | [RO-Crate 1.2](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/index.html) or an equivalent profiled package | Bundle data, code, methods, outputs and a human preview for portable research objects |
| Quality statements | [W3C Data Quality Vocabulary](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dqv/) | Represent metrics, measurements, policies and certificates with provenance |
| Preservation | The National Archives format, checksum and contextual-metadata practices | Preserve content, relations, schemas, codelists, access decisions and executable context |


This is an illustrative composition, not selection of every listed technology. The source uses OpenAPI 3.1 whereas the service report separately lists 3.2.0: different candidate contexts/dated choices, not a silently resolved universal version. The 2026 government identifier profile itself required further governance/implementation work. DQV is a Working Group Note, not a Recommendation, and does not define universal quality. Open specification, implementation rights, software, content/data, service access, governance and substitutability remain distinct dimensions; [09](09-public-value-standards-and-commons.md) gives the fuller standards discipline.

Crosswalks need a declared supported subset, preserved meaning, permitted loss and failure on unsupported constructs. JSON shape, RDF semantics, SHACL constraints, catalogue metadata and provenance are different responsibilities. Round-trip success in a tested subset does not prove arbitrary lossless conversion. A publishing process should generate an accessible human view and appropriate machine sidecar sharing work/claim identities, current version/status and disclosure treatment; restricted relationships appear publicly only when safe.

| Proposed level | Achieved behaviour and minimum proof | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|
|1 Discoverable | Intended object can be found/identified; valid ID, owner, version, metadata and access state | Does not establish relevance, credibility or understanding |
|2 Traceable | Sources/passages, transformations, method, uncertainty, assertion provenance and QA can be followed | Does not establish truth or permission to act |
|3 Decision-linked | Typed claim/option/decision/intervention relations, authorised actor and treatment/reasons | Does not establish evidence caused the decision or intervention worked |
|4 Learning-linked | Evaluation/indicator results can revise the relevant evidence and decision while preserving prior state | Does not exceed causal design or automatically authorise a service change |

Conformance is earned by actual records and receiving behaviour, not field capacity. It needs normative vocabulary, licences/governance, positive/negative examples, versioned validation, semantic/authority and cross-reference checks, extension/deprecation, public issues/decisions, and independent implementations. Test valid/invalid/unknown/withheld states, contradictions, exact source versions, redaction/access, migration/substitution, resolver failure, preservation and semantic/task completion. At least two genuinely independent implementations and a real receiving workflow are needed for a strong portability claim. Publish exceptions and failures as well as successful exchanges.

## 7. AI assistance and human authority

| Use case | Potential contribution | Required control | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research discovery | Semantic search, duplicate-commission warnings, related-question matching | Show source set and matching rationale; human commissioning decision | Plausible, but government-wide outcome evidence remains limited |
| Metadata capture | Extract titles, entities, methods, populations, dates and candidate relations | Preserve source passage; confidence threshold; steward approval queue | Plausible where task-specific precision, recall and protected-data leakage are evaluated |
| Evidence screening | Rank records, deduplicate and prioritise human review | Predefined recall target, transparent stopping rule, adjudication of exclusions | [Active-learning evidence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-020-00287-7) is promising for prioritisation; this is not free-form generative synthesis |
| Claim tracing | Flag unsupported claims, stale citations, version mismatch and missing counter-evidence | Retrieval to exact passage; reviewer accepts/rejects each relation | Plausible high-value pilot hypothesis; no government-wide outcome evidence found |
| Consultation analysis | Suggest themes, map responses and retrieve examples | Human theme design/review; test subgroup bias; never treat response count as population support | A 2025 government evaluation found substantial bounded efficiency potential, with material errors and limitations |
| Analytical QA | Suggest tests, find anomalies, compare planned and executed analysis | Reproducible pipeline; independent code review; no silent changes | Useful assistive role; task-specific validation required |
| Summarisation | Produce audience-specific drafts from approved evidence | Citation-preserving generation; uncertainty/counter-evidence checks; accountable human release | Efficiency plausible; summary quality and omission risk must be measured |
| Living evidence | Detect new versions, retractions or findings and propose updates | Versioned alerts; domain reviewer; explicit supersession decision | Plausible where maintenance ownership exists; outcome evidence remains limited |
| Records and preservation | Triage duplicates and candidate archival value | Records professional makes selection; retain audit trail | [National Archives testing](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/information-management/manage-information/preserving-digital-records/research-collaboration/using-ai-for-digital-selection-in-government/) did not support replacing expert judgement |


Evaluate the complete human–AI workflow against an appropriately reviewed baseline, measuring missed material, false inclusion/exclusion, unsupported relations, subgroup error, correction effort, latency/cost and automation bias. Human review needs competence, time, authority, evidence access and manageable workload; it is not infallible by declaration.

The source's [DfT/Alan Turing Institute consultation-analysis evaluation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-consultation-analysis-tool-evaluation) covered four consultations and **modelled 50–70% time/cost savings** under medium-consultation assumptions against a hypothetical all-human workflow. These were not observed causal savings. Blind theme recall/mapping was materially lower than after live human adjustment; unsupported themes, sentiment errors and limited bias testing remained. This supports an inspectable assisted-workflow hypothesis, not autonomous interpretation of democratic evidence. Active-learning screening evidence ([van de Schoot et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-020-00287-7)) concerns prioritisation, not generic generative synthesis; [human-screening error research](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0227742) does not provide error rates for every task. [National Archives digital-selection research](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/information-management/manage-information/preserving-digital-records/research-collaboration/using-ai-for-digital-selection-in-government/) did not support replacing professional selection judgement.

AI should not invent references, decide universal truth/quality or the weight of values/rights, remove contrary evidence as atypical, infer causality from correlation/text prevalence, make irreversible personal decisions, expose protected records or silently change authority. Treat retrieved documents as untrusted data, verify source/version integrity, keep source content separate from instructions and tool execution, restrict tool authority and preserve retrieval provenance.

ATRS mandatory scope depends on applicable organisation and significant-influence/direct-interaction tests; broad policy analysis can fall outside. The source recommends an ATRS-like record for material AI search/exclusion/synthesis/ranking even when not mandatory: purpose, owner/users, inputs/model, human control, evaluation, risks/incidents and change. That recommendation is distinct from the applicable legal/policy publication obligation.

## 8. Governance, evaluated programme and alternatives

The source proposes public sponsorship across analytical, science, appraisal and digital/data authorities; a standards team with departmental/professional maintainers; rights/security/equality/records expertise; user governance including external and low-resource users; independent evaluation separate from vendor/delivery; and multiple public/commercial/open-source implementers. Publish membership/interests, decisions/minutes, rejected proposals, roadmap, adoption and unresolved risks. No supplier should control the only vocabulary, resolver, conformance decision or export route.

Plural definitions, mappings, contradiction, minority analysis and perspective remain explicit. Evidence may inform or constrain advice while elected or other accountable decision-makers weigh lawful values, distribution and priorities. Record accepted/rejected/set-aside advice and appropriate reasons rather than implying evidence selected policy automatically.

The two historical programme schedules are alternatives, neither commissioned by this reconstruction. The focused report recommends an 18–24 month evaluated pilot with this 24-month staging:

| Stage | Proposed work and exit |
|---|---|
| Months 0–3, authority/discovery | Name SRO and durable owner; establish users/rights/evaluator; inspect existing schemas/IDs/contracts; choose three actual decisions with willing owners/deadlines and baseline search, duplication, errors, handover and reuse. | Months 3–7, minimum profile | Core objects/assertions/access and levels 1–2; examples/validator/licence/version; connectors; access/privacy/security/preservation/exit review. No pilot until two independent teams exchange without material semantic loss. |
| Months 7–15, end-to-end pilots | Research-demand: ARI→commission→protocol→output→residual gap. Consultation: call→submission→theme/claim→response→decision. Policy-learning: appraisal→decision→intervention/version→Registry→finding/revision. Each involves at least two organisations, an independent implementation and migration/export; portfolio includes restricted evidence under approved controls. | Months 15–20, evaluation | Compare baseline and feasible non-profile workflow; test transfer, interpretation, actual reuse; publish outcomes/cost/errors/failed hypotheses, remove unused fields and resolve vocabulary disputes. Add levels 3–4 only with demonstrated value. |
| Months 20–24, operational decision | Stop, extend, adopt bounded workflows or consider wider mandate with funded maintenance, support, procurement/migration and public adoption reporting. Demo/record counts do not establish value. |

The composite source's longer illustrative schedule starts after September 2026: cross-government use cases/legal analysis October 2026 for 4 months; metadata/ID audit November 2026 for 4; minimal profile/schema February 2027 for 4; exploration pilots March 2027 for 5; publishing-sidecar prototype July 2027 for 5; Registry/ARI linking August 2027 for 6; multi-department beta January 2028 for 8; assurance/authoring integration March 2028 for 8; cost/reuse/transparency evaluation September 2028 for 5; formal-standardisation decision February 2029 for 3. These dates preserve an unadopted option, not a current delivery timetable. Its valuable sequencing insight is authority/discovery, small authoring/validator trial, integration, then measured adoption and standardisation only if useful.

## 9. Measure flow, use and impact separately

| Benefit class | Complete useful measures | Methods and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | Median time to relevant object; correct-version link-resolution percentage; required records with current owner; transfer time/manual transforms; duplicate commission rate; round-trip/supplier migration; metadata creation/maintenance cost | System logs, audits and task baselines; custody/movement are not correct interpretation or policy impact |
| Use | Reuse by another team/organisation; typed relations/citations from advice/appraisal/evaluation; successful retrieval and interpretation; multiple-implementation API/export use; assurance defects resolved before decision; accessible/authorised completion | User tasks, interviews, record audits and non-author workflows; downloads/citations alone are insufficient |
| Impact | Inspectable decision chains; documented changes to options/implementation/monitoring; earlier/better evaluation planning; fewer material missing/stale/mischaracterised source claims; result-to-policy-review delay; distribution of benefit/burden | Preregistered mixed methods, decision-file review, cost, phased/matched comparisons where useful. Contribution can be documented without claiming causation; causal attribution needs an appropriate design. |

Include unsuccessful searches, missed evidence, abandoned integrations, rejected advice, excluded users and vendor failure data. A profile creating material authoring cost without discovery/assurance/reuse/decision gains should be simplified, narrowed or stopped. Access failure, information leakage, erasure of dissent/version history, untraceable material assertions, disproportionate burden on small organisations/qualitative work, unresolved supplier/resolver capture, missing operating ownership/funding or inability to preserve the required record are non-compensable scale stops.

### Principal operating risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Standards without adoption | A technically sound profile produces no network effect | Mandate only after pilots; fund connectors and stewardship; publish conformance and exceptions |
| Metadata decay | Broken links and stale owners reduce trust and AI performance | Automated checks, named owners, service levels, tombstones and deprecation policy |
| False semantic precision | Typed relations may conceal contestable judgement | Record assertion agent/method/confidence; preserve dissent and source passages |
| Central epistemic control | One vocabulary or graph could privilege an official view | Federated architecture, plural profiles, public change control and dispute relations |
| Confidentiality leakage | Relation or metadata can reveal sensitive facts even without content | Threat-model metadata, support minimal/opaque records, tiered access and redaction tests |
| AI automation bias | Users may over-trust fluent summaries or rankings | Source-first interfaces, mandatory review, error-cost thresholds and incident reporting |
| Prompt injection or provenance attack | Retrieved documents may contain malicious instructions, altered passages or poisoned metadata | Treat retrieved content as untrusted data; separate content from system instructions; verify integrity/version; restrict tool execution; log source and retrieval state |
| Supplier capture | A nominal standard can be implemented through proprietary extensions | Open conformance suite, multiple implementations, export and migration rights in contract |
| Unfunded preservation | Identifiers and relations fail after programmes close | Durable service owner, operating budget, archive profile and migration exercises |
| Burden on practitioners | Documentation displaces research rather than supporting it | Minimal core, auto-suggested metadata, proportionality and removal of unused fields |
| Metric gaming | Teams optimise record completeness rather than policy learning | Combine quantitative audits with user and decision-file review; publish negative outcomes |


Here “tombstones” means durable technical metadata for withdrawn/resolved identifiers, not repeated admonitions in current service prose. The broader commons design and action-time authority are in [09](09-public-value-standards-and-commons.md) and [06](06-state-rights-and-context.md); this evidence profile does not grant permission to retain or exchange whole-person learner state.

## 10. Worked examples and receiving tests

### 10.1 A fictional policy-research example from the source

Question EX-Q01 asks why applications to a hypothetical high-volume service are incomplete. Descriptive claim EX-C07 says incompletion appears concentrated at two stages. Administrative extracts EX-E12/EX-E18 support it; EX-E21 qualifies it using another population and a flatter pattern. Confidence is moderate because assisted-digital users are incompletely covered. The assumption is that recorded abandonment represents actual abandonment; a rival is technical session expiry. Complete server logs removing the concentration when timeouts are separated would falsify the claim. The practical implication is to investigate those stages before a system-wide intervention, not proof that a chosen intervention works.

This example carries the source's useful distinctions. Its compact JSON instance is deliberately incomplete: it refers to EX-E21 without defining that evidence object locally, and its “two extracts” confidence rationale is not fully represented by the single evidence object shown. ### 10.2 A tuition evidence handover

A subject reviewer records a bounded activity hypothesis with source passage, population/context and contrary evidence. The profile steward authorises a named activity/repertoire revision within their remit. OCE delivery evidence records actual input/output versions and exposure. Independent assessment supplies a protected valid outcome; evaluation tests a defined contrast and publishes a bounded effect with missingness/harms. The SRO receives a decision brief linking that result, gate status, cost, alternatives and uncertainty, then records the actual treatment and review point. A later correction traces affected views and decisions without overwriting history.

This is a proposed reader scenario, not a completed run. Success requires each actor to understand scope and authority, a receiver to find the useful substance without an old private project, and a materially unsupported assertion to be rejected even when its JSON shape is valid.

### 10.3 Minimum successor-only exercises

A receiver should: find a service requirement, rationale, material alternative and proof; identify source author versus later interpretation; recover contrary evidence and unresolved denominators; distinguish current instruction, historical proposal, implementation observation and satisfied acceptance; state remaining compliance questions; distinguish bytes, semantics, engineering, learning, causal value, reuse and institutions; follow a correction and affected decisions; and exchange a supported record with declared losses/access.

Report actual task success and limits. Hashes establish unchanged bytes; indexing establishes retrievability; neither establishes conserved interpretation or decision usefulness. ## Appendix A. Exact useful shape of the historical prototype schema

They are a record of a design experiment, not an approved OCE or government contract.

Source prose intended more than the schema enforces: (a) confidence rationale is optional and not conditionally required; (b) `Decision.authority` is optional even for `authorised`; (c) a relation allows no assertion agent/method/access fields and forbids additions; (d) references are strings without resolution or source-version integrity; (e) the root allows additional properties and claims can be empty; (f) the broader metadata catalogue includes constructs absent from the prototype. The focused report's first-class assertion contract above corrects the consequential relation limitation at the semantic-design level. Before implementation, map written guarantees to shape, semantic, identity, authority and receiving-task checks; include structurally valid but unsupported/unauthorised adverse cases.
