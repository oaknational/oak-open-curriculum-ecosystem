# Inclusion and human service

**Purpose:** specify what effective access, ambitious education, human support and practical agency require across the whole tuition service. **Standing:** source-based requirements and research proposals, with no claim of implemented conformance or learning effect. **Evidence snapshot:** 4–5 September 2026; reconstructed 6 September 2026.

Accessibility and excellent SEND support are non-compensatory service conditions. The aim is **zero preventable exclusion**; a release claim can state only the absence of identified critical barriers within its declared tested journeys, modes and combinations, with effective alternatives and residual limits explicit. Conformance, authentic access, valid accommodated assessment, educational effects and distributional burdens remain distinct.

This document owns functional access, accommodation records, concrete human-support duties, affected-user participation and service burden. [Document 03](03-learner-experience-and-profiles.md) owns educational profiles and learner activities; [document 04](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md) owns the complete service gate and measurement definitions; [document 06](06-state-rights-and-context.md) owns state/rights semantics; [document 11](11-evidence-publication-and-decisions.md) owns publication and institutional force. The five assurance questions below organise evidence within this domain; they do not create a competing release-authority system.

## Five assurance questions

| Assurance question | Question | Evidence that does **not** substitute for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Rights and ethics** | Does the service respect dignity, agency, participation, privacy, equality and the learner’s best interests? | Popularity, efficiency or average benefit |
| **Technical conformance** | Does the relevant service satisfy applicable law and standards, including WCAG 2.2 AA and the wider ICT requirements of EN 301 549? | A design-system claim, automated scan or one successful screen-reader test |
| **Usability and effective access** | Can learners complete authentic journeys with their own assistive technologies, preferences and combinations of need, without disproportionate burden? | Standards conformance alone |
| **Learning and assessment validity** | Does the accessible service improve durable, transferable knowledge and capability, while accommodations preserve the intended construct? | Task completion, engagement, satisfaction or assisted correctness |
| **Distributional effects** | Who benefits, who is burdened, who is absent, who disengages and who is harmed, including at intersections? | A favourable population average |

A system can conform yet remain unusable; be usable yet educationally ineffective; improve outcomes overall yet worsen them for a subgroup. The project should therefore make claims at the narrowest evidenced level: **conformant**, **usable for tested journeys and users**, **learning-effective for a specified population and configuration**, or **distributionally acceptable against declared thresholds**. None should be allowed to imply the others.

Design for all subjects and lifelong contexts, but earn accessibility and learning claims profile by profile. A common public kernel can provide semantic interaction, assistive-technology interoperability, learner control, provenance, safety, human hand-off, evaluation and exit; each subject–phase–context profile must specify its constructs, representations, tools, access requirements and limits. Mathematics and English are useful first contrasts, not evidence for every subject, age or learner.

Evidence supports immediate commitments to multimodal access, learners’ own assistive technology, support without a diagnosis gate, minimal and correctable data, separate coaching/practice/assessment, human escalation, continuous testing and no uncontrolled self-modification. It does **not** show that a general-purpose generative tuition service improves SEND learning. That requires co-design, authentic task testing, construct validation, subgroup efficacy and harm evaluation, and independent lifecycle assurance.

The outcome-evidence limit is concrete: [Paglialunga and Melogno’s 2025 review of AI interventions for students with learning disabilities](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12385150/) included **11 eligible studies and no study at low risk of bias**, despite positive reported outcomes. This is a bounded part of a heterogeneous field, not evidence for SEND generally. Learning effects and the subgroup validity, benefit and harm of state estimation or adaptation remain questions for separately approved, adequately powered research.


## Rights, agency and functional need

This report covers school-age, post-16, adult, vocational, professional, community and later-life learning. “SEND” follows English education usage; “disabled people” is used for equality, accessibility and rights. The groups overlap but are not interchangeable, and barriers can vary by task, environment, health, fatigue, language and technology.

The [SEND Code of Practice: 0 to 25 years](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7dcb85ed915d2ac884d995/SEND_Code_of_Practice_January_2015.pdf) names four broad areas—communication and interaction; cognition and learning; social, emotional and mental health; and sensory and/or physical—but says needs often cut across them and change over time. Identification should lead to action, not category reduction. [Children and Families Act 2014, section 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/6/section/19) requires regard to the child or young person’s views, participation and best possible outcomes.

This has three design consequences:

1. The service should organise support around current functions, barriers, learning tasks and declared preferences, with learners able to select, adjust and correct that support.
2. It should preserve intellectual ambition while changing routes, representations, pacing, tools or assistance where those features are not the intended learning construct.
3. Its evidence must describe the learners, contexts, assistive technologies, tasks and service version actually tested. “SEND-friendly” and “works for neurodiverse learners” are not acceptable unbounded claims.

Statutes and standards establish duties or conformance criteria, not learning effects. Guidance supplies design expectations, not a trial of this service. Assistive-technology, UDL and emerging generative-AI evidence identifies useful mechanisms and failure modes but remains uneven. Design inferences are therefore kept distinct from observed effects.

### Rights and context-specific authority

#### Equality, adjustment and participation

The [Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment duty](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/20) and [Public Sector Equality Duty](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/149) make accessibility and equality part of service design and public decision-making, not optional product enhancements. Exact application depends on the operator, setting and service relationship, but the public-service proposition should adopt the stronger design position: anticipate avoidable barriers, involve affected people, document equality effects and provide effective alternatives.

The [UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-persons-disabilities), particularly Articles 9 and 24, supplies the wider rights frame of accessibility, participation, inclusion and education without discrimination. These rights do not depend on proving that a branded accessibility framework produces a positive average effect. Evidence is needed to choose and improve means; it is not a precondition for respecting equal access and dignity.

#### Agency and non-stigmatising support

Support should normally be available through learner-controlled settings and contextual need rather than requiring disclosure of a diagnosis. The learner must be able to request, refuse, revise and reset presentation and interaction preferences. A support pathway should not silently lower task difficulty, narrow the curriculum, reduce opportunities for explanation or redirect a learner away from ambitious content. If a change affects the construct or credential inference, that fact must be made explicit rather than hidden as “personalisation.”

The service should avoid deficit language and false psychologising. Communication difference, atypical interaction, slow response, repetition, literal language, a request for a full example or an apparent change in affect are not reliable diagnoses of motivation, comprehension, intent, risk or disability. The service may form a bounded hypothesis about what help could discriminate between explanations of a current answer. It must not present a generated narrative about the learner’s mind as fact.

#### Children and lifelong users require different authority routes

UNICEF’s [Guidance on AI and Children, version 3.0](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children) places children’s safety, privacy, non-discrimination, inclusion, transparency, best interests, development and wellbeing at the centre of AI design. England’s [Generative AI product safety standards](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-standards/generative-ai-product-safety-standards), updated in January 2026, add specific expectations for age- and SEND-sensitive filtering, child-centred testing, progressive disclosure, cognitive-development protection, non-manipulative interaction and routes to human help.

Lifelong scope does not justify one undifferentiated safeguarding pathway. In schools and colleges, [Keeping Children Safe in Education 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/keeping-children-safe-in-education--2), institutional policy and the designated safeguarding lead remain authoritative. For adults in England, the local-authority enquiry duty in [Care Act 2014, section 42](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/23/section/42) applies only where the statutory care/support, abuse or neglect, and inability-to-protect conditions are met. Independent adults also need accessible emergency, support and complaints routes that do not falsely imply that every disclosure is transmitted to a public authority. Devolved nations require their own legal and operational maps before use.

### Heterogeneity and the functional access contract

Official 2025/26 statistics record more than 1.8 million pupils with SEN in England: 538,547 with an EHC plan and 1,319,780 receiving SEN support. The most commonly recorded primary need differs between those groups, and free-school-meal eligibility is markedly higher among both than among pupils without identified SEN ([DfE, *Special educational needs in England, 2025/26*](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/special-educational-needs-in-england/2025-26)). These figures establish scale and an intersection with disadvantage. They do not describe every person, measure unmet need, establish diagnosis or identify an effective tutoring intervention.

The service should maintain a **functional access contract**, not a disability template. Its smallest useful representation is:

- learner-declared access and communication preferences;
- current device, browser, input/output and assistive-technology context where the learner chooses to share it;
- task-specific barriers and supports;
- observed interaction failures, clearly separated from inferred causes;
- the provenance, confidence, purpose, expiry and correction history of any derived field;
- a rapid reset and route to human or specialist support.

Access preferences should be separable from identity, curriculum state, pedagogical history and safeguarding records. They should not automatically travel between institutions or contexts. A preference that helps with one construct or moment may hinder another, and a person may deliberately choose different modes at different times.

#### Functional coverage contract

| Functional need or barrier | Baseline service commitment | Principal failure to test |
|---|---|---|
| **Speech, language and communication; AAC** | Text, symbol and AAC-compatible routes; sufficient wait time; turn-taking that tolerates repair and repetition; editable transcripts; vocabulary and sentence complexity adjustable without infantilising content | Fluency or speed treated as understanding; speech required for progress; moderation misreads AAC or atypical language |
| **Blindness and low vision** | Semantic structure; full keyboard operation; screen-reader and refreshable-braille compatibility; magnification/reflow; accessible mathematics and data; image descriptions with uncertainty and verification routes | Visual information omitted; inaccessible canvas or diagram; focus loss; hallucinated description creates an unequal checking burden |
| **Deafness and hearing loss** | Accurate, editable captions and transcripts; visual equivalents for audio alerts; controllable playback; sign-language support where the service commitment requires it | Audio-only teaching or escalation; caption errors change meaning; turn-taking excludes sign-mediated interaction |
| **Motor and speech access** | Complete keyboard, switch, eye-gaze, dwell, alternative-pointer and alternative-input operation; generous and adjustable timing; no drag-only gesture | Fine-motor precision, sustained posture, rapid speech or timed response becomes an unintended prerequisite |
| **Cognition and learning** | Clear structure and language; chunking; worked representations; memory aids; stable navigation; step visibility; low-friction correction and recovery; controllable pace | “Simplification” removes disciplinary meaning; working-memory burden masks knowledge; support becomes answer substitution |
| **Attention, executive function and neurodivergence** | Predictable interaction; distraction and animation controls; pause/resume; explicit task state; adjustable sensory load; planning and review support | Engagement optimisation intensifies stimuli; rigid dialogue penalises atypical interaction; behaviour is pathologised |
| **Fatigue, pain, chronic or fluctuating conditions** | Save/resume, flexible session length, persistent task position, alternative modalities and low-effort routes | Progress depends on uninterrupted sessions; a temporary access pattern becomes a permanent learner judgement |
| **Compound and low-incidence needs** | Test interacting requirements and end-to-end journeys; provide specialist and human alternatives where a digital route cannot preserve access or the construct | Success with one impairment persona is generalised; one accommodation disables another; no practical fallback exists |

This matrix is a minimum design and research map, not proof of effect. NICE guidance supports attention to visual supports and sensory environments for autistic children and environmental modification for people with ADHD ([NICE CG170](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg170/chapter/recommendations); [NICE NG87](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations)), but these are clinical recommendations, not evaluations of a tutoring interface. The individual learner and authentic task remain the unit of design.


## Technical conformance and whole-service coverage

The [Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952/contents) and current [GDS accessibility guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps) establish requirements for relevant public-sector services and accessibility statements. GDS specifies WCAG 2.2 AA. The scope of any school exemption and the exact legal position of a national service require legal analysis; exemptions do not remove Equality Act duties.

[WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) covers a broad range of sensory, physical, speech, cognitive, language, learning and neurological needs and combinations. It also states that even AAA does not meet every type, degree and combination of disability, particularly cognitive, language and learning needs. WCAG therefore supplies a testable web-content floor, not a certificate of excellent or universal access.

[EN 301 549 V3.2.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/03.02.01_60/en_301549v030201p.pdf) extends the view across web and non-web software, hardware, voice and video, documentation, support and assistive-technology interoperability. Its functional statements cover vision, hearing, vocal capability, manipulation, reach, photosensitivity, cognition/language/learning and privacy. This edition incorporates WCAG 2.1, so procurement should pair it with the current WCAG 2.2 AA requirement rather than treat either document as a complete substitute for the other.

#### Required conformance evidence

Before learner-facing release, every in-scope service version should have:

- a manually verified WCAG 2.2 AA audit across representative and high-risk journeys;
- an EN 301 549 applicability and conformance matrix for the full service, documentation and support;
- a current, accurate accessibility statement with known limitations and effective contact/remediation routes;
- documented browser, device and assistive-technology combinations, including actual-user research;
- testing of error, timeout, safety intervention, escalation, authentication, consent, export and deletion paths—not only the happy-path lesson;
- a record of exceptions, impact, workaround, owner, remediation date and release decision;
- regression tests attached to component, content, model and service changes.

Automated testing is useful for frequent defect detection but cannot establish conformance or usability alone. GDS guidance on [testing with assistive technologies](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/technology/testing-with-assistive-technologies) recommends continuous development testing and research with users. Screen readers, magnification and speech recognition are a baseline; this service also needs switch, eye-gaze, braille, AAC, caption and subject-specific notation coverage according to declared scope.

### Version reconciliation in the transferred evidence

The specialist accessibility source inspected **EN 301 549 V3.2.1**, incorporating WCAG 2.1; its whole-ICT analysis above retains that provenance. The service report's Appendix C2 separately records **EN 301 549 V4.1.1 (2026-09)** as a new technical procurement baseline with UK designation/OJ citation unresolved. The later edition is not silently substituted into the earlier audit or treated as a legal trigger. [Document 09](09-public-value-standards-and-commons.md) owns the standards-version register. An implementation must determine the applicable current edition and legal mapping before its own conformance claim; this reconstruction has not performed that fresh determination.


## Generative and multimodal interaction

Generative interfaces introduce dynamic and epistemic barriers beyond conventional page accessibility. [WAI-ARIA 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/) defines the `log` role for sequential message histories, while [WCAG 2.2 guidance on status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) requires important changes to be programmatically available without moving focus. A usable service also needs deliberate behaviour for streaming, generation-complete notification, pause/cancel, corrections, regenerated answers, citations, tool execution, focus restoration, history traversal and errors. Token-by-token screen-reader announcement is likely to be burdensome and must be tested rather than assumed accessible.

The service should render mathematics in MathML or another reliably transformable semantic form, retain accessible plain-text alternatives and test equations, tables, charts, diagrams, code and scientific notation with actual tools. Generated images and descriptions must expose their source and uncertainty. A learner who cannot inspect the source image should not bear an invisible, disproportionate verification burden.

Interviews with 19 blind users of mainstream generative-AI tools found real productivity and information-access benefits alongside unlabeled controls, weak landmark and keyboard structures, difficult message traversal, missing completion notifications, hallucinations and an unequal need to verify visual or factual claims ([Adnin and Das, 2024](https://maitraye.github.io/files/papers/BLV_GenAI_ASSETS24.pdf)). This is a small, mainly US adult sample and not learning-outcome evidence. It nonetheless identifies credible failure modes for direct testing.

Voice is valuable but cannot be the exclusive route. A study of 432 people with self-reported disordered speech found a median word-error rate of 31% for speaker-independent typical-speech models and 4.6% for personalised models, with the greatest gains among more severely affected speakers ([Green et al., 2021](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/green21_interspeech.html)). The study used short prompted phrases and enrolment data; it does not establish performance for children, free conversation, noisy classrooms, varied UK accents or tutoring dialogue. Voice features therefore require text and AAC alternatives, visible editable transcripts, easy correction, refusal recovery and testing of the burden and privacy cost of personalisation.

W3C’s non-normative [COGA content-usability guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) proposes familiar and predictable interfaces, clear and chunked language, stepwise support, memory aids, reduced distraction, easy error recovery, personalisation and readily available human help. These are strong candidate patterns, not a claim that every pattern improves every learner. The project should test them with people who have cognitive and learning disabilities and preserve subject meaning while doing so.

### Assistive technology and effective alternatives

A DfE-funded [rapid review of assistive technology in education](https://www.knowledge-by-design.com/ukat/final_report.pdf) examined 968 documents and found that much of the literature was expert opinion and relatively few studies supplied moderate or strong evidence. It concluded that no single technology fits all learners and that effective selection requires assessment, trial, adaptation, user aspirations and context. Augmentative and alternative communication was among the stronger research areas, but the review was pre-generative-AI, US-heavy and used preliminary evidence classification.

The resulting service principle is **equivalent purpose, not identical interface**. A modality may differ while preserving the learner’s access to the knowledge, action and feedback that matter. Where a digital alternative cannot preserve the intended capability—for example, safe manipulation of equipment, signed interaction, direct observation of a practical performance or specialised communication assessment—the human, specialist or situated route is part of the service design rather than an edge-case exception.

Accessibility spans the whole service journey. Account creation, identity recovery, consent, help, safeguarding notices, complaints, data controls, teacher dashboards, family information, downloaded materials and support channels must be accessible. Phone-only escalation, inaccessible PDFs or a human service that cannot use AAC do not constitute effective fallback.

GDS guidance on [research with disabled people](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/running-research-sessions-with-people-with-disabilities) supports testing with people’s own assistive technologies and settings, asking about requirements rather than assuming them, and obtaining informed consent. Participants and community partners should be paid, research burden should be actively reduced, and inaccessible research methods should not filter out the people whose experience matters most.


## Pedagogy and construct-preserving accommodations

The goal is not easier interaction in isolation but ambitious, durable and transferable learning. The Education Endowment Foundation’s [*Special Educational Needs in Mainstream Schools*](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send) recommends an individual–environment–content perspective, purposeful assess–plan–do–review, high-quality teaching and appropriately selected explicit instruction, scaffolding, metacognition, flexible grouping and technology. Targeted and specialist provision should supplement, not displace, strong teaching and professional judgement. Its scope is mainstream education for ages 5–16 and does not establish AI-enabled tuition efficacy or cover every profound, physical or low-incidence need.

For the service, this implies:

- maintain the same valued curricular or disciplinary destination unless an authorised curriculum decision changes it;
- distinguish a barrier to access from a gap in knowledge and from a different but valid form of expression;
- elicit learner action and explanation rather than infer understanding from smooth conversation;
- use scaffolding, representations and worked examples deliberately, then fade support and check unaided performance;
- permit legitimate additional processing time and communication repair without treating them as failure;
- preserve uncertainty and invite teacher, specialist or learner correction;
- measure delayed retention, transfer and increasing independence, not response completion or session length.

Universal Design for Learning is useful as a design framework for anticipating variability, multiple representations, engagement and action/expression. It is not an efficacy guarantee. A systematic review found only 15 relevant technology-supported secondary studies, all US-based and concentrated on representation; self-regulation, self-assessment, collaboration, negative effects and novelty were under-studied ([Bray et al., 2024](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13328)). A critical analysis found that CAST’s cited evidence more often supported individual techniques than the complete branded framework ([Boysen, 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14782103241255428)). The project should use [UDL Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) to widen design possibilities while separately testing components and outcomes.

Preferences and accessibility needs are real; they do not validate fixed “learning style” labels. The service should not infer that a learner is a visual, auditory or kinaesthetic type and constrain future teaching accordingly. Modality choices should respond to access, content, learner purpose and observed results and remain revisable.

### Coaching, practice and independent assessment

Accessibility changes what a learner can perceive, do or express. Whether a change is an accommodation or a change to the assessment construct depends on the purpose of the task. The [*Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf) distinguish support that removes a construct-irrelevant barrier while retaining the intended inference from a modification that changes the construct and therefore the meaning of the score. Text-to-speech might preserve access to a science-reasoning task but alter a test intended to measure unaided decoding. These are US professional standards rather than UK law, but the measurement principle is directly relevant.

The service should implement three visibly distinct modes:

| Mode | Purpose | Accessibility rule | Permitted inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coaching** | Build knowledge or capability | Use any justified support that advances the learning goal, with learner control and fading where appropriate | Evidence about the supported learning process, not unaided mastery |
| **Practice/check** | Diagnose and rehearse | State which supports remain active and why; vary or fade them deliberately | Bounded evidence about performance under declared conditions |
| **Independent assessment** | Support a specified decision or inference | Only pre-authorised construct-preserving accommodations; isolate tutoring and answer-generation functions | Inference licensed by the assessment contract and conditions—nothing broader |

Each assessed task or family needs an accommodation register containing:

- intended construct and decision use;
- plausible access barrier;
- permitted and prohibited assistance;
- evidence or expert rationale that the support preserves the construct;
- possible side effects and differential impact;
- how support is disclosed and logged;
- what score or inference remains defensible;
- owner, version and review condition.

Automated scoring requires separate accuracy, validity and fairness evidence by relevant subgroup. Agreement with an existing score does not prove that either measure is unbiased or educationally meaningful. The service should not score communication style, speed, eye contact, spelling, handwriting, speech typicality or conformity of expression unless one is explicitly part of the authorised construct.

This record describes the **assistance actually available and delivered**, not merely the requested setting. A selected scaffold that fails transmission, rendering or assistive access is not established exposure. Correctness after answer disclosure licenses a different inference from independent performance. [Document 07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md) supplies proposed bridge scenarios for source-to-output meaning, answer disclosure, failed delivery and changed host/access mode; those scenarios remain unexecuted.


## Access data and service-state boundaries

The service can respond coherently without creating a permanent disability or psychological profile. It should separate:

1. identity and authority data;
2. learner-declared access preferences;
3. observable task responses and exposure history;
4. bounded curricular or strategy hypotheses;
5. safeguarding signals subject to distinct access and retention rules.

The ICO explains that disability may reveal health information and that deliberately inferring health or disability in order to treat someone differently can constitute special-category processing regardless of confidence ([ICO, *What is special category data?*](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data/)). Its guidance on [profiling and automated decisions about children](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-if-we-want-to-profile-children-or-make-automated-decisions-about-them/) advises avoiding child profiling where possible, conducting a DPIA for likely high-risk processing, and supplying meaningful human involvement and challenge for significant decisions. Parts of ICO guidance are under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act, so current legal interpretation must be checked before implementation.

Any derived learner-state field should be task-relevant, probabilistic, time-indexed, inspectable, correctable, resettable and expiring. It should record contrary evidence and the estimator and policy version. Diagnosis, personality, motivation, emotional state, vulnerability, SEND status or future potential should not be inferred from conversational style. Accommodations should not depend on accepting such inferences.

The system may improve, but not through uncontrolled online reward optimisation. The ICO’s [fairness guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/annex-a-fairness-in-the-ai-lifecycle/) warns that adaptive optimisation can create self-reinforcing feedback loops and that aggregate performance can hide different false-positive and false-negative costs. The [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) recommends continuous subgroup monitoring, affected-community feedback, independent evaluation, recourse and lifecycle change control.

Accordingly, live personalisation should be limited to approved choices among tested examples, pacing, modes, hints and representations. A change to prompts, routing, learner-state semantics, safeguards, reward functions, policy or model weights requires a versioned candidate, offline and adversarial evaluation, accessibility and distributional testing, authorised staged or randomised trial where causal evidence matters, stop thresholds and rollback. Engagement, compliance, session length and immediate correctness are unsafe optimisation proxies for learning.

The complete operational definitions and E7 state-necessity requirement live in [document 06](06-state-rights-and-context.md) and [document 04](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md). Access preferences, adjustment needs and actual interaction failures do not constitute diagnoses or evidence of lower attainment. Ordinary support remains available without accepting a sensitive inference. Participant data are not available for supplier/foundation-model training or secondary product improvement; any narrowly approved research use requires its own purpose, necessity, authority, rights and retention basis.


## Human authority, support and safeguarding

Teachers are instructional partners who establish learning purposes, curate sequence and representations, interpret evidence and decide follow-up. Their role includes preparation and ordinary instructional judgement as well as intervention when something fails. In adult learner-directed settings the profile allocates goal, pace, evidence, exit and support authority explicitly to the learner and any accountable educator.

| Journey | Concrete human/service responsibility | Evidence of practical authority and burden |
|---|---|---|
| Before use | Preview source, task, repertoire, constraints, access modes and hand-off; assign purpose and sequence; set permitted support and adjustments | People understand what the service will do, can modify legitimate local choices, and can decline it while receiving the appropriate alternative |
| During a learning activity | Inspect provenance and uncertainty; correct a mistaken interpretation; override, pause or change route; involve a relevant educator/specialist | Controls work at the moment needed; correction reaches actual downstream use; support has adequate staffing and response capacity |
| After an activity | Receive concise evidence separating attempts, assistance, actions and unaided checks; plan next teaching | Information is usable without transferring hidden analysis or verification work to educators; teacher judgement is not replaced by model scoring |
| Incident or access failure | Protect the learner, preserve communication method, reach named support, record the actual effect and reconcile outstanding duties | Acknowledgement, delivery, human receipt and resolved support are separately visible; restarting a worker does not prove the duty completed |
| Continuing operation and change | Review burden, false alarms, incidents, evidence, access coverage and proposed versions; maintain alternative routes and transition support | Measure full workflow minutes, interruptions, checking, correction, escalation, trust calibration and distribution across roles/settings; staff data are protected from punitive use |

The required measures and gate owners are specified in [document 04](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md). Source basis: the service report §§5.2,6.3,6.7–6.8 and Appendix B. This is proposed service design, not an appointment of named people or a report that authority has been tested.

### Safeguarding and accessible escalation

DfE’s 2026 product safety standards expect filtering, activity logging, user signposting and local-supervisor alerts, including for possible safeguarding disclosures. These expectations must be read with the [school and college filtering and monitoring standard](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/meeting-digital-and-technology-standards-in-schools-and-colleges/filtering-and-monitoring-core-standard), which assigns named human responsibility and recognises that technical monitoring does not itself stop unsafe activity. Detection is evidence for triage, not a safeguarding decision.

The service needs to measure false positives and false negatives across age, communication mode, disability and SEND subgroups. Atypical, literal, repetitive, AAC-mediated or context-poor language may be misread; conversely, a system may fail to recognise distress expressed indirectly. Broad logging can also conflict with privacy, data minimisation and learner trust. These tensions cannot be resolved by declaring either safety or privacy paramount in the abstract: purpose, access, retention, thresholds, human review, transparency and proportionality must be designed and evaluated together.

Human escalation must be:

- visible before a crisis and reachable without navigating the generative agent;
- available through accessible text and alternative communication, not phone-only;
- clear about who receives what information, expected response time and limits of confidentiality;
- age-, context- and institution-aware;
- supportive and non-punitive after a false alarm;
- capable of preserving the learner’s communication method and relevant context;
- tested for availability, latency, hand-off completion and return to learning.

The service communicates its educational function clearly and supports learner agency and effective access to human help. Mental-health care and safeguarding decisions belong to appropriately qualified and accountable people. Where immediate danger is indicated, the service uses approved, jurisdiction-appropriate crisis and emergency routes while supporting accessible communication.


## Access, burden and distributional evidence

Every materially distinct **subject/profile × goal × learner group × context × access mode × human-support mode × service version** is an evidence cell. Testing every possible combination is impossible; the programme should select cells by severity of consequence, uncertainty, scale, novelty, plausible differential effect and affected-party priority, while retaining clear limits on transport.

| Programme | Core measures | Minimum decision question |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Conformance** | WCAG/EN findings; severity; journey coverage; documentation/support; remediation; regression | Are all critical paths conformant, and are exceptions explicit and effectively mitigated? |
| **2. Usability and access** | Task completion; time/effort; errors and recovery; AT compatibility; comprehension; abandonment; support burden; dignity and control | Can intended users complete authentic journeys without disproportionate burden? |
| **3. Learning and validity** | Delayed unaided retention; near and bounded farther transfer; explanation; misconception reduction; calibration; independence; construct validity | Does this configuration improve the intended learning, and what inference survives its accommodations? |
| **4. Distributional effects** | Outcomes, harms, refusal, false positives/negatives, missingness, dropout and burden by relevant subgroup and intersection | Are gains and burdens acceptably distributed, including among those least well served? |
| **5. Safety and human system** | Harm exposure; detection error; escalation accessibility, latency and completion; privacy burden; staff workload; incident recovery | Does the whole sociotechnical service prevent, detect and recover from foreseeable harm? |

Evaluation should pre-specify important subgroup and intersection analyses rather than mine small samples after the event. Relevant dimensions may include functional access need, communication mode, age/phase, subject, disadvantage, language, race/ethnicity, gender, care experience, institutional context and degree of prior attainment. Small groups require careful privacy protection, uncertainty intervals and qualitative evidence; an underpowered null result must not be described as parity.

Missingness is an outcome. Learners unable to enrol, consent, configure access, reach a lesson, tolerate the interaction or complete follow-up must remain visible in reach and effect estimates. Report the effort required to verify AI output, correct recognition errors, obtain human support and recover from safety interventions. A nominally equivalent score achieved with much greater burden is not equivalent access.

Representative disabled and SEND learners, families, educators, SENDCOs, accessibility specialists, speech and language expertise, safeguarding professionals and relevant adult/community partners should help choose outcomes, tasks, thresholds and interpretations. Co-design is not one consultation workshop: it continues through need definition, prototype, failure analysis, evaluation, governance and change review.

The global service measurement register remains in [document 04](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md), including baselines, timing, comparators, ownership, publication and gaming risk. The matrix here supplies the domain-specific observations it must carry. Published evidence must expose its actual cell coverage; it must not imply that every combination was tested or that agent reviews replace paid affected-user research and specialist assurance.


## Procurement and continuing operation

GDS states that a public body remains responsible for accessibility when a service is outsourced and recommends putting accessibility into requests, evaluation, contracts and ongoing review ([*Make things accessible and inclusive*](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/make-things-accessible)). A supplier assertion, VPAT or conformance report is evidence to inspect, not a transfer of responsibility.

Procurement and platform agreements should require:

- declared WCAG 2.2 and EN 301 549 scope, methods, exceptions and evidence;
- an assistive-technology and authentic-journey test matrix, including known unsupported combinations;
- accessible documentation, onboarding, support, incident notices and complaints;
- data-purpose, location, retention, deletion and training-use controls, with no participant data used for supplier/foundation-model training or secondary product improvement; narrowly approved research reuse requires a separate necessity, lawful-basis, ethics and rights decision;
- model, safety layer, prompt, routing, dependency and interface version identifiers;
- advance notice and test access for material changes;
- accessibility, safety and subgroup non-regression thresholds;
- remediation service levels proportionate to severity and an emergency disable/rollback route;
- incident reporting, root-cause analysis and notification duties;
- audit rights, subcontractor flow-down and evidence retention;
- export, migration, substitution and termination assistance so an inaccessible or harmful component can be replaced.

Continuous testing should combine automated checks, expert manual audit, component fixtures, end-to-end tests with assistive technology, user research, red teaming and monitoring of real-service failure indicators. New content, subject profiles, models and safety rules can each introduce accessibility regressions without changing the visible application shell. Release authority must therefore attach to the assembled service version, not the front-end alone.


## Experiments and specialist commissions

#### Commit now

These commitments are justified by rights, standards, known failure modes or the need to preserve valid evidence. They should not wait for a POC effect estimate:

- accessibility and SEND excellence as a non-compensatory release gate;
- WCAG 2.2 AA plus applicable EN 301 549 coverage across the full service;
- a functional, learner-controlled access contract rather than diagnosis-driven personas;
- equivalent multimodal access and compatibility with learners’ own assistive technology;
- no speech-, vision-, hearing-, speed-, fine-motor- or memory-only path for a core service task unless that capability is the explicit construct;
- stable semantic generative interaction, accessible mathematics and controllable streaming;
- visible separation of coaching, practice and independent assessment;
- a construct-and-accommodation register for assessed uses;
- minimal, inspectable, correctable, expiring learner-state hypotheses and no diagnosis inference;
- context-appropriate, accessible human escalation and assisted-digital routes;
- versioned, offline-governed system improvement with staged testing and rollback;
- procurement duties, continuous testing, transparent exceptions and effective exit.

#### Experiment within bounded research

These are plausible design choices whose benefits and harms are not settled:

- learner-selected versus system-suggested presentation, pacing and scaffold changes;
- which COGA/UDL patterns help which learners and constructs without reducing subject meaning;
- the best screen-reader announcement, focus and history model for streamed dialogue;
- whether optional speech personalisation produces sufficient access benefit for its enrolment, correction and privacy burden;
- whether persistent cross-session memory improves delayed retention and transfer over an otherwise equivalent no-derived-state service;
- how progressive disclosure balances desirable effort with legitimate need for worked examples;
- when modality changes preserve constructs and when they require a different inference;
- which uncertainty and provenance presentations support calibrated trust across communication and cognitive profiles;
- whether accessible educator-supported, learner-directed, resource-based or human-delivered modes perform best for a given need and setting.

#### Commissioned work before material scale

1. **Paid SEND and disability co-design programme:** include children and adults with SLCN/AAC use, BSL, deafblindness, blindness/low vision, hearing loss, motor impairment, learning disability, dyslexia/dyscalculia, autism, ADHD, chronic/fluctuating conditions and compound needs, together with families and professionals where appropriate.
2. **Cross-subject access benchmark:** authentic maths, English, science, humanities, languages, computing, arts and vocational journeys using screen readers/braille, keyboard/switch/eye gaze, AAC, magnification, captions and voice.
3. **UK diverse-speech benchmark:** children and adults, speech difference/disorder, accents, dialects, multilingual use, noise and fatigue; include correction burden, abandonment and privacy—not word-error rate alone.
4. **Construct/accommodation protocol:** curriculum, subject, SEND and assessment experts define task constructs, barriers, permitted supports and defensible inferences.
5. **Pragmatic efficacy studies:** delayed unaided retention and transfer, subject × age × functional need × disadvantage, with dropout, support time and implementation fidelity.
6. **Safeguarding validation:** false-positive/negative rates by communication and SEND subgroup, accessible hand-off, response latency, privacy effects and failure simulation.
7. **Adaptive-system safety protocol:** versioned offline change, shadow/canary evaluation, subgroup non-regression, rollback, incident review and a prohibition on unbounded reward optimisation.
8. **Privacy-preserving learner-model comparison:** no memory, learner-held memory and minimal service-held memory; test educational added value, correction, deletion, explanation and opt-out.
9. **Procurement and independent assurance pack:** evidence templates, test matrices, model-change notification, regression SLA, audit, incident and exit requirements.
10. **Jurisdiction and lifelong-context mapping:** separate law, safeguarding, human-support and institutional-authority routes for England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and for school, FE/HE, workplace, community and independent adult use.

The commitments are design and release conditions; the experimental configurations and the scale/timing of individual commissions remain proposals. Before learner-facing exposure, the service report also calls for independent subject/profile and assessment panels; paid affected-user governance plus end-to-end AT audit; learner-model/psychometric/privacy/causal-adaptation expertise; and safeguarding/red-team/operations/change assurance. Any affected-pupil council, co-design or task test is participant research requiring its applicable safeguards and authority. A qualified human assurance body is different from a source-comparison review by another agent.


## Defeaters, reopening and claim language

- No robust evidence presently shows that a general-purpose generative tuition service improves learning across SEND groups, subjects and ages.
- Profound and multiple learning disabilities, low-incidence needs, AAC, BSL and deafblind learners are substantially underrepresented in generative-AI research.
- The accessibility of streaming, multimodal and tool-using generative interfaces is not well studied over extended learning journeys.
- Assistive-technology research is heterogeneous and often small, dated or context-specific; technical availability does not establish educational benefit.
- UDL’s complete package has thinner causal evidence than its prominence suggests, particularly for secondary technology use, self-regulation and negative effects.
- Persistent learner models and adaptive feedback loops lack convincing long-term evidence on learning, dependency, misclassification, privacy and cross-context consequences.
- The privacy–safeguarding monitoring tension is not empirically resolved, particularly where disability-related language changes detector error.
- Construct preservation across multimodal and generative accommodations is rarely tested directly.
- Subgroup evidence often omits people who cannot access enrolment or follow-up and is underpowered for intersections.
- Adult, vocational, informal and later-life contexts have distinct access and authority requirements but little direct AI-enabled tuition efficacy evidence.
- No single metric can establish excellent accessibility; conformance, task access, burden, learning, dignity, safety and distribution must remain separately visible.

“No evidence located” means an open question, not evidence of no effect. Conversely, a compelling demonstration, testimonial or average result does not close these gaps.

### Stop and reopening conditions

The accessibility proposition should be declined, stopped or materially redesigned for an evidence cell if any of the following persists after a reasonable remediation attempt:

- a critical learning, consent, data-control, complaint or escalation path is inaccessible;
- the service cannot interoperate with required assistive technology and offers no effective alternative;
- an accommodation changes the intended construct while the system reports the original inference;
- disabled or SEND learners experience materially worse learning, burden, false safety intervention, exclusion or dignity outcomes without an acceptable remedy;
- the system repeatedly mistakes communication or disability-related behaviour for disengagement, non-compliance, low ability or risk;
- human escalation lacks capacity, accessibility, authority or response-time reliability;
- learner data cannot be minimised, corrected, deleted, segregated or protected sufficiently for the proposed use;
- a supplier or model change cannot be identified, tested, rolled back or exited;
- engagement optimisation, uncontrolled adaptation or relational design creates dependency or self-reinforcing harm;
- a simpler resource, assistive technology, educator copilot or human service delivers better access and learning at lower risk and acceptable cost.

Reopen a previously accepted decision after any material change to law, standards, model, safety layer, interface, subject profile, assessment construct, population, context, assistive-technology ecosystem, supplier or human-support arrangement; after a serious incident or credible new evidence of harm; or when affected learners challenge the assumptions on which the decision rested.

### Evidence-bounded public language

Use evidence-bounded language:

- **“Designed against WCAG 2.2 AA and EN 301 549”** only while implementation evidence is incomplete.
- **“Conformant for the audited scope and version”** only after qualified manual and technical assessment, with exceptions disclosed.
- **“Usable in the tested journeys by the tested participants and assistive technologies”** only after authentic user research.
- **“Improved the specified learning outcomes for the tested population and configuration”** only after an appropriate outcome study.
- **“No material subgroup harm detected within stated uncertainty and thresholds”** rather than “works equally for everyone.”
- **“Accessibility/SEND benefit remains a hypothesis”** where only a feature, framework or expert rationale exists.

Avoid: “fully accessible,” “SEND-proof,” “personalised to every learner,” “understands the learner,” “bias-free,” “safe by design,” “UDL-compliant therefore effective,” or any claim that conformance, engagement or reach establishes learning impact.

**Status:** provisional evidence-informed design. Current evidence supports immediate accessibility, rights, agency and human-service commitments; it does not establish universal accessibility, SEND learning efficacy, procurement readiness or permission for a particular participant deployment. Useful work can begin with real subject/AT journeys, accommodation semantics, human hand-off and correction tests, grounded in [document 13](13-oce-work-programme-and-integration.md).
