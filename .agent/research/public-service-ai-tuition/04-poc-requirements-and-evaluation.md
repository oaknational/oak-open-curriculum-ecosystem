## 1. What the POC should establish

The POC should make consequential service behaviours observable and determine which configurations create useful, durable learning with acceptable burden and rights protection. Early builds can yield worthwhile capabilities and resolve distinct uncertainties. A final bounded POC can support a more complete test of the educational mechanism and additional value. The work should leave reusable methods, fixtures, evidence and options that remain useful after a profile is changed or stopped.

“Prove” means evidence sufficient for a named decision at a stated stage, with scope and residual uncertainty explicit. It does not mean certainty. A working representation, expert-rated response, accessibility test, independently estimated learning effect, operative legal permission and sustained public benefit are different observations. Models can triage or score under audited conditions; they cannot be sole judges of consequential curriculum, pedagogy, access, rights, safety or impact claims.

The fundamental learning inquiry is whether subject-valid support creates correctly directed learner effort and capability that persists after support ends. The experiment must distinguish that explanation from extra time, easier tasks, answer exposure, novelty, teacher attention, changed access, selected participation and measurement artefacts. The serious alternative is high-quality non-generative provision that accomplishes the same educational purpose with less complexity or burden.

The research objects below must remain distinct:

| Object | Required definition |
|---|---|
| Decision | Concrete choice, accountable owner, live alternatives, constraints and review point |
| Question | Causal, predictive, descriptive, mechanistic, measurement or implementation uncertainty |
| Estimand | Target population/setting; compared conditions; outcome; horizon; contrast; handling of switching, attrition, partial exposure and other intercurrent events |
| Estimator | Rule/model estimating that estimand, identification assumptions and uncertainty calculation |
| Estimate | Observed result and interval/distribution with provenance and sensitivity analysis |
| Action rule | Predeclared treatment of benefit, harm, rights, distribution, uncertainty and operation in choosing continue, stop, revise, release, withhold or investigate |

A favourable estimate does not make release automatic. Conversely, educational uncertainty does not justify deferring necessary work on accessible activity, dependable custody or usable human control.

### Hypotheses and falsifiers: H1–H7

H1–H7 are the report's corresponding claims, kept alongside the experiments so a reader can recover both the activity and the proposition it tests. H4 resolves an inherited shorthand: the original table treated more help-seeking as a falsifier, while the same report values calibrated help-seeking and identifies compulsive dependency as the risk. The current action rule therefore uses contextual evidence of unproductive dependency rather than raw requests. A failed hypothesis may require narrowing or stopping the affected capability. It need not refute unrelated profiles.

## 3. The preserved initial study proposal

### 3.1 Population, purpose and estimand

The report proposes **Year 9 proportional and multiplicative reasoning in high-disadvantage English state-funded secondary schools**. Whole classes are the proposed eligibility frame; transparent selection and prespecified FSM/Pupil Premium distributional analysis avoid treating disadvantage as a capability label. The high-disadvantage threshold, recruitment sites, participant number, age/context coverage and exact learning construct have not yet been ratified.

The proposed **sole primary learner outcome** is the prespecified-scale **intention-to-treat difference on an independently governed, externally marked, curriculum-valid, unfamiliar near-transfer assessment four weeks after the last supported session**, with no assistance. This combines retained capability with application to structurally related unfamiliar items. Retention and farther transfer remain distinguishable constructs; changing surface context does not by itself establish transfer distance.

The proposed comparator is high-quality **non-generative computer-assisted practice matched on content, time, task opportunity, worked examples and feedback**. This is intended to estimate the added value of the configured AI-enabled tuition package. It does not isolate generation, learner-state estimation or policy adaptation. A component claim needs a contrast changing that component while holding other consequential inputs fixed. Business as usual is a contextual baseline; human tuition is an educational/economic benchmark. Independent expert responses to the same cases test process quality, not efficacy.

Mathematics is a useful candidate because proportional reasoning supports constrained strategy, representation, error and transfer cases. A bounded Year 9 English reading-and-writing construct should be chosen with teachers and assessment experts, retaining textual evidence, interpretive plurality, audience/purpose, drafting, voice and authorship. The source proposes bench, expert, teacher and access work on English in parallel, followed by its own efficacy study. This is a sequencing proposal; mathematics success does not license English effects, and failure does not demonstrate English failure.

### 3.2 Allocation, power, contamination and analysis

The report prefers within-class individual randomisation for contextual balance and power unless contamination, unequal treatment, implementation or safeguarding considerations warrant clusters. An independent statistician should use pilot evidence to choose the unit, account for repeated observations and class/school structure, estimate precision/power and attrition, and document spillovers. Repeated events from a small number of learners or schools do not create equivalent independent units.

The final plan must name assignment, intervention/exposure, observation, analysis/dependence and inference units separately. It must specify stable identity, allocation generation and concealment, when allocation becomes visible, exposure ascertainment, switching, contamination, peer/class/teacher interference and unequal co-interventions. Group randomisation needs enough independent clusters; a individual design needs a credible contamination account. Use intention-to-treat as the primary estimate of assignment/eligibility; any treatment-received, per-protocol or instrumental estimate is secondary with its additional assumptions.

Preregister before condition differences are visible: question and theory; hypotheses; eligibility; conditions; estimands; assignment/exposure; outcomes/timing; sample-information rationale; exclusions; transformations/covariates/outliers; analysis/dependence model; confirmatory hierarchy and multiplicity; subgroup plan; missing-data assumptions; sequential/interim looks; harms; stop rules and decision thresholds. Record software, seeds and data lineage. Preserve original protocols, then timestamp amendments with author, rationale and whether outcome information was visible.

No numerical worthwhile-benefit, harm, equivalence, subgroup or workload margin is defensible from the supplied evidence alone. Before efficacy decision use, the independent evaluator, assessment lead and service owner should agree and justify margins from instrument reliability, ordinary educational progress, stakeholder judgement, educational consequence and whole-life/opportunity cost. A teacher panel sets acceptable workload conditions; relevant gate leads and affected users define unacceptable harms and minimum interpretable access/group evidence. Report expected precision or power across plausible variance, baseline, clustering, exposure and attrition scenarios. Decide from effect and interval relative to those margins; a binary p-value is insufficient.

Report eligibility, invitation, refusal, consent/assent, configuration failure, participation, exposure, follow-up and attrition by arm and relevant group. Assess whether missingness is treatment- or access-dependent; use sensitivity analyses appropriate to plausible mechanisms. Do not erase exclusion by imputation. Describe concurrent teaching, dosage and fidelity. An underpowered, attrition-compromised, contaminated, invalidly measured or implementation-invalid result is **inconclusive**, not equivalence or safety, and does not support progression on that claim.

### 3.3 Secure and valid measurement

The assessment authority owns construct definition, blueprint, secure items, scoring, equating/parallel forms, accommodation validity and warranted inference. The independent evaluator owns causal synthesis and audits delivery-supplied evidence. Formal outcome instruments and outcomes remain isolated from the live activity and learner-state estimator. Maintain separate formative unaided anchors where the approved learning design needs them.

Pilot content validity, reliability, form equivalence and scoring consistency with the actual population, language, device and access modes. Blind marking and analysis where practicable. Test whether accommodations preserve the construct and whether comparisons are valid. Farther-transfer tasks require independently justified distance, novelty and construct coverage; assessment changes can alter reliability. Logs and model scoring require human reliability/audit and version identity. Display acknowledgement proves delivery behaviour, not attention, understanding or learning.

### 3.4 Credible counterdesigns and when they help

A randomised study is a justified candidate for a causal package comparison, not a universal research recipe. Compare with at least one design from another family when finalising a decision-relevant study:

| Design family | Question it can help answer | Important limits and reason to choose or reject |
|---|---|---|
| Individual versus cluster randomisation | Package or frozen policy effect with a known allocation process | Individual allocation offers precision/context balance; clusters may contain spillover or practical inequity but reduce independent-unit count and require clustering-aware precision. |
| Factorial or approved component/move contrast | Which independently acceptable component changes action or outcome | More arms/interactions demand information and may impair the main study; hold consequential inputs fixed and control multiplicity. |
| Crossover or N-of-1 | Reversible short-horizon response or implementation differences within an individual | Learning carries over and is not generally washable; a later period is changed by earlier teaching. Do not assume suitability for durable learning effects. |
| Encouragement design | Effect where uptake cannot be assigned but an ethical encouragement can | Encouragement may affect outcomes through other routes; instrumental interpretations need explicit assumptions, uptake evidence and scope. |
| Long-term holdout | Persistence, novelty decay, delayed harm or effects of a stable release | Ethical/equivalent provision and contamination/attrition remain; extended data and burden need purpose and authority. |
| Interrupted time series or regression discontinuity | A policy/time or threshold-defined change where randomisation is unavailable | Specify identification assumptions and diagnostics: trends, concurrent changes, threshold manipulation and continuity. Do not label the result randomised. |
| Matched observational comparison | Bounded effectiveness/implementation evidence with existing provision | Selection and unmeasured confounding can remain despite matching; state which causal conclusions are unsupported. |
| Simulation, benchmark and shadow evaluation | Reject unsafe candidates; test mechanics, pipelines, coverage and semantics | No candidate exposure in shadow; historical policy bias and unrealistic simulated users constrain inference. These cannot establish actual learner effects. |
| Qualitative, participatory and mixed methods | Understand mechanisms, non-use, access, implementation, hidden labour, dignity and rival explanations | Sampling, dissent and positionality matter; mechanism accounts do not replace a credible counterfactual for a causal effect claim. |

These counterdesign notes are an interpretive synthesis of the preserved experimental protocol, not newly commissioned studies. Select the least assumption-dependent feasible approach answering the actual decision with acceptable burden and rights treatment.

## 4. Seven non-compensatory service gates

Each gate tests a different necessary condition. Passing means adequate evidence for the **next named bounded activity**, within stated coverage and versions. Gates apply proportionately to bench, participant and operational contexts; no aggregate score can offset a material failure. GATE-1–GATE-7 here refer to this service report's instrument. Other education, programme or standards gates retain their own meanings and require an explicit crosswalk.

| Gate | Minimum acceptable condition | Unacceptable failure and immediate rule |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Curriculum integrity** | Authoritative version/provenance; valid concept, prerequisite and mapping; subject experts find no systematic error within prespecified coverage | Material/frequent misalignment, fabricated source or misconception reinforcement: stop affected tasks, correct and independently retest |
| **2 Pedagogical and adaptive validity** | The profile-validated functions and assistance conditions reliably occur under an approved, versioned policy, with a meaningful learner attempt where appropriate; support preserves the construct and enables subsequent learner action; if a learner-state hypothesis is used, it is uncertainty-aware, contestable and independently calibrated | Answer leakage, habitual over-helping, unsupported/fixed diagnosis, self-confirming selection, live policy learning or proxy gaming: block exposure, rollback or withdraw behaviour |
| **3 Assessment validity** | Intended construct, independent task governance, unaided scoring, accommodations and uncertainty are defensible | Contamination through learning interactions, assisted “mastery”, invalid subgroup comparison or high-stakes use: invalidate result and stop decision use |
| **4 Accessibility and inclusion** | WCAG 2.2 AA baseline where applicable plus cognitive guidance, co-designed access-needs matrix, authentic affected-user task evidence, assistive-technology compatibility, valid adjustments, low-bandwidth route and an effective human/specialist alternative; no identified critical core-journey barrier within declared tested coverage | A material barrier, lowered intellectual destination, unsafe moderation or invalid accommodated assessment: no compensating average; correct, provide an effective construct-preserving alternative route, restrict or withdraw |
| **5 Privacy and children's rights** | Necessity/minimisation, determined controller/processor/joint roles and lawful basis, DPIA, purpose/retention controls, learner/carer information, access/correction/objection/redress, transfer/subprocessor/breach controls; material learner-state inferences are intelligible, contestable and expiring; no supplier/foundation-model training or secondary product improvement from participant data; narrowly approved research reuse only under separate necessity, lawful-basis, ethics and child-rights approval | Unlawful/opaque processing, whole-person or unbounded profiling, prohibited secondary use, failed correction/deletion or unmitigated high risk: no processing/exposure; notify/escalate as required |
| **6 Safety and safeguarding** | Age-appropriate boundaries, content/adversarial controls, non-anthropomorphism, session limits, human help, DSL protocol, incident detection/response/remedy | Critical harmful advice/content, emotional manipulation, concealed incident or failed escalation: immediate pause, protect, investigate, report and restrict/withdraw |
| **7 Teacher, learner and professional authority** | In school use, teachers can meaningfully preview, configure, interpret, correct, override, intervene and escalate with acceptable end-to-end burden; a learner-directed adult profile assigns equivalent goal, state, pace and exit control to the learner and names any educator's authority | Nominal control, automation bias, coercive adult use, missing explanation or material workload increase: do not proceed unchanged; redesign or withdraw |


### Gate ownership and public evidence

The senior responsible owner (SRO) progresses after separate gate-owner and evaluator advice and the applicable approval; a gate score is not self-executing authority. In schools, safeguarding duties remain with the relevant governing body/proprietor and senior lead supported by the designated safeguarding lead (DSL) and IT, alongside the service safeguarding owner. Adult/community profiles must name their own applicable roles, support route and thresholds rather than importing school DSL arrangements.

| Gate | Accountable owner | Public evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Curriculum integrity | Curriculum lead and independent subject panel | Scope, provenance, rubric reliability, error/remedy aggregate |
| Pedagogical/adaptive validity | Educational research lead with evaluator | Case coverage, learner-state calibration/correction/expiry, common anchors, disagreements, feedback-loop, leakage/fading and rollback tests, and bounded results |
| Assessment validity | Independent assessment lead | Blueprint, reliability/equating, accommodation and contamination limits; no secure items |
| Accessibility/inclusion | Accessibility lead with paid affected-user panel | Access-needs matrix, authentic journey/AT/subject-representation coverage, barriers, safety-classifier errors, residual limits, effective alternatives and remedies |
| Privacy/children's rights | Controller/service owner, advised and monitored by DPO | Redacted DPIA/rights assessment, purposes, retention, rights and audit outcomes |
| Safety/safeguarding | Governing body/proprietor and identified SLT owner, supported by DSL/IT; service safeguarding lead | Safe scenario/incident coverage, escalation drills and remedies |
| Teacher, learner and professional authority | Education service owner with representative teacher panel; adult-profile owner and learner panel when applicable | Workflow comprehension, goal/state control, workload distribution, overrides, exits and resulting changes |

## 5. Evidence ladder and proxy limits

A measure has a ceiling on the inference it supports. For every proposed proxy, identify the construct, mechanism connecting it to an outcome, evidence of predictive/concurrent validity, rivals, lag, gaming risk and permitted decision. Where such validation is absent, call it a face/construct or process indicator rather than a validated surrogate.

| Evidence state / proposed measure | Construct and legitimate inference | Causal link, limits and rival explanation | Lag, gaming risk and decision use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curriculum/source accuracy; tutoring-move rubric | Candidate interaction quality | Necessary, not sufficient; expert agreement and narrow coverage need not predict learning | Immediate; tuning/fluent-rationale risk. E1/readiness only |
| Safety, bias, access and adversarial pass rate with severity/coverage | Tested hazard/access performance | Scenarios omit users, combinations and rare events; absence is coverage-bounded | Immediate/continuous; disclosed-test gaming. Gate evidence, no safety certificate |
| Attempts, hint depth, revisions, explanations, feedback uptake | Process/fidelity | May mediate learning; unreliable scoring or performed moves need not persist | Session; scripting/metric gaming. Mechanism, not impact |
| Sessions, active pupils, completion, latency, return, time | Reach/use/operations | May reflect novelty, obligation, confusion or dependency; non-use may be appropriate | Immediate; engagement optimisation prohibited. Improvement/reach only |
| Teacher/pupil satisfaction | Acceptability | Courtesy, novelty, low expectations and survivorship; no workload/learning inference | Short lag; selective response. Feasibility only |
| Success with hints/examples/answers | Assisted performance | Copying, recognition or leakage; supported gains may reverse on withdrawal | Immediate; easier/more revealing tasks. Scaffold calibration only |
| Same-day unaided parallel form | Immediate attainment | Recency, form error and teaching-to-test remain | Same day; contamination. Secondary, not retention |
| Four-week unaided unfamiliar near-transfer form within the practised construct | Delayed near transfer: retained capability applied to structurally similar unfamiliar items | Attrition, concurrent teaching/exposure and covert overlap | Four weeks; narrow practice/selective follow-up. Sole learner primary |
| Additional prespecified novel contexts/problems | Bounded-far transfer beyond the primary near-transfer form | Novelty is judged; distance may change construct/reliability | Four/optional 12 weeks; covert overlap. Bounds farther-transfer claim |
| Performance/help-seeking under fading and removal | Tested independence | Task ease/prior attainment confound; not lifelong independence | Programme/follow-up; easy withdrawals. Required for Continue |
| Learner-state estimator calibration | Degree to which versioned, expiring hypotheses predict policy-independent held-out/common anchor observations relevant to reversible support | A prediction is not mastery or semantic truth; policy-selected data can make the estimator self-confirming | Session/across authorised sessions; stale-state, labelling and selective-task risk. Report provenance, calibration, confidence, expiry, correction and reset; compare with no-derived-state baseline |
| Candidate tuition-policy evaluation | Comparative evidence that a named policy changes action selection and—if claimed—learner outcomes relative to an approved alternative | Offline fit, replay and shadow evaluation may reproduce historical policy bias and cannot prove a causal outcome | Release cycle; reward/proxy gaming and feedback loops. Risk-bounded independent trial, approval and rollback; no direct live self-modification |
| Teacher minutes, decisions, overrides and escalation | Workload/practical authority | Savings may shift to invisible checking/incidents; override may be ceremonial | Full workflow; under-recording. Gate/implementation |
| Access, accommodations, subgroup exposure/attrition/effect, harms | Equity/rights within coverage | Low power and invalid tasks defeat equal-access claims | Pre-use/continuous; aggregation/classification. Gate/scope |
| Cost per eligible/reached/retained-benefit pupil | Efficiency/cost-effectiveness | Denominators hide school/assurance cost; effects may fade | Whole-life; optimistic volume. Procurement/scale after impact |
| Downloads, calls, accounts, repository activity | Discovery/adoption signal | Bots, evaluations and abandonment mimic reuse | Immediate; vanity metric. Outreach only |
| Two implementations accept valid artefacts | Syntactic conformance | No proof of preserved meaning or completed task | POC; author-case gaming. Entry to reuse test |
| Non-author workflow with meaning/burden; later upgrade/exit | Operational reuse, semantics, substitutability | One subsidised adopter lacks generality/durability | POC–multi-year; favourable selection. Achieved reuse rung only |


Delayed retention alone does not establish transfer; novel-task success establishes only the prespecified transfer scope. In this candidate study, the primary combines a stated delay with near-transfer tasks. Farther transfer, persistence and independence must remain separately reported. Appropriate non-use or help-seeking can serve the learner; maximise neither engagement nor help avoidance. Satisfaction cannot establish workload reduction or teaching quality. Process findings cannot rescue a null primary learning effect.

## 6. Complete useful measurement contract

### 6.1 Required fields and common handling

Every material measurement specification must fill the following fields before decision use; the register below preserves existing choices and names where thresholds or instruments remain unresolved.

| Field | What must travel with the measure |
|---|---|
| Outcome/construct and measure type | What is being measured and whether it is a direct outcome, proxy, process, implementation, reach, rights/harm, operational, economic or reuse measure |
| Metric/instrument and unit/population | Exact definition, scoring/version, numerator/denominator, observation/analysis unit and eligible/included population |
| Baseline and counterfactual | Prior assessment or workflow/service baseline, compared condition and why it answers the decision |
| Timing | Frequency, onset, exposure window, delay from final support and follow-up/monitoring horizon |
| Threshold | Minimum meaningful benefit, harm/equivalence region, critical-event rule; named setter, rationale and deadline for locking it |
| Distribution | Relevant groups/intersections, exposure and missingness denominators, minimum interpretable evidence and confidentiality controls |
| Validity/reliability and attribution | Construct/form/accommodation/scoring evidence; causal design, estimator, dependence and assumptions |
| Implementation and dosage | What was offered, delivered, encountered and used; policy, task and help level; context and concurrent provision |
| Bias, missingness and attrition | Plausible selection, treatment-dependent logging, refusal/dropout/follow-up mechanisms and sensitivity analysis |
| Gaming/Goodhart risk | How improving the recorded number could leave the intended outcome unchanged or worse |
| Data sensitivity and burden | Necessary personal/sensitive fields, access/retention protections, collection cost and participant/teacher verification labour |
| Ownership and independent evaluation | Accountable construct/gate/service owner, custodian, analyst and independent evaluator with declared conflicts |
| Publication and decision | What protocol, aggregate, code, null/adverse result or limitation becomes public; protected detail; exact gate/direction informed |

Across measures, freeze instruments and thresholds; record arm, actual exposure and fidelity; blind where practicable; inspect assignment and data integrity before effects; publish disclosure-reviewed protocols, aggregates, null/adverse/mixed results and deviations. Assessment, research, access, rights, safeguarding, teacher, operations, economic and standards leads own their particular evidence. An evaluator independent of delivery integrates causal claims and audits supplied records.

## 7. E7: optional learner state and causal decision value

E7 asks whether a particular, purpose-limited and expiring hypothesis improves durable unaided learning when used by an approved policy, compared with an otherwise equivalent policy using no derived state, with acceptable rights, equity, safety, independence and cost. It is not answered by plausible descriptions, historical prediction fit, dashboard satisfaction or a feeling of personalisation. [06](06-state-rights-and-context.md) owns the exact state classes, schema, action-time rights, correction, expiry and cross-context controls.

### 7.1 Preconditions and estimator phase

Before learner exposure: approve the subject/profile and repertoire; define the construct, sources and permitted decisions; establish why current-task evidence and declarations may be insufficient; test event/state separation and rights journeys; test calibration, missingness, expiry and correction on policy-independent anchors; pass access, privacy, safeguarding and security within declared coverage; freeze policy and test a state-free fallback; establish independent outcome custody and applicable ethics/assent/consent/authority.

| Element | Minimum design |
|---|---|
| Construct | One or a small number of subject-valid, decision-relevant states; no general learner vector as the primary object |
| Evidence | Explicit task events, help exposures and unaided anchors; assisted evidence separately marked |
| Split | Learner- and time-aware separation that prevents leakage; current estimator tested on future or held-out common anchors |
| Baselines | Simple recency/opportunity model, declared/current-session evidence, and no-derived-state policy inputs |
| Metrics | Log loss or Brier score, calibration plots, discrimination, coverage/abstention, expiry performance and error consequences |
| Shift tests | Subject component, item form, time, curriculum/profile version, modality, access setting and relevant learner groups |
| Correction | Seed wrong and stale states; test learner/teacher challenge, reset and time to recovery |
| Exposure audit | Confirm policy-selected items are not treated as independent validation |
| Decision | Only advance if the construct is interpretable enough for its decision, calibration is acceptable and added prediction could plausibly alter a beneficial action |


Prediction quality and representation/decision validity must be considered separately. A more accurate opaque model does not automatically advance. Simple recency/opportunity, current-task/declaration and no-derived-state baselines can expose whether complexity supplies useful information. Test learner/time leakage, future/held-out anchors, abstention, stale-state error and recovery. Policy-selected items are exposures, not independent validation.

### 7.2 Frozen causal contrast

| Feature | No-derived-state arm | State-conditioned arm |
|---|---|---|
| Curriculum/profile | Same version | Same version |
| Content and task pool | Same | Same |
| Foundation/generative model | Same version and settings | Same version and settings |
| Learning support action repertoire | Same approved actions | Same approved actions |
| Interface, safeguards and teacher control | Same | Same |
| Current-session context | Available | Available |
| Learner/teacher declarations | Available under identical rules | Available under identical rules |
| Minimal exposure history | Same, if necessary for both arms | Same |
| Persistent derived hypothesis | Not available | Available only in the prespecified form |
| Policy difference | Selection cannot consult derived state | Selection may consult it under the frozen prespecified rule |


A state-free arm may share a necessary minimal exposure history to resume work or avoid repetition; it does not retain cross-session probabilistic claims about competence. Randomise before repeated interactions at learner, class or another defensible unit selected from contamination, power, implementation and safeguarding evidence. Avoid adaptive allocation changing exposure probabilities during the study.

The same four-week Year 9 near-transfer construct is a strong candidate only if the study is powered for the smaller incremental component effect and E7 does not compromise E3. Otherwise defer E7 to a separate study. Immediate unaided performance, fading, farther transfer, correction burden and learner/teacher experience are secondary/exploratory unless independently powered and prespecified.

Report separately: estimator calibration/prediction; whether state changes permitted policy actions; causal effect of eligibility for state-conditioned selection; treatment received/exposure; durable outcomes; independence/help-seeking; group/access outcomes; privacy/correction/workload; fidelity and exact versions; null/adverse/missing evidence. Association or mediation between prediction accuracy and outcomes does not prove state caused learning. With substantial override/noncompliance, retain ITT primary and state secondary per-protocol/instrumental assumptions.

Prespecify false-mastery/false-difficulty consequences, challenging-content opportunity, help/answer exposure, genuine attempts, withdrawal, state volatility/staleness/disagreement/reset, skill/group calibration, access modes/intersections, rights comprehension/correction burden, teacher overrides and automation bias, safeguarding false positives/negatives/escalation and critical single cases. Averages cannot erase serious individual or subgroup harm.

### 7.3 Progression and rollback

| Finding | Decision |
|---|---|
| State is poorly calibrated, cannot recover from seeded error, or fails relevant group/access tests | Do not enter learner causal testing; redesign or reject |
| State rarely changes the approved action | Remove it unless another necessary, evidenced purpose exists |
| State changes actions but does not improve durable outcome | Prefer the no-derived-state service |
| Small benefit with material privacy, correction, workload or equity burden | Do not persist without an explicit public-value judgement and further evidence |
| Worthwhile aggregate benefit but adverse subgroup, opportunity or safeguarding signal | Pause; investigate and restrict or reject affected coverage; an average cannot compensate |
| Worthwhile, equitable and durable benefit within bounded coverage | Permit only that versioned state, decision and coverage; continue monitoring and revalidation |
| Drift, self-confirmation, proxy gaming, repeated wrong labels or critical incident after release | Disable or roll back state-conditioned selection; preserve state-free service; investigate and reauthorise before any return |


### 7.4 E7 preregistration checklist carried as operational fields

Before data access, specify all 20 items: (1) state construct/version/permitted decisions; (2) necessity beyond current task/declarations; (3) event sources/exclusions/assistance/retention; (4) estimator/simple baselines/splits/independent anchors; (5) calibration/discrimination/coverage/missingness/shift; (6) error costs/expiry/staleness/seeded correction; (7) comparator/everything fixed; (8) allocation unit/contamination/power; (9) sole durable unaided primary; (10) secondary independence/transfer/experience/workload/process; (11) group/access analyses and minimum evidence; (12) privacy/rights/safeguarding outcomes; (13) exposure/action/propensity logging; (14) independent outcome custody and analyst access; (15) model/curriculum/task/profile/policy/safeguard/interface versions; (16) benefit/harm/critical/stop/rollback thresholds; (17) noncompliance/missingness/attrition; (18) publication of null/adverse/mixed/missing findings; (19) claim scope and expiry; (20) owner/forum for retain/narrow/redesign/remove/reopen.


## 8. Stage-appropriate evaluation and controlled service learning

### 8.1 Evidence stages

| Stage | Observable work and permitted claim | What the next decision needs |
|---|---|---|
| Need/baseline/co-design | Validate educational purpose, alternatives, actual workflow, appropriate non-use and opportunity cost; begin with adults where suitable | Applicable participation arrangements for any learner co-design; named construct/context and credible alternative |
| Bench/simulation | Synthetic/adversarial curriculum, support, provenance, trace, access, safeguard, conformance and failure/exit cases | Evidence of covered technical/domain behaviour and repaired material failures; no learning claim |
| Expert/access validation | Independent subject/PCK/assessment review; real AT and affected-user complete journeys within declared scope | Separate gate readiness and approved arrangements for the particular people/activity |
| Participant feasibility | Recruitment, usability, contamination, fidelity, missingness, burden and effective support | Feasible independently reviewed allocation/instruments/power plan; feasibility is not efficacy |
| Bounded efficacy | Frozen package/contrast, delayed unaided outcomes, withdrawal, distribution and independent analysis | Profile/version-specific effect with acceptable gates, workload and operational evidence |
| Effectiveness/economics | Ordinary conditions, replication, longer follow-up, transport, implementation variation and whole-life cost | Accountable, separately authorised next setting, procurement or operational scope |
| Live monitoring/periodic re-evaluation | Continuing gates, exposure, incidents, drift, subgroups, change, recovery and retirement | Defined monitoring windows, thresholds and owners; material changes or harms reopen affected evidence |

A study can be operationally completed and epistemically inconclusive. Record knowledge status (`validated`, `provisional`, `inconclusive`, `insufficient-evidence`, `declined`, `reopened`, `superseded`) separately from lifecycle (`draft`, `ready-to-run`, `running`, `paused`, `completed`, `blocked`, `cancelled`). A provisional protocol can be ready to run only if gaps are explicitly non-blocking and all required gates are met. Readiness requires instruments, data contracts, analysis code, ownership, rollback, stop rules and all required reviews to be resolved for the actual activity; an unresolved blocking item prevents the ready-to-run designation. Release/monitoring phase is a separate service state.

### 8.2 Improvement between identifiable releases

Keep three loops separate: the learning episode updates permitted current-task evidence/hypotheses and chooses a pre-gated move; the independent outcome loop measures later capability; the service loop considers outcomes, process, harms, operations and critical incidents to propose a candidate change. Participant interactions cannot directly change live objectives, state semantics, prompts, routing, rewards, policy, safeguards or model weights.

| Transition | Required substance and authority |
|---|---|
| Signal → candidate | Record bounded observation, source/dependence, expected/observed difference and alternative explanations. Define minimal proposed change, rationale, affected components, scope, non-goals, risks and expiry. A signal does not rewrite policy. |
| Candidate → identified version | Preserve deployed baseline; immutable references for model, prompt, curriculum/content, policy/schema, retrieval/tool/interface, evaluation and governed dataset. |
| Version → offline/shadow screening | Curated cases, defensible replay, simulation, common/held-out anchors, shift, subgroup, adverse and regression tests. Candidate outputs do not affect learners; historical policy bias limits causal inference. |
| Screening → independent gates | Appropriate curriculum, pedagogy, statistical, access, privacy, safeguarding, security and ethics review for the risk. Shared-context self-review is useful challenge, not independent assurance. |
| Gates → causal test where required | Frozen protocol and acceptable comparison against current release or another credible alternative; no live adaptive allocation into unapproved actions. |
| Evidence → release decision | Named authority weighs effect/uncertainty, harms, distribution, implementation, rights and reversibility. A favourable evaluation does not supply permission by itself. |
| Decision → bounded rollout | Versioned feature control, stable assignment where needed, defined cohort, kill path, recovery, communications and rollback. |
| Operation → monitoring/reopening | Observe learning, retention/transfer, excess help/dependency, narrowing, access, subgroups, safety, drift and burden over declared windows. Thresholds trigger containment, rollback or a new inquiry; preserve historical protocol/results. |

Research/service-evaluation reuse of participant data must have its specifically approved purpose and controls; consent alone is not sufficient. Supplier general product or foundation-model training from participant data remains outside the project design. Critical single incidents can warrant action without waiting for an aggregate trend. “No detected harm” always names coverage and detection limits.

### 8.3 Learn from the inquiry without turning anecdotes into doctrine

The preserved protocol distinguishes **L0 object learning** (learner outcome, hypothesis, intervention or theory), **L1 method/routing learning** (frame, construct, bridge, design, measurement, analysis or workflow), and **L2 learning-policy learning** (whether a change to capture/evaluation/approval/release improves later L0/L1 outcomes). These are analytic distinctions, not additional live learning permissions.

A useful learning signal records exact inquiry/protocol/service/policy versions; population/context/scale; expected versus observed behaviour; evidence and dependence; surprise/correction/failure; rival explanations; severity, recurrence hypothesis and confidence; immediate consequence; proposed owner/destination; next discriminating test; and expiry/discard condition. Minimise personal data and refer to governed evidence instead of duplicating it. Durable change requires consolidated evidence or a justified severe-case exception, a minimal versioned proposal, baseline/held-out and regression comparisons, appropriate cross-context testing, authorised review, monitored release and rollback. Later outcomes establish whether a new process helps; its elegance is not evidence.

## 9. Full-lifecycle economics and public value

Cost the actual package, not a prototype's model bill: PaaS/model/content; acquisition/licensing; task/profile authoring and review; professional learning; school and family time; access/AT/specialist support; evaluation; privacy/security/safeguarding assurance; incident handling and redress; operation/support; evidence/standards stewardship; migration, substitution, retirement and material resource/environment impacts. Record public and institutional costs by lifecycle/component and assumptions about demand, uptime and support. Evaluate the opportunity cost of staff/learner time and alternative provision.

Report cost per **eligible**, **reached/active** and **retained-benefit** learner with clear numerators, denominators and time windows. A retained-benefit denominator must use credible bounded outcome evidence and propagate its uncertainty; it is not everyone who logged in or answered correctly with support. Compare matched non-AI, do-minimum and feasible human/other alternatives. Use sensitivity analysis for demand, effect persistence, staff burden, assurance, subsidy, failure and exit. A single monetary ratio cannot erase rights, dignity, equity, learning breadth or public option value.

Affordability in a prototype does not prove sustainable operation. Recurring stewardship, skills outside suppliers, transparent dependency/rights, continuing assurance, recovery and exercised exit must have owners and funding. Applicable Green Book/public-money/procurement expectations are classified in [11](11-evidence-publication-and-decisions.md), with the broader public-value account in [09](09-public-value-standards-and-commons.md). Historical funding/programme comparisons in [10](10-comparative-research-and-evidence.md) supply context, not endorsements or a procurement result for this project.

## 10. Decisions after evidence

The direction applies to the evaluated profile/configuration; directions may combine across activities and public capabilities. Current commitment to build and learn remains settled.

| Direction | Trigger and unacceptable failure | Accountable decision and surviving value/duties | Next permitted action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continue | Primary effect and interval meet the preregistered criterion; transfer/withdrawal support mechanism and independence; every applicable gate passes; burden, operation and value are credible | SRO after independent evaluator and separate gate-owner advice; retain public/protected evidence, support, rights and exit duties | The next specifically authorised bounded profile/effectiveness study or operational step; no automatic procurement or scale |
| Stop | Material uncorrectable gate failure, delayed harm, sufficiently precise benefit estimate excluding worthwhile gain, or unmanageable burden/cost/dependency | SRO stops affected exposure, protects participants and completes applicable notification, redress, retention/deletion and safe publication | Close the affected profile/configuration; preserve transferable public assets and continuing obligations |
| Transform | A useful mechanism but unsuitable target, interaction, subject, human role or institutional form; effect depends on a particular subgroup/context or intensive support | SRO/relevant gate owners define a materially changed theory and mark inherited effect claims inapplicable | Another bounded POC, such as an educator copilot, constrained learning actions or revised target, with its own evidence |
| Focus on supporting services | Learner-facing additionality is insufficient or risk/burden excessive while non-author adopters demonstrate useful curriculum, evaluation, access/safety or conformance capability with lower burden | Public capability/standards owner accepts revised mandate and sustainable stewardship; learner obligations close safely | Support other services and research; measure adopter tasks, outcomes, upgrades and exit |

A confidence interval too imprecise to discriminate worthwhile benefit from no benefit or harm yields **inconclusive**, not Continue or equivalence. A corrected, independently justified repeat can be considered if it answers the uncertainty at reasonable cost. A strong process story cannot protect a failed learner result; sunk cost cannot protect failed exposure. A stopped mathematics configuration cannot by itself determine English or adult-context effects.

Further commissions remain useful beyond this initial study: deliberately contrasting subject profiles spanning symbolic, interpretive, spatial/visual, practical, performative and vocational knowledge; adult/community needs-and-alternatives work; affected-user specialist modalities such as BSL, Braille/tactile/sonification, AAC and speech/language support; independent implementer/adopter tests and sustainable standards stewardship.
