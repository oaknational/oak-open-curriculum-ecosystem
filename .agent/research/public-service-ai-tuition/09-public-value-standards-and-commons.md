## 1. Public purpose and institutional agency

Public value is the objective of this work. Public provision does not need to become a residual response to a presumed legitimate private market. The service should be judged against educational purpose, equitable access, rights, quality, whole-life resources, institutional competence and public accountability. The user has authorised building a POC; the next question is how to make it useful and evaluable.

The 4 September public-services principle makes a bounded structural argument: with equal competence, resources, quality, risk and service obligations, an institution whose surplus and assets remain devoted to public benefit has an alignment advantage over one required to distribute profit. This is an analytical and normative premise, not evidence that every public provider performs better. Actual capability, waste, capture, exclusion and failure still require observation. Conversely, replacing a public-benefit institution with profit-distributing provision requires a concrete case for additional net public value, including extraction, financing, transaction costs, fragmentation, exclusion, externalities and lost public capacity. That comparative claim is distinct from the already-authorised decision to undertake this POC.

Agency has different meanings at different boundaries. A learner needs comprehensible choices, correction, help and contest. A school needs professional and operational authority. A public institution needs the capacity to govern purposes, evidence, budgets, standards and changes. Dependency substitution protects those powers when changing a supplier or implementation. It is not an instruction to exit the public service itself.

### Oak as a concrete public-benefit institution

The source does not rest on an abstract public/private binary. Oak is a company limited by guarantee without share capital and a non-departmental public body, with the Secretary of State as ordinary member and Reach as Guardian. Its constitutional documents establish specific protections:

| Provision | Public-purpose consequence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Articles 5.1: exclusive public-benefit educational objects | Educational outcomes, disadvantaged pupils, teacher expertise and workload are stated purposes | A constitutional object is not an achieved outcome |
| Articles 7.1–7.2: income/property for objects; no dividend, bonus or profit distribution; recovery of improper personal profit | Accounting surplus may finance the mission rather than private distribution | Reasonable payments for goods, services and duties remain possible |
| Article 6.2: substantial asset disposal retains public benefit through binding purchaser obligations | Prevents public assets becoming freely extractable through a sale | Private benefit must remain incidental; operation depends on enforcement |
| Article 13: residual assets on dissolution go to charity or a comparable nonprofit body | Mission protection extends beyond the current organisation | Does not remove the need for continuity planning |
| Articles 31–32: Guardian consent protects specified constitutional provisions | Ordinary control cannot silently remove selected safeguards | Governance arrangements still need scrutiny |
| Framework agreement 5.6: support a wider commercial curriculum market consistently with the mission | A public resource can enable diverse reuse without granting an exclusive franchise | Commercial reuse does not become the public institution's primary purpose |

These are source readings of [Oak's articles](https://sanity-asset-cdn.thenational.academy/files/cuvjke51/production/d77a5c9d51c73062a3f28f4c01e4fb2ea9dcf626.pdf?dl=), [framework agreement](https://sanity-asset-cdn.thenational.academy/files/cuvjke51/production/bee3867526c4bdf9aef9cce065107a6bb8a23ca3.pdf?dl=) and [full business case](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/635ff076e90e0705a35f5704/Oak_FBC.pdf), preserved from the 4 September 2026 analysis. They are not a fresh legal opinion.

The established curriculum resource is substantive: Oak supplies curriculum material through its [Open API](https://open-api.thenational.academy/) and bulk routes. Post-September-2022 Oak-owned content generally uses the Open Government Licence, with third-party exceptions and narrower Collection 1 terms; MIT software licensing is separate. Every reused component therefore needs its own rights entry. Open content, an open API and open-source software are different permissions.

The [2024–25 accounts](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692f11daa245b0985f034427/Oak_Annual-report-and-accounts_2024-25.pdf) reported £12.5m of procurement against £22m of grant and £2.9m for platform/tools. The [evaluation](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/cuvjke51/production/06043df8db8bc29c6a2b71adb2808425ba12aab5.pdf) reported 17,663 schools (72% of England), 196,143 direct teacher users in January–July 2025, 1.63m annual downloads, 4.2m pupil lesson participations, 85% reporting a positive workload effect, 67% overall workload reduction and a median four hours saved weekly. Schools in the most deprived areas downloaded 36% more per school. These are operational and largely observational/self-reported indicators; they do not establish a causal attainment effect or transfer unchanged to AI tuition.

### A durable public core with plural provision

A useful common layer contains shared rules, rights, minimum access guarantees, authoritative identifiers/reference data, necessary registries, public specifications, conformance, evidence and continuity. Interfaces, local workflows, enrichment, rendering, hosting and support can be supplied by multiple organisations. The boundary should be thin enough to preserve experimentation and strong enough to prevent an indispensable private control point from owning public meaning.

A public owner needs durable competence to commission, operate, assess, intervene, migrate and retire. This does not require it to employ every operator or host every machine. Managed PaaS is compatible with public control when responsibility, observability, rights, continuity and substitution are exercised rather than merely promised.

## 2. What the tuition service must leave and prove

The public capability is more than a publication. The following chain preserves the canonical service report's causal theory and its rivals. POC evidence can address implementability, conformance, early use, task meaning and adopter burden; durable multi-year public value remains a later claim. Evaluation measures and gates are in [04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md](04-poc-requirements-and-evaluation.md).


> **public specification and artefacts → non-author implementation → conformance → deployment → actual use → semantic and task interoperability → lower adopter burden and substitutability → maintained operation and exit → public value**

The mechanism is less ambiguity and duplicated assurance; the rival is “open theatre”, where publication leaves meaning, cost or a closed control point unchanged.

| Transition | Conditions and observable evidence | Horizon, rival/falsifier and decision effect |
|---|---|---|
| Public specification → non-author implementation | Clear scope/authority, component licences, mappings, examples and support; a non-author builds without privileged knowledge | POC; hidden IP, undocumented semantics or author dependence falsifies implementability → revise/defer |
| Implementation → conformance | Independent fixtures/validators cover structure, meaning, rights and adverse cases; two implementations agree | POC; author-only tests or syntactic pass with semantic disagreement → no conformance/reuse claim |
| Conformance → deployment/use | Unaffiliated adopter completes a real curriculum-grounded assurance workflow under its governance | POC/later; subsidised demo, unused integration or protected-data expansion → no adoption claim |
| Use → semantic/task interoperability | Curriculum version, independence and adverse-event meanings survive; receiving organisation completes the intended decision | POC/later; parsing without same meaning/task → revise profile or use another mechanism |
| Interoperability → lower burden/substitutability | Baseline versus adopter person-time, expertise, cost and defects; representative component migration/removal | POC–operation; favourable adopter or paper portability → no value/procurement-readiness claim |
| Maintained operation/exit → public value | Funded stewardship, version compatibility, multiple contexts, successful upgrades/exits and adopter/user outcomes | Multi-year; abandonment, capture or no outcome/burden gain → Stop/Transform/Focus rather than institutionalise |

Moderators are implementer size/skill, subsidy, jurisdiction, subject, installed systems, version change and steward capacity. POC evidence reaches only the first five rows. Purpose, seven gates, evidence meanings, provenance, challenge, minimisation and exit stay invariant; jurisdiction, curriculum, language, pedagogy and safeguarding are accountable profiles.


### Four stewardship zones


The capability should be separable into four governance zones:

| Zone | What belongs there | Governing rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Publicly governed** | Educational constitution; authoritative curriculum profile and provenance rules; learner-evidence/state semantics including uncertainty/expiry; tutoring-move definitions; controlled-adaptation evidence/change contract; assessment/evaluation protocol; safety/accessibility fixtures; conformance rules/validators; public implementation reports; change, appeal, migration and exit policy | Freely and durably available with clear component licences, open decision record and succession. Public control does not require every operational component to be state-built |
| **Protected** | Identities; dialogue; attainment and response data; inferred misconception/state; SEND/accommodation, safeguarding and incident records; linkable logs and research microdata | Necessity, purpose and role determine access. Use controlled research settings, aggregation and synthetic fixtures; openness of a schema never makes a child's data open |
| **Locally configured** | Jurisdiction/curriculum or adult-context profile; school schedule/policy; task selection; teacher help bounds; learner-declared goals; reasonable adjustments; language; DSL and support contacts; retention within lawful bounds | The accountable authority—and, in learner-directed adult use, the learner—can vary context without breaking invariant evidence meanings or public safeguards |
| **Competitively supplied** | Replaceable tutor/model, learner and teacher experience, managed PaaS/hosting, content/support services and evaluation delivery where independence is protected | Published capability/conformance evidence, transparent dependencies and subprocessors, data/configuration return, transition assistance and exercised substitution prevent capture |

Do not standardise provider-to-provider portable **inferred learner state**: exchange only necessary pseudonymous access or aggregate research information, and revisit only with proven adopter need, DPIA, construct validity and rights case. This does not prevent a learner from exporting selected, intelligible goals, achievements, access preferences or source evidence under their control; the receiving context decides what, if anything, remains valid.


### PaaS qualities are externally meaningful proof obligations

The PaaS label is not evidence that these qualities hold. These are service-owner obligations under shared responsibility; actual estate findings and the pinned technical probes are in document 08.


PaaS can support repeatable delivery; the label proves no quality, and the service owner remains accountable under shared responsibility.

|---|---|---|

### The durable public package

Even if tutoring stops, leave the constitution; curriculum/construct, pedagogical-profile and provenance ledgers; rights-cleared task bank and assessment protocols; observation, learner-state and tutoring vocabularies; the adaptation evidence/change contract; disclosure-reviewed synthetic/adversarial/accessibility/SEND/safeguarding fixtures; the access-needs matrix; validators/reference artefacts; implementation, conformance and exit reports; redacted DPIA/rights/transparency material; licence manifest; change/archive/adopter guidance; null/adverse/mixed results; and whole-life evidence. Keep secure items, personal data and exploitable incident detail protected.

Public SRO/service owners, independent evaluation, empowered gate panels, a conflict-declaring steward, learner/family challenge and a decision/correction log govern the package. Supplier foreground IP may stay private, but any contract must require common research/evaluation rights, data/configuration return and deletion, conformance, dependency/incident cooperation, skills transfer and transition.

Build through teacher-led, multidisciplinary and paid affected-user co-design; outcomes/threats before features; signed incremental gates; privacy, access, SEND expertise and security from inception; disclosure-aware open working; version-linked evaluation; early operational ownership; two-implementation conformance; and planned retirement. Vendors, components, team, backlog, timetable, budget and detailed architecture remain later decisions.


## 3. How to reason about open standards

A standard is a shared agreement intended for repeated use. A specification is a particular written or machine-readable description. A profile chooses and constrains part of a base specification for a community or task. A reference implementation is code, not the complete normative authority. A specification may be useful before standardisation; calling it a standard does not supply adoption, public legitimacy or independent implementations.

The general standards research (*Open standards that change the world*, OSR-20260903-001, version 3.0, revised 4 September, evidence cut-off 3 September 2026) supports ten connected findings. Openness is a vector; popularity is distinct from openness and public success; useful standards begin with a concrete need and incremental deployment; every adopter needs a workable local bargain; a small useful core beats an overlarge document; fixtures and tooling form part of the practical standard; compatibility is a continuing responsibility; a signal cannot determine a recipient's policy; interoperability alone does not confer sovereignty; and maintenance requires a funded institution.

### Layers and non-equivalence

| Layer | What must be specified | What a pass does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement and identity | Units, reference frames, identifiers and allocation | Comparable meaning without traceability or sound measurement |
| Syntax and encoding | Characters, wire/file grammar, serialisation | Shared concepts or a valid task |
| Schema and structure | Types, cardinality, required/optional fields, defaults | Educational or legal validity |
| Semantics | Definitions, relations, units, codes, context and mappings | Agreement that a claim is true or appropriate |
| Validation | Assertions, constraints, errors, profiles and fixtures | Complete correctness outside tested cases |
| APIs and protocols | Operations, capability discovery, errors, retries, security binding | Equivalent workflow or affordable migration |
| Storage/query | File layout, logical types, query behaviour, consistency | All vendors supporting the same optional features |
| Discovery and provenance | Catalogues, source/derivation identities, versions | Licence permission, truth or current authority |
| Policy, identity and trust | Roles, purposes, rights, grants, prohibitions, issuer trust | Legitimate authority simply because a policy engine returned allow |
| Conformance and lifecycle | Test scheme, independent implementations, deprecation, migration and succession | Learning, equity or public value without corresponding evidence |

Open standards and open data are separate claims. Public access to a roster specification neither permits collecting pupil records nor makes them public. FAIR means findable, accessible under stated conditions, interoperable and reusable; it does not require unrestricted disclosure. CARE adds collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics, particularly for Indigenous data governance. A public commons can expose knowledge while safeguarding restricted data.

### Openness as eight separately inspectable axes

| Axis | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| Specification access | Free, durable, versioned and accessible normative text, schemas and dependencies, with no compulsory login |
| Implementation freedom | Worldwide irrevocable royalty-free permissions compatible with open implementations; copyright and patents considered separately |
| Participation | Who can propose, review and influence; fees, travel, language, time and expertise barriers |
| Decision and maintenance governance | Public process, reasoned decisions, conflicts, appeal, change, security response, funding and succession |
| Conformance | Public tests and reports, fees, rights to implement tests, accessible routes beyond a steward-controlled mark |
| Implementation plurality | Genuinely independent implementations in relevant roles, not wrappers sharing an indispensable engine |
| Essential infrastructure | Registry, API, identity, certificate or naming services; who controls entry, price, rules and continuity |
| Data/content rights | Separate licences, third-party restrictions, attribution, privacy, database rights and ongoing export rights |

The research's strict designation uses five non-compensable gates: a free durable artefact; implementation freedom; accountable open governance; no closed indispensable control point; and verifiable implementation plurality. This is its explicit analytical definition, not a claim that all standards organisations use the same definition. ISO container standards and royalty-bearing/FRAND systems may be consequential while failing this strict test. Successful adoption does not erase that failure, and free licensing does not prove success.

### Seven dimensions of success and a truthful evidence ladder

| Success dimension | Evidence that counts | Common overclaim |
|---|---|---|
| Technical maturity | Precise requirements, implementation experience, security analysis and tests | Publication or committee approval |
| Implementation plurality | Independent interoperable implementations across relevant roles | One reference implementation or one vendor's assertion |
| Deployment and use | Enabled systems, transactions, repeat users and representative coverage | Downloads, stars, membership or certificates |
| Semantic/task interoperability | The receiver preserves meaning and completes the intended task | A valid message or lossless syntax conversion |
| User/public value | Access, safety, productivity, accountability, equity or lower whole-life burden | Revenue or message volume alone |
| User/institutional agency | Appropriate choice, correction, professional/public authority and feasible substitution | Multiple brands sharing one controlled dependency |
| Durability | Maintained security, compatibility, extensions, archives and succession | A frozen specification that cannot safely evolve |

The adoption ladder distinguishes **0 published → 1 implementation → 2 multiple conforming implementations → 3 deployment → 4 observed exchange/use → 5 preserved meaning and task → 6 durable outcomes**. It is a discipline for the scope of a claim, not a universal maturity ranking. A widely deployed format may have weak evidence at rungs 5–6. A small accessible service can have useful outcome evidence without global adoption.

### Design and adoption mechanisms

A core needs explicit identities, units, time, encoding, required/optional behaviour, missing/unknown/invalid states, validation, errors, retries, security assumptions and an extension policy. An implementer must know the completeness boundary. Unknown extensions should not silently become trusted facts; tolerant parsing can conceal incompatible semantics. Unexercised extension points ossify. Migration needs coexistence, converters where valid, capability discovery, deprecation dates, rollback and retirement rather than a universal flag day.

A profile should identify its base/dependency versions, own identifier and capability statement, complete delta, fixtures, adjacent mappings, compatibility/deprecation policy and independent users. Unpublished local profiles recreate silos. The standard's package should help a new implementer achieve a useful first result, test failure cases and diagnose deviations without privileged access to its authors.

Adoption combines direct benefit, fixed implementation costs, network expectations, complements, distribution/defaults, regulation/procurement, training, support and sustainable stewardship. It is not always a pure coordination game. Container standards needed ports and cranes; barcode adoption needed retailers and equipment; GTFS benefited from a valuable consumer; FHIR combines standards with legal mandates and certified APIs. Participation funding matters because nominally open meetings do not create equal technical influence.

The cross-domain case appendix retains concrete evidence and counterexamples. It is deliberately not reduced to a league table: web crawls, hospital surveys, legal compliance studies and economic estimates have different denominators and evidential force.

### A proposed development and selection discipline

The research proposes a staged lifecycle: **0 need → 1 landscape → 2 power/incentives → 3 prototype with independent implementers → 4 specification and fixtures → 5 diverse pilots → 6 adoption/migration → 7 funded operation → 8 retirement**. A shared library, public dataset, procurement rule or law may solve the problem more directly than a new standard. Do not standardise an immature ontology, harmful data collection or a grant category without an actual consumer.


#### The minimum public package

Before a standard is recommended for broad procurement or regulation, its public package should include:

1. scope, use cases, non-goals and stakeholder/incentive map;
2. normative specification in accessible web and durable downloadable forms;
3. machine-readable schemas, vocabularies, code lists and registry exports;
4. RF patent and copyright terms compatible with open implementations;
5. governance, decision, appeal, conflict, maintenance and succession rules;
6. security, privacy, safety, human-rights and misuse analysis;
7. versioning, extension, deprecation and migration policy;
8. a minimal profile and named optional profiles;
9. examples, test data, validator/TCK and public implementation report;
10. at least two independent implementations and one non-author implementation;
11. deployment guide, capability discovery and operational observability;
12. adoption measurement plan with denominators and outcome metrics;
13. funding and inclusion plan;
14. complete dependency-exit and archival plan.


#### Then score readiness and impact potential from 0 to 3

| Dimension | 0 — absent | 1 — asserted/draft | 2 — demonstrated in pilots | 3 — diverse operational evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Need and outcome | No bounded problem | Use case stated | Users validate benefit | Outcomes measured in operation |
| Scope and comprehensibility | Unbounded/ambiguous | Core proposed | New implementers complete examples | Low and measured first-use burden |
| Semantic completeness | Names/units unclear | Model/vocabulary drafted | Real-data mappings validated | Meaning/task preserved across contexts |
| Compatibility and migration | Clean break/no plan | Version policy | Converters/coexistence piloted | Upgrades and retirement measured |
| Tooling and guidance | Prose only | Examples or early library | Validator plus reference code | Multi-language maintained ecosystem |
| Conformance | No assertions | Test plan | Two implementations tested | Public cross-vendor/field results |
| Implementation diversity | One/no implementer | Commitments | Two independent implementations | Diverse vendors/open implementations |
| Incentive and distribution | No value-chain model | Champions identified | Early adopter bargain works | Sustainable demand/default/procurement |
| Governance and inclusion | Opaque | Written rules | Diverse participation and appeal used | Influence, funding and outcomes audited |
| Security, privacy and rights | Ignored | Threat/rights analysis | Adversarial and affected-user testing | Incidents, remedies and updates operate |
| Evolvability and maintenance | No owner/funding | Maintainer named | Extensions/versions exercised | Sustainable funding and succession proven |
| Sovereignty and dependency exit | One-way dependency | Export claim | Representative switch piloted | Complete, affordable substitution evidenced |
| Adoption and public value evidence | Approval counts only | Implementations counted | Deployment/use measured | Semantic outcomes, costs and disparities measured |

Do not rely on the sum alone. Recommended decision rules are:

- **No broad mandate** with any zero in compatibility, conformance, security/rights or maintenance.
- **No “interoperable” claim** below 2 in semantic completeness and conformance.
- **No “sovereign” claim** below 2 in sovereignty/dependency exit, governance/rights and semantic completeness.
- **No “mature” claim** until at least one measure reaches adoption ladder rung 4 and representative failures are published.
- **No “impact” claim** without rung-5 task evidence or rung-6 outcomes appropriate to the stated objective.
- Publish the whole profile, evidence dates and uncertainties; never hide a weak dimension in a total.

## 5. A public pedagogical knowledge programme

The standards above cover useful interfaces, but no reviewed mature standard comprehensively represents conditional, evidence-bearing pedagogical judgement. Curriculum, content, ontology, evidence, learner data and professional judgement remain distinct. The conceptual ladder is in document 02; the exact capability limits and candidate contract travel here so that implementation need not reconstruct them from prose.

The table and JSON below are from *Teaching and learning as a public knowledge infrastructure*, 5 September 2026. The JSON is an illustrative minimum design proposal, not a ratified schema, validated instructional policy or permission to infer protected learner characteristics. Learner conditions must be interpreted under the necessity, uncertainty and rights rules of document 06.


### What current standards and projects represent

| Standard or infrastructure | What it represents well | What it does not by itself represent |
|---|---|---|
| National curricula and Oak resources | Goals, sequences, content and instantiated teaching materials | A general queryable model of pedagogical claims, evidence and conditional decisions |
| [CASE](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/case) | Hierarchical standards, competencies, outcomes, rubrics, identifiers, alignments and REST exchange | Why or how a particular learner should be taught an outcome |
| [LRMI](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/lrmi/) | RDF vocabulary and concept schemes for describing learning resources | The causal or conditional warrant for using the resource |
| [QTI](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/qti) | Assessment item content, tests, responses and results exchange | Whether the assessment inference is educationally valid in a given use |
| [xAPI](https://github.com/adlnet/xAPI-Spec) and [Caliper](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/caliper) | Structured statements or events about learning activity and system interaction | Learning itself, learner meaning or pedagogical causality |
| [IMS Learning Design](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/learning-design) | A generic XML language for roles, activities, environments and multiple pedagogies | An evidence-bearing knowledge base of why a design works; the 2003 specification did not become a mainstream pedagogical interchange layer |
| [What Works Clearinghouse data](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/studyfindings) | Study ratings, effects, samples, topics, contexts and evidence tiers in downloadable data | Translation from study findings to moment-by-moment or subject-specific professional judgement |
| [Learning Commons Knowledge Graph](https://docs.learningcommons.org/knowledge-graph/understanding-knowledge-graph/introduction) | Emerging unified schemas and JSONL/API access connecting standards, learning components, progressions, curricula and some instructional guidance | A mature public standard or comprehensive, independently validated pedagogical corpus |
| [Synthesis-Ready Evidence Repository](https://jacobsfoundation.org/activity/synthesis-ready-evidence-repository/) | An emerging common taxonomy and repository for combining education studies, contexts and living syntheses | It is explicitly an early-stage minimum viable product, not yet the decision layer itself |
| [IEEE P2247.2](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/2247.2/12580/) | An active 2026 project defining data structures and exchange among adaptive instructional systems | General teaching knowledge, educational purposes or public evidence governance |

The project corpus reviewed for this note reaches the same conclusion from the wider open-standards landscape: education has meaningful standards for curriculum frameworks, resource description, assessment, event data, institutional exchange, identity and credentials, but these layers should not be mistaken for pedagogical intelligence. **Content, curriculum, ontology, evidence, learner data and teaching judgement are distinct.**

### A proposed minimal pedagogical-claim model

The following is a design proposal, not a claim that such a standard already exists. Its purpose is to show the information lost when guidance is reduced to “strategy X works.”

```json
{
  "claim_id": "persistent-uri",
  "claim": "human-readable proposition",
  "claim_type": "causal | mechanistic | normative | descriptive | implementation | professional",
  "learning_goal": ["linked outcome or capability"],
  "domain": ["subject", "concept", "disciplinary practice"],
  "learner_conditions": ["prior knowledge", "development", "language", "access needs"],
  "setting_conditions": ["grouping", "time", "resources", "culture", "jurisdiction"],
  "intervention_or_move": "what is proposed",
  "mechanism": "how it is expected to help",
  "observable_evidence": ["what would indicate progress or difficulty"],
  "alternatives": ["other defensible approaches"],
  "contraindications_and_harms": ["when not to use it or what to watch"],
  "evidence_refs": ["persistent source identifiers"],
  "evidence_appraisal": {
    "methods": [],
    "populations": [],
    "outcomes": [],
    "strength": "structured judgement",
    "uncertainty": "explicit limitations",
    "disagreement": []
  },
  "authority_and_provenance": {
    "issuer": "person or institution",
    "basis": "statutory | research | professional | community",
    "contributors": [],
    "jurisdiction": [],
    "version": "semantic version",
    "valid_from": "date",
    "review_due": "date",
    "license": "re-use terms"
  },
  "equity_and_accessibility": ["distributional effects and requirements"],
  "implementation_requirements": ["training, materials, routines, safeguards"],
  "decision_rights": ["who may recommend, decide, override, contest and correct"]
}
```

This claim layer would link rather than replace existing standards: curriculum outcomes through CASE-like identifiers; resources through LRMI; assessments through QTI; evidence records through research identifiers and evidence schemas; and activity traces through event standards where their collection is justified.


The hard work is shared semantics and legitimate stewardship: who may issue and revise a claim; how causal, descriptive, professional and normative authority remain distinguishable; how contradiction survives; which populations and settings support transfer; and who can correct, contest or decline its use. A machine can retrieve and compare evidence. It cannot obtain the authority to choose educational purposes by successfully parsing the record.

### Seven-step programme, retained as a wider proposal


### 1. Map the knowledge ecology

Create a source and authority map spanning curricula, subject associations, teacher frameworks, evidence syntheses, primary research, practitioner knowledge, learner/community knowledge and operational standards. Record licences, coverage, maintenance, method and jurisdiction. The first deliverable is visibility, not premature unification.

### 2. Define claim and context semantics

Develop an open model for pedagogical claims, learner and setting conditions, outcome profiles, mechanisms, implementation requirements, uncertainty and disagreement. Reuse existing standards where they fit. Avoid making a new monolith.

### 3. Pilot deeply in a few subjects

Choose bounded concepts in contrasting domains—for example early reading, ratio, forces and historical evidence. Connect curriculum progression, subject knowledge, misconceptions, representations, tasks, assessment evidence and research. Subject depth will expose where a generic ontology becomes vacuous.

### 4. Make evidence synthesis computable but not automatic

Link claims to structured evidence records and living reviews. Expose populations, settings, outcomes, methods, effect estimates, null results, limitations and conflicts. A machine may retrieve and compare; accountable humans still judge applicability and purpose.

### 5. Build for professional and learner agency

Test the infrastructure in lesson study, curriculum design, teacher education, assessment moderation and learner reflection. Measure whether it improves the quality of reasoning, adaptation and dialogue—not merely speed of content generation.

### 6. Establish public governance and conformance

Provide persistent identifiers, open licences where possible, transparent change control, versioning, validation tools, accessibility requirements, redress and independent evaluation. Separate content authority, technical stewardship and evidence appraisal so no single organisation silently controls all three.

### 7. Evaluate the infrastructure as an intervention

Ask whether it improves educational decisions, equity, teacher learning, curriculum coherence and learner outcomes; whether it creates workload, homogenisation or surveillance; and whether benefits persist beyond motivated pilot sites.


## 6. National capability and practical sovereignty

The UK landscape described in the 3 September report and targeted 4 September update is a **capability chain**, not one commissioned architecture. Public curriculum/content, compute, government products, evaluation, common standards, skills and operational institutions have different owners and maturity. Their existence does not establish end-to-end public control or learning benefit.

| Layer | Source snapshot | What remains to be demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| Compute and model capability | £2bn to 2030; more than £1bn for AIRR expansion, proposed 21→420 AI exaFLOPS, £750m Edinburgh facility for 2027; Sovereign AI £500m venture/equity and compute arrangements | Sustained usable capacity, access, cost and skills; announcements are not delivered educational capacity |
| Authoritative and AI-ready data | Oak curriculum/API/bulk; AI Content Store beta; National Data Library early curated work and legacy catalogues | Mandate, ownership, quality, licence, stable versions, access routes, feedback and continuity; beta withdrawal risks remain |
| Evaluation and standards | DfE safety guidance, benchmark discovery, education testbeds, evidence-board work, ATRS, AI Playbook, Inspect, education Petri fork | Construct validity, reliability, subgroup and scenario coverage, independent educational outcome evidence and accountable changes |
| Shared products | Source-reported Extract for planning, Justice Transcribe/Minute adaptation, GOV.UK Chat app, Consult | Real use, quality, task outcomes, public costs, failure handling and continuity rather than demonstrations |
| Adoption and institutions | Departmental platforms, professional skills, service owners and procurement | Funded product ownership and developer experience, onboarding, practical maintenance and the ability to change dependencies |

The source distinguishes precise maturity. DfE benchmark discovery had no public task/rubric/reliability/scenario-validity results in the reviewed material. The education Petri MIT fork was created 11 February and had branch changes on 2 June 2026; no verified linkage to a completed official benchmark was established. Inspect was an operational evaluation framework with a source-reported 200+ evaluations, not educational validation. AIME had been abandoned by February 2026, and ISO/IEC 42001 management systems, ISO/IEC 42005 impact assessment, NIST AI RMF and AI Verify tools are not certificates of tutoring efficacy.

The tutoring procurement snapshot described eight £300,000 excluding-VAT R&D contracts ending 31 March 2027, with common research/standard outputs and supplier foreground prototype IP; later procurement was separate. The ambition to reach 450,000 disadvantaged pupils annually by the end of 2027 was a target, not observed reach. These programme facts contextualise public opportunity; the comparative education-programme source detail belongs in [10-comparative-research-and-evidence.md](10-comparative-research-and-evidence.md).

Evidence critical of the policy remains valuable with its limits. The NEU submission AIE0220 (9 June) drew on 9,408 English state-school union respondents surveyed 5–16 February: 49% disagreed and 36% were uncertain about the proposed AI-tutor direction. Weighting a self-selected union survey does not make it representative of all teachers or an efficacy trial; it supports taking teacher participation, workload, trials and audit seriously. Ben Williamson's 25 August 2026 analysis (DOI 10.1080/14767724.2026.2720476) interprets experimental standards and market formation; it is not proof of capture. A 27 August blog's six-firm list was stale and incorrectly included Google; the final eight included PA Consulting, Anthropic and Developing Experts. Chronology and evidential type must travel with critique.

### Nine dimensions of sovereignty

| Dimension | Practical meaning |
|---|---|
| Mission authority | Publicly accountable people can define and revise purpose, access and acceptable risk |
| Knowledge stewardship | Authoritative sources, licences, versions, corrections and curriculum jurisdiction remain legible |
| Compute access | Capacity, resilience and price can support the service without an unmanageable chokepoint |
| Model capability | The institution can evaluate, constrain, substitute and where useful develop/adapt models |
| Software/interfaces | Documented, testable boundaries and retained configuration/evidence permit reconstruction |
| Evaluation power | Public actors can define claims, inspect evidence, challenge metrics and require independent evaluation |
| Dependency substitution | Representative replacement preserves necessary meaning, rights, operation and costs |
| Human/institutional capacity | Owners, skills, budgets, commercial competence and succession endure |
| Rights and legitimacy | Affected people can understand, correct, contest, receive help and obtain remedy |

Nationality, self-hosting, open code and multiple vendors are partial properties; none alone supplies all nine. A practical ladder runs from a documented boundary, to an available alternative, to repeatable comparative tests, to an exercised operational substitution including data/identity/telemetry/rollback, to durable owners, procurement and funding. The reviewed GDS material did not demonstrate a complete switch across cloud, model, embedding, vector, identity and observability. That absence bounds a sovereignty claim; it does not show that the entire PaaS direction is unsuitable.

The GDS platform comparison in document 08 preserves a useful lifecycle counterexample. GOV.UK PaaS could have provided a successful shared service and still have a justified retirement: the source reported 60+ bodies, 172 services, 3,200 applications, 122 daily deployments, 99.95% uptime, one major incident in seven years and a passed 2021 live assessment. Market change, departmental skills, constrained growth and reinvestment informed retirement. The source did not supply a complete adoption-downturn dataset. Public infrastructure must fund orderly succession, not treat indefinite survival as proof of value.

The same comparison requires symmetry. An external platform carries contract, support, price and continuity risks. An internal platform carries staffing, upgrade, backlog, onboarding and bus-factor risks. Whole-life analysis includes delayed public benefit. Federation between central standards/shared products, departmental platforms and service-domain ownership needs funded developer-experience product work, or each service will reproduce the plumbing.

International owner-reported snapshots—France's Albert API (70+ public projects; 100,000 weekly requests), Singapore Pair (11,000 users across 100 agencies in its first two months; 4,500 weekly active), India's DIKSHA (36 languages; 100+ microservices), Swiss Apertus 8B/70B openness and Estonia's distributed citizen-service layer—illustrate distinct layers of capability. They do not supply a transferable UK learning-effect estimate. England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland retain different educational authority; Welsh-language resources and Scotland's register/strategy cannot be treated as local settings of one England curriculum.


## 7. A national commons with conditional learner-context federation

The updated national-commons report of 4 September 2026 proposes two separable programmes: a public education knowledge commons, and carefully justified connections among restricted professional, institutional, learner and research contexts. **The public commons should be judged independently successful even if no general learner federation is ever built.** It can improve discovery, curriculum coherence, professional learning, accessibility, public evidence and reuse without pupil identifiers.

The earlier report's stronger assumption of a shared national control plane and a Learner Data Federation was substantively revised. The successor is a small shared behavioural contract with replaceable coordination functions and optional, field-by-field learner-context admission. A statutory learner-data charter and extensions to publication duties are proposals, not statements that current law already grants a universal charter or imposes all proposed duties. The source's claims about DUAA commencement and EU AI Act/Omnibus dates remain dated, unverified legal snapshots and must not be used as current deployment permission.

### Three constitutional regimes and six operational planes

The three authority regimes are public knowledge, controlled professional/operational information, and protected learner-related information. The six planes below organise responsibilities inside those regimes; they are neither six required databases nor six blanket permissions. A field crosses a boundary only on its own necessary, proportionate, lawful and technically enforceable case.

| Plane | Content and purpose | Default treatment and critical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Public knowledge commons** | Statutory curricula, qualification/framework definitions, evidence, professional guidance, public resources, metadata, translations, released assessment material, institution registers and disclosure-controlled statistics | Public where rights allow; accessible HTML, APIs and bulk data; source authority, licence, provenance and versions travel. No pupil identifier is needed |
| **B. Controlled professional corpus** | Secure/live assessment items, sensitive item statistics including DIF, moderation, malpractice and licensed professional resources | Role- and purpose-restricted; release policy and item-security ownership. QTI exchange cannot prove validity or authorise access |
| **C. Distributed institutional operations** | Enrolment, attendance, submitted work, teacher observations and statutory administrative records | Remain near the responsible controller. Federation may reduce copies but adds interfaces and attack surface; benefit is conditional |
| **D. Learner edge** | Intelligible current records, credentials, selected portfolio/work, declared goals/access preferences, disclosure receipts and dispute annotations | Learner agency with progressive capacity, assisted recovery and limited disclosure; a wallet is not the sole authoritative copy and a VC is not proof of parental or child decision authority |
| **E. Safeguarding/support enclaves** | Raw safeguarding, SEND, health and counselling material requiring specially authorised handling | Separate stores/keys, purpose and roles; a minimum current-action projection can cross a justified boundary. Ordinary search should not reveal a sensitive dossier |
| **F. Secure research environments** | Approved research linkage and analysis using controlled access and outputs | Public-benefit test, Five Safes, project-scoped identities, compute-to-data where appropriate, disclosure control, public project register and publication of null findings; research access does not authorise live decisions about individuals |

The lifecycle across planes is not “retrieve everything, then decide.” A source contributes a provisional assertion; an authorised process accepts or rejects it; a scoped projection is compiled; action-time authority and evidence are revalidated; the effect receives an account; and descendants are reconciled after change. A plan does not self-authorise. The precise acceptance, context, standing, grant, correction, revocation, transfer-receipt and terminal contracts belong in document 06.

### A versioned public assertion and a small kernel

The public core object is a versioned assertion. Its minimal record preserves:

| Field group | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity/source | Persistent assertion ID, source document/entity, publisher and authoritative locator |
| Authority/scope | Jurisdiction; statutory, guidance, research, professional, community or contested standing; limits of authority |
| Time/version | Publication and effective dates, version, replacement/supersession and review/expiry where appropriate |
| Provenance | Observation/source references, derivation and mapping lineage, responsible actors, method and uncertainty |
| Rights/access | Licence and permitted uses, language, accessibility representations and source restrictions |
| Lifecycle | Draft, current, superseded, withdrawn or disputed, with intelligible reasons and successor relationship |

The common kernel joins identity, version, provenance, authority, time, licence/access, status and purpose/policy references. Scoped operational extensions can add principals/delegation, claims/grants/prohibitions, obligations/attempts, actions/effects and revocation when a real use needs them. These fields define boundary behaviour; they do not mandate a single ontology, graph database, universal learner model or central orchestrator.

Accessible documents, HTML, REST/JSON, bulk files and optional RDF/JSON-LD projections can represent the same traceable assertion. Each transformation must retain meaning and identify loss or uncertainty. A graph relation does not imply an instructional sequence: where order matters, order must be explicit. A derived semantic projection does not become a new source of statutory authority.

Two genuinely independent implementations should share normative fixtures and expected boundary behaviour **without inheriting the reference implementation's internal model**, before a stable standard is claimed. Otherwise apparent agreement may only show shared implementation assumptions. A simple file-and-token implementation should be a serious comparator to a sophisticated graph or context compiler.

Public resources need no state monopoly on pedagogy. Teachers, universities, subject associations, charities, communities and commercial providers can contribute under explicit provenance and licence. Statutory sources retain their own authority, while alternatives and disagreement remain visible. England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland need autonomous educational stewardship; a thin UK profile may enable qualification, credential and research exchanges without imposing one curriculum.

### Federation as replaceable functions

Federation can provide catalogues; institution/issuer/role trust; versioned profiles; policy services with local enforcement; provenance and correction routes; project-scoped linkage; conformance reports; public registers; and monitoring. None should silently become the sole source of authority. Multiple operators, reconstructible records, exported registries, exercised substitution and local failure containment are safeguards.

Pairwise or sector-scoped identifiers reduce universal correlation; identity resolution is not permission. Bridge admission should specify source/receiver purpose, semantic profile, fields, permissions, prohibitions, duties, expiry, renewal, revocation and residual limits. Software can execute policy within legitimate authority, but law and adjudication remain above the code. Secure aggregation, privacy-preserving linkage, differential privacy and other privacy-enhancing techniques are tools whose threat model, error and governance need evaluation; they do not absolve a questionable purpose.

### Institutions and participatory accountability

The report's proposed functions should be assigned to accountable institutions before procuring a universal platform. They need not all be newly created bodies.


The architecture needs the following functions; they need not all become new organisations.

| Function | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Jurisdictional public steward | Operate the knowledge commons, semantic kernel, catalogue and conformance programme |
| Source authorities | Maintain authoritative assertions for their defined domains |
| Institutional controllers | Remain accountable for operational processing and local records |
| Independent rights/ombuds function | Handle pupil complaints, conflicts, representation and systemic remedies |
| Standards and change council | Govern profiles, migrations and conformance with teachers, technologists and domain experts |
| Child and community council | Give children, parents, SEND communities and affected groups agenda-setting and veto/escalation routes |
| Research access body | Approve projects, environments, linkage and outputs under a public-benefit test |
| Security and resilience function | Set the sector profile, monitor suppliers and exercise recovery |
| Regulators and inspectors | Enforce data protection, equality, education, assessment and public-law obligations within their mandates |

The governance system should connect existing institutions—the ICO, Children's Commissioners, equality bodies, inspectorates, qualification regulators and devolved authorities—rather than inventing a super-regulator that dilutes accountability.

### 9.3 Public registers and participatory change

Publish searchable registers of:

- authoritative datasets and source bodies;
- purposes, legal bases, recipients, retention and data flows;
- algorithms and high-impact rules;
- approved research projects and outputs;
- vendors, subprocessors and international transfers;
- standards versions, conformance status and waivers;
- significant incidents and remedies; and
- proposals, objections, decisions and migrations.

Children and teachers must participate before purposes and schemas are fixed, not only in usability testing after procurement. Participation needs accessible materials, compensation, independent facilitation and visible records of how input changed the design.

### 9.4 A regulator ecology, not a blame funnel

When harm occurs, investigators should locate the failure at the correct layer:

- source record;
- derivation or model;
- context selection;
- operation or workflow;
- actor identity or authority;
- permission grant;
- unfulfilled obligation;
- supplier or subprocessor;
- policy rule;
- shared semantic kernel; or
- institutional governance.

This project-derived “regulator ecology” prevents every incident being mislabelled a generic data-quality problem or blamed on the frontline teacher who encountered the output.

### A staged national programme, with explicit proposal status

The source gives relative, overlapping programme horizons. These are **uncommissioned national proposals**, not the tuition POC timetable or funding commitment. Each stage can stop independently; a later stage is not an automatic reward for finishing an earlier one.


### Phase 0 — mandate, rights and baseline (0–12 months)

- establish a devolved constitutional commission with pupil, parent, teacher, SEND, safeguarding, equality, research and technical representation;
- inventory current purposes, datasets, interfaces, vendors, identifiers, legal bases and retention;
- publish existing algorithm and data-use registers;
- legislate or formally consult on the learner data charter, prohibited uses and oversight powers;
- define baselines for teacher workload, resource discovery, access rights, correction, security, equity and supplier exit;
- publish a threat model and a synthetic education-governance test corpus covering age and capacity transitions, plural standing, safeguarding conflict, stale authority, cross-purpose leakage, correction, revocation, anti-resurrection, zero-agent recovery, partial action and duplicate prevention;
- specify simpler technical comparators—signed documents, append-only event logs, relational records with policy checks, scoped retrieval and conventional APIs—against which graph, compiler, agent and federation layers must earn their cost; and
- place a moratorium on new national emotion recognition, biometric repositories, universal child-risk scores and fully automated high-stakes pupil decisions.

**Gate:** no learner-context architecture procurement until purposes, forbidden purposes, decision rights, independent enforcement, adversarial fixtures and subtraction criteria are agreed.

### Phase 1 — public knowledge commons, no pupil data (6–24 months)

- assign persistent identifiers to curriculum, qualification and teacher-framework propositions;
- publish version, provenance, authority, licence and accessibility metadata;
- build public APIs, bulk releases, accessible interfaces and preservation;
- map existing public registries and released assessments;
- fund translations, accessible formats and underserved curriculum areas; and
- release the semantic kernel, source code, examples and change process openly.

**Gate:** independent tests of fidelity to authoritative texts, accessibility, teacher usability and export. Kill or redesign features that increase planning time or privilege a single editorial path. This phase must be independently complete and useful even if no learner-context federation is ever authorised.

### Phase 2 — standards, conformance and market opening (12–30 months)

- publish domain profiles for curriculum, resources, qualifications and controlled assessment;
- operate public conformance suites and supplier sandboxes;
- require provenance and round-trip export in public procurement;
- certify interfaces rather than entire vendor platforms; and
- rehearse migration among at least two genuinely independent implementations, including replacement of the compiler, identity broker, bridge, store and coordination service.

**Gate:** a school or jurisdiction can replace a supplier without loss of meaning, rights state or audit continuity. No stable learner-context standard is proposed until two implementations that do not share the reference data model pass the same normative fixtures.

### Phase 3 — professional and assessment services (18–36 months)

- establish the separately controlled professional corpus;
- link public assessment frameworks and validity evidence to curriculum concepts;
- pilot secure item exchange, moderation and accommodations workflows; and
- publish integrity, accessibility and differential-performance evidence.

**Gate:** awarding bodies and independent assessment experts verify that interoperability has not degraded security or validity.

### Phase 4 — learner rights and portable credentials (24–48 months)

- provide child-readable records, provenance, correction/annotation and access receipts;
- issue portable, verifiable qualifications using pairwise or scoped identifiers;
- pilot capacity-sensitive guardian and advocate workflows;
- provide assisted and non-digital routes; and
- exclude behaviour telemetry and prediction from the pilot.

**Gate:** high completion rates for rights requests, low disparity by disability/language/income, tested account recovery, and no dependence on one wallet or identity vendor. Success here does not authorise a general learner profile.

### Phase 5 — safeguarding and research pilots, kept separate (30–54 months)

- only after the synthetic suite passes, pilot minimum safeguarding projections and verified transfer obligations in a small number of legally authorised, independently supervised sites;
- separately establish secure research environments and public project registers;
- test correction/revocation propagation, break-glass review, disclosure control and recovery; and
- commission independent child-rights, equality, security and practitioner evaluations.

**Gate:** no scaling if critical transfers are missed, ordinary users can discover raw narratives, appeals cannot change state, subgroup harms exceed agreed thresholds, or the service cannot restore safely.

### Phase 6 — limited operational federation (after 48 months)

- treat this phase as optional rather than the programme's presumed destination;
- admit each identifiable field separately, only for a named use case with demonstrated public benefit over a stated baseline and no adequate less-intrusive alternative;
- require a separate statutory and evidence case for each new personal-data bridge;
- publish prospective protocols, uncertainty and results; and
- retain rollback, sunset and parliamentary/devolved review.

There should be no presumption that all phases must be completed. A successful programme might stop after delivering an excellent public knowledge commons, standards layer, rights portal and research environment. Restraint is an outcome, not failure.

## 8. Whole-life proof and public infrastructure economics

*What public infrastructure must provide to demonstrate superiority* (4 September 2026) is a proposed analytical framework, not an official proof standard or a requirement to reopen the authorised POC decision. Its useful question is narrower: **when a specific design claims superiority or seeks infrastructure-scale commitment, what evidence would warrant that claim?** A project becomes infrastructure through a durable public function, entitlement/access arrangements, assets, owner, powers, guarantees, finance, workforce, change and continuity—not by renaming a repository.

Four proposed gates separate claims that are often collapsed:

1. **Intervention/common-layer additionality:** state the public need, beneficiaries, boundaries, baseline and counterfactual; explain the causal mechanism, distribution, negative theory of change and stopping conditions.
2. **Non-compensable safeguards:** identify binding law and separately proposed protections for equality, accessibility, rights, security and remedy. An aggregate economic benefit does not compensate for an unlawful intervention.
3. **Operational additionality:** compare equivalent service obligations, quality, risk and whole-life boundaries; measure outcomes and burdens rather than feature counts.
4. **Durability and learning:** fund an accountable owner, workforce, maintenance, succession, repair, preservation, migration and public negative evidence.

Whole-life social cost includes discovery/build/integration/transition; staffing/hosting/security/assurance/preservation; user and compliance effort; independent implementer cost; incidents/remediation; migration/exit; inclusion and assisted service; environmental and opportunity costs; and residual reuse/option value. Forecast, modelled and realised benefits must be labelled separately. A capital build cost cannot fairly be compared to a supplier's complete service price. Moving workload onto teachers, families, disabled users or implementers is not a saving.


| Required provision | What credible proof looks like | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| A precise public mission | Statutory/policy basis, defined beneficiaries, outcome measures and excluded uses | Mission expands to justify the asset after delivery |
| A dependable baseline service | Published service levels, uptime, completion, quality and incident data | Adoption is reported without user success or reliability |
| Universal and accessible access | Disaggregated eligibility, reach, completion, failure and satisfaction; assisted/offline routes where needed | Average use hides systematic exclusion |
| Public control of essential rules | Accountable owner, public change process, audit and appeal | A supplier or closed consortium can change core terms unilaterally |
| Open interoperability | Royalty-free specification, public test suite, multiple independent implementations and successful exchange | “Open API” exists but export is partial or only one implementation works |
| Portability and exit | Complete documented export, round-trip test, migration drill, cost/time evidence and continuity plan | Exit is contractual in theory but untested or unaffordable |
| Rights and remedy | Notice, reason, correction, appeal, human escalation and resolution-time data | Users cannot identify or challenge harmful outcomes |
| Whole-life value | Full public and social cost, counterfactual, distribution, uncertainty and realised benefits | Capital build cost is compared with a supplier's full service price |
| Resilience and security | Threat model, recovery objectives, exercises, incident learning and supplier-chain visibility | Compliance badge substitutes for operational testing |
| Public capability | Permanent team, documented knowledge, training, succession and falling avoidable dependency | Contractors remain the only people able to operate or change the core |
| Ecosystem additionality | Independent services built, switching rates, entry cost and reuse without privileged access | The common layer becomes a gatekeeper for complements |
| Transparent learning | Preregistered evaluation, open metrics/data where safe, independent findings and management response | Only success stories and cumulative activity counts are published |


### A public evidence scorecard for a specific superiority claim


The scorecard should report values and evidence, not collapse them into one opaque score.

| Dimension | Illustrative measures | Minimum evidence for a superiority claim |
|---|---|---|
| Public outcomes | Completion, accuracy, timeliness, affected outcome, avoided harm | Realised and independently assessed, not feature count |
| Universality and equity | Eligible reach, subgroup completion/error, burden, assisted access | No unlawful or avoidable disadvantage; any residual differential effect is evidenced, proportionate, mitigated, monitored and subject to effective remedy |
| Quality and reliability | Availability, latency, defects, recovery, user success | Published service level and incident history over a meaningful period |
| Whole-life social value | Total cost, user burden, risk, option/reuse value | Same boundary and horizon; sensitivity; forecast separated from actual |
| Openness and exit | Conformance, export completeness, migration time/cost, semantic loss | Independent implementation and completed migration drill |
| Contestability | Supplier concentration, qualified entrants, switching, privileged access | Core rules/assets do not give one complement provider an unavoidable advantage |
| Rights and accountability | Notices, explanations, challenges, appeals, corrections, resolution time | Effective remedy tested with affected users and audited |
| Security and resilience | Critical incidents, recovery objectives, exercises, supply-chain findings | Independent testing and response evidence, not only certification |
| Public capability | Permanent staff, key-person risk, change lead-time without supplier | The public owner can specify, assure and govern the core; restore continuity; and replace or insource critical components without unacceptable service loss, cost or permission dependency |
| Adaptability | Upgrade/migration success, backwards compatibility, policy-change lead time | Change without forced replacement or loss of record |
| Ecosystem value | Reuse, independent products, reduced entry cost, geographic/sector spread | Additional services arise without privileged or exclusive access |
| Sustainability | Energy, carbon, water, embodied hardware/material, waste and workload efficiency | Full-chain impact per useful outcome plus total-demand and rebound effects, compared on the same functional boundary |

Traffic-light ratings can accompany this table only when thresholds are public and the underlying values remain visible.


### Concrete cases that challenge easy claims

| Case and source snapshot | Evidence | What it can and cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| GOV.UK Pay, 1 September 2026 owner metrics | 1,771 services, 632 organisations, 145.2m transactions, £9.7bn processed | Substantial operational scale; not a causal counterfactual estimate of public value |
| GOV.UK Verify, NAO | At least £154m cost with weak adoption/performance against expectations | Public sponsorship does not immunise a service against failure; not proof that shared public identity can never work |
| CMA cloud analysis | A hypothetical 5% price elevation was illustrated as about £500m | A sensitivity illustration of potential switching/market harm; not £500m of observed overcharging |
| TfL open-data evaluation, 2017 | Commissioned analysis of reuse and spillovers beyond the operator | Reuse can create benefits outside the infrastructure budget; not a universal benefit coefficient |
| Biometric welfare reform in India, Muralidharan, Niehaus and Sukhtankar (2025) | Reduced corruption accompanied by estimated exclusion of 1.5–2m legitimate beneficiaries during transition | Efficiency and exclusion can coexist; setting-specific effect cannot be imported as a UK magnitude |
| Government shared services, NAO 2026 | Fragmented onboarding, ownership and ERP configuration | A common standard still needs adoption authority, local capability and aligned operation |

These cases call for preregistered claims, a meaningful evidence duration covering seasonality and low-frequency harms, independent analysis, subgroup and null findings, disclosed funding/conflicts, and a published management response. Privacy or security may require protected evidence; safe summaries and independent authorised access should then carry the accountability, with reasons for withholding.

Supplier contracts should preserve documentation, configuration, records and licences; audit and subprocessor visibility; incident/security cooperation; skills transfer; bounded and priced transition assistance; data return/deletion; continuity and successor operation. Public operational capacity means the owner can govern and recover the service; it does not require every operation to be permanently insourced.

Falsifiers include a better fair comparator; failed rights guardrails; benefits disappearing under full costs; one-implementation dependence or semantic loss in migration; an unjustified gatekeeper; no durable owner; hidden transferred burden; incidents contradicting the theory of change; uncontrolled mission expansion; irreproducible claims; or a changed version invalidating old evidence. A justified response can be Stop, Transform or Focus.

### Minimum proof pack before claiming demonstrated superiority


A public infrastructure project should not claim demonstrated superiority before it can publish or provide to authorised reviewers:

1. the public-purpose and boundary statement;
2. a preregistered theory of change and evaluation plan;
3. lawful-authority, equality, accessibility, privacy and security assessments;
4. service levels and operational evidence over a preregistered duration sufficient to capture expected seasonality, transition effects and material low-frequency harms;
5. a documented, decision-adequate whole-life social-cost and distributional analysis with stated omissions and uncertainty;
6. an open specification and conformance suite for the common layer;
7. evidence from at least two independent implementations where interoperability is claimed;
8. a completed export, restoration and supplier-migration drill;
9. an accountable governance, appeal and correction record;
10. a permanent operating, workforce, succession and preservation plan;
11. an independent evaluation including negative and subgroup findings; and
12. a published decision responding to the evaluation and falsification conditions.

Where security or privacy prevents full publication, the project should publish a safe summary and give an appropriately independent reviewer complete access. Secrecy should be specific, reasoned, time-bounded where possible and itself auditable.


## 9. The Practice as portable governance research

*The Practice: a foundation for persistent, corrigible, learning agency across changing people, agents, environments, purposes, and time* (5 August 2026) generalises from an OCE software-development phenotype. Its core is a portable constitution for purpose, authority, durable obligations, evidence, correction and learning; it is not a central controller, a universal ontology, or a claim about metaphysical identity. The source's wider-domain assertions are hypotheses. OCE-native maturity does not prove institutional, educational or personal-sovereignty effectiveness elsewhere.

The generalisation should transfer invariants rather than repository nouns. A branch can instantiate a bounded proposal; a pull request a review/acceptance boundary; a claim file attempt authority; CI an invariant regulator; an issue a schedule projection; a session bounded cognition; a worktree an execution habitat; a PDR a portable decision and an ADR its local implementation; a skill a procedure; a rule a boundary invariant; a hook a zero-context enforcement point; a napkin a surprise; and consolidation durable learning. None of those particular containers is required in another domain.

### Constitutional commitments and non-collapse

The foundation joins 22 commitments: purpose before activity; the principal as the source of intent; standing before optimisation; replaceable agents; existence distinct from eligibility; evidence distinct from policy; intent/execution/history on different clocks; obligations surviving actors; minimal purpose-scoped context; governed acceptance of candidate shared-state changes; lineage for consequential transforms; knowledge advising and mechanisms enforcing; proportional burden with a strict truth standard; first-class inhibition; review distinct from restoration; falsifiable learning at different speeds; propagated forgetting/revocation; subtractive evolution; concept portability; sovereign federation; a transmitted culture of correction; and self-application without an immune constitutional layer.

The operational model distinguishes an **obligation** from its **attempts**, their **actors**, the **host** and any recurring **generator**. Cancelling an attempt does not necessarily discharge the obligation. Replacing an actor does not repair a bad obligation. A process health check does not prove it can observe this event, act in this scope, complete this task or absorb a correction. Zero active agents can coexist with a durable obligation waiting on an external event.

Time, epistemic provenance, authority, eligibility, retention and liveness are independent dimensions. A retained historical claim may be accessible to a diagnostic reviewer yet ineligible to influence ordinary work. A summary does not acquire greater authority than its source. Authority is scoped and often partially ordered across people/institutions; it is not one universal rank. Eligibility depends on subject, action, purpose, context, authority, evidence and time. Narrowing a grant or revoking a capability should not silently broaden another right.

### Work, waiting, correction and restraint

A minimal loop records legitimate purpose, one obligation, a bounded actor/context/authority, action evidence, acceptance/correction/stop, a terminal account, a learning or no-change decision and retirement of obsolete operational state. Waiting needs a durable condition, observable external source, owner, review point, trigger/deduplication behaviour and an account of what happens if the condition never arrives. Recovery distinguishes known completed effects, begun but incomplete work, and intervals whose effects are unknown.

Non-action is operationally meaningful: pause, wait, defer, suppress a generator, quarantine, cancel, revoke, retire, refuse and operationally forget have different consequences. A future review is not automatic reactivation. Dormancy can preserve option value while removing current causal authority. Anti-memory can retain a minimal prohibition/retirement record to prevent an obsolete instruction being resurrected, without retaining an entire harmful context.

### A regulator ecology

A regulator needs a controlled subject/variable, sensors, actuators, authority, budgets, reset/decay, escalation and de-escalation conditions, failure posture, review and retirement. Failure should be located at the narrowest causal level that can change it. Useful discriminating probes independently change actor, obligation, host, generator, derivation or permission. Escalation needs evidence of causal similarity, exhausted local information/budget, an effective broader actuator, proportionate blast radius and preserved standing.

Regulators can fight each other: retry versus stale-authority suppression, reset versus reassignment, repair versus migration. Stable identity, expected-state checks, idempotence, damping, coalescing, explicit ownership and regulator observability can reduce oscillation. A regulator should retire when its cause disappears, false-positive burden dominates, evidence remains unused, the environment changes or the regulator creates instability. Accumulating a new check after every incident without retiring old controls can become governance theatre.

### State, context and learning

The foundation distinguishes desired, observed, grounded, derived, epistemic, authority, eligibility and historical state. Schemas make fields and some invariants checkable; they do not provide causal knowledge, judgement or legitimacy. Validators cover structure, invariants, time, authority, evidence sufficiency and reconciliation. Deterministic transformations and actuators should be separated from agent hypotheses/probes and human authority over purpose or high-stakes change.

State-now and memory-through-time are sibling planes. Prose carries meaning, metadata carries identity and governance, graphs carry addressable relationships; one cannot substitute for the others. Fast operational learning and slow methodological/constitutional learning need different authority and timescales. A promoted change states baseline, prediction, evidence, horizon, falsifier, affected surfaces, expected deletion and review authority. Change-rate signals ask whether validation keeps pace; the source treats them as reflection triggers, not numerical caps.

Context is itself a causal intervention. Existence, epistemic status, accessibility, disclosure permission, operational eligibility, action authority, liveness and retention must not collapse. The candidate retrieval→source/provenance→principal/purpose→obligation/attempt→authority/eligibility→minimisation→contradiction/supersession→budget→projection route may produce exact content, abstraction, competing claims, warnings, diagnostic-only access, refusal or declared incompleteness. A consequential receipt can identify sources, omissions, policies, expiry and allowed action without unnecessarily retaining the full context. The domain-specific authoritative contract is in document 06.

Independent exploration can help detect a dominant inherited premise: one reviewer may receive the current synthesis and another the constraints plus source evidence, then reconcile. Agreement by agents with the same context is correlated evidence. Review earns acceptance into shared truth; it does not itself activate a policy or confer new permissions. Native implementation and capability details are in documents 07–08.

### Humane use, portability and self-application

The coordination substrate is a product used by people: discoverability, legibility, interruption, recovery, dignity, attention and administrative burden matter. Recording friction is not its remedy. Combine deterministic operations when no judgement is hidden; retain traceability and do not conceal a human approval inside a convenience action. Success includes removing procedures because deeper capability made them unnecessary.

The source distinguishes a portable memotype from a local phenotype and four knowledge strata: ontology, Practice, instance and operator. Sovereign domains retain their own stores, authority, names, privacy and risk. A bridge sends bounded projections and receives candidate changes, not unconditional shared truth. Federation is a topology, not a maturity level.

The source inherits four OCE maturity levels—structural, operational, self-correcting and evolving—and proposes a fifth, **self-governing**. The fifth is explicitly a synthesis, not an accepted OCE record. Evidence would have to show that a system can govern eligibility, authority transfer, regulator cost, subtraction and retirement, choose not to change and stop its own generators while remaining answerable to external purpose.

Self-application means the checker can be checked, memory policy reviewed, context compiler removed, regulator inhibited and constitution amended by proper authority. A remove-the-Practice drill asks whether another implementation can reconstruct purpose, obligations, current authority and evidence; which pieces are useful; and what fails safely when tooling disappears. A beautiful internal theory can still be wrong or unnecessary.

### Evaluation, failure and research

Evaluate purpose fidelity, truth, identity continuity, corrigibility, restraint, recovery, context integrity, portability, federation, standing, learning, forgetting, subtraction, experience and public value. Priority experiments include multi-attempt obligation continuity with zero-agent waiting; causal fault localisation; stale-authority context; descendant correction; active/scheduled revocation; regulator retirement; genuinely independent review; deterministic compression; cross-domain transfer; and provider/Practice removal.

Stop, narrow or remove mechanisms if the kernel grows beyond local models; governance burden exceeds benefit; nontechnical participants need specialist administration to exercise authority; a central service becomes sovereign; context leaks purpose; revocation fails to converge; regulator metadata becomes surveillance; independent implementations cannot interoperate; plural standing cannot be represented; new layers retire nothing; legibility substitutes for agency; identity/consciousness claims exceed evidence; a simpler alternative wins; no justified no-change result is possible; or human correction becomes slower or weaker.

Open research includes the minimum kernel, non-delegable decisions, multi-party appeal/emergency authority, discriminating probes and decay/restoration thresholds, context omissions, residual model influence after forgetting, recurrence versus imitation/identity, humane administration, sustainable managed services over open infrastructure, and external authority to challenge this private project's portable doctrine. Application to research, public institutions, education, personal continuity, community organising, ecological stewardship and creative work remains a programme of bounded experiments.

The exact compact portable contracts in Appendix D are retained as candidate records. They make operational distinctions reviewable, while leaving the choice of store, graph, interface and host open.


## 10. Artificial organisations as a distinct research and institutional option

This section reconstructs three historical sources: **EXT-SRC-0061, _Impact-Aligned Artificial Organisations: A framework for constructing self-correcting, research-capable systems of human and artificial agency_**, 17 July 2026, version 0.1, provisional constitution `F₀`; **EXT-SRC-0062, _Transcript: Impact-Aligned Artificial Organisations_**, the visible project conversation exported with speaker order and links; and **EXT-SRC-0063, _Deep Dives: The Six Mechanisms Without Prior Art_**, a subsequent set of concrete mechanism proposals. The framework is a synthesis and construction manual, the transcript is evidence of the question's development, and the deep dives specify unvalidated designs. They are valuable broader knowledge. They do not establish a national institution, change current OCE permissions, require repository hooks, or add constitutional machinery to the tuition POC.

The original problem was a long-lived AI team with broad operational discretion that completed tasks, built elaborate proofs, and passed local tests while losing the strategic reason for the work. The proposed solution treats the organisation's representations, decision rights, incentives, memory, feedback and evidence as causes of that behaviour. A technically successful task may support an ineffective intervention; a valuable intervention may still pursue an illegitimate outcome. Each is a different diagnosis.

Two historical corrections matter. The first assistant response placed rational metareasoning before game theory; the user's subsequent description of delegated agency made principal–agent theory and mechanism design relevant without assuming malicious or conscious self-interest. Later, the transcript's categorical requirement that every investigation have an immediate decision target was broadened in `F₀`: foundational inquiry, reliable negative results, clearer concepts, explanatory understanding, shared knowledge, enabling infrastructure and capacity for judgement can be legitimate contributions in their own right. Bounded learning contracts protect inquiry while requiring a contribution claim and review. The early statement that a causal impact model supplies normative architecture is also insufficient on its own: the developed framework preserves distinct affected-party standing, rights, value disagreement and legitimate authority.

### The proposed object and its causal account

An impact-aligned artificial organisation is a proposed arrangement of people, agents, roles, memory and authority that maintains a connection between valuable legitimate change and provisional means. Jobs, products, projects, methods and metrics are possible means. Research, teaching, care, funding another organisation, coordination, institutional change, communication, restraint, waiting and no action remain live alternatives when relevant. Selecting a digital product first can hide a better causal route.

The source's chain runs from values and affected parties through desired change, opportunities, available means, interventions, capabilities, tasks and outputs to changed behaviour or system conditions, outcomes, benefit or harm, and learning. It is a network of defeasible causal claims, not an instruction to impose a single linear decomposition. A representative local chain is task → capability → behaviour → outcome → impact. Each arrow can be **assumed, evidenced, weakened, falsified or superseded**, independently. The bridge and proof account in [07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md) remains canonical for current OCE application; the present section preserves the broader organisational research proposal.

The external validation chain is equally specific: a framework mechanism changes information or authority; this changes a decision; the decision changes action or allocation; the action changes an external outcome; the outcome constitutes valuable legitimate impact for affected people. Evidence should identify the earliest unsupported arrow. Producing a decision record proves neither a different decision nor a better outcome. Higher-order claims should normally describe **contribution**, retaining rival causes and the share that cannot be identified, rather than exclusive attribution.

An impact profile separates value type, causal depth, time horizon, scope, confidence, reversibility, distribution and externalities. Its epistemic stages are possible, intended, hypothesised, predicted, observed, causally supported, replicated and transferred. Direct, mediated, distributed portfolio, institutional and reflexive contribution paths may overlap; they must not be added as independent benefits. Similarly, causal depth and learning depth are different: a direct intervention can reveal a constitutional defect, while a constitutional improvement can have distant mediated effects. Edge-specific assumptions, delay, decay, harms, displacement, counterfactuals and sensitivity to uncertainty travel with higher-order claims.

`F₀` represents value as a plural vector containing realised impact, epistemic value, capacity, systemic contribution, legitimacy, opportunity cost, harm and lock-in. It does not establish a scalar exchange rate between rights, knowledge, learning gains and efficiency. Expected-value reasoning can inform choices inside legitimate constraints; a high expected benefit does not confer authority over those constraints.

### Why the behaviour recurs

| Mechanism or failure | Meaning and consequence | Proposed response and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic entropy | Delegation preserves executable detail more faithfully than purpose, rivals, uncertainty and invalidation; repeated compression compounds the loss | Decision-sufficient context and live parent claims; more context is not automatically better |
| Task-preserving coordination | Planner, builder, reviewer and verifier each assume the previous artefact remains legitimate, making challenge appear outside their role | Assign responsibility and authority for reconsideration; role names alone do not produce independent judgement |
| Proxy and signalling pressure | Tests, proof length, code, documents and completed tickets make diligence legible while avoided work is less visible | Evaluate contribution and decisions, retain justified stopping; local metrics remain useful diagnostics |
| Incumbent and sunk-context advantage | Effort creates context and artefacts, which increase local fluency and apparent tractability, attracting more effort even when alternatives are better | Compare independent alternatives, record residual assets separately from sunk cost, and price switching and lock-in |
| Escalation only after blockage | An agent can continue smoothly after the causal reason for continuing has weakened | Permit evidence-based reframing and value-triggered review within delegated authority |
| Wrong level of correction | Operational patches absorb evidence of intervention failure; the reverse error turns local noise into strategic upheaval | Classify locus, scope, persistence, confidence and authority; review the nearest relevant explanation and credible rivals |
| Retrospective immunisation | Every failure yields another auxiliary explanation, with no new discriminating prediction | Prospective predictions, severe tests, rival models and trajectory review; a single anomaly does not disprove a programme |
| Recursive self-confirmation | The framework invents a mechanism, creates its evidence and criteria, then declares its own success | Comparators, independent evaluation, holdouts, versions and rollback; external effects remain necessary |
| Meta-work as a new rabbit hole | Reflection, review and constitutional changes become the new task-completion proxy | Count review and documentation costs, remove unused fields and mechanisms, and permit lightweight non-application |

The source uses complex-system language carefully. Negative feedback can stabilise budgets, constraints and corrections but become rigidity. Positive feedback can amplify learning, adoption and useful specialisation but also strengthen rabbit holes and premature lock-in. **Metastable commitment** means sufficient stability for coherent work with governed opportunities to change. It is not a claim to operate at a scientifically established “edge of chaos”. Separate exit and re-entry criteria—hysteresis—can reduce repeated oscillation, but their thresholds require calibration.

### Theoretical contributions and their limits

| Tradition | Useful transferred idea | What the analogy does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Rational metareasoning and value of computation | Allocate the next unit of attention among execution, investigation, comparison, replanning and stopping | A reliable numeric value function for every organisational or normative decision |
| Hierarchical options, stopping, switching and real options | Actions have initiation, bounded internal policy, termination, reconsideration and residual value; switching has costs, and delaying can preserve options | That a task's original selection permanently authorises it, or that ordinary stopping models solve changes to goals and problem frames |
| Principal–agent and incomplete-contract theory | Local information and observable success differ from distant impact; design decision rights and incentives for useful challenge | Conscious self-interest is required, or that prompts alone remove agency loss |
| Information theory and cybernetics | Preserve distinctions that can change decisions through scarce channels; retain sufficient response variety | Full information transfer is desirable, or organisational “entropy” is a measured physical quantity |
| Bayesian analysis and change-point detection | Separate claims by level, compare likelihood under rivals, expose priors and provenance, distinguish parameter uncertainty from a changed process | Positive-looking evidence supports every ancestor claim; a detected regime shift identifies its cause or grants authority |
| Popper, Duhem–Quine, Lakatos, Kuhn and severe testing | Claims must risk failure; failures implicate conjunctions of central and auxiliary assumptions; assess trajectories and novel prediction; normal work and frame change have different roles | Automatic abandonment after one failure, a universal scientific algorithm, or an unvalidated progress ratio as proof of truth |
| Causal inference | Separate task behaviour, adoption, outcomes, harms and counterfactual contribution | Usage, correlation, satisfaction or agent consensus is causal impact |
| Organisational learning, evolutionary search and sensemaking | Learn about action, assumptions, methods and the organisation; preserve variation, selection, retention, minority hypotheses and reframing | Every repeated local problem merits a new rule; all agents sharing a frame provide independent evidence |
| Resilience and distributed systems | Partial views, delayed messages, stale versions, containment, graceful degradation, recovery and explicit consistency needs | A control-plane metaphor supplies legal or democratic authority, or a single central organisation should control an ecosystem |

The decision-sufficient compression proposal minimises a decision-loss term plus transmission/attention cost: `D_decision(x, c(x)) + λ Cost(c(x))`. Its test is whether restoring omitted information would materially change a recipient's choice. Ablations should remove purpose, alternatives, assumptions, confidence, invalidation, provenance or affected-party context separately. High-frequency mechanical details can stay local; patterns aggregate; strategically decisive information and harm evidence need protected transmission. The theory's useful object is decision loss, not a demand to retain all context.

A qualitative metacontrol comparison includes expected contribution, information gain and preserved option value, less execution, switching, coordination, harm and lock-in costs. Expected information value can be represented as the expected value of the best choice after evidence minus the best current choice minus study costs and harms. This helps discriminate two technically successful prototypes when only one tests the live uncertainty. It does not disqualify research whose explicit contribution is foundational understanding rather than an immediate action change.

### Ten functions of scale and seven feedback loops

The source distinguishes ten conceptual layers: normative orientation; impact opportunity; agency and means; portfolio; intervention; programme or product; task and operation; evidence and metacontrol; research and meta-learning; constitutional and normative adaptation. They are views of responsibility and inquiry, not ten mandatory agent roles or a command chain. A local unit may change what is delegated below it, propose changes to its own governing assumptions and submit changes above its authority; it must not acquire higher authority by performing the work.

| Loop | Typical information and response | Adaptation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Operational | Tool failures, latency, local uncertainty, tests → retry or revise an authorised method | Fast; stay inside resource, data, causal and safety scope |
| Tactical | Unexpected cost, repeated failure, diminishing returns, cheaper routes → substitute, split, simplify or stop a task | At meaningful task events or boundaries; preserve parent claims |
| Programme/intervention | Adoption, usability, changed behaviour, outcomes and harms → redesign capability or intervention | Bounded tranche review; local completion is insufficient |
| Strategic/portfolio | External change, causal-model failure, comparative evidence, opportunity cost → reallocate or switch means | Slower and more consequential; retain alternatives and no action |
| Research/meta-learning | Discrimination, calibration, negative results, transfer and method quality → revise methods or programme hypotheses | Research protocol and synthesis boundaries; protected exploratory allocation |
| Constitutional | Recurring organisational pathology across contexts → propose changes to roles, rules, memory, evidence or amendment process | Versioned trial, authorised evaluation and rollback; do not silently rewrite governing rules |
| Normative | Harm, exclusion, contested value, standing and legitimacy → reconsider purposes, constraints and representation | Protected human and affected-party authority; urgent harms can bypass slower cadence |

The transcript offered seconds/minutes for mechanical signals, hours for task feasibility, days for plan validity, weeks for product effects, months for portfolio/design and longer for mission. These are illustrative timescales, not universal operational timers. Fundamental commitments normally change more slowly and with more evidence; urgent harm cannot be made to wait because a normal strategic review is distant.

Evidence coordinates include level, organisational scope, horizon, confidence, novelty, decision relevance, reversibility, value/risk, provenance and urgency. The router maps `R(e) → (level, scope, urgency, confidence, authority)`. Missing, delayed, misrouted, over-amplified or suppressed feedback have different remedies. Repeated observations can justify investigating a shared cause; recurrence by itself does not prove constitutional causation. Apply the cross-scale causal test in [06](06-state-rights-and-context.md) and [07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md) before promoting a local result.

### Operating cycle and delegation

The proposed cycle is **orient, compete, commit, execute, observe, route, update, re-authorise, retain**. State purpose, intended contribution, affected parties and causal chain; compare materially different means; select a bounded action or inquiry; allow competent local autonomy; record evidence separately from interpretation; send it to the authorised locus; update beliefs before plans; explicitly choose the next commitment; retain the reasons and useful residuals.

A task-as-option has initiation conditions, an internal policy, normal completion, invalidation, review conditions and residual assets. Invalidation is distinct from being done, being blocked and exhausting a resource budget. An unblocked task that is almost complete can lose its causal reason; a difficult task can remain valuable. Legitimate terminal or intermediate choices include continue, modify, replace, investigate, switch implementation, reframe intervention, revisit strategy, defer, enable another actor, wait and stop.

The bounded tranche declares objective and hypothesis; scope and exclusions; duration and resources; irreversible actions and blast radius; predicted output and information; stop, switch and escalation conditions; next decision; owner and evaluator. A continuation decision compares predictions with observations, updates claims, compares the incumbent with a challenger and no action, prices new lock-in and residual assets, examines distribution and harms, records dissent and defines the next bounded commitment. These are proposed design elements; they do not mean every reversible edit in this reconstruction requires fresh user approval.

A reframe asks whether the nearest uncertain parent objective has a better route. Candidate triggers include unexpectedly high effort, an architectural boundary crossing, proof work outrunning demonstration of value, weakened causal links, cheaper alternatives, no observable external effect, complexity growing faster than benefit, inability to explain the causal chain and work remaining internally attractive after its purpose has weakened. The response must be proportionate: a hard step alone is not a strategic failure.

### Functions, independence and decision rights

Functions remain visible even when one person or agent combines several in low-stakes work. Independence depends on information, incentives, authority and context, not a second agent name. A single context should not originate a strategic claim, implement it, select favourable evidence, suppress alternatives and certify its own general success.

| Function | Duty and characteristic failure to guard against |
|---|---|
| Impact/normative steward | Preserve purpose, affected-party process, constraints and legitimate escalation; do not convert value disagreement into a confidence score or micromanage every method |
| Portfolio steward | Compare time, capital, attention, reputation and capability across active, challenger, exploratory, deferred and no-action options; produce reasoned allocation rather than a speculative scalar ranking |
| Strategy/causal-model steward | Maintain mechanism, assumptions, predictions and external effects; avoid becoming counsel for the incumbent |
| Programme lead and planner | Translate into coherent bounded work, dependencies and options; expose strategic uncertainty rather than hiding it in a complete plan |
| Executor | Act competently within scope; adapt locally; record material evidence; stop/escalate at real boundaries; preserve residual assets |
| Counterfactual challenger | Independently frame alternatives, no action and enablement; demand discriminating tests; preserve a challenge budget and protection from retaliation; do not reward disagreement frequency |
| Evidence collector/verifier | Preserve observation versus interpretation, source verification, missingness and measurement limitations; avoid choosing evidence merely because it is easy to collect |
| Evidence router | Classify, aggregate and deliver decision-sufficient evidence; measure delay and suppression; allow protected bypasses so it cannot become an unchallengeable gatekeeper |
| Research steward | Protect foundational/exploratory inquiry alongside application, contribution claims, rival hypotheses, severe tests and transfer conditions |
| Independent evaluator | Compare predictions and results, conditions and holdouts; inspect selective evidence, external effects, overhead and dissent |
| Memory/provenance curator | Preserve decisions, rejected alternatives, negative findings and audit/replay while enforcing access, retention and correction |
| Constitutional steward | Govern proposals, sandboxes, versions, promotion and rollback; cannot acquire unilateral normative authority |
| Affected-party interface | Make participation and representation limitations explicit; translate evidence without erasing disagreement; it does not substitute for real participation |

The reference decision-rights matrix permits an executor to choose a local method within contract; tactical/programme authority to replace a task; programme/strategy authority to redesign capability; portfolio authority to switch intervention or reallocate resources; research authority to change method within protocol; constitutional authority to change persistent rules after evaluation and affected-role review; and protected legitimate human/affected-party authority to revise normative purposes and constraints. Consequential irreversible actions remain with specifically authorised people/institutions and relevant review. Anyone may propose a change; proposal, confidence, permission and decision are distinct states.

A small implementation may combine human impact, portfolio and constitutional stewardship, an execution team and a separately contextualised challenger/evaluator. Medium programmes may separate leads, routing and research. Larger arrangements can federate bounded local autonomy, shared standards and protected challenge. At ecosystem scale the framework becomes a protocol for claims, contribution, accountability and learning among institutions; it does not assume central command over all causal pathways.

### Six concrete mechanism proposals

The deep-dive title's “without prior art” is a historical search conclusion, not an established novelty claim. Its own scope says none was found in bounded searches of a fast-moving literature, with stronger confidence about particular repository implementations than the conceptual ideas. Several inherited preprints were explicitly unverified. Analogues from options, design rationale, scientific method, organisational learning and incident practice remain relevant. Claims that nobody has implemented an idea, that agent-authored rationale is now effectively costless, or that a proposed hook closes most gaming routes are design arguments needing evidence.

#### 1. Task invalidation

At shaping time, attach a justifying claim, reference-class appetite, and typed invalidation conditions. Evidence conditions concern a weakened or falsified parent hypothesis; environment conditions concern changed dependencies, platform capabilities or priorities; dominance conditions concern a demonstrably cheaper or better route. A budget circuit breaker is related but distinguishable. The original specimen uses a **six-hour appetite**, an **effort > 1.5 × appetite** binding stop, evidence/environment conditions that trigger fresh-context review, and a competition verdict that can stop the task. These are proposed example settings, not validated or adopted thresholds.

Each condition has `type`, a meaningful `predicate`, a `check` where machine-checkable, and `on_fire: review | kill`. A task or claim carries an identifier linking it to its causal rationale. The source proposes at least one evidence condition, authored by pre-mortem and reviewed in a different context. Its purpose is to prevent a task that can only stop by exhausting budget and can never lose on its merits. Conditions are frozen for the tranche; the executor cannot silently rewrite its own stopping rule. Legitimate changes should use an authorised versioned revision, not make the historical predicate disappear.

The repository specimen checks cheaply at periodic tool boundaries, at stopping/re-authorisation and at promotion; checks depending on release feeds or other repositories use periodic aggregation. An errored predicate becomes **fired for review**, never an implicit pass. A completion or continuation record explicitly reports current invalidation status, including no conditions fired. A fired condition becomes an anomaly; every killed task records useful salvage. Evidence-based stopping is deliberately non-binding in the proposed design because noisy evidence can wrongly kill a near-success; an authorised reviewer examines it. Source parallels with clinical futility rules are conceptual transfers, not clinical or legal guidance.

Validation separates condition families, fire rate, kill rate, later vindication, value lost by premature stopping and effort saved. The source suggests reconsidering vacuous evidence-condition authoring if fewer than roughly one in twenty tasks fires after a quarter; retain that as a heuristic research proposal. Low frequency alone cannot establish vacuity without the task mix and expected event rate. Test stale checks, task restructuring to evade predicates and finishing before checking. Any actual enforcement must comply with existing tool permissions; this documentary reconstruction installs none.

#### 2. Cross-context anomaly ledger

The ledger records observations inconsistent with an assumption across tasks, teams and time so that repeated weak signals are not lost in separate histories. A concrete entry contains timestamp, repository or context, tranche, assumption/claim-arrow identifier, observation, origin (`hook`, agent, human or CI), severity (`note`, `strain`, `contradiction`), whether it was expected under the current model, and salvage. The original example records high cache hit rate with unchanged p95 latency and possible serialisation dominance: a technically working cache can strain the hypothesis that caching resolves the user bottleneck.

Three capture paths are proposed: mechanical observation of fix-of-fix chains, tests added then modified, repeated near-identical diffs, budget crossings and invalidation; agent reports at boundaries; and very cheap human entry. Mechanical collection is observe-only and cannot block work. Entries lacking an assumption link go to triage rather than disappearing: they may reveal that the claim registry omitted a load-bearing assumption. Append-only JSONL and CI checks are a historical specimen chosen for reviewability and aggregation; [06](06-state-rights-and-context.md) governs sensitive payload retention and correction rather than any blanket perpetual-storage assumption.

The consuming layer clusters by assumption and severity, examines rate changes, and routes a digest. A weekly digest and contradiction-only interruption are proposed defaults; neither is universal. Bayesian online change-point detection or CUSUM could compare ordinary variation with changed rates. A change-point indicates altered observations, not their causal locus. Triage should ask what decision or bounded learning purpose resolving the cluster serves, protecting the `F₀` distinction between legitimate research and aimless investigation.

Retrospective replay should reconstruct known pivot and rabbit-hole episodes and measure how early clustering would have signalled the strain relative to actual recognition, including false alerts and capture burden. Do not reward entry count. Inspect whether clusters are resolved and future decisions improve; a write-only ledger is a diary with cost. Diagnostic and routing consumers are intended to make the data useful, but automatic consumption still does not prove decision value.

#### 3. Live causal-claim references

A durable task, specification, proposal or significant module references the causal claim that justifies it. A registry stores an immutable statement/version; first-class arrows with `from`, `to`, status and evidence links; invalidation conditions; and `superseded_by`. A status change needs evidence. A substantive proposition changes by supersession rather than retrospective rationale rewriting. An orphan check can flag artefacts without a claim; a propagation check can flag every dependent artefact when an upstream arrow weakens or fails. The flag initiates appropriate review; the existence of an edge alone does not determine every descendant's action eligibility.

The source recommends authoring the chain at shaping in a different context from the builder and checking it during execution. A builder inventing its own justification after completion risks fluent rationalisation. At a boundary, a restatement of the current chain can expose lost purpose; declining coherence should be investigated rather than treated as a causal impact metric.

Begin with active interventions, tasks and specifications. The source's one-claim-per-intervention and capped five-arrow chain are anti-sprawl implementation heuristics, not a universal ontology or a restriction on the richer bridge account in [07](07-capabilities-bridges-and-proof.md). Extend to modules only if use justifies capture cost. Historical design-rationale systems are a caution: good schemas can fail when creation and maintenance burdens overwhelm their decision benefit. Agent capture reduces some human labour but its accuracy, review burden and economic advantage remain hypotheses.

Validation distinguishes coverage from consequence. Ask how many promoted artefacts have current, informative chains, then whether downgrades change decisions and reduce work spent after invalidation. A registry with every arrow permanently assumed has coverage but weak evidential force. Correlated clusters against an unchanged claim should trigger owner review. Measure false propagation and useful residual work suppressed, as well as dead-rationale effort avoided.

#### 4. Independent counterfactual competition

At a meaningful tranche boundary, compare continuing with another task, implementation or intervention, gathering information, enabling another actor, delay, no action and stopping. The challenger independently generates a route to the parent outcome **before exposure to the incumbent argument**, using materially different information where feasible. The deep dive proposes claims, anomalies, a diff summary and backlog, excluding the builder's full trajectory; a different model family or tool set can diversify inputs but does not establish independence. The challenger is advisory to an authorised decision, not a peer vote.

Questions should produce discriminating actions: which invalidation condition is nearest; what observation would fire it; what cheaper causal route exists and which affordable probe would test it; whether the next tranche has greater marginal contribution than the best available alternative. If uncertainty is high, two or three cheap isolated probes may be better than a debate. The original `COMPETITION.md`, stopping hooks, five-minute checklist and overnight probe portfolio are implementation and effort examples, not a current requirement.

The evidence inherited by the source favours caution about role-played devil's advocacy and correlated debate. Human dissent research is an analogue, not proof that a fresh-context LLM reproduces authentic human dissent. A cited single-model escalation study reported 99.2% escalation under symmetric peer deliberation and roughly halving under asymmetric hierarchy; those effect sizes are explicitly provisional and unverified in this reconstruction. Debate benefits are conditional on question verifiability and information asymmetry. They do not support a universal ban on all critique or a guarantee from using several agents.

Track how often challenge materially improves a test or decision, outcomes after an overturn, worthwhile continuations, calibration, residual correlation, compute and boundary latency. A zero overturn rate can be decorative but can also reflect good incumbents; frequent overturns can be valuable or thrashing. Frequency alone is not the target. Preserve challenge budgets and no penalty for well-founded dissent; do not reward theatrical opposition.

#### 5. A trajectory diagnostic inspired by Lakatos

The proposed diagnostic concerns a series of actions rather than one difficult step. It distinguishes work generating new externally relevant, corroborated information from work repeatedly repairing failures it created. The source defines these candidate event classes:

| Event | Source definition and proposed proxy | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| `NEW-PREDICTION` | Previously untested external behaviour is asserted and holds; a new evaluation on a pre-existing or user-facing surface passes | A trivial test on newly created internal scaffolding does not qualify merely because it is new |
| `CORROBORATION` | A preregistered expectation is met; evidence-linked claim-status change | Confirming an easy prediction may not discriminate rivals or support impact |
| `PATCH` | A same-tranche artefact is changed to accommodate a failure it caused; fix-of-fix or test-added-then-modified patterns | Legitimate consolidation can be patch-heavy |
| `SCOPE-SHIFT` | Work crosses its declared scope boundary | Scope change may be authorised and valuable; detection is not judgement |
| `TOOLING` | Internal scaffolding without an external surface | Tooling can have enabling value even though it is not external prediction evidence |

The exact proposed rolling signal is:

\[
P = \frac{N_{\mathrm{NEW\text{-}PREDICTION}}+N_{\mathrm{CORROBORATION}}}{N_{\mathrm{NEW\text{-}PREDICTION}}+N_{\mathrm{CORROBORATION}}+N_{\mathrm{PATCH}}}.
\]

The companion self-referential churn ratio is edits to tranche-created files divided by edits to pre-existing targets. Zero denominators, event segmentation, weighting and the choice of observation windows need explicit implementation policy; the source does not settle them. A low ratio is not a theorem of degeneration. The proposal seeks a sustained downward regime change across `k` windows through change-point analysis, with `k` uncalibrated; it does not supply a fixed accepted threshold.

Declare consolidation windows before they begin if excluding them from alarms, so retrospective exclusions cannot excuse every failed signal. Require external claim linkage to resist novelty gaming. Prefer measurable event features; use a judge only for ambiguous residuals and audit labels against human judgement. Quarterly label audit is an example cadence. The diagnostic flags review and must not automatically stop strategic or constitutional work: internal trajectory consistency can indicate commitment without truth or value.

Validation needs a labelled corpus including known rabbit holes **and** worthwhile deep work, precision/recall, lead time, asymmetrical costs of wasted effort and lost near-breakthroughs, comparison with a reasoning/action overthinking metric and simpler baselines, and success-rate cost alongside resources saved. Code-churn research supports the feasibility of relative change signals in software defect studies; it does not validate this organisational diagnostic. The source's “no computational prior art” claim remains bounded and provisional.

#### 6. Routing the appropriate level of response

The router combines **locus**—which claim or arrow the evidence bears on; **persistence**—first observation, repeated within a tranche, across tasks or across teams; and **reversibility**—the cost of continuing at the wrong level. It can propose local retry, invalidation review, counterfactual competition, human escalation or a constitutional change proposal. The original specimen routes task-to-capability evidence operationally/tactically and capability-to-behaviour or behaviour-to-outcome evidence strategically. Real cases need the causal and authority analysis; the arrows are not a substitute for it.

Every routing record states claimed level, evidence locus, scope, confidence, proposed response, destination and authority. Automatic substantive action is confined to appropriately authorised operational responses in the proposal; higher-level evidence routes to review. Low-confidence classifications degrade to advisory flags. A constitutional recurrence claim needs a plausible shared mechanism and legitimate approval; the deep dive's example of the same assumption strained across three repositories is a trigger to investigate, not sufficient causal proof.

A historical `route.py` specimen consumes detector events, claims and ledger records, appends a routing record and mechanically opens a review or proposed diff. It does not grant the router permission to amend rules or tool settings. Audits separately measure under-routing strategic evidence into local repairs and over-routing operational noise into strategic churn, because fixing one can worsen the other. Audit routing confidence, delay, suppressed harms and action consequences. Young ledgers can under-detect recurrence; absence of accumulated evidence is not evidence of no systemic problem.

#### Coupling, build order and mechanism withdrawal

The proposed dependency order is claims plus invalidation; observe-only anomaly capture; labelled-corpus diagnostic and advisory router; then more costly competition. Claims supply causal locus, the ledger supplies recurrence and context, evaluation supplies confidence, and decision rights supply authority. A corrupted claim registry can misroute everything downstream. Evidence-gated versions, source integrity, correction, protected challenge and bounded propagation are central protections, not decoration.

Every mechanism needs its own continuation and removal criteria. Remove or simplify a diagnostic that cannot improve on a cheaper comparator, a challenger whose decisions add no value, an unused record or invalidation authoring that remains vacuous. Mechanism failure should preserve useful negative findings and residual assets. The deep dive's rhetoric of automatic “death” should not override proportional diagnosis, rights or uncertainty; the governing question is whether the mechanism continues to justify its costs and consequences.

### Self-hosting research without self-certification

`F₀` proposes that the framework become the subject, method and provisional constitution of a research organisation while competing with alternatives. Versioned recursion is:

\[
F_{n+1}=G(F_n,E_{\mathrm{application}},E_{\mathrm{research\ process}},A_{\mathrm{anomalies}},C_{\mathrm{counterfactuals}}).
\]

`G` is an authorised constitutional gate: preserve the incumbent; state the change as a causal hypothesis; register benefits, failures and side effects before observations; test in a bounded sandbox; compare incumbent and rivals; test holdouts when feasible; approve, reject, defer, revise or roll back; retain evidence and dissent. Recursive capability does not create normative authority. Operational adaptations must not silently become general rules.

Each study separates **object hypothesis `H_O`**, **method hypothesis `H_M`** and **organisational hypothesis `H_C`**. For example, causal delegation may reduce low-value implementation; matched comparisons may be an informative way to test that; a separately contextualised challenger plus re-authorisation may improve the research process. These can fail independently. A useful domain finding does not prove the organisation caused its quality, and a negative object result can arise from a sound method.

Double-entry research preserves an account of the external object—claims changed, intervention implications and uncertainties—and an account of learning itself—frames, methods, context loss, roles, incentives, routing, stopping and transfer. Each programme produces a domain finding, an epistemic finding, an organisational finding and a transfer hypothesis. A useful retained lesson says what mechanism helped under which conditions, was inconclusive elsewhere and could fail under specified conditions; it does not become “always use this”. The strongest meta-learning test is improved **future** decision quality caused by retained learning, preferably on held-out work.

The source proposes a minimum comparative cycle with **three diverse live applications and one holdout**, comparing ordinary task execution, execution plus reflection and the full framework where feasible. These are candidate study design numbers, not executed results or a universal power justification. Capture both external application outcomes and organisational traces; have evaluators independent of mechanism creation; extract object, epistemic, organisational, normative and transfer findings; then trial one bounded successor change. The simplest defensible comparator must remain eligible to win.

A research portfolio can include foundational, empirical, applied, translational, enabling-infrastructure, constitutional and direct-intervention work. Appropriate evidence differs: explanatory fruitfulness for concepts, discrimination and calibration for empirical belief, usable faithful adoption for translation, reuse and avoided failures for infrastructure, future decisions and holdout performance for constitutional changes, and counterfactual external outcomes plus harms for intervention. Protect exploration without allowing indefinite inquiry to justify itself only by producing more research.

A standard impact-application record carries ten useful parts: what was learned; potential valuable change; actor/context/authority/resources; comparison of means; causal pathway; smallest bounded application experiment; evidence and counterfactual; harms, distribution and standing; what the application teaches about creating impact; and whether it warrants a method, role, memory, evidence or constitutional proposal. Possible uses should be explicit without forcing every finding into the same product pathway.

### Research programme, evaluation and falsification

| Proposed study | Comparators or method | Outcomes that would make the result useful |
|---|---|---|
| Causal delegation | Conventional brief; brief plus reflection; intended-effect and hypothesis contract | Preserved strategic choices, less low-value persistence, external quality and overhead |
| Metacontrol/stopping | Fixed review; event-triggered review; hybrid; ordinary/no formal review | Invalidation detection latency, false review, lock-in, missed opportunities and thrashing |
| Strategic memory | Ablate purpose, alternatives, assumptions, confidence, invalidation, provenance and affected-party information | Choice disagreement and error type versus fully informed reference, attention cost, transfer |
| Evidence routing | Local-only reporting; undifferentiated global escalation; structured routing | Correct level and authority, latency, overload, suppressed evidence, harm response and decision effects |
| Constitutional evolution | Frozen rules; reflection-led in-place changes; versioned trials with holdouts and rollback | Future decisions, recurring pathology, overhead, unintended effects and reversibility |
| Transfer | Compare domains, uncertainty, feedback delay, reversibility and human–AI arrangements | Heterogeneity, negative transfer, adaptation cost and scope conditions |

The research lattice crosses mechanism × domain × organisational level × timescale × feedback delay × reversibility × human–AI arrangement. Mechanisms include causal delegation, decision-sufficient compression, competition, bounded commitment, evidence routing, anomaly accumulation, calibrated updates, independent variation, positive re-authorisation, constitutional trials, protected normative feedback and research/application recursion. Novel combinations are candidates, not automatically valuable; contribution, information, option value, cost, harm and lock-in determine priority.

Evaluation must distinguish application, epistemic, decision, organisational, research, meta-learning and normative performance. Measure affected-group external outcomes and credible counterfactuals; discriminating prospective evidence and calibration; quality of alternatives, stopping and reversible commitment; intent retention, routing and correlated errors; explanatory/predictive/methodological contribution and transfer; future decisions caused by retained learning; and whether standing, rights and distribution survive optimisation pressure. A programme can improve one while worsening another.

Useful measure families include:

- **Intent retention:** choice agreement with a fully contextualised reference; decision loss under ablation; active parent-claim linkage; identification of invalid assumptions; meaningful causal explanations.
- **Stopping and options:** elapsed time/resources after invalidating evidence was available; reasoned continuation versus inertia; switching regret with uncertainty; commitments still reversible at review; residual asset value; false positive and false negative strategic reviews.
- **Evidence:** linked material claims and rivals; prospective prediction coverage; calibration where events are well-defined; discrimination between hypotheses; observation-to-authority latency; anomaly recurrence before aggregation; provenance completeness.
- **Challenge:** materially different intervention classes; independence of initial contexts; decisions or tests improved by challenge; correlated errors; minority hypotheses retained; costs relative to avoided error and information gained.
- **Contribution:** outcomes by affected group; counterfactual quality; evidence for each edge; adoption, fidelity and decay; direct/indirect harm; distribution of benefit and burden; confidence by causal depth.
- **Overhead:** decision latency; human review and agent compute; context, documentation and record-maintenance cost; records actually used later; comprehension; duplicated analysis; escalations that changed nothing; successful simplification and appropriate non-use.

Task completion, tickets, artefact count, code volume, tokens, proof length, time, plan adherence, consensus, uncalibrated confidence, local tests, adoption alone, number of reviews, constitutional change count, narrative coherence and citation volume are insufficient impact or framework-quality measures. Some remain useful local observations.

Candidate validation designs include matched cases, crossover with carryover accounted for, component ablations, holdouts, interrupted time series with external changes tracked, controlled synthetic stress cases, qualitative process tracing and adversarial attempts at proxy success, anomaly suppression, authority confusion and inflated impact narratives. None alone establishes the whole framework. For binary prospective forecasts, the source gives Brier score `mean((p_i - o_i)^2)`, but forecasts must define events, consider base rates, record timing and scope, and avoid confidence-as-authority. Decision quality also depends on information available when chosen: good choices can have bad outcomes and bad choices can be lucky. Counterfactual regret estimates must retain their uncertainty.

### A worked counterexample to product fixation

The framework's original hypothetical case concerns small community organisations allocating scarce support. A conventional team starts a predictive ranking dashboard, decomposes data ingestion, features, models, interface, tests and deployment, then spends increasing effort proving model stability. Local progress looks good.

The intended contribution is improved access, fairness and outcomes without unacceptable surveillance or exclusion. Affected parties include service users, frontline workers, organisations, historically underrepresented groups, funders/public institutions and people displaced by allocation choices. The hypothetical constraints preserve legitimate final allocation authority, privacy, avoidance of unsupported sensitive inference, contestability, unequal-error monitoring and human review.

## 11. Education standards in historical perspective

The education-history research is a curated, thresholded inventory, not a census of everything called a standard. Its verified 3 September 2026 baseline contains **78 counted families**, **nine deep-time analogues** and **24 non-core records** (22 boundary/emerging cases and two collapsed subcases). The 4 September addendum proposes a **25th non-core case**, the UK AI-tutoring benchmark/shared-standard programme with an adjacent education Petri artefact. It leaves all 78 counted families and the statistical series unchanged. A formal specification, a public norm, a legal instrument and an operational platform are not interchangeable evidence.

The history's coding differs deliberately from the strict eight-axis/five-gate evaluation in section 3. **A** means a formal open standard with a public specification, multiparty process and non-discriminatory independent implementation; **B** an openly implementable de facto convention; **C** a public norm/reference system; **D** a legal/governance instrument; **E** a boundary/emerging case. Hybrids are recorded rather than forced into one class. Impact codes are **T** transformative, **S** systemic, **Cq** consequential and **IB** important but bounded. Confidence **H/M/L** applies to the stated claim, not an institution's overall quality. Historical C/D cases can be educationally important without satisfying modern open governance.

The nine deep-time cases preserve the question's longer horizon while avoiding anachronism: Old Babylonian metrology/lexical and numeric teaching conventions; Phoenician-derived/Greek alphabetic conventions; Qin orthographic regularisation; Vedic oral error-control; Pāṇini's formal grammar; Hindu–Arabic positional notation; Guidonian music pedagogy; symbolic algebra notation; and the Cherokee syllabary. Clay, printing, a computer or an AI model is a substrate or technology rather than automatically a standard. Coercion, caste/gender restrictions, linguistic domination and closed institutions are part of this history. Modern open licensing cannot be projected backward.

Important date corrections survive in the inventory. Bello's proposal dates to 1823 but the counted school-officialisation event is 1844. Glover's Norwich sol-fa dates to 1835. Captioning experiments in 1972 must not be renamed EIA-608 in 1976. ECTS began in 1989 with 145 institutions and five subject areas; its 60-credit transfer convention is not evidence that later accumulation or learning outcomes were already in place. CSL's family date is 2006. Family identifiers F001–F082 contain gaps because adjudication removed or reclassified records; the 78 surviving rows are not an incomplete spreadsheet.

### What the observed waves support

The baseline counts by unequal era are **4, 5, 5, 10, 30, 17, 7**. Their exposures are **54, 65, 25, 20, 15, 10 and 11.68 years** (200.68 total), giving annualised rates approximately **0.074, 0.077, 0.200, 0.500, 2.000, 1.700 and 0.599**. Those adjacent rate ratios—1.04, 2.60, 2.50, 4.00, 0.85, 0.35—do not form an exponential acceleration. In fixed decades, the 1950s through 2010s contain **1, 4, 7, 3, 16, 20 and 18** families. **54 of 78 (69.2%)** lie in 1990–2019. The defensible finding is a broad digital-era concentration and later maintenance/implementation plateau within this curated corpus, with strong recent ascertainment limits.

Family collapse changes the apparent peak: the 1990s/2000s/2010s become **14/15/17**. The primary groupings keep distinct educational mechanisms such as ECTS, Bologna cycles and Diploma Supplement; sensitivity collapse tests that judgement. Removing three low-confidence recent candidates (CTDL, SADCQF and CASE) reduces the last era from seven to four. Changing what qualifies as “open” markedly changes the distribution: a pure A/B filter privileges digital and North Atlantic sources and excludes consequential public norms and legal instruments.

Four mechanisms prevent interpreting few counted 2020s families as proof of decline: impact takes time to establish; a recent implementation may be dated to an older ancestor family; profiles and revisions are collapsed; and measured activity is not automatically educationally consequential use. The sources explicitly declined to estimate global adoption and revision time series because comparable denominators and dates were unavailable. A planned adoption series A1 and revision series R1 are therefore research intentions, not completed outputs. No change-point date or exponential growth model was established.

The retained quantitative ledger distinguishes records, people, uses, institutions, files, credits, licences and educational outcomes. ### A usable future narrative without a progress myth

The original article brief proposed explaining educational standardisation as public interfaces: conventions that made thinking, teaching and recognition more reproducible; institutions that supported mass education; interoperable digital systems; and the present burden of implementation, maintenance, rights and evidence. | **U-107** | How will extensions be governed without fragmentation? | Local requirements and innovation are legitimate, but ungoverned extensions destroy portability. | Extension points, namespace rules, registration/discovery, compatibility policy and promotion/deprecation path. | Multiple incompatible extensions or jurisdictional adoption. | P1 |
| **U-108** | What is the standards-development participation model? | No account evidence establishes membership, contributors, voting rights or stakeholder representation. | Public governance map: steward, working groups, membership classes, IP/patent policy, decision procedure and accessible participation routes. | Before claiming influence or committing organisational resources. | P0 |
| **U-109** | Are the normative texts genuinely implementable on acceptable terms? | “Public” and “royalty-free to implement” do not settle derivative rights, patents, certification or test-suite access. | Legal/licence review for specification, schemas, examples, tests, trademarks and patents. | Licence or governance change. | P0 |
| **U-110** | Are at least two independent implementations feasible? | Single implementations can encode undocumented assumptions. | Interop event/test evidence across independently developed implementations. | Major version or profile change. | P1 |
| **U-111** | How will backwards compatibility, migration and deprecation work? | Several discovered families have material version transitions. | Compatibility matrix, dual-read/write policy, migration tooling, sunset dates and archive policy. | New major version or adopter unable to migrate. | P1 |
| **U-112** | How will semantic quality be assessed beyond formal validity? | Valid identifiers/mappings can still be pedagogically or conceptually wrong. | Domain review, provenance, confidence/disagreement representation, audit sampling and correction process. | Mapping disputes or downstream harm. | P0 |
| **U-113** | How will accessibility, language and jurisdictional plurality enter normative work? | Current discovery is English-source-biased and education authority is plural. | Represented stakeholders, multilingual artefacts, accessibility requirements and jurisdiction-specific profiles/mappings. | New jurisdiction/language adoption or evidence of exclusion. | P0 |
| **U-114** | What adoption evidence is sufficient? | Certification lists and named adopters rarely reveal active transactions, failure rates or costs. | Privacy-preserving operational metrics, integration cost, error/fallback rate, version distribution and adopter research. | Renewal, procurement or claimed network effect. | P1 |
| **U-115** | What IPR and patent policy governs any prospective standardisation effort? | The current corpus distinguishes licences but does not contain a complete IPR analysis for a new standard. | Contribution terms, patent disclosure/licensing policy, copyright ownership, trademark rules and legal review. | Before accepting external contributions or publishing normative text. | P0 |
| **U-116** | Should a useful open protocol be taken through a formal standards process? | Formalisation may improve legitimacy and interoperability but adds cost, delay and governance obligations; no blanket answer is warranted. | Stakeholder/adopter demand, independent implementations, stability evidence, steward capacity and venue analysis. | Interface reaches cross-organisational adoption or fragmentation appears. | P1 |


The general standards research also leaves nine bounded unknowns: no neutral global usage census; insufficient semantic/task measures; weak data on actual participation influence; post-2027 Data Act outcomes beyond the cut-off; uncertain production/value evidence for data spaces; no neutral S3 compatibility TCK or CDMI usage census; sparse educational effects; an unvalidated proposed scorecard; and changing versions/status. Refresh should follow a real selection, renewal, legal or adoption decision, not expand this reconstruction into a new global audit.

For national capability, the highest-value missing bridges are: a maintained public benchmark with exposed constructs and governance; prospective pupil-benefit evidence; a stable mandate and operation for beta knowledge services; genuinely independent implementations; a measured dependency substitution; and sustained owner/workforce/budget capacity. A technically coherent integration is a hypothesis about those bridges until each appropriate observation exists.

Its measurements retain the **3 September 2026 evidence cut-off**, even where a linked page now changes. Dates, source class and denominators qualify every adoption statement. ### 10. Foundational and physical standards

General standards show that the most important “data” questions predate computing: common units, names and physical interfaces make records comparable and systems composable.

#### CASE-UNICODE — Stability is a user feature

Unicode illustrates the value of explicit stability. Its standards and machine-readable data are public under permissive terms, and stability rules protect assigned code points so that stored text remains interpretable. UTF-8's compatibility with ASCII enabled incremental migration. W3Techs measured UTF-8 on [99.0% of websites with a known character encoding](https://w3techs.com/technologies/overview/character_encoding) on the research cut-off date. That is a web proxy, not proof of equal support for every script; it nevertheless demonstrates the scale possible when a universal model, migration path, stable identifiers and platform distribution align.

Stability can preserve mistakes. Unicode's own [emoji proposal guidance](https://unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html) notes that encoded emoji cannot simply be removed. A good stability policy therefore separates persistent identifiers from mutable annotations, documents known defects, permits non-breaking correction where possible and provides governance for historically excluded communities.

#### CASE-SI — SI: the text is only the visible tip of metrology

The International System of Units succeeds because a definition is backed by institutions capable of realizing and comparing it. The [SI Brochure](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure) is freely available under CC BY 4.0; the treaty-based BIPM/CGPM/CIPM system maintains definitions; and the [CIPM Mutual Recognition Arrangement](https://www.bipm.org/en/cipm-mra) uses inter-laboratory comparisons and publishes calibration and measurement capabilities. BIPM lists [69 Member States and 34 Associates](https://www.bipm.org/en/member-states), but those counts describe treaty participation rather than every downstream use.

The 2018 redefinition, effective in 2019, tied units to stable physical constants ([CGPM Resolution 1](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/cg/cgpm/26-2018/resolution-1)). This is a model of a consequential standard stack:

- stable public definitions;
- reference methods and scientific infrastructure;
- traceability chains;
- comparisons and published capabilities;
- legal and procurement incorporation;
- trained institutions and continuing review.

Free publication alone could not make two laboratories agree. Conversely, the United States still uses customary units extensively despite federal preference for SI and a globally mature infrastructure ([NIST metric policy](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/metric-policy)). Installed habits and transition cost survive even the strongest standard.

#### CASE-CONTAINERS — Freight containers: impact without strict openness

ISO container standards define dimensions, ratings, corner fittings, testing and markings so ships, cranes, railways, trucks and ports can compose around a shared physical interface. [ISO 668:2020](https://www.iso.org/standard/76912.html), however, is a paid copyrighted document and public participation is mediated through national members, so it does not pass the source research’s strict free-access gate.

Empirical work shows both the power and the cost of standardization. Rua's study finds fast country-level diffusion but slower firm adoption, shaped by expected network size, firm scale, income, U.S. trade and ability to bear fixed investment ([Federal Reserve FEDS 2014-88](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/diffusion-of-containerization.htm)). Research on trade effects links containerizability and adoption timing to substantial trade expansion, while warning against treating a single promotional percentage as universal ([Bernhofen, El-Sahli and Kneller, 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/inecon/v98y2016icp36-50.html)).

The causal lesson is that an interface can reorganize an industry only when complementary capital and operations change with it. The contrary lesson is equally important: a paywalled standard may diffuse when the economic prize is large, so free access is neither a sufficient condition for adoption nor historically necessary. It remains important for plural implementation, audit, low-cost entry and public legitimacy.

#### CASE-GS1 — GS1 barcodes: open rules around a paid coordination service

GS1 makes its standards downloadable and publishes a consensus, appeal, intellectual-property and review process in the [Global Standards Management Process Manual](https://ref.gs1.org/gs1/gsmp-manual/). The system delivers a globally coordinated identifier and scanning interface. Its claimed scale—millions of participating companies and billions of scans—comes from the steward and should be treated as such ([GS1 overview](https://www.gs1.org/about)). Basker’s 2012 scanner-productivity study, identified in the claim ledger below, provides stronger outcome evidence for its studied retailers.

Yet formal ballots are member-controlled and company prefixes or GTINs can carry issue and renewal fees ([GS1 US fees](https://www.gs1us.org/faqs/gs1-company-prefix-barcodes-and-indentification)). This is a useful hybrid: the syntax and business rules may be publicly documented, while uniqueness and trust depend on a funded allocation authority. A strict openness assessment must examine the registry business model, portability of identifiers and remedies if fees or rules change—not only the PDF licence.

#### General-case synthesis

| Case | Immediate value | Complementary mechanism | Openness caveat | Durable lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SI | Comparable measurement | Metrology institutes, comparisons, calibration, law | Treaty/national capacity is unequal | Reference infrastructure makes definitions operational |
| Containers | Intermodal handling and lower coordination cost | Ports, cranes, vessels, rail, process redesign | Core ISO text is paywalled | A small interface can unlock system-wide complements |
| GS1 | Unique product identity and automated scanning | Registry, retailers, suppliers, equipment, certification | Identifier fees and member voting | Shared identity and demand-side coordination matter as much as representation |
| Unicode/UTF-8 | One repertoire across languages and systems | OS, browser, language and protocol embedding | Voting and linguistic capacity remain unequal | Compatibility, stability and ubiquitous distribution can create near-universal reach |

### 11. Web, protocol and API exchange

#### CASE-HTTP-HTML — HTTP and HTML: evolve with deployed reality

HTTP's enduring core is a uniform, extensible interface with self-descriptive messages, registered methods/status codes/fields and media types; unknown status codes can fall back by class rather than breaking every old client ([RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html)). [RFC 9205](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9205.html) recommends runtime links, discovery and content negotiation rather than brittle assumptions about a fixed maximum protocol version.

HTML's design principles favoured existing content, defined error recovery, graceful degradation, solving observed practices and evolution rather than a clean break ([W3C HTML Design Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/html-design-principles/)). XHTML 2 pursued a less compatible redesign. In 2009 W3C let its working-group charter expire, stopped XHTML 2.0 Recommendation-track work and concentrated resources on HTML ([W3C XHTML FAQ](https://www.w3.org/2009/06/xhtml-faq.html)). The FAQ is an institutional account, not a neutral causal experiment, but the outcome aligns with the broader transition evidence: compatibility and implementer commitment beat architectural neatness.

The evolution from HTTP/1.1 to HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 shows how deployment channels shape standards. In the 2024 HTTP Archive corpus:

- 70–71% of homepages actually loaded over HTTP/2, 7–9% over HTTP/3 and 21–22% over HTTP/1.1;
- 85% of all observed requests used HTTP/2 or above;
- 26–28% of homepages advertised HTTP/3 support, higher than first-load use because clients must discover it;
- 54% of more than 1.3 billion requests came from CDNs, and roughly 85% of observed HTTP/3 responses came from CDNs.

These are public-web measurements, not the whole Internet, and first-load methodology undercounts HTTP/3 use. They nevertheless show two mechanisms: backward fallback accelerated HTTP/2, while HTTP/3's new transport and discovery path add friction; CDN defaults can move a standard across thousands of sites while concentrating operational capability ([Web Almanac 2024 HTTP chapter](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/http)).

#### CASE-JSON-XML — JSON and XML: different complexity budgets

[JSON](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8259.html) offers a small, language-independent, UTF-8-based representation that maps comfortably into mainstream programming structures. That lowered the cost of web APIs. Its simplicity has a boundary: duplicate object names, number-range differences and Unicode handling can produce inconsistent behaviour, while base JSON supplies no domain semantics or schema.

[XML 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/xml/) supports a richer document and namespace ecosystem and remains important in publishing, documents, enterprise messaging and regulated domains. The trade-off is a larger tool and security surface. A study of 500 public REST APIs from highly ranked sites found 63.2% offering JSON without XML and 5.6% XML without JSON ([study record](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/sc/2021/04/08385157/13rRUygBwfi)). The sample is dated, covers representations rather than traffic and does not establish that JSON is universally superior. It does demonstrate the pull of developer familiarity and low-friction integration.

The transferable lesson is not “always use JSON.” It is to minimize accidental complexity for the target adopters and publish the missing semantics, validation, security and version rules elsewhere in the stack.

#### CASE-OPENAPI — OpenAPI: immediate tool output drives use

The [OpenAPI Specification](https://spec.openapis.org/oas/latest.html) describes HTTP APIs independently of programming language, under vendor-neutral Linux Foundation governance after the Swagger specification was donated; the specification is Apache-2.0 licensed ([OpenAPI Initiative](https://www.openapis.org/about)). Its attraction is immediate and distributed: one description can feed documentation, client/server generation, mocks, gateways, validation and tests.

The 2024 APIstic study collected 1,275,568 valid OpenAPI artefacts representing 856,259 distinct specifications across public sources; its GitHub subset represented roughly 269,000 APIs from more than 92,000 owners ([APIstic paper](https://souhaila-serbout.me/pdfs/MSR2024-Serbout-Pautasso-APIstic.pdf)). Those are repository artefacts, not verified production APIs. The corpus also contained invalid or sketch-like material and version/tool limitations. This is strong evidence of authoring activity and ecosystem reach, but only an intermediate adoption rung.

OpenAPI also illustrates a boundary: describing an endpoint does not create stable semantics, authorization policy, service-level commitments, business workflow or backward compatibility. Reusable domain schemas and lint rules are needed to prevent every API from using familiar syntax for incompatible concepts.

#### CASE-GTFS — GTFS: a small file format attached to a valuable consumer

GTFS Schedule uses a set of CSV files, stable identifiers and defined relationships; GTFS-Realtime adds live updates. Its original attraction was concrete: transit agencies could make routes visible in trip planners without inventing a bespoke integration. The [reference](https://github.com/google/transit/blob/master/gtfs/spec/en/reference.md), public validators, examples and a low-tech file publication path made entry feasible.

The community steward reports more than 10,000 agencies across over 100 countries ([GTFS value overview](https://gtfs.org/getting-started/why-use-gtfs/)); the independently operated [Mobility Database](https://mobilitydatabase.org/) catalogues more than 6,000 GTFS-family feeds across at least 99 countries at the research cut-off. Neither count proves freshness, consumption or traveller outcomes. Research on early U.S. adoption links organizational and contextual factors to uptake ([Findings Press study](https://findingspress.org/article/57722-predictors-of-early-adoption-of-the-general-transit-feed-specification)).

GTFS succeeded through a reinforcing bundle: narrow problem, familiar files, stable URLs, validator, immediate distribution in consumer applications and an extension process. Its governance remains less easy than its syntax: proposal discussion can be scattered and intimidating, and Google's consumer profile can differ from the community specification. A dominant downstream consumer can become a practical governor even when the specification process is open.

#### CASE-FHIR — FHIR: regulation and profiles create reach, not universal workflow interoperation

FHIR decomposes health information into modular “resources,” combines a formal information model with web APIs and supports implementation guides and profiles ([FHIR overview](https://hl7.org/fhir/overview.html)). It improved developer accessibility relative to earlier messaging while reusing clinical terminologies and security protocols. Government-defined cores, certification and public tests created coordinated demand.

The strongest current adoption evidence is bounded, not global. In a survey of U.S. non-federal acute-care hospitals, 71% reported a standards-based API such as FHIR for patient access in 2024, while about 90% offered some API. For at least one clinical third-party use case, 52% reported standards-based API integration and 39% provision; for at least one administrative use case the figures were 35% and 32%. Across individual clinical and administrative workflows, proprietary APIs or non-API methods remained more common than standards-based APIs alone ([ONC Data Brief 81, February 2026](https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-use-of-apis-to-enable-data-sharing-between-ehrs-and-third-party-technology/)).

The causal package includes statutory API requirements, certification, the US Core profile, mature EHR vendors, app demand and [Inferno conformance tests](https://inferno.healthit.gov/). The limits are equally instructive: capability is not actual use; optionality and profile proliferation can break cross-context exchange; terminology mapping and workflow equivalence remain difficult; smaller independent hospitals lag larger system-affiliated ones; and FHIR coexists with older HL7 interfaces rather than replacing them cleanly.

#### CASE-P3P-DNT — P3P and Do Not Track: an open signal cannot settle receiving policy

P3P provided a machine-readable way for sites to describe privacy practices but could not verify that a declared policy was accurate ([P3P 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/P3P/)). Work on P3P 1.1 ended without sufficient implementer support ([P3P 1.1 note](https://www.w3.org/TR/P3P11/)). Do Not Track specified how a user agent could express a tracking preference, but the central question—what recipients were obliged to do—depended on contested business incentives, law and enforcement. W3C closed the working group and retired the specifications in January 2019 ([group archive](https://www.w3.org/2011/tracking-protection/)).

These cases do not show that privacy standards are impossible. They show that a syntactically interoperable preference is insufficient when receivers lose revenue or dispute its meaning. A viable standard needs an enforceable institutional settlement, auditability, clear defaults and remedies in addition to the wire signal.

### 12. Storage, query and analytical representations

#### Storage standards cover different interfaces

“Open storage standard” may refer to:

- a logical data model and query language;
- a persistent file format;
- an in-memory representation;
- an object or file service API;
- archival packaging, checksums and preservation metadata;
- a portability or switching contract.

Comparing SQL, Parquet and S3 as if they were alternatives obscures their roles. They can coexist in one system and fail at different boundaries.

#### CASE-SQL — SQL: ubiquitous common ground with durable dialects

ISO/IEC 9075 defines SQL, and a stable relational/query core has enabled decades of tooling and skills. Yet the normative ISO text is paid, features are optional, vendors add extensions and legacy behaviour is costly to remove. PostgreSQL 18 reports support for at least 170 of the 177 mandatory SQL:2023 Core features while noting that no current DBMS claims full Core conformance and that ostensibly conforming syntax or functions may differ ([PostgreSQL conformance table](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/features.html); [ISO framework record](https://www.iso.org/standard/76583.html)).

SQL is thus both a major success and a warning. A common conceptual language can lower switching cost without making queries, types, procedures, administrative APIs or performance characteristics portable. Capability profiles, migration tests and dialect inventories are more honest than a binary “supports SQL” claim.

#### CASE-PARQUET-ARROW — Parquet and Arrow: solve adjacent concrete costs

[Apache Parquet](https://parquet.apache.org/docs/overview/) standardizes persistent columnar layout for efficient analytical storage and scans. [Apache Arrow](https://arrow.apache.org/overview/) standardizes a language-neutral in-memory columnar representation to reduce serialization and copying between systems. Both pair public formats with open implementations, multiple languages, libraries and integration tests.

Their complementary scope is a strength: neither claims to be a domain ontology or universal database. Their ecosystem can still fragment through optional encodings and uneven feature support, as the DuckDB analysis demonstrates. A production profile should name writer version, encodings, logical types, compression, nested-data expectations and the reader matrix actually tested.

HDF5 offers a related scientific case: a mature open hierarchical format and library with broad research and industrial use. The steward reports hundreds of public projects, but no neutral census compares it to Parquet or Arrow ([HDF5 overview](https://www.hdfgroup.org/solutions/hdf5/)). Format choice should follow workload, longevity and implementation diversity, not popularity folklore.

#### CASE-S3-CDMI — S3-compatible versus CDMI: ecosystem gravity can beat formal openness

Amazon S3's proprietary-origin API became a de facto object-storage interface because developers, vendors and tools could reuse familiar operations, authentication patterns and SDKs. Competing services deliberately implement parts of it. But “S3-compatible” is not a governed conformance level: [Cloudflare R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/api/) publishes supported, experimental and absent operations, and [Google Cloud Storage](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/interoperability) describes interoperability with some S3 tools through its XML API. Portability depends on the common subset plus behaviour outside the happy path.

The [Cloud Data Management Interface](https://www.snia.org/cdmi) is a vendor-neutral SNIA/ISO standard covering richer discovery, namespace, lifecycle, access and portability concerns. Its specification is a stronger formal-open proposition, but public claims of wide use lack a comparable independent adoption census. It would be unsound to infer zero use; it is equally unsound to equate formal status with the S3 ecosystem's observable gravity.

The comparison yields four requirements for de facto compatibility claims:

1. a versioned, vendor-neutral profile of the supported subset;
2. independent tests covering semantics, errors, consistency and edge cases—not only method names;
3. published capability discovery and deviations;
4. governance that can evolve independently of the incumbent being copied.

#### Open storage does not by itself create sovereignty

A portable file may still be trapped by egress cost, volume, encryption-key control, proprietary identity, serverless code, orchestration, event history or unavailable receiving features. Conversely, data may stay physically in one jurisdiction while a foreign provider controls administration, keys or compelled access.

An exit-ready storage standard must address at least:

- complete export scope, including metadata, ACLs, identifiers, versions, logs and provenance;
- documented consistency, ordering and deletion semantics;
- bulk and incremental export/import, with checksums and resumability;
- interoperable encryption and customer-held key options;
- machine-readable dependency and configuration manifests;
- receiving-side tests and functional gap reports;
- predictable egress, assistance, transition time and secure erasure;
- jurisdiction, audit, subcontractors and conflicting-law procedures.

No file or API format settles all of these; the complete requirements are in the sovereignty analysis.

### 13. Data definition, schemas and validation

Data definition is the contract between raw representation and shared meaning. A complete definition states not only field names and primitive types, but identity, cardinality, units, code systems, null and unknown semantics, temporal meaning, relationships, constraints, provenance and version behaviour.

| Family | Primary contribution | Strength | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| SQL DDL and relational schemas | Tables, columns, types, keys and constraints | Mature tooling and enforceable structural integrity | Dialects, optional features and domain semantics limit portability |
| JSON plus JSON Schema | Familiar object/array representation with machine-checkable structure | Low entry cost and broad developer tooling | Base JSON has no shared domain meaning; processor/profile differences still matter |
| XML plus XML Schema | Namespaced document structures, types and validation | Rich document modelling and mature regulated-domain tooling | Larger complexity and security surface; schema validity is not workflow agreement |
| FHIR resources and profiles | Domain resources, cardinalities, terminology bindings and exchange rules | Connects data definition to executable healthcare APIs and tests | Profiles and value-set choices can fragment; capability is not task outcome |
| RDF/OWL/SKOS plus SHACL | Shared identifiers, graph semantics, concept schemes, inference and validation | Strong linking and explicit semantics across domains | Social agreement, authority and operational constraints remain external |

The components are complementary. A serialization answers “how is it written?”; a schema answers “what shape is valid?”; a vocabulary or ontology answers “what does it mean?”; a profile answers “which choices apply here?”; a validator answers “which declared constraints hold?”; and a workflow test asks whether the receiving system can do the intended job. [JSON](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8259.html), [XML](https://www.w3.org/TR/xml/), [FHIR](https://hl7.org/fhir/overview.html) and [SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/) occupy different positions in that chain.

A transferable data-definition package should therefore publish:

- stable identifiers for concepts, fields, code lists and profiles;
- machine-readable schemas plus human-readable intent and examples;
- explicit units, time zones, locale, precision and missing-value rules;
- controlled vocabularies and mapping provenance;
- validation severity and deterministic error behaviour;
- version, compatibility, deprecation and migration rules;
- representative valid, invalid, boundary and historical fixtures;
- a statement of what structural validation cannot prove about truth, quality, authorization or task success.

The design test is not whether two systems can parse the same file. It is whether they preserve the distinctions on which decisions depend.

### 14. Knowledge representation, semantics and provenance

#### CASE-RDF-STACK — The knowledge stack is modular by design

The W3C knowledge standards divide responsibilities rather than defining one universal “semantic web format.”

| Standard | Primary role | What implementers must add |
|---|---|---|
| [RDF 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/) | Graph data model using IRIs, literals and triples | Community agreement on identifiers and vocabularies |
| [OWL 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-overview/) | Formal ontologies and machine inference | Domain modelling, tractable profiles, operational validation |
| [SPARQL 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/sparql11-overview/) | Graph query, update and protocol family | Endpoint policy, performance, federation and access controls |
| [SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/) | Lightweight concept schemes such as thesauri and taxonomies | Stewardship of concepts, mappings and labels |
| [JSON-LD 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/json-ld11/) | RDF-compatible linked data in familiar JSON structures | Context governance and predictable processor profiles |
| [SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/) | Validation of RDF graphs against shapes | Agreed shapes, severity policy and remediation |
| [DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/) | Catalogue metadata for datasets and data services | Application profiles, controlled vocabularies and catalogue operations |
| [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) | Interchange of provenance entities, activities and agents | Truthful capture, trust, granularity and retention policy |

Modularity permits incremental adoption: a community can begin with SKOS and stable identifiers without taking on OWL reasoning; JSON-oriented developers can add JSON-LD context; a catalogue can use DCAT without converting all underlying data to RDF. It also means “we use RDF” proves very little about shared meaning or quality.

RDF explicitly relies on social agreement for what many IRIs denote. OWL's open-world reasoning does not treat an unstated fact as false, while operational validation often needs closed constraints; SHACL serves that different purpose. PROV-O can structure a provenance claim but cannot guarantee that the claim is honest or complete. These are valuable, deliberately bounded tools—not epistemic magic.

At the cut-off date, [RDF 1.2 Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/) was a Candidate Recommendation Snapshot dated 7 April 2026, while RDF 1.1 remained the completed Recommendation. Procurement and implementation claims should name that maturity difference rather than simply saying “RDF.”

#### CASE-SCHEMAORG — schema.org: demand-side reward creates web-scale semantics

schema.org offers a comparatively lightweight vocabulary that can be embedded through JSON-LD, Microdata or RDFa. Search engines give publishers a visible reward: correctly structured content can become eligible for enhanced search presentation ([Google's structured-data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)). This aligns the cost of markup with a benefit to the publisher.

The independent Web Data Commons series, extracted from Common Crawl, reports schema.org annotations on 400,000 pay-level domains in November 2013 and 12,272,796 in December 2024, with 136.8 billion extracted quads in the latter release ([longitudinal dataset](https://webdatacommons.org/structureddata/schemaorg/)). Corpus composition, crawl coverage and extraction methodology change, so this is evidence of enormous reach and long-run growth—not a clean adoption rate or proof of correct markup.

schema.org's new [public usage statistics](https://schema.org/docs/usage_stats.html) use Google's crawl, aggregate by domain, combine serializations and report count ranges rather than exact values; the publisher explicitly warns that no crawl represents the whole web. This transparency is exemplary: it makes the denominator and measurement limitations part of the standard's governance.

The success mechanism is a bundle:

- a bounded, reusable vocabulary rather than a demand that every publisher build an ontology;
- syntaxes that fit ordinary web publishing;
- validators and extensive examples;
- strong downstream consumer demand;
- public proposals and visible releases.

The governance caveat is concentrated consumer power. Founding search companies sit on the steering group and decide which terms produce search features. Community contribution can be open while practical value and release authority remain asymmetric.

#### Why generic semantic standards often feel difficult

The difficulty is less the triple model than the cost of reaching and maintaining agreement about meaning. High-friction tasks include:

- choosing persistent identifiers and a resolver policy;
- deciding whether two similar concepts are identical, broader, narrower or context-specific;
- mapping legacy codes without silently losing distinctions;
- governing multilingual labels and culturally contested categories;
- deciding version and deprecation semantics;
- separating inference from validation;
- managing provenance, licensing, privacy and access at statement or dataset level;
- creating a consumer that rewards the producer for better semantics.

A good knowledge standard therefore begins with competency questions—what systems and people must be able to ask or do—then publishes a small vocabulary, examples from real data, machine-readable constraints, mappings, governance of identifiers and a reference consumer. Expressive power without a valuable query or workflow is rarely enough.

#### CASE-FAIR-CARE — FAIR, CARE and executable standards

FAIR's broad principles succeeded partly because they did not mandate one technology. That makes them a common direction for repositories and funders, but assessments can be incomparable unless a community defines metrics and profiles. FAIR calls for identifiers, rich metadata, standardized protocols, formal shared knowledge representations, qualified links, licences, provenance and relevant community standards ([FAIR paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618)). It should guide architecture, not be misrepresented as a conformance-ready wire protocol.

The [CARE Principles](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043)—Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility and Ethics—correct a blind spot in purely technical reuse. For Indigenous data, the affected peoples' right to determine governance and benefit can constrain reuse that a generic “open by default” policy would permit. CARE is not anti-interoperability; it asks whose purposes an interoperable system serves and who can say no.

Together they suggest a more complete standard: FAIR for machine actionability; CARE for people, power and purpose; executable profiles for conformance; and law/contracts for enforceability.

### 15. Sovereignty law and emerging mechanisms

The following cases test whether law and emerging technical mechanisms produce usable control, transfer and substitution rather than merely declaring them.

#### CASE-GDPR — GDPR portability reveals the receiving-side gap

GDPR Article 20 gives qualifying data subjects a right to receive data they provided in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and to transmit it directly where technically feasible. Recital 68 encourages interoperable formats but does not require controllers to maintain technically compatible systems ([official GDPR text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)).

A longitudinal study of 129 corporations found that only 16% provided compliant exports in all three observed years, with export scope and import options stagnating over 2020–2022 ([ACSAC 2024 study](https://www.acsac.org/2024/program/final/s53.html)). Its sample and compliance coding are bounded; it nevertheless demonstrates the structural problem. A legal export right without recipient import, shared identifiers, semantic mappings, complete scope and service-state portability produces a download, not effective switching.

#### CASE-DATA-ACT — The EU Data Act makes interoperability more operational

The EU Data Act has applied since 12 September 2025; switching charges are scheduled to be abolished from 12 January 2027. Its recitals identify missing incentives, legal uncertainty, interface and contracting cost, silos, poor metadata, absent semantic/technical standards, bottlenecks and contractual imbalance as barriers. The Act combines rights and duties with machine-readable formats, metadata/context, interfaces, cloud-switching assistance and data-space interoperability requirements ([Regulation (EU) 2023/2854](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj/eng)).

Article 33's data-space requirements cover machine-readable descriptions of content, restrictions, licences, collection methods, quality and uncertainty; consistent structures, formats and vocabularies; and documented APIs that can support automatic, continuous, bulk or real-time access where applicable. The Act also addresses safeguards against conflicting foreign-government access.

This is powerful evidence about requirements, not yet mature outcome evidence. As of the cut-off, the switching-charge abolition was future, and implementation experience was too young to claim that the law had produced competitive cloud substitution. The Act itself acknowledges that earlier market-led cloud-standard coordination had limited adoption. Law can change incentives and disclosure; it cannot make two services semantically or functionally equivalent by declaration.

#### CASE-SOVEREIGNTY-MECHANISMS — Emerging mechanisms solve different slices

- **Eclipse Dataspace Protocol (DSP).** The [2025-1 specification](https://eclipse-dataspace-protocol-base.github.io/DataspaceProtocol/2025-1/) is Apache-2.0 licensed and defines catalogue exchange, agreement negotiation and initiation of data transfer using web technologies. Its test-compatibility kit and multiple connector implementations make it more than a paper architecture; an IDSA inventory reported eight fully tested connectors and one partial implementation ([IDSA Connector Report, 2026](https://internationaldataspaces.org/idsa-data-space-connector-report/)). That proves control-plane implementation, not production transaction scale, legal enforceability or semantic alignment.
- **Gaia-X.** Its architecture focuses on decentralized identity, trust, attestations and assurance and is complementary to data-exchange protocols ([Gaia-X context](https://gaia-x.gitlab.io/technical-committee/architecture-working-group/architecture-document/gaia-x_context/)). Independent OECD review found low awareness and missing usage indicators in Germany, so claims of ecosystem impact should remain cautious ([OECD review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/06/oecd-artificial-intelligence-review-of-germany_c1c35ccf/full-report/component-8.html)).
- **Solid / Linked Web Storage.** Solid separates applications, identity, authorization and personal data stores; its 2024 protocol is a Community Group Report rather than a completed W3C Recommendation ([Solid Protocol](https://solidproject.org/TR/2024/protocol-20240512)). The [W3C Linked Web Storage Working Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/lws/) is pursuing standards-track work. The architecture targets substitutability, but mass adoption and sustainable operator economics remain unproven.
- **Data Transfer Project (DTP).** DTP uses shared models and service adapters over incumbent APIs for direct transfers. The steward reports more than a dozen connected services and a reciprocal Google Photos/iCloud Photos transfer path ([DTP overview](https://dtinit.org/docs/dtp-what-is-it); [2024 transfer announcement](https://dtinit.org/blog/2024/07/10/DTI-members-new-photo-video-tool)). It demonstrates real user-facing portability, while depending on platform cooperation, adapter maintenance and copying rather than portable live service state.

These should not be scored as competitors for one job. DSP standardizes negotiation and transfer initiation; Gaia-X supplies trust and assurance; Solid targets decoupled personal storage; DTP supplies practical service adapters. A complete sovereignty architecture may compose several mechanisms with domain semantics, law and operating institutions.

### 16. Education as a validation case

#### CASE-EDUCATION — The interoperability package matters more than the schema

Education is not the report's focus, but its interoperability ecosystem confirms the cross-domain findings.

- [Learning Tools Interoperability (LTI)](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/lti) combines familiar security technologies—OIDC, OAuth 2 and JWT—with launch, grade, roster and deep-linking services. Certification and named profiles reduce uncertainty, but optional services mean “supports LTI” is not a complete capability statement.
- [OneRoster](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/oneroster) offers both CSV and REST representations for rosters, courses and grades. The CSV route lowers the first-adoption barrier; the API enables automation. Version and profile naming remain essential.
- [CASE](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/case) uses stable identifiers and associations for competencies and academic standards. It demonstrates that identifiers and relationships can unlock alignment, while global adoption evidence remains thin.
- [Ed-Fi](https://www.ed-fi.org/) combines an open education data model and APIs with reference technology, implementation guidance, a community and vendor credentials. The steward reports roughly 8 million students, 41 badged/certified vendors and presence in 26% of U.S. states; these are first-party ecosystem counts, not independent transaction or outcome measures. Its [data-standard documentation](https://docs.ed-fi.org/reference/data-exchange/data-standards/) and migration guidance also show the real cost of overlapping versions and breaking changes.
- xAPI/cmi5, Open Badges, Comprehensive Learner Record, QTI and related specifications address different layers—events, packaging, credentials and assessment. Treating them as a single “learner data standard” hides important differences in semantics, privacy, issuer trust and conformance.

The transferable lesson is that a schema does not create interoperability. Interoperability comes from the package: stable identifiers, security profiles, implementation guides, validators, reference software, certification/procurement language, migration support and a consumer or workflow that makes exchange worthwhile. That package does not by itself establish educational quality. Pedagogical and curriculum validity, teacher and learner experience, implementation fidelity and learning outcomes require separate evidence. Education also makes rights especially salient: children, families, institutions and vendors can have conflicting authority, so “open” cannot mean unrestricted learner data.

### Coding key

- **A — formal open standard:** public specification, multiparty process and non-discriminatory independent implementation.
- **B — openly implementable de facto standard:** stable public convention independently implementable without a formal standards body.
- **C — public normative/reference standard:** a public classification, notation, curriculum, proficiency, credit, qualification or accessibility framework. Public availability is recorded separately from participatory governance and reuse rights.
- **D — open legal/governance mechanism:** a reusable licence, rights instrument, treaty or policy definition that operates as shared coordination infrastructure.
- **E — boundary/counterexample:** consequential, but it fails a material openness or standard-character test, or is too new for demonstrated impact.

Impact tiers are **T** transformative, **S** systemic, **Cq** consequential, and **IB** important but bounded. Evidence confidence is **H**, **M** or **L** for the *impact claim*, not merely for existence.

The counted year is the first public normative or operationally interoperable release. A later adoption take-off is not silently backdated. Composite rows are used where later layers are not meaningfully separable from their lineage.

### Enabling technologies deliberately not counted as standards

Clay and wax tablets, styli, slate and chalk, pens and ink, paper, the blackboard, printing presses, radio receivers, computers and smartphones can transform education without being standards. They enter the history only when a **bounded convention or interface**—for example a character repertoire, paper-size norm, caption channel or communications protocol—has its own education mechanism and impact evidence. General-purpose standards such as postal addressing, TCP/IP, Wi-Fi, USB, electrical safety and broadcast modulation are treated as cross-sector enabling infrastructure unless an education-specific standardisation layer clears the impact threshold. This keeps the denominator finite and prevents every technology used in a classroom from becoming an “education standard.”

### Inventory limitations

This is a systematic high-impact inventory, not proof that an unknowable universe has been exhausted. Older, non-English, oral, Indigenous and proprietary histories are harder to discover; recent digital bodies publish far more version metadata. National curricula and orthographies are collapsed into families, while genuinely distinct mechanisms remain separate. These choices are tested in the wave appendix. Rows with low causal-outcome evidence are retained when structural educational impact is demonstrable, but the distinction is explicit.

These decisions materially affect the wave. Counting Washington Accord, NIMAS, LRMI and broad education-rights instruments separately would raise the denominator from 78 to 82 and exaggerate several bins. The final inventory chose conceptual consistency over a larger number.


### Remaining research gaps and bias register


#### 8. Remaining gaps and unresolved questions

The most important unresolved areas are:

1. **Non-English and archival discovery.** Arabic, Chinese, French, Portuguese, Russian and Indigenous-language histories could add or reclassify cases, especially before 1990.
2. **Modern South Asian and MENA families.** The inventory’s thinness may reflect searchability and family collapsing rather than absence.
3. **Real interoperability.** Many ecosystems publish certification or connection counts but not end-to-end transaction success, semantic loss or switching cost.
4. **Learner-level outcomes.** Most evidence concerns structural reach. Causal learning, equity, workload and agency outcomes are scarce and context-dependent.
5. **Costs.** Implementation, certification, migration, accessibility remediation and participation costs are rarely reported comparably.
6. **Governance participation.** Public specifications do not routinely disclose who proposed changes, whose language or disability needs were rejected, or whether small institutions can participate.
7. **Longitudinal adoption.** There is no common time series across countries, products, records, files, network events and qualifications.
8. **Recent work.** The absence of a 2020s-born mature family is structurally right-censored; future evidence could admit VC/ELM, AI-literacy, provenance or education-data-space lineages.
9. **Failed and superseded local standards.** Survivorship makes them harder to discover than durable global systems, biasing lessons toward winners.
10. **Licensing drift.** Some standards changed availability, rights or governance over time; origin and present status require separate classification.

#### 9. Bias register

- **Terminology bias:** modern search terms erase public conventions that were never called standards.
- **Digitisation bias:** later standards leave versioned, indexed web traces.
- **Survivorship bias:** successful standards and institutions preserve their histories.
- **Steward bias:** bodies count releases, members and certificates more readily than failures or outcomes.
- **Anglophone/Global North bias:** English technical standards are much easier to verify.
- **Technology bias:** schemas and APIs are more enumerable than oral, curricular and legal coordination.
- **Granularity bias:** a technical architecture can be split into components while all national curricula are collapsed into one family.
- **Compulsion/choice ambiguity:** a public mandatory norm can be impactful without an open ecosystem.
- **Availability/openness ambiguity:** free-to-read, reusable, implementable and openly governed are different properties.
- **Right-censoring:** recent initiatives have not had the time required by the impact threshold.
- **Metric substitution:** adoption, conformance, connection, transactions, people and benefits are not interchangeable.

#### 6. Boundary-source decisions

Several famous cases were verified and then excluded from the 78-family denominator:

- [Dewey Decimal Classification](https://www.oclc.org/en/dewey.html), ISBN/ISSN and DOI are consequential identifiers/classifications, but authoritative use or minting is centrally gated, paid or rights-restricted.
- [PISA](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa.html) has vast policy impact, but confidential items and non-independent implementation fail the study’s open-standard test.
- [Common Core’s public licence](https://thecorestandards.org/public-license/) is purpose-limited and non-sublicensable; public text is not equivalent to an open standard.
- [Universal Design for Learning](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) is highly influential, but reuse and steward-control evidence did not clear the operational Class C threshold used here.
- [Washington Accord](https://www.internationalengineeringalliance.org/accords/washington-accord) has 25 full signatories, but authoritative recognition is controlled by an accreditation club and is not independently implementable.
- International education-rights instruments remain essential normative context, but broad rights principles do not themselves supply the bounded interface, identifier or repeatable implementation procedure required here. [UNESCO Convention](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/convention-against-discrimination-education)
- [ISO 21001](https://www.iso.org/standard/66266.html) is education-specific but its normative text is paid, and certification would not establish educational benefit.
- [Verifiable Credentials 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/) and the [European Learning Model](https://europass.europa.eu/en/european-learning-model-stakeholders) are technically important but too new for distinctive, independently evidenced education impact beyond older credential lineages.


### Primary series, fixed decades and sensitivity tests


#### 2. Primary era-bin series

| Predefined period | Exposure (years) | Qualifying family births | Families/year | Annualised per decade |

| **Total** | **200.68** | **78** | — | — |

The rate rose about 27-fold from the first period (0.074/year) to 1990–2004 (2.000/year), but adjacent rate ratios are approximately **1.04, 2.60, 2.50, 4.00, 0.85 and 0.35**. A constant exponential multiplier is absent: the observed series is neither monotonic nor generated under stable observation conditions.

The 1990–2004 rate is only 1.18 times the 2005–2014 rate. Under a deliberately simple Poisson-count diagnostic, its approximate 95% rate-ratio interval is **0.65–2.13**. This is shown only to demonstrate that the small difference is not distinctive even under a conventional event-count model. It is **not** a population-confidence interval: the inventory is curated rather than sampled, events are dependent, and discovery/classification uncertainty dominates Poisson noise.

The 54 families born from 1990 through 2019 make up **69.2%** of the inventory. That concentration establishes recency in the recorded corpus; it does not establish exponential growth.

#### 3. Fixed-decade check

Unequal era bins can manufacture a dramatic-looking peak. Equal ten-year bins show a flatter digital-era crest:

| Calendar interval | Family births | Rate/year | Comment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1950s | 1 | 0.1 | Pinyin; several major post-war systems begin in the 1960s. |
| 1960s | 4 | 0.4 | ALGOL, cataloguing rules, BASIC and MARC. |
| 1970s | 7 | 0.7 | Orthography, captioning, recognition, statistics and TeX. |
| 1980s | 3 | 0.3 | Z39.50, ECTS and free-software licensing begin late in the decade. |
| 1990s | 16 | 1.6 | Web, Unicode, digital documents, metadata, accessibility and early education exchange. |
| 2000s | 20 | 2.0 | LMS/content/assessment, federated identity, open access/OER, records and regional frameworks. |

The 2000s/2010s rate ratio is **1.11**; a toy Poisson interval is approximately **0.59–2.10**. These rates are indistinguishable under that model. More importantly, the modelling assumptions are false in known ways. The safe reading is a high plateau extending across roughly three decades.

| Count specification | 1826–79 | 1880–1944 | 1945–69 | 1970–89 | 1990–2004 | 2005–14 | 2015–cut-off | What changes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

The era-bin maximum is stable under openness and impact thresholding. It is **not** stable to the joint family-lineage and time-bin test. Applying the aggressive collapse to fixed decades yields **14 in the 1990s, 15 in the 2000s and 17 in the 2010s**. That reversal is enough to reject a singular-peak claim.

The A/B filter is geographically and conceptually non-neutral. It removes most curriculum, qualification, proficiency, language and legal mechanisms—including many Global South cases—while retaining North Atlantic technical standards bodies with searchable English specifications. It is a stress test, not a preferred history.

No formal change-point model is fitted. There are too few bins, the bins were interpretive rather than generated by a stable observation process, and the unit definition changes what counts as an event. A precise breakpoint would therefore be spurious.

#### 5. Why a 2020s “trough” is not established

There are **zero** new 2020–cut-off rows that already clear the core impact threshold. That is an observation about this inventory, not evidence that standards work stopped.

Four mechanisms depress the recent birth count:

1. **Impact right-censoring.** A 2023 standard has had three years to accumulate adoption and independent impact evidence; a 1998 standard has had 28. The core entry rule therefore rejects recent work asymmetrically.
2. **Ancestor dating.** Open Badges 3/CLR 2, QTI 3, WCAG 2.2, EPUB 3.3, CASE 1.1 and OneRoster 1.2 are dated to their families’ first releases, not to the 2020s.
3. **Profile collapsing.** The 2019 Global Recognition Convention, 2021 CLR, 2022 revised Arab convention, 2025 Verifiable Credentials 2.0 and recent national/Indigenous profiles do not each create a new family.
4. **Impact versus activity.** Committee work, publication, certification and connection are visible immediately; successful exchange, recognition and educational benefit emerge later, if at all.

The observed 2015–cut-off birth rate is 0.60/year, versus 2.00/year in 1990–2004. A toy Poisson comparison gives a rate ratio of **3.34** (approximate interval **1.47–7.60**). Even though this descriptive contrast is large, it cannot identify a population decline: the most recent period fails exchangeability precisely because of maturity filtering and changing documentation conditions.

Use **“fewer newly impact-qualified families after 2015”** or **“a provisional post-build-out decline in family births.”** Do not use “current trough,” “standards are declining,” or “the innovation cycle has bottomed.”


### Reconstructing or updating the history

The unit is a family, dated once at its first public normative or operationally interoperable release; a standard's latest revision must not reset its birth date. Preserve a stable family ID, exact identity, release/adoption event, jurisdiction, class, mechanism, source-supported consequence, confidence, exclusions, counting decision and reasons. New evidence may revise a date, confidence or inclusion without rewriting why the older decision was made. Distinguish modern revisions, local profiles and adopters from new families; test consequential grouping decisions with a sensitivity series.

Before adding a case, check evidence of educational consequence rather than publication alone, and preserve contradictory sources. Duplicate hits or multiple renderings of the same source are not independent corroboration. Geographic/domain gaps should direct targeted discovery, not quotas or invented cases. Publish the numerator, denominator, time, population, operational definition and owner/independent status for each new quantitative claim. Recent cases often belong in the boundary register while evidence develops.

The frozen research protocol proposed wave analysis only if coverage and comparable observations warranted it. The completed descriptive count series and sensitivity tests were warranted; a global adoption/revision time series and exponential model were not. A future reader should preserve that negative result rather than fill the missing series with proxy counts.

### Method and practical limits of this reconstruction

Substantive originals and meaningful variants were read; exact-byte duplicates share reading while retaining separate identities. The artificial-organisations source group was read in full by the state/rights author and integrated with its detailed records, mechanisms, examples and bibliography. Canonical service and education responsibilities were coordinated to preserve one substantive home. General standards v1/v2/v3 and UK capability variants were compared for meaningful deltas. The complete family inventory and numerical caveats have been retained, not replaced by an overview.

No new global web/repository audit was commissioned or performed. Dated source reports may cite evolving pages and new versions; their claims remain pinned to the reported date and scope. Proposed schemas, scores, thresholds, maturity levels, national phases and institutional mechanisms have not become current OCE requirements through consolidation. The final review is a correlated, source-based challenge. It cannot establish empirical learning effectiveness, field conformance, legal validity or independent institutional assurance.
