# Stewardship, evolution and adoption

- **Status:** proposed definition
- **Owns:** investment posture, context placement, Kit admission, contribution, reuse,
  divergence, compatibility, migration, provider exit, deprecation, preservation, adoption and
  retirement
- **Does not own:** domain semantics, organisational staffing, release tooling or provider choice

## The Kit must remain a living boundary

Shared capability is valuable only while it reduces recurring burden without centralising meaning,
blocking legitimate difference or creating maintenance greater than the work it removes. The Kit
therefore needs an explicit way to admit, evolve, split, reject and retire responsibility.

Accumulation is not stewardship. A permanent catalogue of every useful mechanism would recreate
the compromise under a larger framework.

## Context-specificity gradient

Responsibility is considered across four contexts:

| Context | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| **General framework** | Product-creation grammar and mechanisms that remain meaningful outside Oak-specific semantics |
| **Oak profile** | Oak-wide provenance, design, public-interest, accessibility and ecosystem commitments supplied by their legitimate authorities |
| **Domain or curriculum capability** | Curriculum, pedagogy, assessment, rights and domain-specific referents, operations and invariants |
| **Proposition/product** | Audience, situated outcome, interaction grammar, product language, local policy choices, state schema and claim-specific evidence |

Implementation may be deliberately placed toward the reusable end before several consumers exist
when isolation, invariant, lifecycle or assurance makes that the coherent responsibility; the
[investment posture](#investment-posture) below states the frame that funds such placement. Its
claim to **generality** remains provisional until unlike use or a meaningful counter-instance
tests it. This placement judgement governs Kit capabilities; estate-wide code and documentation
consolidation keep their own triggers in their own governing surfaces, such as
[consolidate-at-second-consumer](../../../rules/consolidate-at-second-consumer.md) and
[closed-shape-design-optionality](../../../rules/closed-shape-design-optionality.md).

Mechanism placement is a separate axis: semantic contract, adapter, provider binding and host may
sit at different contextual levels.

## Admission test

A local mechanism or repeated burden should enter a Kit-placement inquiry when it reveals one or
more of:

- recurring consumer archaeology or plumbing;
- an invariant several products could otherwise violate;
- a lifecycle, compatibility, migration or provider-exit burden;
- a cross-boundary failure or recovery obligation;
- an assurance need that local checks cannot demonstrate economically;
- an invalid-combination or diagnostic problem; or
- a reusable composition that preserves externally owned meaning.

Admission still requires:

1. a real enduring need, not code similarity or trend;
2. a coherent semantic contract and legitimate owner;
3. evidence that Kit ownership does not capture curriculum, product, policy or other authority;
4. a context-specificity decision and explicit exclusions;
5. consumer experience better than a direct/product-local path;
6. representative adverse, absence and correction semantics;
7. compatibility, migration, support, observability and retirement commitment; and
8. unlike-use, independent-composition or counter-instance evidence appropriate to the reach
   being claimed.

The result may be admit, retain local, split, reject, deprecate or retire. Every result conserves
the observation and rationale.

## Reuse and divergence

Reuse is not one thing:

| Form | Stewardship implication |
| --- | --- |
| **Semantic reuse** | The same capability meaning applies; contracts and conformance should be shared |
| **Mechanism reuse** | A similar adapter/provider mechanism is useful under different semantics; avoid pretending the contract is shared |
| **Pattern reuse** | A reasoning or design pattern transfers while implementation and authority remain local |
| **Generated expression** | One authoritative contract produces host-specific types, SDKs, tools or docs; generation identity and loss are part of the release |
| **Fork/divergence** | Context requires a different contract or lifecycle; record relationship, compatibility and support rather than forcing convergence |

Governed product-specific substitution or divergence must be easier than an unrecorded fork. It
may replace a Kit mechanism but can never bypass applicable semantic, authority, quality, evidence
or lifecycle contracts. A divergent consumer states which contract it still follows, where it
differs, why, who owns it and what would permit convergence or require permanent separation.

Unlike use tests commonality; it does not transfer authority. Two consumers descended from one
template or sharing the same hidden assumption may not be independent evidence.

## Compatibility and migration

Change is compatible only relative to named semantics and consumers. Version numbers and passing
types are insufficient.

The Kit should distinguish:

- additive semantic capability;
- adapted representation with declared loss;
- behavioural change within a compatibility promise;
- source/domain breaking change;
- adapter or provider binding change;
- state/schema migration;
- projection rebuild or reindex;
- host/control-boundary change; and
- proposition or authority change.

Every consequential change names old/new contracts, coexistence window, consumer obligations,
data/projection transition, evidence, rollback limit and forward-recovery/retirement path.
Mechanical codemods and generated migrations are welcome only where the change is genuinely
mechanical.

## Provider independence and world return

A generic interface is not provider independence. The Kit must preserve future choice through:

- provider-neutral semantic meaning with explicit provider capabilities;
- configuration and credential boundaries that do not leak into product semantics;
- at least one meaningful alternative composition or valid omission for claimed portability;
- export, restore, replay or deterministic rebuild appropriate to the state type;
- reconciliation and cutover identity;
- operational diagnostics that survive provider change;
- a tested exit or replacement path with acceptable loss; and
- clear ownership of provider-specific extensions.

For authoritative state, provider exit includes data, identity, ordering, migration history,
backup/restore and service continuity. For derived projections, clean rebuild from named authority
may be the stronger contract.

## Lifecycle and forgetting

Candidate lifecycle states may include experimental, supported, deprecated, retired and preserved,
but a future implementation should select exact terms through an authority decision. Whatever the
labels, the system must support distinct operations:

- correct a false observation;
- supersede a decision or contract;
- expire a perishable claim;
- invalidate and rebuild a projection;
- deprecate a capability or binding;
- withdraw from ordinary discovery or agent context;
- archive inactive history;
- quarantine unsafe or poisoned state;
- preserve significant objects and provenance; and
- erase data where continued existence is illegitimate.

Retirement defines consumer notice, migration or export, final support state, operational shutdown,
data/projection disposition, documentation withdrawal, preservation and non-resurrection. “No
longer maintained” is not a complete ending.

## Preservation and custody

Operational backup restores a service. Long-horizon preservation keeps a meaningful object
identifiable and intelligible across changing software and providers. Where applicable, the Kit
must help name:

- the object and significant properties being preserved;
- authoritative origin, provenance and authenticity;
- fixity and custody history;
- representation information and independent readability;
- relationship to corrected or withdrawn source claims;
- lawful retention, erasure and disposal; and
- the future authority allowed to interpret or migrate it.

Preservation must not keep obsolete guidance operationally active or prevent legitimate correction.

## Adoption is capability, not installation

Adoption paths differ by consumer:

- **Oak product and design teams** need the Kit to integrate existing authorities and practices
  without becoming a parallel product methodology.
- **Curriculum and professional practitioners** need legible review, challenge and correction
  points, not technical ownership transferred to them.
- **Engineering consumers** need coherent contracts, paved paths, diagnostics and governed
  product-specific substitution.
- **Partners and ecosystem builders** need public documentation, source/provenance, independent
  composition, provider choice and a contribution boundary that does not assume internal context.
- **Agents** need stable identity, authority/status and machine-readable relationships while
  preserving human decision gates.

Installing a package or copying a template is not adoption. Useful evidence includes activated
use, reduced archaeology, correct independent composition, retained divergence, successful
upgrade/exit, and decisions or outcomes improved without authority drift.

## Investment posture

The Kit is deliberate public infrastructure. Capability is built ahead of the propositions that
will use it, through the admission and placement discipline this document defines, as a conscious
investment whose return is long-term public benefit: creators reach truthful, excellent
experiences with most recurring work already settled. Effort-collapse for creators is a designed
consequence of the up-front excellence the
[foundational thesis](concept-lineage-and-thesis.md) states, and prioritisation weighs one
investment of effort against another within that public-benefit frame.

## Sustainability

Every admitted capability creates stewardship cost: release, compatibility, documentation,
security, operation, migration, support and retirement. Admission should name maintenance
capacity, licensing, supply-chain obligations, provider dependency, resource/cost externalities
and what happens if support ends.

The right measure is lifecycle cost per correct outcome, not size of the Kit or number of reused
packages. A small capability with high semantic leverage may be valuable; a widely copied helper
may not belong in the Kit.

## Reopening conditions

Placement should be reversed when unlike consumers reject the same contract for a semantic reason,
governed extension becomes harder than local ownership, stewardship cannot be sustained, provider
exit proves false, or the Kit becomes an authority bottleneck. History and migration remain
inspectable when the responsibility moves.
