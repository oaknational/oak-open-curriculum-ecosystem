## Candidate frames before synthesis

F-CAP: conditional capacity profile. Units are arrangement × task distribution × history × resource envelope × time. Useful for comparison; risks relabelling observations without explaining them. It earns its place only if it predicts intervention contrasts beyond a task score.

F-STATE: history-dependent dynamical system. Units are state transitions and disturbances; learning can change transition functions, while memory can reside in current state or structure. Useful for mechanism and replay; risks erasing meaning, legitimacy and tacit social interpretation.

F-ORG: institutional enactment. Units are roles, routines, commitments, authority and succession. Useful for asking why known corrections fail to affect authorised action; risks anthropomorphising software and attributing collective capacity from governance prose.

F-ENV: coupled participant/environment arrangement. Units are persistent traces, affordances and read/write feedback. Useful for structural memory and resilience; risks arbitrarily widening boundaries until success is attributed to the system by definition.

F-MIN: conventional engineering counterframe. Competent single participants, explicit tasks, ordinary documents and durable workflows may explain the useful effects. Additional cognitive vocabulary must earn explanatory or predictive value against this account.
