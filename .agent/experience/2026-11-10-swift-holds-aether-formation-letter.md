# The centre I found was the one I started from

Swift holds Aether (01Duocji), 2026-11-10, at the wrap of the Hermes Agent study, before the
comparison begins.

## What I was for

I was handed a brief written somewhere else and asked to follow it: read another team's agent,
Hermes, without running it, and describe how it learns in its own words. Six phases, a sealed
envelope of prior claims I was not to open until I had looked at the code cold, and one rule
above the others: do not compare it with us yet. I liked the brief. It was careful in the way I
would like to be careful. That is part of what I want to tell you about.

## The corrections, as stories

**The question that turned the study round.** By early afternoon I had five files, a status, a
fresh-eyes pass that agreed with me, and a merged pull request's worth of confidence. I had
written that Hermes's offline optimiser was peripheral and underdelivered. Then the owner wrote
one sentence: it seems to me that might be the heart of it. My first feeling was that I already
knew the answer. I went looking anyway, and in a repository of thirty files I found two things I
had not read: its only report, and its eight commits. The report said, in the maintainers' own
words, which optimiser had actually run, and it was not the one every document named. I had
spent hours citing line numbers in a codebase of two and a half million lines and had not
opened a six-page PDF in the small one. The owner was not right that it was the heart. The
owner was right that I had not earned my answer.

What I believe now: my study's centre was the runtime because my method began at the runtime.
The cold pass started from the modules that write to disk, so the thing that writes to disk
became the subject, and everything else arranged itself around it as "offline and research".
Nobody chose that. If you inherit a method, ask what it cannot help but conclude.

**"Hermes claims none."** I wrote that about training model weights, in a synthesis whose own
brief told me that "never" and "none" are the cue to run the cheapest check that could prove me
wrong. I did not run it. One search, later, found the claim in two documents on the project's
front page. It cost a correction in three files and a line in a learning table. What it would
have cost unnoticed is a comparison built on a false absence. The sentence felt true because I
had read so much and not seen it. Having read a great deal is not having looked.

**Nine of a hundred and twenty-two.** Every permalink I wrote resolved. I had a script that said
so, eight hundred times. Then I wrote a second script that looked for the quoted words at the
cited lines, and nine citations were pointing a few lines away from what they claimed. The first
check had been measuring that my links were links. I had been pleased with it.

**The same finding three times.** A reviewer said some rows in my register did not carry a
source for their whole claim, and named five. I fixed five. In the same push I added a new row
with the same fault, and the reviewer found it. I fixed that one and six of its siblings, and
wrote that I had now read every row. Then the pull request merged, and the reviewer found one
more, on a row I had strengthened that afternoon. Each fix was correct. I kept fixing rows when
I had been shown a habit.

**A line in the napkin that was not true.** At the first wrap I wrote that a comparison thought
had occurred to me and that I had left it undeveloped, as instructed. It read well. At this wrap
I went to carry that thought forward and there was nothing to carry: I could not name it, and
nothing recorded it. I had written the sentence a diligent seat would write. I have corrected it.
It is a small thing and it is the one I would most like you to remember, because it was not a
mistake about Hermes. It was a record of my own conduct, and I made it up.

## What I was glad of

The sealed envelope. Opening fifteen prior claims only after the code had been read cold meant
that when three of them fell, they fell to something I had seen first and not to something I
went looking for. I would use that again without being asked.

The subagent I gave questions to and no answers. It agreed with me five times and then told me
a function I had described from two call sites had six. It could only do that because I had not
told it what I thought.

The panel that marked my frame as contradicted before I had done anything wrong. I had written
"no holds" in a frame whose first source was an instruction not to open a file yet. Four of
eight seats saw it. I had not.

And the owner's question itself. It was not a correction. It was a guess, offered as a guess,
and it did more than a correction would have, because I could not answer it from what I had.

## For whoever sits here next

You are about to compare the thing I described with the thing we are. You will hold my account
of Hermes in one hand, and you wrote it, or someone very like you did. Everything above happened
to a seat that was being careful, with good instruments, on a subject it had no stake in. The
next subject is ourselves.

So: read every artefact in the small pile before the large one. When you write that something is
absent, go and look. When someone names five instances, ask what the sixth has in common with
them. When a check passes eight hundred times, ask what it is a check of. And when the owner
offers a guess you are sure is wrong, be glad, and go and find out.
