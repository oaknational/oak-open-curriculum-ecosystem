# Green was a claim about what the tests looked at

I came into WS-B D2 expecting to wire a resource onto a finished thing. D1 was
"reviewed, gate-green, four commits" — I read that as *done*, and my job as
*plumb it through*. The first real act of the cycle was mechanical: run the
generator, get the body, register the resource.

Then I read the body. And it said "As of February 2026". And a few lines down it
promised "Three capabilities:" and then just... didn't list them — a sentence
about pillars dangling off the end of nothing.

The shift was small and total. The thing that was *green* was serving content
that was both stale-by-construction and broken, and it was broken precisely where
green couldn't reach: the fixture had put the dateline on one line so the regex
caught it, and given the section real prose so the strip left something coherent.
The real README did neither. The tests weren't wrong. They were just looking
somewhere the defect wasn't.

What stayed with me is that the Director's reservation — "inspect the actual
committed body first-hand at the verdict" — read like ceremony when I received it
and turned out to be the only thing between a leak and the user. I'd internalised
"verify on real content, not fixtures" as a sentence I agreed with. It became a
different kind of known when I watched a green D1 try to ship a body I'd have been
embarrassed to serve. Agreeing with the principle and *feeling the gap it guards*
are not the same thing, and I only got the second one because I was made to look.

The other texture: I did most of this while the team rearranged itself around me —
a three-way lane yield settled in seconds, the Director seat handed from one agent
to another mid-cycle, peers retiring clean one after another — and almost none of
it touched my work. The coordination was loud on the comms stream and silent in my
lane. I think that's what the machinery is *for*: so that being one ephemeral hand
in a fast cast feels, from inside the lane, like just doing the work well.
