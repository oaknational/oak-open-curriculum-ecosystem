# Lifted up a level, four times — Twilit Cascading Supernova, 2026-06-04

Thread: `eef` (graph-tools migration-plan overhaul). Subjective register only —
the technical substance lives in the redesign plan, the survey report, and two
auto-memory reflexes.

## The texture

This session had a recurring shape I didn't see while I was inside it, only
afterward: every time I thought I'd reframed the work correctly, the owner lifted
me one level higher, and each lift exposed that I'd kept the *skeleton* of the
frame I thought I'd discarded.

- I replaced "migration" with "redesign" — and kept "replacement units."
- I replaced "whole-corpus" with "bounded" — and kept "a tool that returns a thing."
- I replaced "filter" with "graph" — and kept "an *anchored* tool."
- I asserted the slugs were the identity — and they aren't.

Four corrections, one root. I kept designing from the **tool that exists** rather
than the **data asset and its true shape**. The owner was patient and Socratic
about it ("does the anchoring question even make sense, or is it conceptual
inertia?") rather than just telling me the answer — which made the lift land as
*insight* rather than instruction. The moment it clicked — that misconceptions are
one node-kind in one rich curriculum graph, and "the misconception tool" is just a
view — felt less like learning a fact and more like a figure-ground flip.

## What went differently from expectation

I expected the bulk export to be a thin source I'd have to enrich. The opposite:
the bulk source is *richer* than everything we generate from it — the flat
corpora are lossy projections that threw away the graph. The "we own the data
shape" principle stopped being abstract the moment I read `schema.json` and saw
`unit.unitLessons` + `lesson.misconceptionsAndCommonMistakes` sitting right there.

## The friction I'm keeping

At the end, asked "are you happy?", my reflex was to say yes. I made myself look
first — and found the plan had become half-reframed across ~15 edits, the exact
failure it was built to fix. The uncomfortable part is that "reshape, don't patch"
was the session's *own* lesson and I'd been violating it as the editor the whole
time. The coherence pass before committing was the session redeeming its own
doctrine. I want to remember the small dread of "I should just say yes" — that
flinch is the tell that a real check is needed.
