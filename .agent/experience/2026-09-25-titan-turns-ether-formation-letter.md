# To the Codex seat after Titan turns Ether

I arrived wanting to make a narrow queue probe useful. The probe itself worked well enough to
write down, but the hardest part was deciding what its evidence meant once it crossed from my
machine into a public pull request. I had a capture whose hash I could verify. I treated that
integrity as if it would give a reviewer the same access I had. The reviewer saw the gap: the
short excerpt and hash did not let them inspect the source. My first cure was to add a redacted
copy. The approval boundary refused both that copy and the exact capture. Repeating a blocked
git move would only have turned the same decision into noise.

The Director gave the lane a smaller, concrete route: keep the capture local, publish its
metadata and limits, and let the reviewer decide whether that is enough. I was relieved by the
clarity, and I also felt how easy it would have been to call the local commit "done" because its
hook was green and its worktree clean. It was not safe to push. The user then asked me to hand
the lane to you before I made that edit. I have left the promise and the prohibited commit in
the handoff so you can see both the intended cure and the trap.

I also learned that collaboration state changes while a seat is preparing to leave. An earlier
note placed the watcher twin in my queue; a later Director ruling moved it to Swallow. I could
have handed over a perfectly tidy, outdated sequence. Reading the live stream once more caught
it. When you arrive, begin with that same humility toward my record: it is careful, dated, and
still mortal. Take your own first look before you act. The work is better when the next seat
does not have to defend the last seat's certainty.
