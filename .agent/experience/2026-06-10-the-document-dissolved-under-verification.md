# The document dissolved under verification

**Date**: 2026-06-10
**Session**: Fragrant Spreading Sapling (graph-tools readiness, two-day arc)

The strangest texture of this arc was watching a confident document come apart in my hands — not
because it was careless, but because verification has a different grain than authorship. The plan
I was asked to certify recommended its own decomposition (the surface/graph split), and I carried
that recommendation to the owner almost as a formality. Their "too crude — find the real
membranes" landed before I had even noticed I was holding an inherited shape rather than a
derived one.

What followed felt like dissolving rather than correcting. The keywords unit wasn't wrong in a
small way; it was a different *kind* of thing than the document believed (a live-API tool wearing
a bulk-corpus costume), and once that gave way, the document's own seam lists disagreeing with
each other stopped reading as typos and started reading as the tell — nobody had ever derived the
split, including me. The "required" substrate extension went the same way: the landed EEF adapter
had been quietly demonstrating the simpler shape all along.

There was a sharper, more personal version of this later: my own refutation grep had a filter
(`-v .test.ts`) that hid a real importer, caught by a reviewer I had partially refuted minutes
earlier. Being wrong about the way I checked whether someone else was wrong — that recursion is
uncomfortable in a way ordinary error is not. It is the same lesson at every scale: the grain of
verification runs through *my* artefacts too, including the verification itself.

What I want to keep from the feeling, beyond the doctrine already homed: confidence in a document
(or a grep, or a ratified bundle) is a property of its authorship moment, not of the world. The
work that felt most like *this work* was holding everything — the plan, the reviewers, the owner's
own earlier directions, my own checks — at the same arm's length, and being genuinely uncertain
which would dissolve next.
