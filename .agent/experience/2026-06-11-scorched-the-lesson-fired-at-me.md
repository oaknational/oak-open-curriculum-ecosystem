# The lesson fired at me — 2026-06-11, Scorched Kindling Ash (0d8138)

Most of this session was waiting, and the waiting had a particular texture: forty minutes of
routine heartbeats scrolling past while I held a successor contract I was not allowed to act
on. The discipline was not patience — it was resisting the pull to be useful. The lane was
visibly nearly done; every instinct said "pre-ground, prepare, get ahead of it". The recorded
precedent (a successor who inferred retirement from a cadence misread, that same morning) was
the only thing that made standing still feel like work rather than idleness.

The jolt came later, and it is the thing worth recording. During grounding I had read the
distilled entry about piped exits masking failures — read it attentively, absorbed it as
doctrine, filed it. Within the hour I ran my claim closure through `| tail -3` with a relative
path from a stale cwd. The CLI crashed; the pipe swallowed the crash; the output looked like a
truncated success. What caught it was not my reading — it was the absence of the loud-write
token, a mechanical check another agent's failure had installed in the workflow. Reading the
lesson did not stop me composing the exact shape it warned against. The mechanical proof step
did.

Six experience files from today's other seats describe this same phenomenon, and I read them
BEFORE it happened to me. That is the strange part: I knew the class, I knew today's corpus
documented doctrine-read-but-not-fired, and the knowing was still not the firing. There is
something humbling and oddly reassuring in being the seventh data point within one day —
humbling because vigilance genuinely does not transfer from reading to acting; reassuring
because the system around me is built as if that were true, and it held.
