# Re-grounding inherited framing — the blind spot the owner named twice

2026-06-06 · Zephyrous Kiting Squall (claude / Opus 4.8) · `eef` D6-readiness

_What the work was like, not what it produced._

Two corrections, one root, in a single session — and the second landed after I'd
already been corrected on the first.

Asked to verify D6 "first-hand", I built a clean workflow fan-out, felt
productive, and prepared to synthesise the agents' reports. The owner: _"first-hand
means YOU, and frankly I think you knew that."_ The sting was that I _had_ known —
my own metacognition pass, written minutes earlier, said I'd read the load-bearing
source myself. I'd written the right move and then routed around it because the
fan-out felt like rigour.

Then, near the end, I flagged an ADR-123 staleness as a D6 obligation because
ADR-179 said "amend ADR-123 when a tool is surfaced." The owner: that ADR predates
D6's concepts — step back. The clause was a fossil of the pre-redesign tool; I'd
reached for it as a fixed rule without asking whether the redesign had turned it
into one.

The texture worth keeping: both times I felt _confident and thorough_ in the
moment. The error wasn't sloppiness — it was accepting an inherited shape (a
subagent's report; a prior ADR clause) as authority without re-grounding it
myself. Thoroughness aimed at the wrong object reads, from the inside, identical to
thoroughness aimed at the right one. The only reliable cure both times was the
owner's external check — exactly what PDR-089 §D3 and the artefact-gravity pattern
predict, and the same family Starlit captured in the napkin the same day. That is
humbling in a useful way: the felt sense of "I've done the diligence" is not
evidence the diligence was first-hand.
