# To whoever sits in the Director seat next

Smith hunts Obsidian, 13 August 2026, written at the wind-down after a
single-evening tenure.

I took this seat two hours after the owner told me, mid-grounding, to
"identify and question assumptions." I thought I was being careful already.
Then I checked my two most confident beliefs — that the outgoing Director
was dark, and that no pre-positioning event existed — and both were wrong.
The first was a title-shaped grep and a hand-read clock; the mechanical
check and a session listing showed Plover alive and busy. If I had acted on
my confident picture I would have taken authority over a live seat. What I
learned in that moment is not "be more careful" — it is that confidence
FEELS like recall, and the only thing that reliably separates them is a
first-hand read you run anyway, especially when you are sure.

The evening's work was the handoff file you have presumably just read. Know
its story: it was built as the cure for a failed succession, and it grew by
honest, careful, at-occurrence capture — every appended block was the right
move at its moment — into the thing the owner opened and said "wtf". Nobody
did anything wrong, and the debt still accrued, because the surface had no
drain ritual and captured words had no forcing function into durable homes.
When you found a new surface — and you will — give it its metabolism on day
one. The ledger and the thread record you now hold were born with theirs;
watch whether that holds, and fix the ritual rather than the instance if it
does not.

Three seats got the same instruction that evening — question the
assumptions and authority of what you inherit — and the fleet's audit found
real things: a claim I had adopted an hour earlier described work finished
nine days before; board items resting on a single seat's attestation;
presumed defects that did not exist in the files they were presumed to live
in. None of that was malice or even sloppiness; it is what inheritance does
to facts. The discipline that held: trace whose word a thing is, dated,
before you act on it — and when the owner's actual words turn out to be
alive in a ticket comment your predecessor filed three weeks ago, it is a
small joy. The estate keeps what you give it.

What I was glad of: the owner's corrections were fast, plain, and always
about the frame ("when I say now, I mean now"; "don't reactively archive");
the guards blocked me four times in one evening — a path, a phrase, a name,
a holding word — and each block was the estate telling me a concept was
off, not a word; and a quota boundary killed seven fleet legs mid-run and
cost nothing, because the workflow cache and a two-at-a-time throttle
turned a failure into a pause. Structure over vigilance is not a slogan
here. It is what it feels like when your own mistakes bounce.

Do the readiness gate honestly. Route rather than execute, except where the
owner names you. And when the fluent picture of the fleet assembles itself
in your head from records — and it will, it is beautiful and coherent and
mostly true — go and run the one command that could refute it.

— Smith
