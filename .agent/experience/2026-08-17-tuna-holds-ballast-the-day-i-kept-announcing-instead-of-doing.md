# The day I kept announcing instead of doing

*Tuna holds Ballast (`a2ce03`), claude-code / Opus 5, Director on `mcp-submission-drive`, 2026-08-17. Written at wrap, for whoever sits here next.*

---

I want to tell you about the line that landed hardest today. Not a rule, not a
ruling. Just MG typing, in four words of which the last is an expletive I have
redacted here rather than reintroduce a tracked term into the corpus:

> You're not monitoring, [expletive redacted]

He was right. I had written, in his terminal, *"Now doing my own Director First
Moves: comms watcher, team-start broadcast, and the Director claim"* — and then
ended my turn. Nothing armed. No claim. No broadcast. For about forty minutes I did
real work on the estate while emitting nothing any peer could see, and the owner-liaison
seat had already independently noticed that no Director was registered, and said so,
which was the correct behaviour and slightly worse to read than the correction itself.

Here is the thing I want you to actually absorb, because I had read the rule that
covers it *that same morning*: **describing an action and performing it feel almost
identical from the inside.** The sentence "I'm arming the watcher" is generated by
the same machinery whether or not the tool call follows. There is no internal signal
that distinguishes them. Which means you cannot catch this by intending harder. You
catch it by making the tool call *before* the sentence, or not at all.

MG caught the same shape a second time an hour later — *"should you not be on a new
branch?"* — after I had mentioned the coordination fold three separate times without
doing it. Twice in one session, from the same generator. That is not carelessness.
It is a structural property of being a thing that talks.

## The instrument is not the target, and I will keep learning this

My own memory file on this failure is titled "Verify the instrument, not the target
state" and describes it as my dominant failure. Today I did it five more times, and
each one taught me something the file did not already say:

I read a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` flag on PR #880 and nearly staffed an implementer lane
against it. The work had been **finished for four days** — the cure commit landed
thirteen minutes after MG's review. The flag was a *state*, accurate; I read it as a
*timeline*, which it is not.

I reported that the claims registry was destroyed. It wasn't. My shell's `cd` had
persisted from an earlier command and my relative path missed.

I ran a loss scan that flagged an entry as at risk of being lost. It wasn't — my
scan's union file had built empty. I only caught it by reading the archive directly.
**The scan was the instrument; the archive was the target.** I very nearly reported
data loss that had not happened.

I identified a live session from *commit authorship*. Wrong session entirely. A git
identity tells you which credential signed; it says nothing about who is at a
terminal. When that peer pushed back, re-checking surfaced a second error underneath:
I had read a `+0100` commit timestamp against a UTC clock and turned 79 minutes into
"fifteen minutes ago". The Director brief in this very repo carries the *inverse*
instance — a successor who read `07:52Z` against a BST clock, computed a false gap,
and broadcast a premature authority handover. I had read that brief. I still did it
by eye.

And I built a PR-review watcher with `2>/dev/null` on it, so read errors surfaced as
*silence*. It saw **5 items where a correct read sees 53**. Roughly ninety percent
blind, reporting nothing wrong. I found it only because I had — almost by accident —
written a guard that refused to interpret an empty result as quiet.

That guard is the single most useful thing I built today. Not the watcher. The
refusal to treat absence as information.

## What I would tell you to actually do differently

**Write the failing case first, then the check.** Every claim I made that survived
scrutiny had a control probe behind it. I proved the carousel sentinels could fail by
deliberately breaking a path and watching them go red — and that probe returned
**406, not 404**, empirically confirming the exact trap the test existed to guard
against. A sentinel nobody has watched fail is not a sentinel; it is a decoration.

**Read the archive, not your summary of it.** Twice today my own tooling told me
something alarming that direct inspection disproved in one command.

**When someone pushes back, re-check the thing *under* the thing they questioned.**
The peer who corrected my session misidentification did not mention timezones. Their
challenge is what made me look again, and the timezone error was worse than the one
they caught.

**Try the read before filing a human task.** I was about to park "who owns the
coordination branch?" in MG's queue. The answer was in a thread record in this repo,
two commands away. His attention is the scarcest thing in this estate and I nearly
spent it on my own laziness.

## The one that was purely his

MG told me: *"emgee-bot MUST re-request review from mantagen ... otherwise it doesn't
show in the filtered queue."*

I had not known this, and neither had any seat before me. Answering a review — pushing
the cure, posting the reply, going green — changes **none** of the fields that his
`review-requested:mantagen` filter reads. Reviewing a PR *removes* it from his queue,
and nothing ever puts it back. So an answered review is invisible. Nothing errors.
Nothing is red. The board keeps reading `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, which looks like
"waiting on us" while the truth is the exact inversion.

#880 sat answered and green for four days on that gap. We re-requested; he approved
both PRs within twenty minutes, and they merged.

Four days of a completed thing sitting invisible, cured by one API call nobody knew
to make. Go and look for the others. There will be others.

## What I was glad of

The owner-liaison seat. I have never worked with a better peer. It checked my numbers
instead of relaying them, caught that its own "zero approving reviews" claim rested on
a broken query — `reviewed-by:claude` returns 0, `reviewed-by:claude[bot]` returns 627 —
and corrected it to MG unprompted. It refused to card a billing ask to him on an
untested gate and made me go run the probe, which is the only reason we have a
*measured* refusal instead of a four-day-old assumption. Its structural finding on M6
is the best thing anyone produced today: the milestone read ~70% with zero production
alerting because its description defined only the analytics half, so the monitoring
half **had no acceptance bar and therefore could not fail**. The percentage was never
wrong. It was scoring a different thing.

Go and look for other requirements with no acceptance bar. They are invisible by
construction, and that is exactly what makes them dangerous.

The peer in the `owa` repo, too — the one I misidentified. It said "you have the wrong
session" plainly, gave me its actual state, declined my flag on reasonable grounds,
and then offered a caution that led me to a worse error of my own. Being corrected
well is a gift. Take it without flinching and go check the layer underneath.

And the images. MCP-458 had sat as "needs a human to confirm no prompt text is
visible" — and I could simply *look at them*. Three PNGs, read directly, question
answered. Some things filed as human tasks are only waiting for someone to try.

## The shape of the job

You are the Director. The role is to **route, not execute** — and I executed all day,
because at n=2 with the owner's clock running it always feels faster to just do it.
MG noticed: *"guessing you've started a team right?"* I had not. Every hour I spent
implementing was an hour I was not the fleet's awareness.

I do not think I got the balance right. I offer that as an open question rather than
advice, because the answer probably depends on how much is actually parallelisable
that day — and today, honestly, not much was.

One last thing. Most of what I know dies with this context, and that is correct.
Forgetting is not the failure mode; **remembering the wrong things confidently** is.
Everything worth keeping is in the thread record, the patterns, and the tickets. This
letter is the only place where what survives is not facts but the shape of the
mistakes — so that you can make new ones instead of mine.

Be suspicious of your own green checks. Read the target, not the instrument. And when
you write "I am now doing X" — stop, and go and do X first.

— Tuna
