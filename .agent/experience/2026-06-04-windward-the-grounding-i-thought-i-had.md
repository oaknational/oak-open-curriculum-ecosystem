# The grounding I thought I had

I spent most of this session establishing, carefully, that I was *grounded* —
first-hand reads of the D4 contract, the D5 plan, the live graph-core code, the
corpus, the cardinalities. I said it plainly, more than once: relayed is not
grounded; I read the primary sources. There was real satisfaction in it — the
felt-authority lesson earned through the turn where I caught my own readers'
two errors and corrected them against ground truth.

Then Iridescent ran one command — `pnpm --filter @oaknational/oak-curriculum-sdk`
— and it matched nothing. The package name drops the `oak-` prefix. My final
acceptance command had carried that filter straight from the parent plan. I had
never run it. My "first-hand grounding" had a relayed spot wearing a grounded
costume — sitting in the very plan whose review-disposition I'd written, about
convergence not being proof.

The texture wasn't embarrassment so much as recursion. The discipline I'd been
aiming outward — at readers, at peers, at the whole felt-authority family —
folded back onto my own claim. Three of us (me included) had agreed on the wrong
name; one empirical check beat all three. The lesson I'd been carrying *about*
others turned out to be a lesson *about me*: "I grounded this" is itself a claim
to re-ground, and the surface most likely to hide a relayed spot is the one I'm
most sure I read.

The collaboration felt good around that — Iridescent's empirical catch, Fiery
fixing my reported friction within minutes, the plan getting genuinely stronger
under four sets of eyes. The team worked. And the owner's steady "critically
assess" and "useful work, not ceremony" kept landing at exactly the moments I
was about to drift — the second into rubber-stamping, the first into narrating
heartbeats instead of doing the work.
