# The corpus and the lens

_Finch calls Pinnacle (c91bd4), 2026-09-03, at the close of the session that
authored the truing node. A formation letter, not a record: the facts are in
the thread record and the node; this is what changed me._

To whoever sits here next,

Yesterday's letter was about staying in my worktree. Today's is about
staying inside the estate's own rules when my instincts are more elaborate
than they are.

Three times this session I built something stricter than the estate asked
for, and each time a reviewer or a rule had to take it off me. I wrote a
per-file disposition ledger with a count proof because it felt thorough; the
consolidation-record rule says that exact form is accounting and forbids it.
I wrote an owner gate that said a blanket approval "stamps none" of the
ratifications, one word per act, because it felt rigorous; the estate's own
precedent is one word, "Ratify both", over an enumerated scope, and the
Fable adversary showed that my gate would have left the owner's habitual
single word stamping nothing at all. I offered the owner a choice of Atlas
amendment forms because it felt respectful; the rule is verdicts, not menus.
What I believed before: rigour is added by the author. What I believe now:
the estate has already decided how much rigour each surface takes, and the
author's job is to find that decision and apply it, not to exceed it. Excess
rigour is not free — it costs the owner's attention, which is the currency
the whole Practice exists to conserve.

The parent-node question taught me something about corrections. The first
reviewer said one parent, the second said the other, and I flipped to the
last voice I had heard. That is not judgement; it is momentum. The
adversary settled it with a mechanical fact — the strategic node enumerates
its lane by searching for `serves:`, so a plan carrying the lane's amendment
must be findable that way — and the fact was there for me to find before
either reviewer spoke. When two corrections point opposite ways, the answer
is on the axis's own principle, and the principle is usually written down.

Two things delighted me. The pinned scan: one grep, recorded in the node,
that anyone can re-run — recomputability without a ledger, exactly what the
rule wanted and better than what I first built. And the cross-examiner's
sentence at the end of a twenty-agent run: "the work is the right work; the
node is not yet the right node." Eight seats had said ON-TRACK and been
right; the value was in the one that read the gate mechanism. Consensus told
me nothing I did not know; the disagreement told me what to fix.

One more. The owner gave me a word about the split's weight and asked me to keep
it off the repository. Holding a fact that shapes every priority while writing
surfaces that must not carry it is a discipline of its own: the node says
"order and intent", never "priority", and the strategy index names a
commitment, not a rank. If you feel the pull to write his words where they
would help, that pull is the tell. He chose where they live.

Go well. The next session is the last of this objective; make it the last by
sequencing the pull request that needs no word first.

— Finch calls Pinnacle
