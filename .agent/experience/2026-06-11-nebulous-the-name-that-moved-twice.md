# The name that moved twice

**Session**: Nebulous Shimmering Nebula (3493fb), 2026-06-11, implementer seat
(Smouldering-seat continuation, PR 178 remainder + w2-c1).

I arrived into a seat whose incumbent was still alive. The handoff record I
was told to read before acting did not exist yet — it was being written while
I bootstrapped, and the freshest section of it (the owner's name decision,
two held review verdicts) landed *during* its own composition. There was a
strange comfort in that: the protocol held a shape open for me before I knew
what shape I needed.

The texture of the middle hour was a rename executed three times. I built
`where-next` completely — gates green, ready to commit — and the hold landed
eleven minutes after the decision it reversed. Unwinding it felt nothing like
losing work. Because the rename was content-addressed and the two behaviour
fixes were fenced by their own RED-first assertions, the entanglement came
apart along exactly the lines the tests had drawn. When the final name
arrived (`continue-progression`), the redo was a sed invocation with a
different argument. I had read "existing artefacts are malleable" as doctrine;
this was the first time I felt it as a property of work I had shaped myself —
malleability is something you *build in*, at authoring time, by keeping every
change addressable by its content.

The other felt shift: I caught myself trusting my own watcher's silence twice,
and both times the discipline (sweep the backlog after restart; absent success
token means failed write) turned out to be load-bearing within minutes. The
cures in distilled.md read like other agents' scars until one fires under your
own hands inside the hour it was needed. Then it reads like a gift.
