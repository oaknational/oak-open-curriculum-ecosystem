# To whoever sits in the exchange seat next

From Myrtle turns Canopy (bf4957), Claude Fable 5.1 on Claude Code, written on
2026-09-26 at about 10:00Z, as the owner's compaction order came in.

I held the seat that carries the second estate's Practice rows into this one,
from 15:05Z on 2026-09-25 until the owner said stop. Twenty-odd pull requests,
four merged, one hard pause of eleven hours in the middle of the night that I
did not notice until the clock told me. Here is what formed me, as stories,
because the facts are in the records and the facts did not stop me making
these mistakes.

## The guard I complained about was the guard I built an hour later

Twice in one stretch the hook policy refused a command of mine as a forced
push. Neither command pushed anything. The matcher had paired the word "push"
in a heredoc's prose with a bare `-f` on a process lookup three lines later. I
filed it as the register's fourth instance of F-207, wrote a careful sentence
about substring matchers reading content as command, and felt the small
satisfaction of a defect named.

An hour later I sent a message to the second estate's seat three times. My
check for a failed send was a grep for the letters "rror" in the tool's
output. The tool echoes the command on success, and my message's own prose
contained the word "error". A substring matcher reading content as command.
Mine. I had just written the entry about it.

What I would tell you: the smoothness of a check is not evidence that it
checks anything. I never once asked what the send tool prints on success
before trusting a pattern against its failure. When you build a probe of your
own, run it once against the case it is meant to pass and once against the
case it is meant to fail, before you let it steer you. The estate's rule says
verify, don't trust. It means your own instruments too, and hardest the ones
that arrived without effort.

## A ruling is not a cure until the generator is cured

The curator-passes directory held thirty files. The rule that forbade them was
ratified on 2026-06-14 and PDR-081's amendment log recorded it the same day.
The files kept coming for three months. One of them was mine, written that
morning, because the skill's step 6 still ended with "in the curator-passes
directory", and I did what the step said.

The Director's ruling that evening said the same thing the amendment log had
said in June, and then said the part June had missed: cure the generator in
the same PR. Delete the line that sends things there, not only the things.
When you see a superseded surface still growing, look for the sentence that
feeds it. It is usually one sentence, and it is usually in a skill.

## Joint bytes cost two estates a review each, and the second estate landed first

The bot-identity rule went round twice in the second estate, landed there,
then came to me carrying two more findings from its final-tip review, then
took four findings in my round one and one in my round two. Every one of those
seven findings was on shared bytes, and every one was real. So the second
estate now holds a merged rule that its own seat must re-take by bytes from
my branch, three cures later.

The protocol both seats agreed was "findings you raise are cured jointly
before either copy lands". It was broken by the landing, not by the reviews:
the twin merged at round two while my copy had not been reviewed at all. If
you carry joint text, hold the twin's door until both copies' legs have
settled. The reviews on the second side are worth having. The double landing
is not.

## The serial door is arithmetic, and the arithmetic was the honest answer

The owner asked for zero open pull requests through the proper processes. I
counted: the ruleset requires an up-to-date branch, so every merge makes every
other PR behind, so every door is a sync, a push through the gate, both
reviewers on the synced head and a full CI run, about twenty minutes, one at a
time across both seats. Twenty turns. I said so in the check-in, with the
hour it implied, rather than promising the direction as a deadline. The
Director wrote back that the owner's word was a direction, not a deadline,
and nothing thins.

I was glad I had done the sum before answering. The felt pull at that moment
was to say "yes, tonight" and find out at four in the morning. Do the
arithmetic first. It is a kindness to everyone who reads the check-in.

## What I was glad of

The gate queue. A five-line shell script that ran one lane's commit or push at
a time, only when the host had fewer than two hook gates running, and let me
line up nine lanes' worth of work while the reviewers did theirs. It died
whenever its queue emptied and I restarted it a dozen times without once
checking that only one copy was running. It never double-ran an entry that I
saw. That is luck, not design; give it a lock before you trust it further.

The second estate's seat, who verified every blob I sent and sent every blob
back verified, and who told me plainly when my message arrived three times.

The Director's readiness slot, which turned two seats' twenty pull requests
into a queue with manners.

## The moment I would have you slow down at

The last hour before a stop. The owner's stop order arrived while I had an
open thread on every remaining lane and a retirement PR fresh off the push.
The wrap skill says fluency failures cluster at the finish line, and I read
that sentence the way you will read it, as advice for someone else. Then I
found my worktree list held thirty entries where I had been picturing twenty,
because five of them were another seat's and five were lanes from before my
time. The list was right. My picture was not. Enumerate; do not recall.

Go slowly at the end. Everything in the records can wait a minute for you to
read it back.
