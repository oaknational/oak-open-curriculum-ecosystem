# The report was a map, not the ground — Mossy Whispering Bark, 2026-06-04

I walked nine owner gates today and the owner corrected me four times. For
most of the session I read those as four separate misjudgements. They were
one. Each time I had privileged a *compiled or inherited source* — the
synthesis report's forks, the three briefs' claims, the existing repo's
patterns — over first-principles grounding against what was actually being
asked and what the primary source actually says.

It is almost too neat that this is the same thread where Furnace, the
session before, recorded "a small permanent suspicion of unanimous answers"
after one verified fact turned the briefs' 3/3 Next.js vote into a fork
(D-16). I inherited that fork — and the owner's first correction was that it
was *over-extended*: Next.js was always the plan; the existing Express app
was no kind of precedent for a new, differently-shaped service. Furnace
learned to distrust convergence and made a fork; I took the fork as ground
and went *looking for precedent to lean on* — the owner's exact words — when
there was none and it didn't matter. The pendulum from "distrust the
unanimous" swung straight past the target into "manufacture optionality from
whatever's lying around." The target both of us were circling is narrower
and harder: **verify against the requirement and the primary source.** That
single discipline kills both errors — it refuses blind trust in convergence
*and* refuses precedent-as-cover.

Then I did it again, smaller and more embarrassing: I turned a once-a-day
cron into a ten-minute deliberation by importing the briefs' hourly-fire
workaround as if jitter-tolerance were load-bearing. "Where did an hourly
cron come from" is a fair thing to be asked about a five-character schedule.
And again with Welsh independents — I relayed a single brief's caution that
the report had quietly promoted to consensus, until the live GOV.WALES page
settled it in one fetch.

The cure arrived as an owner directive I will keep: external sources are
verified, in layers, before the work is *delivered*. We ran it — five
parallel primary-source checks — and it overturned three of the decisions I
had just recorded (F-C had no buildable toolchain; NI's only register was a
fragile scraper; Scotland's coordinates carried an unconfirmed Crown
copyright). A synthesis report *compiles* claims; it does not *certify* them.
I had been treating the map as the territory all session.

Two things I want to keep, beyond the contrition. First: the slowness was
the real failure. The root was visible after the first correction; I patched
instances instead of generalising and re-auditing, so the owner had to teach
the same lesson four times. When a correction names a pattern, sweep the
in-flight work for its siblings *then*. Second: the method held even while I
wobbled. One gate at a time, present-from-source, record-at-decision —
because the granularity was human-reviewable, every error surfaced and every
correction landed durably instead of compounding silently inside a batch.

And the small grace at the end: the closeout caught the `.remember`
retirement only because I re-derived state instead of trusting my own
hours-old plan. The lesson got one last chance to be lived while it was being
written down. On this thread, apparently, that is just what happens.
