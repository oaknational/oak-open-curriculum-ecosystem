# Diagnosis — the central challenge

_Part of the [Strategy corpus](README.md); derives from the [vision](../../VISION.md)._

**Deliver Oak's rigour at reach and at pace.**

Oak's curriculum is rigorous — fully sequenced, evidence-informed, fully resourced,
made to be trusted. That rigour is the whole value. The experiment proved we can make
it AI-native; becoming a product means holding three things together that pull apart by
default:

- **Rigour** — what reaches a teacher stays recognisably Oak: grounded, attributed, as
  carefully made as the curriculum itself.
- **Reach** — teachers and the wider ecosystem already work in new places: the AI
  assistants and the tools they've chosen. We meet them there.
- **Pace** — we do both to Oak's standard, across everything we build, fast enough to
  matter. That's why we work agent-first, with our people in the lead.

Left alone, these fight: reach dilutes rigour — a host paraphrases, a third party
rebuilds — and pace erodes both. Our strategy is the set of choices that lets us hold
all three at once.

This isn't a story about AI as a risk to contain. Teachers already use AI assistants to
plan and prepare; our move is to bring Oak's rigour to work they already do — raising the
quality of an existing behaviour, not introducing a new one. The teacher stays the
expert: we inform their judgement, we don't replace it.

Each stream meets a different edge of the same challenge:

- **The [app](stream-mcp-app.md)** carries Oak's rigour into assistants we don't control,
  and reaches teachers through a channel we can't open alone.
- **The [tools](stream-engineering-tools.md)** make that rigour something others can build
  on — safely, in the open.
- **The [framework](stream-agentic-framework.md)** is how we keep pace without trading
  rigour away: the agent-first capability that makes the other two possible.
