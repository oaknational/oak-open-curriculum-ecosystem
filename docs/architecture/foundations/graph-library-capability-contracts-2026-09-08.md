# Graph library: required capability contracts

8 September 2026 · Working definition 1.1 · Inquiry `graph-library-envelope-2026-09-08`, revision 5

This catalogue owns the required capability envelope for the [working architecture](comprehensive-graph-library-capability-architecture-2026-09-08.md). Its minimum profiles are working design choices made under Jim’s request for a comprehensive definition. They are not claims that the earlier research demonstrated every implementation or every OCE caller.

The [algorithms and data structures governance](algorithms-and-data-structures-governance-2026-09-08.md) fixes the current implementation route: our own SMALL Reliable Atoms and layers of composition, informed by the best openly licensed examples at each relevant responsibility. References inform algorithms, representations, contracts, data structures, composition and assurance; they do not supply an adopted implementation or qualification by reputation. The [general architecture](reliable-atoms-and-composition-architecture-2026-09-08.md) owns the common assurance and complexity requirements.

All rows are **required target coverage**. None is satisfied merely by a generic extension hook, serialisation or a drawing. Implementation can proceed incrementally; missing required rows remain missing. Additional profiles can extend this baseline through the same contract and assurance disciplines.

## 1. Scope and support claims

The target is a comprehensive **family** of graph capabilities with explicit interoperability. It does not promise every conceivable mathematical structure or every combination of all dimensions. It does require the named families, their characteristic operations and the required interactions below.

The [requirements register](graph-library-requirements-register-2026-09-08.json) is the canonical machine-readable source of coverage keys, support-entry fields/statuses and coverage rules. The model/operation prose below owns their meaning. Each independently named minimum has a stable leaf key; parent row IDs group those keys and cannot close them. Required interactions use H01–H10. The register is a coverage aid, not a decomposition into hundreds of atoms or separate tests.

A support entry binds one or several leaf requirements to the same exact model/operation/execution profiles, implementation version, accountable contract owner, invariant-owning composition, contract reference, conformance scope, assumptions and revision-pinned evidence. Grouping claims with the same contract and evidence avoids repeated bookkeeping. Ownership is a stable accountable team/role, not merely the name of a software module. Support entries are immutable and revisioned; ownership transfer appends a successor referring to its predecessor, preserving the earlier owner’s evidence attribution. The register defines the exact history rule.

The canonical schema distinguishes supported, unimplemented, unsupported-by-this-implementation and inapplicable. Missing work is named; inapplicability requires a semantic reason and cannot discharge a required library-wide minimum. A supported entry has actual evidence and no remaining required work within its scope. Its referenced contract supplies legal objects, result behaviour and the construction/inspection/operation/change/exchange obligations applicable to that profile. Unassigned bindings are permitted only for explicitly unimplemented entries.

Library-wide coverage validation rejects missing or unknown leaf IDs and duplicate claims for the same leaf/profile combination. It validates all required fields and statuses and checks evidence at the declared scope. A valid incomplete manifest remains incomplete. Comprehensive completion requires qualified supported coverage of every target minimum and interaction, with cross-cutting requirements applied to every relevant realised contract. An evidence item may establish several leaves when its actual scope does so.

For static/immutable models, “change” can mean checked construction of a new value and an explicit mapping; an in-place mutation API is unnecessary. Global enumeration is not promised for an implicit source. Algorithms whose premises require it remain unavailable on that access profile.

Qualification identifies the independently derived laws, reference models, discriminating counterexamples and complete composition journeys that support the claim. A reference implementation and tests derived from it share assumptions; differential agreement alone cannot establish the correctness or completeness of our contract. The governing document owns the survey-to-design record and reference provenance requirements.

A support claim is qualified only for the stated combination. Separate RDF and temporal implementations do not establish temporal RDF support. Hyperedge storage without its characteristic incidence and reachability operations does not establish hypergraph support.

## 2. Required model families and bounded minimums

Every row inherits architecture rules G01–G10, applicable identity requirements I01–I12, seams X01–X09, lifecycle L01–L13 and assurance A01–A08.

| ID  | Required model family                         | Minimum native behaviour and discriminating obligation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| M01 | Binary graphs                                 | Finite directed, undirected and mixed graphs; identified edges; simple and parallel-edge profiles; loops, isolates and disconnected graphs. Construct/check, membership, endpoints, incident edges, neighbours, degree and checked change. Undirected loops contribute two incidences; neighbour count and degree are distinct. A simple profile specifies loop policy and endpoint-based uniqueness.                                                                                                                                              |
| M02 | Constrained graphs                            | Directed DAGs; undirected trees/forests; rooted tree/forest profiles with parent/root conventions; bipartite and multipartite profiles. | M03 | Labels, types and properties                  | Typed vertex, relationship and incidence properties; single/multiple labels; explicit key identity, value/cardinality model and schema revision. Equality, recursion, numeric domain/units and payload ownership are declared.                                                                                                                   |
| M04 | Hypergraphs and n-ary relations               | Finite undirected set-based hyperedges and directed tail/head hyperedges; parallel relationship identities; an occurrence-based role/ordered/repeated-participant profile. Construct/inspect/change incidences and role/position mappings; hypergraph dual where meaningful; ordinary incidence traversal and separately named conjunctive activation/reachability. Nullary and singleton cases are explicitly admitted or rejected by each profile; the n-ary relation profile can represent a zero-argument relation without inventing a vertex. |
| M05 | Ports, open boundaries and fragments          | Typed/named ports, scoped fragment identities and explicit input/output/interface maps. Validate attachment, compose/glue selected interfaces, inspect residual boundaries, rename safely and apply a declared rewrite. Two fragments’ identical local names never imply intentional gluing.                                                                                                                  |
| M06 | Collections, contexts, layers and containment | Explicit graph/context inventory and many-to-many membership, including empty structures; overlapping layers, multiplex node-layer occurrences, interlayer edges and compound/hierarchical views. Entity identity, node-layer occurrence, graph membership, ownership and hierarchy are distinct.                                                                                                                                                     |
| M07 | RDF and linked data                           | RDF 1.1 terms/graphs/datasets and an independently qualified RDF 1.2 snapshot profile; legal term positions, scoped blank nodes, default/named graphs, explicit empty named-graph inventory, matching, dataset operations and selected query/shape/exchange paths. Required addressability composes with this model.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| M08 | Addressable relationships and statements      | All I01–I12 behaviours: relationship, content and assertion targets; typed references; further statements including statements about statements; reverse-reference inspection; unasserted content; multiplicity; lifecycle; deliberate traversal and preserving representation mappings. The three-assertion journey J02 is mandatory.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| M09 | Graphs about structures                       | Address whole graphs, subgraphs, paths, views and derived artefacts with explicit identity and revision/selection scope. Resolve/inspect references, query statements about them and distinguish a structure from its description or occurrence. A query-defined subgraph states whether its reference captures a snapshot or a revisable definition.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| M10 | Temporal and changing graphs                  | Valid-time events/intervals, recorded-time correction history and graph revisions; slices, historical comparison and time-respecting journeys. Provide a discrete instantaneous-contact profile with declared same-time chaining, and a duration-aware interval-availability profile with waiting, departure/arrival and boundary rules. Dynamic updates/incremental views preserve the declared temporal result.                                                                                                                                  |
| M11 | Partial, implicit and external graphs         | Successor/incidence-generated sources; local and paged/async readers; selected materialisations and potentially unbounded domains. Bounded exploration, visible frontiers, defined paging/cancellation, snapshot or explicit weaker consistency and independent completion/coverage status. Positive witnesses must meet their own consistency premises. No promise of terminating global enumeration on an unbounded source.                                                                                                                      |
| M12 | Simplicial and cellular structures            | Finite abstract simplicial complexes with simplex/face/coface operations, explicit closure and boundary operators; a finite oriented cell-incidence/chain profile with supplied attaching/boundary information appropriate to that profile. Validate incidence and boundary laws, inspect skeletons, compute bounded chain/homology operations under a named coefficient domain. A boundary matrix alone is not a complete description of an arbitrary topological space. Generated faces remain distinguishable from observed interactions.       |
| M13 | Graph-based execution models                  | Finite automata with labelled transitions, initial/accepting states, transition stepping and word acceptance; ordinary place/transition Petri nets with markings, weighted arcs, enabling and firing; finite typed graph-rewrite rules with match/application and boundary-conflict handling. These are separate model profiles. Reachability over a Petri net’s incidence graph cannot substitute for marking semantics.                                                                                                                          |
| M14 | Factor and probabilistic models               | Finite discrete factor graphs with variable domains and factor tables; factor evaluation, conditioning, marginalisation and normalisation with impossible/zero-mass outcomes. Provide an exact bounded enumeration profile as a semantic reference and a separately qualified efficient tree-message-passing profile. General approximate inference declares approximation/convergence limits. A weight or confidence property alone is not a probabilistic model.                                                                                 |

M12–M14 are first-class required families in this working definition. Their bounded minimums prevent the phrase “specialised extension” from becoming a way to avoid their semantics. More general topological spaces, continuous distributions, coloured/timed Petri nets and specialised automata can be additional profiles; the baseline does not imply their conformance.

### Property model decisions

The standard property profile is finite, schema-checked data with explicit scalar and collection constructors. Keys and labels have declared identity; lists, sets and bags remain distinguishable. Missing means no property association; null is a value only where its schema allows it; an empty collection is present; an invalid value is a rejected input or explicitly quarantined import diagnostic, never an ordinary stored property. Cyclic foreign object graphs require a reference model rather than accidental JSON-like value recursion.

Property schemas and indexes are revisioned dependencies. A schema change must validate/migrate or explicitly invalidate affected data and indexes. It cannot change predicate meaning invisibly. The exact scalar domains and codecs are profile declarations; no generic JavaScript object promise establishes them.

### Temporal model decisions

The contact profile uses ordered discrete instants and zero-duration contacts. A query explicitly chooses strict or non-strict time progression and whether waiting is allowed; neither can be omitted from a persisted query identity. The interval profile defines half-open availability windows and nonnegative durations in its chosen time unit. It specifies whether an edge must remain available for the entire traversal, the admissible departure/arrival conditions, waiting, and source/target availability. Baseline duration-aware journeys require availability throughout traversal.

For example, A→B available [0,10) with duration 8 cannot be followed by B→C available [5,7): arrival at B is too late. Equal-timestamp contacts discriminate strict from non-strict progression. Time-dependent/FIFO assumptions belong to the selected optimisation algorithm; temporal storage alone does not establish them. Uncertain time uses a separate profile with possible/necessary interpretations, rather than converting uncertainty into exact instants.

## 3. Required operation families

These rows state target operations, with restrictions rather than a Cartesian promise across all models. Each public operation resolves to a named profile before its result is relied on.

| ID  | Required operations                         | Minimum premises, outcomes and evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| O01 | Checked construction, inspection and change | Builders/constructors; membership, identity, incidence/endpoints, neighbours, degree, properties and inventories; coherent add/remove/update and explicit missing/duplicate/conflict outcomes. Profile conventions cover self-loops, parallel edges, empty structures and legal boundary elements.                                                                                                                             |
| O02 | Selection, traversal and reachability       | BFS/DFS, incoming/outgoing neighbourhoods, vertex-induced and edge-selected subgraphs, filtering, frontiers, reachability and bounded exploration. Define walk/path/trail, zero-length paths, self-reachability, edge witnesses and tie order. Distinguish hypergraph incidence traversal from conjunctive reachability.                                                                                                       |
| O03 | Connectivity and order                      | Undirected components, bridges and articulation points; directed SCCs, condensation, cycle witnesses, DAG topological ordering and reachability/partial-order views. Whole-source results require sufficient enumeration/coverage. A cycle or insufficient coverage cannot become an empty valid order.                                                                                                                        |
| O04 | Shortest and alternative paths              | Unweighted shortest paths; nonnegative finite-cost shortest paths; a negative-edge profile with reachable negative-cycle handling; multi-source/target operations and a named finite k-simple-path profile. Specify algebra/order, attainability, disconnected outcomes, ties and witnesses. Dijkstra-like settled-distance reasoning is confined to its justified premises.                                                   |
| O05 | Spanning structures                         | Undirected minimum spanning tree/forest with declared weight domain and disconnected behaviour; directed rooted arborescence under a distinct operation profile. Return selected edge identities and feasibility/optimality evidence appropriate to the algorithm.                                                                                                                                                             |
| O06 | Flow, cuts and matching                     | Finite capacitated directed maximum flow/minimum cut; general and bipartite matching as distinct profiles; weighted bipartite assignment. State capacities, conservation, loop/parallel handling, numeric arithmetic and infeasibility. Lower bounds, gains and min-cost flow require additional profiles, not an accidental extension of ordinary max-flow promises.                                                          |
| O07 | Query algebra and patterns                  | Selection/projection, joins, unions, grouping, aggregates, existence/negation and path patterns; graph/hypergraph matching with homomorphic versus injective semantics. Set/bag multiplicity, null/unbound/errors, repeated matches and closed-world scope are explicit. RDF includes a qualified SPARQL 1.1 query profile. Query planning must preserve the chosen algebra.                                                   |
| O08 | Structural transformations and algebra      | Rename/reverse, disjoint and aligned union, intersection/difference, merge/split, quotient/contraction, closure, DAG reduction, explicitly named graph products, line/incidence constructions, hypergraph dual and boundary composition. Each is typed by model premises and identity/multiplicity outcome. Baseline products include binary Cartesian, direct and strong products; other product notions need distinct names. |
| O09 | Numerical operators and analytics           | Sparse adjacency/incidence projections, degree measures, PageRank, connected-distance/betweenness profiles, spectral Laplacian operations, similarity, motifs, clustering/community output and sampling. State direction/weights, disconnected conventions, dangling handling, approximation, convergence, random seed and normalization. No universal meaning of “centrality” or “community.”                                 |
| O10 | Validation and inference                    | Structural/property constraints; RDF shape validation with a named SHACL profile; finite positive-rule least-fixpoint evaluation; named RDF entailment profiles and derivation explanations. Validation and inference remain separate. Negation/aggregation have explicit strata, completeness and termination premises. Contradiction status is preserved under the selected logic.                                           |
| O11 | Temporal and incremental operations         | Slices/windows, event/interval queries, temporal journeys, valid/recorded-time historical queries, changes/diffs, maintained indexes/views and support-aware retraction. Update and rebuild results are equivalent for the same revisions, rules and policies.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| O12 | Comparison, morphisms and rewriting         | Identity-aware diff, finite labelled/unlabelled isomorphism, checked morphism/embedding/quotient maps, bounded canonicalisation, rule matching/application and rewrite conflict diagnostics. Each equality and boundary contract is explicit; canonicalisation may be expensive or unsupported outside its bound.                                                                                                              |
| O13 | Exchange and federation                     | Versioned codecs; schema-aware import/export; RDF 1.1 N-Triples/N-Quads and Turtle/TriG paths; a qualified JSON-LD 1.1 profile; exact RDF 1.2 formats only where qualified; structured graph exchange and external query/source adapters. Record blank-node scope, source locations, data loss, context resolution and resource limits. Other codecs, including YAML-LD, require their own profile and qualification.          |
| O14 | Layout, interaction and editing             | Layout as a view operation; selected/aggregated topology, mapping to source identities, accessible nonvisual inspection/export and checked edits. Dragging a node does not alter domain order unless that is the declared edit. Derived/aggregated views need an explicit update mapping.                                                                                                                                      |
| O15 | Higher-model characteristic operations      | M12 faces/boundaries/homology; M13 automata acceptance, marking/firing and rewriting; M14 conditioning/marginalisation and qualified inference. Acceptance uses each native model’s semantics, not generic topology alone.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| O16 | Addressability, evidence and explanation    | Targeted statements, reference resolution/reverse lookup, assertion lifecycle, support/derivation inspection and explained queries. Distinguish source assertions, descriptions, derived structural facts and explicit promotions into an assertion context.                                                                                                                                                                   |

O09’s minimum analytics profiles are finite binary graph degree measures, nonnegative-weight PageRank, unweighted shortest-path betweenness, undirected combinatorial Laplacian, neighbourhood Jaccard similarity, triangle counting, a declared modularity-based community method and seeded vertex/edge sampling. Each defines applicable graph conventions. Vector features and message-passing access are reusable extension contracts; a general ML training framework is outside the required minimum.

O10 requires RDF 1.1/RDFS entailment query support on finite admitted datasets with a declared finite query/active-vocabulary strategy and correct axiomatic-triple treatment; it does not require materialising an infinite axiomatic closure. Publish the exact strategy and conformance evidence. Partial rule implementations remain incomplete target coverage and cannot be called full RDFS entailment. SHACL Core is the required validation baseline; a subset is progress toward it, not completion. Full OWL reasoning, SHACL-SPARQL and arbitrary rule languages are additional contracts.

## 5. Discriminating acceptance journeys

All journeys below are **specified, not executed**. They qualify the exact named implementation/profile and do not prove every cross-product combination.

| ID  | Journey                                          | Pass observation / important failure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| J01 | Binary identity, change and algorithms           | Include isolates, loop, parallel equal-endpoint edges, numeric/string-like keys and foreign handles. Preserve identities/degree; reject foreign scope; return edge-bearing path and cycle/order outcomes. Failed batch leaves topology and indexes coherent.                                                                                                                                       |
| J02 | Addressable relationship and assertion lifecycle | Two distinct equal-endpoint relationships; one proposition asserted twice by source A and once by B; occurrence mappings; further statement targeting one assertion; unasserted description. Query and round-trip targets exactly; retract one assertion retaining other support; retained history does not retarget. Three assertions are not automatically three independent evidential sources. |
| J03 | RDF construction through exchange                | Independent imports use distinct blank-node scopes; named/default and empty named graphs remain distinguishable; legal term positions and lexical/language/direction distinctions survive selected factory→store→query→write→reconstruct paths. Equal quad deduplication does not delete assertion/occurrence records.                                                                             |
| J04 | Higher arity and interfaces                      | A AND B→C activates only after both tails; roles/order/repetition survive. Intentional interface gluing differs from equal local names. Invalid port/type attachment fails. Three-fragment reassociation satisfies the promised equivalence; global DAG constraints are checked after composition.                                                                                                 |
| J05 | Temporal correction and layered views            | Contacts A→B at 10 and B→C at 5 yield no forward journey. Include duration and same-time counterexamples, valid-time correction, recorded history and node-layer occurrences. Historical/view revisions and changed downstream results remain explainable.                                                                                                                                         |
| J06 | External/partial query                           | Search spans pages and a concurrent update, then deadline/cancellation. A mixed-revision path is rejected or explicitly given weaker semantics; interruption cannot yield complete absence. | J07 | Derivation and retraction                        | Closure or a rule generates A→C from A→B and B→C. Output is derived, with separate identity/support. Remove one proof while another remains, then remove the last; preserve supported results and invalidate unsupported ones. No export fabricates direct source assertion.                                                                                                                       |
| J08 | Numerical and optimisation boundaries            | Preserve absent versus zero entries and isolate dimensions; show parallel aggregation loses individual identity unless mapping retained. Negative-edge/cycle, overflow, disconnected forest, directed arborescence, general/bipartite matching and capacity probes select a lawful profile or fail explicitly.                                                                                     |
| J09 | Specialised model distinction                    | Triangle boundary versus filled triangle; supplied cell-boundary laws; automaton acceptance; Petri marking/enabling/firing; zero-mass probabilistic conditioning and exact small marginal. Generic binary topology cannot substitute for these outcomes.                                                                                                                                           |
| J10 | View edit and recovery                           | Edit an authorised source through a declared projection; reject ambiguous aggregate edits. Exercise revision conflict, crash/indeterminate commit, reconciliation, reconstruction and stale-cache invalidation without duplicate assertion identity or silent partial publication.                                                                                                                 |
| J11 | Reusable consumer and interface                  | A separate consumer completes explained exploration/impact/history/editing through packaged public APIs. No private imports, erased types or duplicated invariant rules. Nonvisual inspection and source-preserving exports expose the same permitted information as visual views.                                                                                                                 |
| J12 | Property/schema evolution                        | Preserve absent/null/empty/invalid distinctions, list versus bag semantics and schema revision. Cross-cutting validation includes reference codec collisions, custom callback law violations where callbacks are admitted, controlled payload mutation, transformation composition/lifting, cancellation cause propagation, disclosed scope, structural versus assertion status and old proof witnesses after update. Test identifier generation after reconstruction as well as retained membership: rebuilding equal topology does not establish that a newly allocated identifier cannot collide with a historical reference. Test count/membership/incidence consistency after every supported removal and builder transition; simple maintained-versus-rebuilt oracles must agree on the same stated semantics. Use bounded small oracles and model-specific metamorphic laws; no universal random test can establish every contract.

## 6. Source boundaries

The model and operation minimums above are a design synthesis. Existing research supplies dated package-specific observations and reference candidates. Its acquisition rankings do not govern implementation sourcing. Those historical observations can supply adversarial cases and survey questions; they are not freshly executed or sufficient to qualify our implementation. This revision preserves every required coverage key and does not claim an implementation or mutation campaign.

Primary conceptual anchors include [Boost graph concepts](https://www.boost.org/doc/libs/latest/libs/graph/doc/html/graph/concepts/graph_concepts.html), [GraphBLAS C API 2.1](https://graphblas.org/docs/GraphBLAS_API_C_v2.1.0.pdf), [RDF 1.1 Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/), [RDF 1.2 pinned snapshot](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/CR-rdf12-concepts-20260407/), [RDF/JS data model](https://rdf.js.org/data-model-spec/), [SPARQL 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/sparql11-query/), [SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/) and [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/). These standards have distinct scopes; citing them does not certify an implementation.

The hypergraph counterexample uses the conjunctive reachability profile discussed by [Allamigeon](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1444). Port/open composition is informed by [Fernández, Kirchner and Pinaud](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7929) and [Dixon and Kissinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.4114). Layer/occurrence distinctions are informed by [Kivelä and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.7233v4); simplicial operations by [GUDHI](https://gudhi.inria.fr/python/latest/simplex_tree_user.html). These are profile design anchors, not a claim that arbitrary encodings preserve all their operations.
