# Sources and attribution

## What this skill is based on

The curriculum principles in this skill are Oak National Academy's. They are reproduced and synthesised here from Oak's own published and internal materials:

- **Emma McCrea, "Our 6 principles guiding our approach to curriculum"**, Oak National Academy, 5 March 2023 — <https://www.thenational.academy/blog/our-approach-to-curriculum> (the canonical statement of the six overarching principles and their evidence base).
- **Oak National Academy curriculum pages** — <https://www.thenational.academy/curriculum> and the subject curriculum explainers linked in `subject-principles.md`.
- **"Curriculum Principles at Oak"** — Oak National Academy reference document (the source of the subject guiding principles).

The lesson-level structure referenced in the skill (keywords with definitions, key learning points, misconceptions with teacher responses, content guidance) reflects Oak's published curriculum data, accessible via Oak's open curriculum API and the companion `oak-curriculum-principles-mcp-enabled` skill.

## Evidence cited by Oak

The principles draw on, among others: Agodini et al. (NCEE/IES) on curriculum effects; Willingham on knowledge and learning; the EEF on literacy and vocabulary; Muijs on evidence-informed practice; Mary Myatt on curriculum threads; the DfE SEND Code of Practice (2015); and Ofsted's research underpinning the Education Inspection Framework. Full numbered citations are in `principles-in-depth.md`.

## Using and attributing

The **principles themselves are educational and transferable** — you can apply them to Oak work or to entirely independent curriculum work. This skill is deliberately not brand-locked: it does not require Oak's name, logo, or voice in your output.

If you reproduce the principles in published work, attribute them to **Oak National Academy** and link to the source blog. If you are producing **Oak-branded** material, follow Oak's brand and editorial guidance.

Verify and re-cite primary research sources before quoting them in formal or published outputs — the citations here are reproduced for fidelity to Oak's rationale, not as a substitute for checking the originals.
