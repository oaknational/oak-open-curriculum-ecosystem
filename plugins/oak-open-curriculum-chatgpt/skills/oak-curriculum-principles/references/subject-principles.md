# Subject guiding principles

The six overarching principles describe great curriculum in _every_ subject. But subjects are not the same — knowledge is organised differently in history than in maths, and "diverse" means something specific in art versus in languages. These **subject guiding principles** capture that disciplinary nuance. They are the same across primary and secondary phases.

**How to use this file.** When you create or review material in a subject, read that subject's entry _alongside_ the six overarching principles. The subject principles tell you what the six should look like inside the discipline. Where a subject principle names an overarching one (e.g. "applies our diversity principle"), that is the hook back to the general framework.

Each subject links to Oak's fuller curriculum explainer, which shows how these principles play out in the published curriculum.

## Maths

- Pair procedural knowledge with conceptual understanding.
- Align with the Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CPA) approach to teaching and learning.
- Use an agreed set of models and representations that bridge mathematical concepts.
- Use variation theory in practice tasks and modelling.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/maths-secondary/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## English

- Pupils regularly read a range of text types as a group and independently, have frequent opportunities to develop personal responses to texts, and build background knowledge, tier-2 vocabulary and fluency to support comprehension.
- Writing for a range of purposes, expressing creativity and building writing stamina are developed alongside a deep understanding of grammar, sentence construction, disciplinary writing and underlying spelling patterns.
- Frequent opportunities to develop the components of spoken language run throughout; each lesson gives pupils multiple opportunities to talk and express their opinion.
- Text choice represents a diverse range of voices, backgrounds and cultures, so the curriculum provides **windows and mirrors** to all pupils.
- A single pathway through the resources is ambitious for all pupils by offering a **low floor and high ceiling** in each lesson.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/english-secondary-edexcel/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Science

- Build knowledge of key concepts in a way that reflects how knowledge is organised in the three scientific disciplines.
- Pair substantive and disciplinary knowledge, particularly around practical work.
- Take a "big ideas" approach to developing subject concepts.
- Where there is a practical focus, build knowledge through carefully planned, purposeful practical activities.
- Where mathematics is taught or used in science, align with the sequence, language and approach used in the mathematics curriculum.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/science-secondary-edexcel/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Art and design

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to art, craft and design, including:
  - drawing, painting and three-dimensional work — practised discretely to develop technical proficiency within a broad programme that includes pupils' experiences and original thinking;
  - cultural and contextual knowledge about artists, craftspeople and designers;
  - how art and design is studied, discussed and judged, enabling pupils to develop their own appreciation and opinion.
- Facilitate both **convergent** (pupils work towards similar outcomes) and **divergent** (pupils work towards different outcomes) approaches to acquiring knowledge and making work.
- Apply the **diversity** principle by exposing pupils to art and design from a diverse range of contexts.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/art-secondary/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Citizenship

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to citizenship, ensuring coverage of key concepts including communities, democracy and rights, justice and the law.
- Focus on the application of knowledge through dialogue and debate, civic responsibility and democratic engagement.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/citizenship-secondary-core/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Computing

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to computing, including:
  - how computer systems and networks function;
  - the use of data to represent complex information and inform decision-making;
  - the design and application of algorithms and data-driven models to create programs and AI solutions.
- Enable pupils to use technology to present information and create solutions to real problems.
- Examine the impact of current and emerging technologies on individuals and wider society, including the legal, ethical and moral implications.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/computing-secondary-core/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Cooking and nutrition

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to food and nutrition, including:
  - planning, preparing and cooking a repertoire of predominantly savoury dishes, for themselves and others, using a range of ingredients, food skills and cooking techniques, safely and hygienically;
  - applying the principles of nutrition and health, food provenance, food science and consumer awareness.
- Apply the **diversity** principle by enriching learning through the experience of a range of international cuisines.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/cooking-nutrition-secondary/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Design and technology

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to design and technology, including the fundamentals, integration and application of:
  - designing (communication, understanding contexts and users, iterative design strategies, avoiding fixation);
  - making (techniques, tools, processes, equipment and machinery, materials and components);
  - evaluating (ideas and products, past and present designers, new and emerging technologies);
  - technical knowledge (structures, properties of materials, electronic and mechanical systems).
- Facilitate the appropriate use of **restrictive** (focus on specific tasks and procedures) and **expansive** (autonomy and creativity encouraged) approaches to how pupils apply knowledge.
- Apply the **diversity** principle by exposing pupils to the wider influences on design and technology — historical, social, cultural, environmental and economic factors.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/design-technology-secondary/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Geography

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to geography, including:
  - world knowledge (locational, place, environmental, physical and human processes) developed through thematic and place-based studies at a range of scales;
  - how geographical knowledge originates and is revised.
- Develop pupils' knowledge and application of geographical investigation skills, including:
  - using maps, Geographical Information Systems (GIS), spatial statistics, aerial and satellite imagery;
  - using the fieldwork enquiry process and associated data collection, presentation and analysis techniques.
- Develop understanding of contemporary geographical debates and real-world challenges through organising, connecting and applying what pupils have been taught.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/geography-secondary-aqa/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## History

- The overall selection and blend of content should match or exceed the ambition of the national curriculum for history, represent the diversity of the past, and respond to evolving historical scholarship.
- Pupils' substantive and disciplinary journeys are shaped by rigorously historical enquiry questions, so they learn systematically to recognise and carry out differing types of historical argument, and so medium-term analytic and narrative journeys are well blended.
- The programme incrementally introduces a wide range of interpretations of the past, showing pupils how interpretations arise and are constructed, and their diverse forms, frameworks, origins and effects in the present.
- Historical narrative secures coherence and retention on micro and macro levels: from world-building and hinterland secured by rich stories in individual lessons, to seeing temporal and spatial relationships between societies, civilisations, trends and contrasts across time.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/history-secondary-aqa/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Languages

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to languages, including:
  - phonics (sound–spelling correspondences); vocabulary (which words); grammar (which grammar points);
  - listening, speaking, reading and writing, the use of reference materials, and the integration of these skills where appropriate.
- Base knowledge selection on **frequency**, which also enables cultural enrichment and the development of a personal repertoire.
- Apply the **sequencing** principle through frequent, planned recombination and application of phonics, vocabulary and grammar across listening, speaking, reading and writing — first to develop understanding, then to make independent communication possible in new and authentic contexts.
- Develop pupils who have cultural curiosity and are inspired and equipped to communicate across the wider world.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/french-secondary-aqa/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## Music

- Develop pupils as musicians through performing, engaged listening, composing and improvising.
- Develop understanding of the elements of music and how they combine expressively through their application in sound.
- Sequence learning over time so that it:
  - builds musical knowledge, techniques and specialist language;
  - promotes understanding of a diverse range of genres, traditions and styles;
  - develops pupils' analytical skills in responding to different types of music.
- Promote co-curricular learning for all pupils by signposting opportunities beyond the classroom.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/music-secondary-edexcel/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## PE (Physical Education)

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to physical education, taught through safe practical and theoretical units, including:
  - foundational movements and physical activity;
  - how pupils connect with each other and feel about physical activity, with an emphasis on positive experiences and self-reflection;
  - health and the encouragement of lifelong active lifestyles;
  - rules, tactics and strategies in a range of sports and other activities.
- Ensure sustained movement for the majority of curriculum time.
- Promote inclusive and purposeful competition.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/physical-education-secondary-core/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>

## RHE / RSHE

- Focus on the knowledge and skills specific to RSHE, including:
  - ensuring all content is grounded in the statutory RSHE guidance and the law;
  - keeping children safe and happy.
- Apply the **diversity** principle by encouraging pupils to understand, appreciate and analyse a broad range of viewpoints.
- Enable pupils to make balanced and informed choices about their own life.

_Note: the RHE/RSHE curriculum has been subject to review in line with updated statutory guidance; check the current status on Oak's site before relying on specifics._

Explainer (status varies): <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum>

## RE (Religious Education)

- Represent the diversity and complexity of religious and non-religious worldviews, nationally and globally.
- Give pupils opportunities to build awareness of their own presuppositions and values, draw on their own experience, and interpret the significance and meaning of what they have been taught.
- Develop knowledge of scholarly approaches and methods within RE — and the wide range of related disciplines it draws on, including theology, philosophy and the social sciences.

Explainer: <https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/religious-education-secondary-aqa/overview#header-oak-subject-principles>
