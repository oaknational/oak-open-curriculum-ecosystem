Pupil outcome

I can describe how water evaporates from a puddle and where it goes.

Key learning points
  1. Water can change from a liquid into a gas called water vapour.
  2. This change is called evaporation.
  3. Warming a liquid makes evaporation happen faster.
evaporate
—
to turn from a liquid into a gas.
water vapour
—
water as a gas, spread out in the air.
Starter quiz — checks prior knowledge
  1. Name the three states of matter. (solid, liquid, gas)
  2. Which state spreads out to fill its container? (gas)
Worked example
  1. Draw around the puddle with chalk at 9am.
  2. Draw around it again at midday — the outline shrinks.
  3. The missing water went into the air as water vapour.
Misconception and common mistake

"The puddle soaks into the ground, so it's gone."

Response: some soaks in — but watch the dry pavement: the water that vanished from the surface went into the air as water vapour. Show both routes side by side.

Practice

Label the diagram of the puddle experiment, then explain to your partner where the water went. Use both keywords.

Teacher tip

Cold call after the demonstration: "Where has the water gone?" Take three answers before revealing the chalk outlines.

Content guidance · supervision level

This lesson mentions hot surfaces. Adult supervision recommended for the playground observation.

Exit quiz — checks the key learning points
  1. Which process turns water into water vapour? (evaporation — a distractor like "melting" targets the solid/liquid confusion)
